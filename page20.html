#4954. Bitcoin Sees Massive $1 Billion Exchange Exodus

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

According to data from the on-chain analytics platform CryptoQuant, the Bitcoin exchange netflow has dramatically dropped to -12,570 BTC or so as of Oct. 27. The number is among the top daily outflows this month.

Net Bitcoin outflows from exchanges recently soared to over $1 billion in just the last 24 hours. On a net basis, over $1 billion of BTC has been withdrawn from exchanges in the last 24 hours.

A CryptoQuant chart indicates that the Bitcoin exchange netflow has dramatically dropped to -12,570 BTC or so (as of October 27). It's one of the biggest daily outflows this month.

The latest net outflow (if the BTC price is around $80,487) is equivalent to around $1.01 billion.

The chart from CryptoQuant points out that exchange activity has dramatically reversed. Just a day ago, it witnessed a positive net flow of over 7,000 BTC before this metric plummeted into a deep negative zone.

This drop on October 27 is one of the biggest spikes in Bitcoin exchange outflow since October 6, when almost 15,000 BTC were withdrawn from exchanges (on a net basis).

Note that the latest number may not be final, as the data is still incomplete. So the daily number may change.

Generally, mass Bitcoin exchange outflows are seen as potentially bullish. That is because it means investors are withdrawing their funds from exchanges.

When Bitcoin is withdrawn from exchanges, it may go to private wallets, institutional custodians, or long-term storage.

This may decrease the immediately accessible supply of BTC for trading, reducing the potential for selling pressure.

On the other hand, mass inflows of BTC to exchanges often signal a potential spike in selling pressure. It means investors are sending their coins to exchanges for a potential sell-off as they transfer their coins to trading platforms.

The recent event is interesting amid Bitcoin's recent weak performance.

According to a chart from CryptoQuant, the price of Bitcoin was hovering around $80,487 (as of October 27). The flagship cryptocurrency is trying to recover its momentum after a recent drop to the psychological level of $80,000.

Beasley's lawyer confirmed on Tuesday that the former NBA players had reached a deal with the prosecution to enter a guilty plea in New York on Dec. 9. The specifics of the plea deal and which charges he will enter a guilty plea for are not known at this time.

Beasley was indicted on June 29 for allegedly manipulating his own statistics in multiple games during the 2023-24 season to allow bettors to win on prop bets. According to the indictment, Beasley worked out the scheme alongside former NBA player Ed Davis and four other named co-conspirators as part of an effort to pay off millions of dollars in gambling debts.

Prosecutors were able to obtain text messages from the other defendants celebrating when Beasley shoved a teammate out of the way to go over his rebounding prop with 1.1 seconds left in a March 10 game against the Los Angeles Clippers. The scheme fell apart when Beasley went over his rebounding prop in a March 21 game against the Brooklyn Nets, causing the co-defendants to demand Davis refund their money.

Beasley initially denied involvement through his lawyer and pleaded not guilty to charges of bribery in sporting contests, wire fraud conspiracy, honest services wire fraud conspiracy and money laundering conspiracy.

"An indictment is nothing but a probable cause one-sided charging document," Beasley's lawyer, Steve Haney, told CBS Sports in a June statement. "It is not evidence and Malik maintains his presumption of innocence throughout this two-year investigation. We ask that people reserve judgment until all the facts are known."

In reaching a plea deal, he becomes the second NBA player to do so after Jontay Porter pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud in 2025 for his role in a similar gambling ring. Terry Rozier also faces multiple charges in an unrelated gambling ring and has pleaded not guilty. Chauncey Billups and Damon Jones have been arrested as well in relation to an illegal poker ring, with Jones also facing illegal sports betting charges.

Along those lines, GHA Discovery is currently offering a lucrative paid status match promotion in partnership with statusmatch.com, which may interest some people. GHA Discovery is offering a status match to either Titanium or Platinum status. This is an outright match, and status is valid through December 31, 2027 (so there’s no status challenge element to this). This offer is valid for applications through November 30, 2026, though that remains subject to change, and the offer could be pulled earlier, depending on interest.

Matching to GHA Discovery Titanium comes with a fee of $150, but offers a reward of D$75, while matching to GHA Discovery Platinum comes with a fee of $100, but offers a reward of D$50.

For those not familiar, the “D$” refers to Discovery Dollars, and the idea is that each D$ gets you a dollar off an eligible hotel stay. In other words, this match will really only cost you $50-75, assuming you are able to use the D$.

If you go to the application page and click on the FAQs, you can see a matrix that shows how status maps over between programs. GHA Discovery is matching status with airlines, hotels, and cruise lines, and generally upper tier status earns Titanium, while lower tier status earns Platinum. Certain credit cards are also being matched.

I slept on the GHA Discovery program for far too long. I don’t want to say it’s the world’s most exciting loyalty program, but a few things stand out to me:

I decided to submit a GHA Discovery status match request. I appreciate how there’s no challenge requirement to it, how it’s valid for well over a year, and also how many brands are included. How did my status match application go? It was super painless, and took maybe one minute. When you go to the page to request a match, you’ll first be asked to enter your first and last name, your email address, and whether you’re trying to match airline status, hotel status, or a credit card.

On the next page, you’ll be asked to enter specifically which program you’re trying to match, what status you have, and what your membership number is.

On the next page, you have to upload proof of your status. This can either come in the form of taking a picture of a physical card, or otherwise taking a photo of your computer screen or tablet, while logged into your account. You can’t edit or crop the screen image.

Next, you’ll be asked to review the application, and pay the fee.

You then receive confirmation of the successful request, noting that it can take two business days for status to be upgraded, and that it can take 10 business days for the bonus D$ to post.

A little under 24 hours after submitting my application, I received an email indicating that my status match was approved, and it included a link to activate my new status.

That link asked me to log into my GHA Discovery account, and then I could immediately confirm that I wanted my status upgraded.

Sure enough, after clicking that button, my status was instantly upgraded.

When it comes to the bonus D$ offered as part of the status match, those ended up posting to my account after six days, well under the promised 10 business days, so that’s good news.

He also worked on shows hosted by Bill Maher and Dennis Miller, authored books and penned speeches for President Obama.

Kevin Bleyer, who won four writing Emmys for his work on The Daily Show, died Sunday in a car crash, his family announced. He was 54.

Bleyer was driving on a rural road in Millrift, Pennslyvania, where he had a home, when he was killed, his publicist told The Hollywood Reporter. No one else was hurt.

Bleyer worked on the ABC late-night show Politically Incorrect, hosted by Bill Maher, from 2000 until its 2002 cancelation and wrote for Dennis Miller’s CNBC show during its lone 2004-05 season before he landed on Jon Stewart’s Daily Show at Comedy Central in 2005. The Peabody and WGA award winner also served as a writer and producer on NBC’s Today show and as a supervising producer for MSNBC’s Ronan Farrow Daily and wrote a weekly column for The Daily Beast.

He was a co-author with Stewart and others of 2010’s Earth: The Book and the author of another best-seller, 2012’s Me the People: One Man’s Selfless Quest to Rewrite the Constitution of the United States of America.

As a speechwriter, Bleyer wrote for President Obama, contributing to all of his addresses at the annual White House Correspondents’ Association dinners. He also served for a time as the deputy chief speechwriter for New York Mayor Bill de Blasio.

Upon returning, he co-founded Pilot Media Initiatives, which launches civics-minded media programs in democratically challenged countries. “Kevin spent his life finding the perfect words to make people laugh, think and see the world a little differently,” his wife said in a statement. “I was lucky enough to know the humor, curiosity, mischief and tenderness behind those words — and to be loved by a man who made my own world more joyful, more adventurous and infinitely more beautiful.”

#3163. Prospective Mizzou basketball guard CJ Gunn eligibility chase hits snag

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Prospective fifth-year transfer guard CJ Gunn has hit a snag in his court battle to play in the 2026-27 campaign for Missouri basketball.

Gunn's hearing for a preliminary injunction against the NCAA in Boone County, scheduled for Tuesday, Oct. 27, was canceled earlier in the day.

Gunn was granted a temporary restraining order against the NCAA on Oct. 14 that was valid through Tuesday, Oct. 27. In order to procure a more permanent eligibility ruling, and in alignment with the expiration of the TRO, a hearing for a preliminary injunction was set up for Oct. 27.

It did not happen. According to CaseNet filings, attorneys representing both Gunn and the NCAA met virtually, but not to discuss a possible injunction.

According to court documents reviewed by the Tribune, Gunn's representation "does not request a new setting for a preliminary injunction hearing," the judge wrote in a written order extending the guard's TRO.

Judge Jeff Harris, who is overseeing the case, extended the temporary restraining order in the case through 5 p.m. Monday, Nov. 2, "because the grounds for relief still exist." But that won't be enough for Gunn to be eligible for the official beginning of the new season.

If Gunn wants to play for the Tigers in their season-opening game Nov. 3 against Cleveland State, which is one week away as of the date of the canceled hearing, he will need another court order and injunction against the NCAA.

The extension of the TRO to Nov. 2 is an important date, because it coincides with the approximate time Mizzou needs to submit an official roster to the NCAA to declare which players it plans to play to begin the 2026-27 season.

It's immediately unclear why the hearing was canceled. It appears as though it was terminated at the request of Gunn's legal representation.

"Plaintiff shall advise by Casenet filing by 5 p.m. on November 2, 2026 if he will seek a preliminary injunction and a preliminary injunction hearing," the judge wrote as he extended Gunn's TRO.

Because his TRO has been extended through Nov. 2, Gunn will be eligible to play for the Tigers in their exhibition against UNLV on Thursday, Oct. 29, in Las Vegas.

Unless he receives an injunction, that will be his last (and only) chance to play for Missouri. Although eligible, Gunn did not play for Mizzou in an exhibition win over Kansas State this past Friday.

If Gunn is not granted a court order for additional eligibility and is not part of that submitted roster, he will not be eligible to play for the Tigers for at least the beginning of the 2026-27 campaign.

It's possible that Missouri could add him later in the season with some sort of roster amendment and mid-year addition, but the guard would, of course, need a court order for that to occur.

There is currently no indication that Gunn's representation has scheduled a new hearing. Without a hearing, it will be impossible for him to earn an injunction.

The former DePaul guard is a high-volume 3-point shooter, taking more than 90% of his field-goal attempts from behind the arc for the Blue Demons, for a total of 7.7 attempts from deep every 40 minutes he was on the floor. That's a higher 3-point attempt rate than any player on the Mizzou roster last season, including Jacob Crews (7.6 3-point attempts per 40 minutes).

Gunn averaged a 31.4% mark from 3-point range and shot 41.4% from the field during his two-season stint at DePaul. He averaged 13.0 points per game and 3.9 rebounds per game with the Blue Demons.

The Indianapolis native originally signed with Indiana in the Class of 2022, playing 49 games over two seasons, including a rookie year in Bloomington that overlapped with former Mizzou standout Tamar Bates.

Having used four years of eligibility and with no clear path, at the time, to return to college, Gunn signed a professional contract in the Canadian Basketball League and played three games for the Calgary Surge.

As part of his TRO, he offered to repay the $524 he earned for appearing in those pro games in Canada. The guard also served a three-practice suspension as part of the terms of the order.

As of Tuesday, Gunn's eligibility case is in limbo.

And until there is a development on the court docket, Gunn is trending toward being ineligible and unable to play for Missouri.
