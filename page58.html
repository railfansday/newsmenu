The parent company of the Best Western family of hotels has decided to rename itself WorldHotels to appeal to a broader range of travelers. Its toughest audience may be its own members.

BWH Hotels, the parent of Best Western, is renaming itself WorldHotels after one of the smallest brands in its portfolio.

The company told hotel owners about the change Tuesday at its annual convention in Phoenix. Its 18 brands keep their names, so a Best Western stays a Best Western. The change applies mainly to the parent company, though the group's two loyalty programs, Best Western Rewards and WorldHotels Rewards, will merge under the WorldHotels name on January 5.

"We've evolved faster than public awareness," CEO Larry Cuculic said in an interview. The group has gone from one brand in 2010 to 18 spanning luxury, boutique, upscale, extended-stay, and economy. "Travelers only know us as a portion of our portfolio."

That matters most to the people who own the company. BWH has been a one-hotel, one-vote cooperative, and three-quarters of its hotels carry the Best Western name in some way. Members didn't get a vote on the change.

BWH is a one-hotel, one-vote cooperative and members did not vote on the change; in 2019, North American owners voted down a board proposal to convert the group into a for-profit corporation.

Ripple unveiled additional Ripple Treasury capabilities at Swell 2026 on October 26, presenting a platform intended to connect cash forecasting, reconciliation, liquidity decisions and payments across conventional and digital rails. The announcement includes a feature called 24/7 Returns for qualifying corporate customers. Ripple says eligible assets held in a Ripple wallet, including its RLUSD stablecoin, may generate returns outside the hours when ordinary bank-based cash investments are available. The language is conditional: the company has not promised a universal guaranteed rate for all wallet holders.

Corporate treasury is less about speculative token exposure than ensuring obligations can be met on the right day, in the right currency, with a reliable record of available balances. Ripple says its orchestration layer works with treasury-focused AI tools to connect information and execution. That may reduce handoffs between reports, bank portals and payments systems, but integrations and risk approvals determine what a finance department can actually automate. Cash visibility without dependable settlement can be as frustrating as fast settlement without trustworthy books.

The 24/7 framing responds to a genuine operational mismatch: businesses and crypto markets run beyond banking opening hours, while many familiar cash-management products still have cutoffs. Stablecoin rails could reduce that gap for approved users. However, an available balance in a digital wallet is not automatically equivalent to a protected deposit at an insured bank. Finance teams must distinguish counterparty risk, liquidity, redemption access and the nature of any return-generating activity before allocating working capital.

Ripple has positioned the system for large enterprises already accustomed to formal policies, authorisation chains and audits. A careful implementation would begin with narrow limits, segregation of duties, tested exception handling and clear rules for when an automated proposal can become a transfer of money.

The decisive proof will be how smoothly the orchestration system links approved decisions to executed payments, and how 24/7 Returns discloses risks, eligibility and the origin of returns. Studio working to lock things down and work out how breach happened

Kingdom Come Deliverance 2 developers Warhorse, who're also working on a Lord of the Rings RPG, have revealed that they've been targeted by a cyberattack aiming to access their systems and data. The studio are currently working to make sure their locks are all clicked back into place and tolearn exactly how the incident happened.

"Warhorse Studios confirms that it has been the target of a cyberattack," the studio announced a statement posted to their website. "We are working with cybersecurity experts to secure our systems, investigate the incident, and assess its full impact. The attack involved unauthorized access to company systems as well as access to company data. We are coordinating with the relevant authorities as the investigation continues."

"This is a serious incident for Warhorse Studios and everyone affected," CEO Martin Frývaldský added. "Our immediate priority is to secure our systems, establish the full extent of the breach, and take the necessary steps to protect our employees, partners, and community. We are dedicating the resources needed to address this situation and will communicate further information as soon as we responsibly can."

The studio don't plan on offering any further comments until they've got more "verified information" to share, so one would assume we'll next be hearing from them once their investigation's run its course or drawn some concrete conclusions beyond what was outlined in these statements.

That's not too surprising, given Warhorse seem to have potentially gone public about this attack at an earlier stage than, for example, Capcom did with the Capcom ID data breach they suffered the other week. Obviously, no company wants unwanted intruders getting ahold of any of their stuff regardless of what it is, but in the games industry there's the risk of bits of games being leaked prematurely on top of all of the other problems a successful attack could lead to. CD Projekt Red, Insomniac, and Rockstar are among the studios a quick glance back through the archives have turned up as sufferers of cyberattacks or hacks in the not too distant past. In each case, the attack precipitated serious headaches for not just the companies, but the staff working hard to make their games.

It's unclear if this Warhorse cyberattack is any way linked to a recent uptick in AI-enabled hacking, as the world continues to become a less nice place to live by the minute thanks to the tech.

Here's hoping the studio are able to make sure things are secure again quickly and that their workers haven't been affected too badly by this attack.

#3665. Cillian Murphy's Scarecrow Holds A Unique Record Among Batman Movie Villains

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Which Batman villain has appeared in the most movies? Unsurprisingly, it's the Joker. As of 2026, the Clown Prince of Crime has appeared in nine theatrically-released films (live-action and animation), played by eight different actors. Close behind the Joker in frequency are Catwoman, the Riddler, and the Penguin. All four of these villains have a leg up: Together, they're the most famous villains from the Adam West "Batman" show, and they formed a quartet of evil in the 1966 "Batman" spin-off movie. But there's another villain who never made it to the Adam West show yet has a similar record.

That'd be Jonathan Crane/the Scarecrow. After Joel Schumacher's third Batman film (intended to feature Scarecrow) fell through, Crane finally made it to the silver screen as one of two main villains in Christopher Nolan's "Batman Begins." Crane escapes at the end of the movie, setting up a sequence early in the sequel, "The Dark Knight," when Batman (Christian Bale) busts Scarecrow during a drug deal with the mob. Come trilogy capper "The Dark Knight Rises," Bane (Tom Hardy) turns Gotham into a city ruled by the criminals — with the freed Crane serving as judge of any dissident.

Scarecrow is the only villain to appear in every chapter of the "Dark Knight" trilogy. That also gives the character a unique record among Batman movie villains: He's appeared not just in three films, but three films in a row, without a recast or a reboot. "Batman Begins" was the first time that Nolan and Murphy worked together, a partnership that eventually yielded multiple Oscar wins come 2023's "Oppenheimer."

But despite the recurring appearances, has Scarecrow gotten his laurels? Even with his supporting role across the Nolan films, there's never been a Batman movie with Scarecrow as the primary villain.

In "Batman Begins," Crane is subordinate to Ra's al Ghul (Liam Neeson). The League of Shadows provides Crane with the plants he needs to make his fear toxin, and it's the League's plan to destroy Gotham by spreading that toxin across the city. Scarecrow winds up as a glorified henchman, with the pathos of a villain as Batman's foil all saved for Ra's.

Frankly, this is a problem that Scarecrow has faced across "Batman" media. Even the superlative "Batman: The Animated Series" didn't use him that much. Scarecrow has a sadistic compulsion to scare people and an academic's interest in studying fear's effects, i.e. qualities that are hard to make sympathetic. The best Batman villains are usually tragic monsters, and Scarecrow doesn't quite fit —but some of the "Batman" stories that use Scarecrow the best suggest this monster doesn't need a tragedy.

In a red carpet interview with Deadline, "Clayface" star Tom Rhys Harries suggested that Scarecrow should be the next Batman villain to get his own movie: "You could do some kind of like 'Inception,' the audience [doesn't] know what's real kind of thing." But I'd push back a little. Scarecrow is best when he's a way to bring out Batman's fears, and there has to be someone to overcome the fear he inspires. A recent iteration of Scarecrow, and one that shows both that and Cillian Murphy's lasting legacy in shaping the villain, comes from Scott Snyder and Nick Dragotta's "Absolute Batman."

"Absolute" Scarecrow is the villain of the arc "The Straw Man," and his design somewhat resembles Murphy-as-Crane in the "Dark Knight" trilogy. He wears a jacket and tie, resembling a business suit instead of the farmer-like clothes of a typical Scarecrow.

What sets "Absolute" Scarecrow apart from Cillian Murphy's is his face. He has buttons stitched over his eyes, with similar stitching across his mouth. Crane even hints that his Scarecrow "mask" isn't really a mask.

Some Batman fans have noted the lanky "Absolute" Scarecrow looks even more like another one of Murphy's roles: J. Robert Oppenheimer. Scarecrow's suit and dress shirt outfit is a classical early 20th Century style, he wears a wide-brimmed pork pie hat, and he's rarely seen without a cigarette — as was Oppenheimer, in film and reality. If an "Oppenheimer plus Scarecrow" design is what Nick Dragotta was going for, that's a clever meta approach. But is that all there is to it?

"Absolute" Scarecrow is less about terrorizing individual people and more about spreading paranoia. In "Absolute Batman" #20, Scarecrow explains why he does what he does: It's because the modern day is "an age of dread," one where people have to compartmentalize slow-moving disasters and go about "fearing things too big to comprehend let alone affect." For a fear-fascinated sadist like Crane, that's heaven. What created the "age of dread"? I'd argue Oppenheimer's atomic bomb, for it birthed a world where one day, by accident or impulse, everyone might die in a nuclear fire.

"The Straw Man" casts Crane in a role similar to his part in "Batman Begins." This Scarecrow is still only a pawn of a greater villain (Jack "The Joker" Grimm), and the comic never invites sympathy for him. Batman ultimately shuts out the doubts and fears Crane planted in his head, then leaves him strung up on a cross like a real Scarecrow. It's as fitting a punishment as Murphy's Crane getting a taste of his own fear toxin in "Batman Begins."

Milan bounced back well from their defeat against Bournemouth in the Europa League by beating Udinese 2-0 on Sunday. Now, two home games in four days await Ruben Amorim’s side, with the derby to follow on Sunday. It is 180 minutes that will tell us a lot about the ambitions for the season.

As mentioned, last week was a real mixed bag for Milan. They were comprehensively beaten in Bournemouth as Mike Maignan saw red on the south coast and the Rossoneri gave away two penalty kicks plus a free-kick that led to the third goal.

There was a need to bounce back on Sunday in Udine, and that is what the team did. Christian Pulisic scored early on and Omari Hutchinson got his first Milan goal in the second half to seal a relatively comfortable win against a struggling side.

Milan remain in a state of injury crisis, which actually worsened on Sunday as Ruben Loftus-Cheek became the latest to enter the treatment room. He tore his muscle at the Stadio Friuli, which will almost certainly see him miss over one month.

Adrien Rabiot, Diego Moreira and Mario Gila were all ruled out of the game having picked up minor issues. The hope is that all three can recover for the derby on Saturday, but they will miss this one as caution has prevailed.

Samuel Chukwueze, Matteo Gabbia, Alexis Saelemaekers and Pietro Terracciano remain absentees who will take a while longer to recover, so the depth available to Amorim has been very much eroded.

It was a summer of big changes for Bologna, who saw Vincenzo Italiano depart as the head coach. In a somewhat surprising move he headed to Turkey, joining Besiktas, while Domenico Tedesco was the one who replaced him at the Stadio Renato Dall’Ara.

Bologna spent the bulk of their summer budget on midfielders Mikel Amondarain (€10m, from Estudiantes), Tommaso Pobega (€7m, loan made permanent from Milan) and striker Jay Enem (€6m, from Red Star).

They also signed full-back Rahim Alhassane from Real Oviedo for €3.5 and they welcomed winger Samuel Mbangula, centre-back Arthur Theate and striker Artem Dovbyk on loan deals. Matteo Darmian also joined on a free.

There were some big sales, too. Santiago Castro headed to Roma for €30m, Jhon Lucumi went to Juventus for €20m, Giovanni Fabbian was bought by Fiorentina for €13m and Jonathan Rowe headed to Atalanta on an initial loan that will net them €40m next summer.

After finishing eighth in 2025-26, Bologna were probably hoping for another campaign of challenging for the European spots. However, Tedesco would last just four games, being sacked after defeats to Lazio, Atalanta and Bologna, all 1-0s.

Raffaele Palladino was the man chosen to take over from him, and things have picked up a bit. A draw against Torino was followed by wins against Lecce and Cagliari, either side of a defeat to Inter in which they were the better team for the first half.

There are three players at the moment who Palladino will definitely be without for Wednesday’s game. Riccardo Orsolini, Roberto Piccoli and Emil Holm are all absent, while Enem will be evaluated closer to the match.

After that win against Cagliari, there are very few changes that need to be made, given the dominant fashion of the victory.

In defence, a clean sheet means there will be no selection headaches. Skorupski, Darmian, Casale and Theate will remain as the back three. Flanking them will be Zortea and former Rossoneri target Juan Miranda.

The midfield pivot is another point of familiarity, with Tommaso Pobega returning to San Siro alongside Lewis Ferguson, who has been linked to the club several times in recent years.

In attack, one change from the weekend is expected. Odgaard and Bernadeschi will retain their spots, but Cambiaghi will be replaced by former Juventus man, Mbangula, who’ll complete the front three.

Wednesday’s encounter will be the 192s played between the two sides, with Milan having the edge historically, winning 90 of them. The Rossoblu have racked up 54 wins, whilst the spoils have been shared 47 times.

In 2024-25, it was Bologna who were left smiling, winning their home game and also the Coppa Italia final. Last season the Rossoneri got some revenge by doing the double, winning 1-0 at San Siro with Modric scoring, and 3-0 away as Loftus-Cheek, Nkunku and Rabiot bagged.

➤ AC Milan have lost only one of their last 21 Serie A matches against Bologna (1-2 on February 27, 2025 – W16 D4), winning the three most recent ones.

➤ Milan are unbeaten in their last 10 home matches against Bologna in Serie A (W8 D2), their last home defeat dating back to January 6, 2016 (0-1 with a goal from Emanuele Giaccherini). ➤ Milan have won their first three home games of this Serie A season and could win four of their first four home games of the season for only the fifth time in the three points for a win era (since 1994/95), after 2021-22, 2005-06, 2002-03 and 1995-96.

➤ After the 2-0 against Cagliari, Bologna could keep two consecutive Serie A clean sheets for only the second time in 2026, after the two clean sheets between February and March (1-0 against Udinese and Pisa in that case).

On a brighter note, Omari Hutchinson is expected to make a third consecutive start and after his goal at the weekend, he could create a discussion about his role moving forward. The Englishman showed instinct at the weekend, but it cannot be a one-off.

Pobega is an easy selection for the first one to watch in the visitors’ team. His complete departure from the Diavolo was confirmed in the summer after a series of loans; now it’ll be a chance to see whether parting was the wrong decision.

Then, Juan Miranda’s impact on the game could be decisive. He’s been Bologna’s biggest threat so far this season and against a makeshift right-side, he could threaten a Milan backline which boasts one of the best records in the league.

Bologna have been a mixed bag in recent weeks, and their most recent win against Cagliari shouldn’t be taken for granted. Nevertheless, they have lost to every ‘typical’ European chaser so far this season; Milan cannot break that trend.

A company called Arctic Fox Technologies has announced a new multifunction mouse that forgoes a myriad of customizable shortcut buttons peppered across its surface for a compact touchscreen sitting beneath your hand. The Super Mouse may not prioritize ergonomics with its boxy design, but it can replace other peripherals on your desk, like a trackpad, macro pad, or streaming controller. If you’re faster at typing on your phone, the Super Mouse could potentially even replace a full-size keyboard with a smartphone-sized alternative that even includes swipe typing.

The Super Mouse is now available for preorder through a Kickstarter crowdfunding campaign in which it’s discounted to $199 for backers, while full retail pricing will be $249. Delivery is expected to start in February 2027, but take that estimate with a grain of salt, as many Kickstarter campaigns have faced unforeseen delays.

That price makes the Super Mouse more expensive than other companies’ attempts to merge mice and touchscreens. But while alternatives like Turtle Beach’s Command Series MC7 has just a 2.25-inch narrow display on the side, the Super Mouse features a 3.1-inch, 720 x 720 touchscreen positioned front and center beneath a pair of standard mouse buttons, a scroll wheel, and two customizable “hyper keys.”

That touchscreen will be most useful as a way to quickly launch apps through what its creators call a “Control Panel” that can include up to 16 shortcuts, macros, or launchers. The display’s functionality will change contextually and will present relevant shortcuts based on what app you’re using. “For investors, this reinforces the logic of not relying only on the real,” Pedro Fontes, a research analyst at Mercado Bitcoin, wrote in an analysis shared with CoinDesk. “In this scenario of higher exchange rate pressure, stablecoins, such as USDT and USDC, are a simple way to dollarize part of your assets, at any time and starting from low amounts.”

Stronger opposition in Congress could complicate efforts to address fiscal problems, reinforcing the appeal of assets whose prices are set in global markets, he said.

Corporate concerns materialized the next day, when the benchmark Ibovespa stock index crashed 14% in its worst performance since March 2020 and the dollar jumped more than 8% against the real, its largest daily gain since 1999.

The response had been different three weeks earlier, when Bolsonaro finished ahead of Lula in the first round of voting. The Ibovespa jumped 7.7% and the dollar weakened more than 4% against the real as investors anticipated a change of government and tighter control over public spending.

The burst of real-denominated trading extended beyond Mercado Bitcoin. Data from Biscoint, which tracks crypto prices and volume across 10 venues including Binance and MB, shows turnover in USDT, the largest stablecoin, against the real rose 150% from the previous Sunday. The surge extended into Monday, with combined USDT and USDC turnover reaching 1.84 billion reais ($350 million), 65% higher than in the previous week, while bitcoin trading rose more than ninefold to 1,336 BTC.

The increases came as volumes fell elsewhere. On Binance, for example, bitcoin turnover against the real rose 370% while the global BTC/USDT pair recorded a 25% decline.

Bitcoin gained 9.4% against the real while slipping 0.2% against USDT, consistent with a repricing of Brazil’s currency as investors reacted to the election.

The expectation of a Bolsonaro victory was also reflected in prediction markets. In an Oct. 24 preview, Kalshi put his chances of winning at 84%, even as its own article described polling as showing an exceedingly close race.

Kalshi and Polymarket have both been blocked in Brazil since April.

“[Prediction markets] were trading the first-round result, poll headlines and the story built around them,” Einar Knobel, CEO and co-founder of sports data and technology company TxODDS, told CoinDesk in an emailed statement.

“I would not read this as a failure of prediction markets,” Knobel said. “The mechanism did what it is designed to do with the information it was given.”
