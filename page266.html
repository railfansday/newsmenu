Younes Ibrahim marked his Brighton first-team debut with an assist seconds after coming on in a 4-0 Conference League victory.

The 18-year-old, who has Egyptian heritage, replaced Ibrahim Osman in the 67th minute with Brighton already three goals ahead.

He immediately crossed for Matt O’Riley to head home the fourth, registering his first goal contribution for the senior side within moments of entering the pitch.

Ibrahim had previously been an unused substitute in Brighton’s 5-0 Premier League win over Coventry City in September.

Georginio Rutter scored twice for Fabian Hürzeler’s side, with Jack Hinshelwood also on target as Brighton opened the league phase with three points.

Rutter broke the deadlock in the 41st minute after a VAR check confirmed that Osman had kept the ball in play during the build-up.

Hinshelwood doubled the lead in first-half stoppage time, before Rutter added his second after the break. O’Riley then completed the scoring from Ibrahim’s delivery.

Ibrahim arrived in the first team after scoring five goals and providing an assist in nine appearances for Brighton’s development sides this season.

The left-footed attacking midfielder, who can also play out wide, spent time in Luton Town and Chelsea’s youth systems before joining Brighton.

Egypt’s senior and youth national team staff are reportedly monitoring Ibrahim following his inclusion in Brighton’s Premier League squad.

The youngster has Egyptian parents and a Moroccan grandmother, alongside English and Australian citizenship.

Brighton next travel to Czech side Jablonec in the Conference League on October 22.

#4135. 10 Hacks Every Goodreads User Should Know

Goodreads is a popular platform for managing your To Be Read (TBR) pile, finding recommended titles, tracking your reading stats, and sharing reviews, ratings, and notes with friends, but there are ways to make the experience better. If you're an avid reader, use these tips, tricks, and hidden hacks to get the most out of what Goodreads has to offer.

The Goodreads mobile app has a built-in scanner that uses your device's camera to scan either the book cover or barcode. It then pulls up information about the title, including a description and ratings/reviews from other Goodreads users, so you can decide whether to add it to My Books while you're in the store or perusing a friend's shelf. Simply tap the camera icon in the search bar. You can also see a history of scanned books in the camera tab.

There are some books that are worth reading multiple times, and Goodreads allows you to track each reread as a separate activity so you don't overwrite your original entry or affect your reading challenge stats. If you just hit the Read button on the title page, it'll simply log the item as read without any additional information. Instead, select Edit my activity > Add read date to enter start and finish dates for each reread.

If you care about nitty-gritty reading stats, make sure you choose the specific edition of the title you're reading. Different editions have different page numbers, and you can also select audiobook versions if you're listening rather than reading to keep those pages from being counted in your totals. Find available editions on the book's title page, or tap Show all editions and select Switch to this edition next to the correct one.

Goodreads counts all books read within a calendar year toward your annual Reading Challenge, but there may be titles you don't want included in your year-end stats. For example, if you're tracking audiobooks, graphic novels, or children's books in Goodreads, you can exclude them from your Reading Challenge so they don't count toward your page goal. You can also exclude titles if you want your Reading Challenge to focus specifically on one type of book. In the app, go to More > Reading Challenge > Challenge details, select the book you want to remove, and hit Remove from challenge > Remove. (This can be found under your profile on the web interface.) To add titles back later, remove and re-add the read date.

If you're a Kindle user, you can link your Amazon and Goodreads accounts to see your Kindle notes and highlights centralized on Goodreads, including those for library books read through Kindle. You can add to or edit existing notes, which sync back to your Kindle book, as well as share notes and highlights with other users. On the web, open the profile menu and select Kindle Notes & Highlights > Connect your accounts.

Goodreads doesn't have the option to make a bookshelf private, meaning anyone can view your activity. Even if you set your profile to private, your shelves and reading activity are visible to your Goodreads friends. To keep a reading log that no one can see, you can create a secret group of which you are the only member and use the group's bookshelves to track private activity. On the web, go to Community > Groups > Create a group and select This group is secret.

A DNF shelf is spot for titles that you started but abandoned, giving them a dedicated place separate from your other categories. Earlier this year, Goodreads added a default Did Not Finish shelf alongside Want to Read, Currently Reading, or Read, so you no longer have to create one manually. If you give up on a book before finishing, you can still track progress but put it on your DNF shelf. (Note that these books will still count toward your goals if you include a read date with the entry, so you'll need to exclude them using the steps above if you only want to count books you've read all the way through.)

If it's difficult to find books that both you and your friends want to read together, you can use Goodreads' Compare Books feature to see a head-to-head comparison of your shelves for shared interests. Go to your Friends list and tap compare books next to their name to pull up an analysis of your libraries, including a list of books you have in common and a score for how your tastes line up.

Goodreads has granular settings for what criteria you can view and sort by on your bookshelves. If you have hundreds of titles saved on your Want to Read list, customize and save a category to sort by so you always see the highest-rated books (or those with the lowest page count or most recently published, for example) at the top. Each shelf must be sorted separately. Open the Settings tab at the top of the list, select inclusion criteria, and then choose a sort option from the dropdown. Press Save Current Settings to your shelf to lock them.

Goodreads tracks your reading history and stats, but you may want to do a regular backup of your data to ensure you don't lose years of logs if something happens to your account. Library exports include fields like book title, author, publisher, year published, and number of pages, as well as read dates, ratings, and notes. When downloaded as a CSV, the data can also be sorted and analyzed in myriad different ways. To export your books on desktop, go to My Books and click Import and export under the My Tools section. Click Export Library followed by the file name to download it to your device.

#4143. I Tried a Smart Band That Measures Body Temperature During Workouts, and I'm on the Fence About It

📅 Oct 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The market for smart bands is getting pretty crowded. It’s hard to beat the Fitbit Air on value or the Whoop on performance analytics, and anything in between struggles to make itself stand out. But the Speediance strap caught my eye for an unusual feature it includes: a sensor that claims to measure your core body temperature during workouts.

The only other device I’ve seen make a similar claim is the Core 2 thermal sensor, which I wore for a summer’s heat training and found it valuable and useful (though not without a few caveats). A Speediance rep told me their device works the same way as the Core 2: It has a sensor for skin temperature, and another for heat flux, which can estimate how much you’re warming up over the course of a workout.

Core 2 requires that you pair its device to a chest strap heart rate monitor because its algorithm can only relate data from the sensors to your core temperature if it knows how your heart rate is responding. It also asks you to wear the sensor on the chest strap, so that it’s as close as possible to your actual core temperature. Speediance doesn’t have those requirements; you can get temperature estimates while wearing the device on your wrist and without any extra equipment.

To test Speediance’s claims, I wore both devices together for several workouts and compared the heat strain each device says I was under. While the Core 2 device is geared toward heat adaptation, Speediance uses heat data mostly to tell you when you’re done warming up and when your performance may be suffering due to heat.

The Speediance app shows your “heat load” during workouts, right on the same screen that shows your heart rate, time, and running mileage. At the beginning of a workout, you’ll see a label that says you’re insufficiently warmed up. Once your body temperature rises by about 0.5 to 1 degree Celsius (about 1 to 2 degrees Fahrenheit), you’re deemed to be in the “optimal performance” zone. A numerical strain index increases as you get warmer; above 5.0, you’re told you’re in “performance decline,” and above 7.0, you’re in “heat overload.”

For comparison, the Core 2 sensor uses a slightly different scale, or at least has different cutoffs: Between 1.0 and 2.9, you’re increasing your heat training load, and from 3.0 to 6.9 you’re more effectively increasing that load. Above 7, you’re not recommended to train because risks of overheating are too high.

It’s fall as I was testing the Speediance strap, and the weather was beautiful, so I wasn’t able to test this feature in any extreme temperatures. I did notice that, in a strength workout in my garage, my warmup was supposedly over before I had barely done any work. And I hit the “performance decline” level of heat load toward the end of several relatively cool runs.

To get a better sense of how the Speediance band does at measuring temperatures, I took it out alongside the Core 2 for an hour-long trail run on an unseasonably warm day (it was in the high 70s), and compared the heat load readings at various points during the run.

The numbers didn’t match up directly; the Speediance band always read several points higher than the Core 2 sensor, and seemed to show more ups and downs. But both climbed more or less together. I don’t think I’d say the Speediance band is as accurate—and in any sense, I don’t have a gold standard to compare to—but it seems like it could certainly be useful in monitoring how your performance varies with temperature in hot weather.

If you’re going to get a Speediance band for its temperature features, you’re probably also going to use it for general activity and sleep tracking. On that front, I have plenty of good news. It’s comfortable to wear, with a soft band that feels a bit nicer than Whoop’s. I often completely forgot I was wearing it.

The app has a lot of nice little features. You get a circular visual of your day, showing when you slept versus when you exercised. My only complaint here is that the background is a generic visual that suggests you’re looking at a graph of daytime and nighttime, but it’s not lined up with the actual times of sunrise and sunset. Aligning those times with the visual would do a much better job of showing how your habits are (or aren’t) matching up with your circadian rhythms.

The band and app measure all the usual things, and display the data well: My HRV, skin temperature, and resting heart rate are all reported as vital signs, and I can easily see how they’ve changed over the past week, month, and year. A “TRIMP” number shows up among my daily metrics, which I know refers to the same thing that other apps call cardio load. A little obscure in the naming, but a good metric to track. The app suggests a bedtime, and its sleep tracking seems relatively accurate (not counting the time it read a dentist appointment as a nap—although, to be fair, I was lying still for half an hour).

But I’m disappointed by a few things. One annoyance is that, since Speediance is primarily a home gym company, you can’t log a strength workout without creating an exercise routine. (I ended up logging my strength sessions as “mixed cardio” instead.) If you want to share your exercise data on a graphic, you get stock images of Speediance equipment for backgrounds.

And I was never able to properly sync my workouts to Strava. I got a message in Chinese after attempting the connection, which a translation app told me means “the binding is successfull!” and the settings in my app told me in English that Strava was connected. Still, nothing ever showed up on my Strava page. That’s a bummer, both for everyday use if you use Strava, and for me personally since I could have used Strava to download more specific data from my workouts than I can get from just viewing workouts in the Speediance app.

At $159, I’m on the fence. The temperature features of the Speediance strap could be useful for anyone who wants to keep track of how heat affects their workouts, especially in the summer. It doesn’t track heat adaptation, so it can’t replace the Core 2 sensor, but I do appreciate that Speediance is offering something more than just another heart rate measurement.

But the glitches (especially the lack of Strava connection) are a dealbreaker for now, and I wasn’t able to get as much information as I’d like about how well it measures heart rate. If I could overlook those, I don’t think this band is particularly worse than the Polar Loop or the Garmin Cirqa, which both cost a bit more. But I certainly can’t say it’s better than the Fitbit Air or the Whoop. Perhaps with time, this will be a better device, but at this point, I’m more inclined to watch for improvements than to recommend it as is.

#4313. Nekome: Nazi Hunter Director On Whether The Game Is Political, Art Inspiration, Metaphysical Themes, Steam Demo, And More

📅 Oct 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

I've been tracking Nekome: Nazi Hunter here at Game Informer since its reveal earlier this year. I finally went hands-on with it during Gamescom 2026 and relished just how disrespectfully brutal it is to Nazis. You will soon get the chance to experience that brutality yourself because developer ProbablyMonsters is releasing a demo on Steam soon on October 19, and it will be available through October 26.

Ahead of that demo launch, ProbablyMonsters also released a new trailer highlighting the game's narrative and villainous Hyena troupe of Nazi leaders as part of today's Galaxies Autumn Showcase. These Hyenas are at the center of protagonist Vano's Nazi hunting, and they are a notorious squad of Nazi agents whose extreme brand of cruelty made them feared across occupied Europe and the world, according to a press release. Players will control Vano as he kills countless Nazis in violent and bloody ways while tracking down each of the Hyenas (who he will presumably also kill violently and bloodily).

Check it out in the latest Nekome: Nazi Hunter trailer for yourself below:

Game Informer also spoke to Nekome: Nazi Hunter game director Jeronimo Barrera about the game in the lead-up to this trailer and the incoming Steam Next Fest demo. In the interview, which took place over email, Barrera talks about the game's politics (or lack thereof), the Hyenas, visual inspiration, and more:

Game Informer's Wesley LeBlanc: How did the team decide on the Hyenas as the antagonists/villains of the game, rather than say, the obvious choice of Hitler?

Barrera: As we developed the narrative, we had already committed to pursuing the grindhouse aesthetic that would drive the presentation. We needed a disgusting set of villains that would be fun to hunt down and murder. Although we are rooted in real-world events, the player fantasy we are after is to hunt down and murder as many Nazis, both big and small, and that needs to deliver over-the-top fun.

Can you tell me about the Hyenas? What are their differences from a narrative perspective, and what are their differences from a gameplay perspective? Does each Hyena approach the hunt for Vano in different ways?

We introduce the Hyenas almost immediately in the narrative. After that, the player won’t be confused as to why they need to hunt them down, not the other way around! Vano hunts them down, and each Hyena boss fight is a unique challenge that pushes the players' abilities.

It sounds like Vano will be hunted across occupied Europe and beyond? What can you tell me about that beyond?

Our research into how we can tell a new story and give a new experience has given us a lot of surprising locations to explore. For example, we were shocked when we realized that we could have our first encounters with Nazis on U.S. soil! Vano’s adopted home is New York City. It’s hard to believe, but there were Nazi Party rallies in New York. This is where Vano starts his hunt through a tragic coincidence, which then takes Vano to Europe. But he’s not being hunted; he is the hunter.

Is there anything larger than life or supernatural or demonic happening narratively, or is it Vano versus some Nazis that are led by the Hyenas? I ask because scenes in this new trailer almost imply some monstrous transformation, or maybe even something vampiric, as blood drips from the Hyenas' faces (though I recognize that could just be a style choice).

The Nazis were hideous monsters. We play on those themes for our abilities and how we present them to the player. There are themes we explore that could be considered metaphysical. But with that said, it’s up for people to interpret the narrative on their own.

How would you describe the art style/visual design of the game?

Our biggest inspiration came from the grindhouse film genre. Turning the stylized pulpiness and violence of movies like Inglourious Basterds into a video game was a challenge that the team was excited to tackle, and we believe this grindhouse spirit gives our game a distinct visual style.

Graphic novels were another major influence. Our goal with the art style was to create something over the top and entertaining, while respecting the serious subject matter. The game would lose its sense of fun if it looked too real, and graphic novels helped us find a look that is visually interesting yet able to handle the story’s darkness and violence.

How long has the team been working on the game?

The team began working on the game in 2024. The genesis of the game was a meeting inside ProbablyMonsters where we started quoting Inglourious Basterds, and we all became set on the idea of making a game about hunting down and killing Nazis through an untold Second World War story.

Nekome: Nazi Hunter’s development is led internally at ProbablyMonsters by Brett Bourbin, Brand Inman, Sergei Kuprejanov, Kay Willis and me, with assistance from co-development partners.

What has it been like working on a smaller title like this and what does your AAA experience bring to the project?

Going from concept to the player’s hands in 18 months is an epic lift that’s only possible with amazing devs that bring extraordinary experience and expertise. The AAA sensibilities run deep at ProbablyMonsters; there are some legends in our house. What makes us different is that our independent approach allows us to tell unique stories and make experiences like Nekome that would have a hard time getting to market.

After Gamescom, ButWhyTho? published a story about how Willis said the game isn’t political, and it caused a lot of discussion/backlash about the game. I wanted to see if the team had any follow-up on the idea that Nekome isn’t political now that some time has passed. At least from my perspective, all art is political; not to mention, making a game where you’re very much visibly and graphically killing and mutilating Nazis is sending a very clear message, especially in 2026 when we have people proudly stating they are a Nazi today. But that’s just my line of thinking – I’m curious where the team lands on this discussion now that some additional time has passed.

I think we're overcomplicating this. Nazis are bad. That shouldn't be a political statement, just common sense. Nekome is a revenge story about people who suffered horrific atrocities at the hands of Nazis. We're not making a statement about modern politics. We're telling a story with villains who are unquestionably evil.

If someone feels politically attacked by a game where you kill Nazis, maybe they should take a long, hard look in the mirror.

Anything I missed you’d like to touch on?

We created Nekome because we saw an opportunity to tell a Second World War story through a perspective that games have rarely depicted. World War II is one of the most common settings in media. And yet, there are almost no games that explore the persecution of the Roma and Sinti during that period.

That is a real, deeply horrible part of history, so our story also comes with responsibility. The team has worked with historians and cultural consultants to help us approach Vano’s identity, his community and the historical context thoughtfully and credibly. We promise to tell Vano’s story honestly and approach that history with care.

#3278. Brian Sullivan: Why the 'real' price of oil is likely $30-$40 higher than where it is now

📅 Oct 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Oil, in reality, is $125 per barrel or higher.

That's the real cost of a barrel of oil because buying the physical commodity is a very different story from a number on traders' screens.

Oil futures are just that: paper contracts indicating a price today, tomorrow, next month or further out.  If you want to buy the physical commodity, it may be a totally different story — and price.

As I've reported, prices for oil delivery have skyrocketed. Last week, a VLCC (aka a "supertanker" in non-shipper parlance) was chartered from the U.S. Gulf Coast to China for $76 million. Another very large crude carrier went for $77 million from the U.S. to South Korea. That's 10 times more than the price of the same trip less than one year ago.

Assuming these ships hold about 2 million barrels of oil, that charter price means each barrel on the ship costs about $38 over the price of the oil itself.

I'm certainly not saying to avoid looking at oil futures prices, but you need to know that the price on the CNBC screen or CNBC.com isn't always the full price being paid. These days, any company that needs oil shipped by sea, rail or truck will pay a hefty premium over the price of the oil itself. So, while the volume of oil moving through Hormuz is closing in on pre-war levels, costs are not. And there's no sign that they're going anywhere but up.

You know what else is up? The stocks of the shipowners. Look at their moves this month and this year.

Untitled Goose Game developer House House launched Big Walk, its multiplayer cooperative puzzle-adventure game, on PlayStation 5, Nintendo Switch 2, PC, and Mac in August to critical acclaim. Since then, players have asked the studio to release the game on Xbox Series X/S, the only console absent from its launch lineup. Today, House House released an explanation stating that Big Walk is not on Xbox in accordance with the Palestinian-led BDS boycott against Microsoft.

"We have made the decision not to release Big Walk on Xbox, in accordance with the Palestinian-led BDS (Boycott, Divestment, Sanctions) movement call to boycott Microsoft, and their designation of Microsoft Games (and Xbox) as a priority boycott target," the statement reads.

House House later writes that it would love for players to experience Big Walk on Xbox, but notes that won't happen until Microsoft and/or Xbox have been removed as BDS boycott targets.

BDS is an international boycott movement "for freedom, justice, and equality," and "upholds the simple principle that Palestinians are entitled to the same rights as the rest of humanity," according to the organization. BDS states that Israel is occupying and colonizing Palestinian land, discriminating against Palestinian citizens of Israel, and denying Palestinian refugees the right to return to their homes. The movement, which has been around since 2005, is inspired by the South African anti-apartheid movement and urges action to pressure Israel to comply with international law, which BDS says the country has been noncompliant with since 1948.

As for the Microsoft and Xbox boycott target prioritization, BDS says on its website that "Microsoft is perhaps the most complicit tech company in Israel's illegal occupation, apartheid regime, and ongoing genocide against 2.3 million Palestinians in Gaza. [...] Microsoft partners with the apartheid regime of Israel and its prison system. It provides the Israeli military with Azure cloud and AI services that are central to accelerating Israel's genocide of 2.3 million Palestinians in the illegally occupied Gaza Strip. After 34 years of deep complicity with Israel's military, the Israeli army relies heavily on Microsoft to meet technological requirements of its genocide and apartheid regime."

The BDS movement also calls on people to boycott Microsoft's gaming products, including canceling Xbox Game Pass subscriptions, boycotting Candy Crush, Minecraft, and Call of Duty, and boycotting all Microsoft Gaming products, including Xbox-branded consoles, headsets, and all games published by Microsoft-owned publishing labels.

House House closes its statement, which you can read in full via the image below, stating, "We've made this decision with the belief that meaningful change can be made in the world, that coordinated collective action can confront even the most powerful interests, and that one day, Palestine will be free."

#3287. CoreWeave credit default swaps hit distress levels as investors sour on AI debt

📅 Oct 15, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

CoreWeave's credit stress continued to spiral on Thursday amid a broader sell-off in AI-related names.

CoreWeave — a so-called neocloud company — has taken on more than $51 billion of debt and obligations to finance its AI infrastructure build-out. It's now facing a rebellion from stock and bond investors as concerns mount about AI overspending and debt levels.

Yields on CoreWeave debt have continued to climb, with the yield to maturity on its 9.75% bond due in 2031 reaching 15.78%. Bond yields move inversely to prices. Meanwhile, the spread on its five-year credit default swaps, which are insurance against default, at one point soared above 1,140 basis points. It last stood at nearly 1,038 bps — a 16% week-over-week jump and 164% higher than a year ago.

Credit concerns are spreading into AI equities too. CoreWeave's stock dropped during trading Thursday before recovering and closing 5.94% higher. The moved followed Wednesday's 15.5% nosedive.

Other neocloud stocks were under similar pressure. Nebius also sank before climbing higher to close 1.29% and and Iren ultimately ended the day down less than 1%.

Anxiety about AI overspending is also spreading. For instance, shares of Micron Technology and Advanced Micro Devices sold off following a big sell-off in South Korean chip stocks. Micron closed up 1.87%, while AMD fell 1.5%.

While AI debt worries have been rising steadily, investors have suddenly grown skittish about the underlying finances of companies borrowing heavily to buy chips, build data centers, and rent out computing power.

Coreweave's credit default swaps are one place to see that stress.

Credit default swaps are contracts where buyers pay for protection in case the borrower can't repay its debt. The spread widening in CoreWeave's CDS means investors view the debt as riskier.

The market is "basically saying 'no mas' to AI debt without operating cash flow support," the Evercore ISI trading desk said.

A spread level of 1,000 basis points or above is generally a sign of credit distress, said Gavan Nolan, executive director of fixed income pricing at S&P Global Market Intelligence.

No one is questioning whether demand for CoreWeave's services is real, since the company has contracts with all the big hyperscalers, Nolan said. The question is whether the cash flows will ultimately arrive.

"It's obviously heavily negative free cash flow at the moment, and it needs that cash flow to arrive really to justify all the capex that it's spending," Nolan said.

CEO Mike Intrator dismissed the market moves on Wednesday, according to a memo obtained by CNBC.

"When you are building a new type of technology company there will be periods of time when the market misunderstands and acts irrationally," he wrote. "We are confident in our backlog which more than supports all of our debt obligations."

The backlog is over $100 billion, with around two-thirds of it with investment-grade counterparts including Microsoft, Meta, IBM and Caterpillar, Deutsche Bank said in a note Thursday. The firm reiterated its buy-rating on the stock Wednesday, calling the dislocation a buying opportunity.

"The problem is this kind of concern can prove to be a self-fulfilling prophecy, and risks triggering broader issues in other AI-related names," said Jim Reid, global head of macro and thematic research.

Domino effects may already be happening. Oracle's five-year CDS spread also spiked this week. It was last at 282 basis points, a roughly 10% increase in one week.

"A downgrade from its BBB rating would risk pressuring IG-only investors into forced sales," Reid said.

#4183. 10 Shows Like 'Crystal Lake' You Should Watch Next

Ross has been an actor on stage, in commercials, and in independent films since childhood, and has written hundreds of articles and essays on genre literature and cinema for Barnes & Noble, Gizmodo, Kotaku, and Yahoo! This is Streamalikes, where you can find a curated list of recommendations to stream based on what you already like.

The new streaming series Crystal Lake takes us back to the early days of the Friday the 13th franchise to explore the origins of hockey-masked killer Jason's power and fury—by focusing, once again, on his mom (Linda Cardellini). Is this backstory we needed? Probably not! But I'm happy to revisit the bloodiest (and horniest) summer camp in America. If you're looking for other like-minded horror adaptations, here are 10 chilling shows to stream next.

You want some more backstory on an iconic serial killer and his mom? Say no more. Freddie Highmore stars as Norman Bates, the character author Robert Bloch based on the real killer Ed Gein, with Vera Farmiga playing his mother Norma (in her pre-desiccated-corpse days). Norman might not have accrued nearly the body count of Jason, but he's a lot more charming. Like a lot of media spun off from Alfred Hitchcock's seminal Psycho, it's better than it has any right to be, with impressively compelling character development and several surprises, even if we already know more or less where it's headed. Stream Bates Motel on Prime Video.

More high-minded than Crystal Lake, this reboot/prequel/fresh take on the Thomas Harris novels came from Bryan Fuller, who took a first crack at the Friday series before A24 decided it wanted something a little closer to the movies than what he'd proposed. Fuller goes back to the source material here, adapting the first Lecter novel with grand, operatic style and a visual flair unmatched on network television (you're still unlikely to find more gorgeously constructed scenes of carnage). What's more, the deeper, sexier relationship between the Doctor (Mads Mikkelsen) and profiler Will Graham (Hugh Dancy) adds some brilliant subtext as the two work together to hunt serial killers. It ended a bit too early, but the three seasons still make for a satisfying meal. Stream Hannibal on Prime Video.

Just a British meta-slasher-comedy on a boat (initially). Oscar Kennedy plays Jamie Walsh, who takes a job on board a cruise ship in order to investigate the disappearance of his sister, last seen working on the same vessel. Once on board, he soon realizes that everyone is a suspect and, being that he's on a boat, there's no escape from the murderous web of conspiracy in which he's deliberately entangled himself. The show has an infectious energy that blends Agatha Christie-style mystery elements with gnarly kills among a largely queer group of suspects. Season two moves the action to an island camping retreat for the ultra-wealthy, offering up an even more explicit Friday the 13th vibe. Stream Wreck on Hulu.

Drawing inspiration from Evil Dead, Stan Against Evil stars John C. McGinley as the crotchety, obnoxious former sheriff of a New Hampshire town overrun with vengeful spirits. Janet Varney comes to town as his replacement, and the two soon realize the uneasy spirits and demons aren’t going to be quiet for much longer. The awkward chemistry between the leads is a big selling point, as are the old-school practical monster effects that harken back to the great slasher horror movies of the late '70s through the '80s. The show leans more toward funny (and it’s often very funny) than scary, but the monster effects are well done, and the show doesn’t shy away from great 80s-style gross-out gags. Stream Stan Against Evil on Shudder and AMC+.

One of our most reliable horror franchises went the series route with impressively smart results—not really surprising for fans of the tongue-in-cheek, increasingly meta film series. Zackary Arthur is Jake Wheeler, a queer teen subject to relentless bullying at school and abuse at home from his homophobic father. Jake picks up America's favorite serial-killing doll at a garage sale school art project, and Chucky begins to see the (justifiably) angry kid as a potential protégé. Before long, characters from Chucky's past pop in to complicate things, but the relationship between Jake and Chucky remains the show's beating heart. Even with its dark sense of humor and over-the-top murders, the show became an unlikely critical favorite. Stream Chucky on Peacock and Prime Video.

Each season of this series tells the story of a single serial killer and their would-be victims, stretching out a traditional slasher-movie narrative over several hours. With that time, the show (like Crystal Lake) manages to actually build lives and backstories for its characters, something that movies don’t always have time for or can't be bothered with. It works here, and each season has introduced fairly compelling characters—it doesn’t hurt that the focus is largely on adults, rather than more typical teenage victims. The whole series is excellent, but for our purposes, I'll direct your attention to season two's "Guilty Party," in which a group of former counselors returns to the scene of the crime to retrieve the body of the camper they all accidentally killed, and whose death they purposely concealed years earlier. Stream Slasher on Netflix.

Cheating just a tiny bit by including this series, technically a run of movies, but it plays so much like a miniseries as to be pretty indistinguishable. Adapted from the R. L. Stine books, Fear Street Part One: 1994 kicks off the series by introducing the town of Shadyside, which the local kids call “Shittyside,” and which has a dark history of multiple murders. A group of teens upsets the grave of a witch, kicking off the revival of a killer cult, which requires a trip back in time to truly understand. There is some legit gore and scares (it’s YA, more or less, but definitely not kids’ stuff) as Janiak pays homage to a wide range of horror movies past, particularly with an impeccable camp slasher homage in Fear Street Part Two: 1978 and then an origin tale that brings the initial trilogy to a conclusion in Fear Street Part Three: 1666. Stream Fear Street on Netflix.

Mike Flanagan never really misses, and this series has a summer camp vibe with a deeper, darker twist while rarely feeling entirely gloomy. Based on a few different YA Christopher Pike novels, it concerns a group of eight terminally ill young patients at a bucolic hospice home run by a secretive and mysterious doctor (A Nightmare on Elm Street's Heather Langenkamp). Each night the kids meet to share scary stories covering a wide range of genres and styles, with each also promising to return from beyond the grave when their time comes. It's spooky and often moving without ever being schmaltzy or precious. It was planned as more than a miniseries, so the cancellation leaves several questions unanswered—but that works in terms of the overall tone and subject matter, which grapples with unanswerable mysteries about life and death. Stream The Midnight Club on Netflix.

AHS is always wildly divisive and more or less critic-proof. The fun 1984 season is as over-the-top as you'd expect, with the show's style grounded thoroughly in the milleau of '80s slasher sequels. Starting with a prologue involving a summer camp threesome that turns into a slaughter, we jump forward 14 years to revisit the same camp with a new group of counselors, hunted this time by both the fictional Mr. Jingles (John Carroll Lynch) and the real-life Night Stalker Richard Ramirez (Zach Villa)—at least until the mid-season twist throws everything out the window. The references come fast and furious and the period vibes are exquisite. Stream American Horror Story: 1984 on Prime Video and Hulu.

How does Friday the 13th: The Series tie into the Jason mythology, you may well be asking? It doesn't—not even a little bit, sharing nothing with the film series other than a name, the presence of a couple of actors, and the involvement of producer Frank G. Mancuso Jr. And yet, this creepy, late-night gem has much to recommend it, blending an anthology format with bits of serialized storytelling and a recurring cast. The premise: Cousins Micki Foster (Louise Robey) and Ryan Dallion (John D. LeMay) inherit an antique shop from an uncle who died under mysterious circumstances that turn out to involve years of dealings with Satan that eventually went wrong. Now, the two are stuck trying to reclaim a bunch of cursed artifacts with the help of family friend and occult expert Jack (Chris Wiggins). There's no Jason, but much of the horror goes impressively hard, even if we know that our leads will (mostly) make it through to the end. And if you aren't convinced, consider this: Guest directors David Cronenberg and Atom Egoyan helmed standout episodes. Stream Friday the 13th: The Series on Fubo.

"Games must be made commercially available to customers on or before Friday, November 20, 2026, at 9pm PT," The Game Awards' website FAQ states.

The fit is so snug it might appear an exception has been made for Rockstar's game, but as Shinobi602 also noted, cut-off dates have been pretty similar each year, varying only by a day or two.
