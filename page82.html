#5172. New-Home Sales Dip Less Than Expected as Builder Incentives Bolster Sales

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Home sales dipped a bit less than expected last month, apparently as homebuilders used incentives to entice hesitant buyers.

Contract signings for new-construction properties reached a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 676,000 in September, the U.S. Census Bureau and Department of Housing and Urban Development reported Thursday. That's 0.6% below August's figure and down 5.3% from a year earlier, but more than the 653,000

The median sale price was $400,000, 3.9% above August 2026 where it hit $385,000, and 3.9% below September of last year, when it hit $416,800.

New-home sales in the Northeast rebounded 28% higher than last month and 10.3% higher than last year. But, all of the other regions of the country posted declines. The West had its best month since April but still fell 17.4% year-over-year. The Midwest is down 8.2% year-over-year, while the South is down 0.7% year-over-year.

For the first nine months of the year, overall new-home sales are 2.9% below where they were in 2025, Realtor.com® economist Joel Berner says.

"Buyers are reluctant in this environment of elevated mortgage rates, and builders are having to offer lower prices and buyer incentives to entice them," Berner says.

Last week, three major builders, PulteGroup, M/I Homes, and NVR, Inc., all reported major net income declines. That's caused many of them to be more selective about the markets where they're active, but they have also reported using incentives to entice buyers.

“The rapid increase in interest rates has stretched affordability even further, made selling an existing home even harder, and has pushed even more consumers to the sidelines,” PulteGroup CEO Ryan Marshall said as he reported the company's year-over-year net income dropped by 17% in the third quarter.

Still, the housing market is showing stress amid rising interest rates, even as the Federal Reserve seems primed to leave interest rates unchanged this month. The August sales figure was down 2% from a year ago. And the prices of new builds ticked up, with August's median sales price registering at $393,700, up 0.4% from July's $392,200.

Earlier this month, the National Association of Realtors® reported existing-home sales rose 1.5% on a monthly basis to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.99 million. NAR Chief Economist Lawrence Yun said the tough market has resulted in higher inventory. That means more homes on the market competing with new builds.

Still, this year has been marked with falling builder confidence across the industry. The National Association of Home Builders (NAHB) and Wells Fargo reported that builder confidence in the market for newly built single-family homes fell three points to 29 this month.

Berner noted that builders are conservative in such a market, avoiding constructing homes that they won't sell quickly. For instance, the number of new homes for sale that have not yet been started hit 115,000. That's a record for the Census data. And the number of new homes under construction or completed is falling.

This keeps the market in buyer's territory. Builders are competing with a limited pool of interested would-be homebuyers. But unlike sellers, builders can't keep their inventory off the market until the market improves, Berner says.

So while those builders are slowing construction of new homes, there are still 111,000 new, completed homes for sale around the country.

"Home shoppers should consider new construction not only because of the competitive prices and incentives offered by builders, but also because of the lower total cost of ownership that newly built homes offer over the long run," Berner says.

Earlier in his career, he spent a decade reporting on business and real estate in Dayton and Columbus, OH. A Cincinnati native, he holds a journalism degree from Ohio University.

#4915. Bitcoin mining infrastructure firm Luxor raises $16 million to expand AI business

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Get The Block’s latest crypto news and price alerts for the coins you follow, right in Chrome. Add our free extension here.

CMT Digital led the round, which includes new funding and share sales, as Luxor expands into an AI infrastructure business.

Luxor Technology, a bitcoin mining infrastructure firm, has announced a $16 million investment round as it expands into an AI infrastructure business.

CMT Digital led the round, with participation from Canaan, MicroBT, Halo, Steelhead, and unnamed angel investors. The round included primary and secondary capital.

"Primary is preferred equity, and secondary was a mix of preferred and common equity," Luxor COO Ethan Vera told The Block. "Primary was used to drive growth in the company, and secondary was used to bring new strategics into the round. I can't share the exact breakdown."

Luxor began raising the new capital in August and closed that part of the round in October, Vera said. He did not share the valuation or whether any investors received board seats, observer roles or advisory positions.

Luxor's last priced equity round was in 2021. Before this deal, it had raised $6.575 million in primary capital across three rounds, Vera said. The firm's headcount has grown from 11 at the time of the 2021 round to 106 now, mainly using cash generated by the business, Vera added.

The new funding comes about five years later and follows the September launch of Luxor AI, which takes the hardware, software, energy, data, and trading services Luxor developed for bitcoin miners into AI and computing.

"We have been waiting to raise capital until there was a clear use of proceeds, which is now present with our rapid expansion into the AI industry," Vera said.

Luxor's bitcoin business includes a mining pool, software for running mining machines, hardware trading, energy services, and hashrate derivatives. Its AI business similarly covers hardware, cloud computing, energy and financial contracts.

"Compute markets are converging: the same miners, power sites and capital that served digital mining are now powering AI," Nick Hansen, Luxor's co-founder and CEO, said in a statement.

Luxor's hardware desk has traded more than $1 billion in specialized bitcoin mining equipment, known as ASICs. Its energy business services more than 75 megawatts of load across the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, or ERCOT, and Southwest Power Pool, or SPP, power markets, Vera said.

Vera said some of the funding will support Luxor's hardware business, which sources GPUs and AI servers for customers.

Luxor also plans to expand its cloud platform. It provides sandboxes and runners for AI agents, along with AI code review, Vera said. The platform had run 1.8 million workloads this year as of September, according to the company.

"Luxor Cloud is focused on servicing the exponentially growing AI-assisted code generation," Vera said.

The funding will also go toward Luxor's compute derivatives trading and market making. Luxor said its over-the-counter desk offers options, forwards and backstops based on compute-rental indexes. The contracts are settled in cash.

"We are offering financial contracts for AI companies to hedge, finance and speculate on AI compute," Vera said.

In August, Luxor also launched an AI Hardware Price Index through its Hashrate Index data platform to track GPU node prices.

Asked how Luxor would compete with AI startups raising much larger rounds, Vera said those companies are potential customers for its hardware, energy and derivatives services.

"Luxor has built a business that services over 3,000 clients today, not off the back of large funding rounds but of a dedicated client focus," he said.

Luxor plans to hire about 12 more people across engineering, product, and sales and marketing before the end of the year, Vera added.

#4874. McDonald's McRib Doesn't Actually Boost Bitcoin's Price, Sorry Everyone

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

McDonald's McRib Doesn't Actually Boost Bitcoin's Price, Sorry Everyone

The McDonald's McRib has become a crypto meme in recent years, due to many traders believing that its reintroduction to the menu precedes a boost in Bitcoin's price. And, with the tangy pork sandwich back in certain states across the U.S., crypto traders are getting excited.

But experts have taken the McRib theory off the menu, saying that those parroting the theory are forgetting all of the times that the McRib hasn't coincided with a Bitcoin price increase.

The McRib is back on 10/27. BTC historically goes up >2x after the McRib returns. Don't fade the McRib.

Not financial advice. pic.twitter.com/RxBuzcVht5

— internbrah (@internbrah) October 22, 2026

"There is no correlation at all," Luke Nolan, senior researcher at CoinShares, told Decrypt. "Whilst it is a fun exercise, it seems to be that people omit McRib returns when it doesn't fit the narrative."

Nolan shared a chart tracking the McRib's return compared to Bitcoin's weekly close, and only three out of 10 McRib returns posted clear positive Bitcoin returns. In fact, it often comes before major drops in valuation.

Carlos Guzman, vice president of research at crypto trading firm GSR, told Decrypt that there is "no causal mechanism” and that any price increases following the McRib's reintroduction is just a "funny coincidence."

"Let's say, hypothetically, it did indicate the start of a bull run, it would be quite nonsensical, similar to when people overlaid the Dune movie releases," Nolan said. "So yes, it's fun, generates some excitement, but even in the best case, purely coincidental."

Guzman agreed, adding that the crypto community likely just clings to the theory as it's "silly," "obviously absurd," and because people find it funny.

This year's McRib return comes as Bitcoin sits in what André Dragosch, head of research at Bitwise Europe, called "wait and see mode," with traders waiting for the Federal Reserve's next moves before making big bets.

Bitcoin has seen fairly limited activity over the past month, according to Decrypt data, dropping just 6% as it sits at $79,750.

The Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023, and CPI then rose 0.6% for the month. Its next rate decision is on Wednesday.

That decision is one of three catalysts coming in the next two weeks, Jake Koch-Gallup, senior research analyst at Blockworks, told Decrypt. The other two are an options expiry of $11.69 billion worth of Bitcoin on October 30 and the U.S. midterms on November 3.

"If the Fed keeps rates the same and ETF inflows keep coming, I’d expect the break to be upward," Koch-Gallup told Decrypt last week. "If the Fed hikes again, the $75,000 to $77,000 floor comes back into play."

So if the Fed gives the market what it wants, then Bitcoin's price could actually jump over the coming days… just as the McRib returns to McDonald's stores. Coincidence? Experts think so.

#2240. West Ham Clean Sheet Statistic Hides Mavropanos Defensive Problem

West Ham have kept four clean sheets in their opening 12 Championship matches, and Konstantinos Mavropanos has featured in every one of them. The Greek defender has started 11 league games, missing only the draw against Watford, which makes his potentially lengthy absence through injury a significant concern for Nuno Espírito Santo.

However, whilst those statistics underline Mavropanos’s importance to the Hammers, they don’t necessarily tell the whole story. The Hammers have struggled defensively at times this season, particularly down the right-hand side, and I believe some of Dinos’s mistakes have been overlooked because of his remarkable goalscoring form.

Mavropanos is undoubtedly West Ham’s most important defender, but that doesn’t mean he has been our best-performing one. With the Greek international now facing a possible spell on the sidelines following his knee injury against Southampton, Nuno has an opportunity to rethink a defensive partnership which has not always looked particularly comfortable.

Mavropanos always looks more shaky alongside Walker Peters

I do believe Mavropanos has been hindered by the disruption on the right-hand side of him. He always looks more comfortable when Joël Veltman is playing at right-back rather than Kyle Walker-Peters.

Mavropanos often gets caught in no-man’s-land when trying to cover the gaps vacated by Walker-Peters, and West Ham have repeatedly been torn apart down our right flank this season.

That said, his passing has been poor too. Almost a feature of every game is Mavropanos passing the ball to the opposition, and that is something he must take responsibility for rather than blaming the players around him.

There is also a belief that he is not at his best when partnered with Max Kilman. Personally, I think Kilman has been the better defender this season, even if Mavropanos has attracted more attention.

Undoubtedly, it was never Nuno’s plan to pair the two of them together. It became a necessity once it was clear West Ham had been unable to bring Axel Disasi back for a second spell, leaving the manager with limited alternatives in central defence.

The temptation might be to move Veltman into central defence to cover for Mavropanos, but I would resist that.

For me, Veltman provides exactly the sort of defensive stability West Ham have been missing on the right. Moving him inside would solve one problem while potentially recreating another.

Personally, I would go with Airidas Golambeckis. He’s right-footed, partners Kilman well and deserves the opportunity to show what he can do at Championship level.

As Claret & Hugh reported earlier today, Mavropanos’s injury could force Nuno into a defensive reshuffle against West Bromwich Albion. There are other possible solutions, including a change of system, but I would prefer to keep the back four and give the youngster his chance.

The clean-sheet statistic is interesting, but it shouldn’t disguise the fact that West Ham’s defence has looked vulnerable even with Mavropanos playing.

Losing him is a blow, undoubtedly. But if it encourages Nuno to establish a more settled back four, with Veltman at right-back and Golambeckis alongside Kilman, there may yet be something positive to come from it.

#1527. How Ferguson saved himself from the brink of the sack at Man Utd

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Travel  |  Read Source →

When Manchester United were drawn away to Nottingham Forest in the third round of the FA Cup in January 1990, there was a sense that this was the end for the young Scottish manager Alex Ferguson.

It remains as true now as it did then that you cannot predict the future, and that very often attempts at doing so fall miserably short of the mark.

Yet at that time you would have been as close to certain as possible that the writing was on the wall for Ferguson.

If you were able to time travel and went to Manchester, January 1990, walked into a pub and started to explain how Ferguson would lead United into an unmatched era of English footballing dominance, winning the treble in nine years time with a core group of local boys and then cementing himself among the greatest managers of all time you’d be at the very least laughed at.

In pure titles alone it is beyond impressive, an almost impossible record of success: 13 Premier League titles, five FA Cups and two Champions Leagues. That is before considering the way in which he won them and where the club was before his arrival.

But things got much worse for Ferguson and United before they got better. Much, much worse.

You’ve doubtless heard it mentioned before how he was almost fired, but you may not appreciate just how close he came. Here’s the story of one goal in Nottingham, January 1990.

When Ferguson arrived at Old Trafford in November 1986, he was considered one of the brightest young managers in the game, having led Aberdeen through an unprecedented period of success culminating in their European Cup Winner’s Cup triumph in 1982-83.

He remains the last manager to win the Scottish Premiership with a club other than Rangers and Celtic.

The United job was no small challenge. Ferguson replaced Ron Atkinson, the most successful post-Matt Busby manager who, despite winning two FA Cups and finishing in the top four every season, was never able to bring the First Division back to Old Trafford.

With a third of the season gone, United lay in 21st and Ferguson began uprooting things straight away with his first target the drinking culture at the club.

“I had to get rid of the idea United was a drinking club,” he said to David Meek, Author of Sir Alex Ferguson.

“I had enormous difficulty with Norman Whiteside and Paul McGrath.

“[Drinking] created conflict with my concept of a Manchester United Player.”

In his first season, Ferguson dragged United to an 11th placed finish despite their poor start and things began looking up.

Then, in 1987-88 Ferguson led United to a second-place finish. They ended up nine points behind leaders Liverpool but there was not much of a substantive title challenge with Liverpool leading nearly all season.

Either way, Ferguson’s revolution was underway with Viv Anderson, Steve Bruce and Brian McClair brought in by the Scotsman.

Yet the winds of change turned the following season. Far from the title challenge expected, United finished 11th in 1988-89. No doubt a sackable offence today, the old First Division was much more volatile in terms of positions – but regardless, there was concern amongst the Old Trafford faithful.

The new summer window meant more players for Fergie’s arsenal; out with Whiteside and McGrath, in with Gary Pallister, Paul Ince and more.

But hopes of a title challenge once again collapsed in on themselves as the season progressed. Despite a 4-1 home win on the opening day of the season to champions Arsenal, United’s form was inconsistent and worsening.

It culminated in a 5-1 destruction of the big-spending Red Devils at Maine Road by Manchester City in September and the knives began to be sharpened.

It’s the 30th anniversary of City beating United 5-1 in the Maine Road Massacre.

“Ferguson said he drove home and went straight to bed, putting his head under the pillow in complete shock,” Meek writes.

And well he might have; the media smelt blood and the fans had had enough.

Infamously, a banner reading “3 years of excuses and it’s still crap… tara Fergie” could be seen at Old Trafford in the following months.

With pressure building, the general consensus was that Ferguson would be sacked when, not if, United lost to Nottingham Forest in the FA Cup.

“Martin Edwards [United’s former chairman] and Sir Bobby Charlton have tried to rewrite history by claiming that wasn’t the case,” former United board member Michael Knighton said in The Mirror.

“But when we played QPR at Old Trafford on New Year’s Day, I was stood in the boardroom at half-time when Martin sidled up behind me and whispered, ‘We’ve got to be talking about the manager’.”

The stakes, then, for the match against Forest could not have been higher.

A cup run would provide Ferguson some much-needed breathing space and earn him a distraction from United’s league form.

Forest was no easy task: a decade on from their European Cup double, Clough’s side were still an impressive unit from which the FA Cup was the only thing missing.

The match was a tight and nervy affair, The City Ground packed to the rafters.

Watching footage of that game back, so much of is feels old and unusual, namely, the pitch, which was just a field of mud. The ball bobbled along it, occasionally bouncing up unprovoked.

But in an act that proved to be timeless, Ferguson managed to deliver a victory when all the odds seem stacked against him.

The goal came in the 56th minute when Mark Hughes, stunning hair and all, found himself in what we’d now call a half-space on the outside of Forest’s box. He spotted the run of young Mark Robins, an Oldham boy who had developed in the United youth system and the youngest player on the pitch.

With the outside of his boot, Hughes curled the ball perfectly past two Forest defenders, bouncing straight onto the head of Robins. Stuart Pearce desperately tried to stop him, but it was too late and Robins dutifully nodded home.
