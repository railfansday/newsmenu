#3567. Amazon Is Gutting Its AI Division After Sustained Failure

📅 Jul 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Published Jul 29, 2026 1:57 PM EDT

Can’t-miss innovations from the bleeding edge of science and tech

By now, you’d be hard pressed to find anyone who isn’t broadly familiar with the AI heavyweights and their flagship models. OpenAI has ChatGPT, Anthropic has Claude, Google has Gemini. But what does Amazon have?

Not a whole lot, it turns out. Unlike its competitors in the tech megacorp scene, Amazon’s standout AI model — organized under the name “Nova” — is about the farthest thing from a household name. And that’s not likely to change anytime soon, because the ecommerce giant is now gutting its in-house AI labs, Bloomberg reports.

Per the outlet, Amazon is scaling back the ambitions of most of its Nova AI models and reorganizing its AI development teams as part of an effort to narrow its focus on “highest priority” goals, after its power-hungry large language models turned out to be a bust.

Accordingly, the company has put most of its AI models on ice, Amazon insiders told Bloomberg. This includes its text-based Nova models, as well as video- and image-generating models, all of which have been placed in a state of “keep the lights on,” meaning they’re technically still supported, but only receive the bare minimum resources needed to sustain them.

Instead, labor and computing power are being diverted toward a singular “frontier-model effort” led by Pieter Abbeel, director of the Berkeley Robot Learning Lab whose robotics company Covariant was hoovered up by Amazon in 2024. At the time Abbeel joined the tech giant, Covariant’s founders were working on AI models for robots, which lines up pretty well with Amazon’s push to automate everything it possibly can.

News of the shift in Amazon’s strategic focus comes a week after it closed one of its key AI offices in San Francisco, an 80-person site specializing in research on artificial general intelligence, the supposed next-level of AI development where the tech obtains human-level intelligence and reasoning skills.

With urgent questions swirling over whether LLMs can ever usher in a return on investment — let alone AGI — it may be that Amazon is jumping out of the AI hype cycle while it still can.

#4410. 2027 Audi SQ9

If you need to get your heirs to school at autobahn speeds, the 2027 Audi SQ9 might be the speedy family transport you're looking for. A twin-turbocharged V-8 engine powers this full-size SUV, and it makes a whopping 591 horsepower. All-wheel drive is standard, but the SQ9 is meant for the streets rather than the trails.

A racy appearance inside and out differentiates the SQ9 from its more luxury-oriented Q9 sibling; so does its price tag, which starts well north of $100,000 and goes up from there with options. All three rows of seats are relatively accommodating, although third-row legroom is at a premium with the second-row seats in their rearmost position. Nonetheless, the SQ9 joins rivals such as the BMW X7 M60i and the Mercedes-AMG GLS63 in the large, luxury SUV race.

Expert Tip: If you like the idea of a V-8–powered performance luxury SUV but the SQ9 is too big for your garage, consider the mid-size SQ7. It gets the same 591-hp V-8 but comes in a tidier mid-size package.

Under the hood of the SQ9 is a twin-turbocharged 4.0-liter V-8 engine that makes 591 horsepower. An eight-speed automatic transmission and all-wheel drive are both standard, as is an electronically controlled locking rear differential and a staggered tire fitment on either 22- or 23-inch wheels. An air-spring suspension system can be adjusted for ride height and roll stiffness while a rear-wheel-steering system can turn the rear wheels up to five degrees. When we drove the SQ9, we loved the prodigious thrust from the V-8 and found the all-wheel-drive system especially adept at putting power to the ground.

In Comparison: The BMW X7 M60i managed to hit 60 mph at our test track in 3.8 seconds, which should make for an interesting acceleration shootout once we get a chance to test the SQ9 for ourselves.

In Comparison: The SQ9 has the same 7700-pound towing maximum as the Q9. That beats the BMW's 7500-pound capacity, and falls just 16 pounds short of the Cadillac Escalade's 8100-pound max.

The EPA hasn’t yet released fuel-economy estimates for the SQ9, but we estimate it will deliver around 15 mpg combined. We’ll update this section when more information is available, and when we’ve gotten the chance to run the Q9 on our real-world highway fuel economy loop.

First Row: The SQ9 wears a similar interior design as the Q9, albeit with elements that project a racier ambiance. For example, sport seats and carbon-fiber interior trim are exclusive to the SQ9. Leather upholstery is standard and can be ordered in an array of colors, including red, black, silver, and Baikal Blue. A five-zone automatic climate control system is standard.

Second Row: While a three-passenger bench for the second row is standard, a pair of captain's chairs can be swapped in as an option. The second-row seats feature power adjustments and the outboard seats are heated. Passenger space appears to be plenty roomy enough for adult riders.

Third Row: A pair of third-row seats comes standard on all SQ9 models. Legroom appears limited with the second-row chairs pushed all the way back, but if those in the middle row can compromise by sliding their seats forward, the third row is likely to be more livable.

Cargo Capacity: A grocery store pickup should fit behind the SQ9's third row, but road trip adventures requiring more luggage and cargo will likely necessitate stowing those seats, which will limit seating capacity. When we get an SQ9 to our office for testing, we'll see how many carry-on suitcases can fit inside.

Like the Q9, the SQ9's dashboard is covered in screens. There's a 14.5-inch infotainment touchscreen in the center, an 11.9-inch digital gauge display, and a 12.3-inch passenger touchscreen. All models come with Bluetooth, wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, an on-board Wi-Fi hotspot, and in-dash navigation. All SQ9s ship with a Bang & Olufsen stereo. The base setup features 16 speakers while the upgraded version comes with 22.

A host of driver-assistance features come standard, including lane-keeping assist and adaptive cruise control. A trailer maneuvering assist feature is optional and may come in handy if you plan to tow.

For information about the SQ9's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

Audi's standard warranty package is nothing special, but it does include three years of complimentary scheduled maintenance.
