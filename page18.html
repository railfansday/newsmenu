Invesco is working with Anthropic to let financial advisers use its model portfolios and practice tools inside Claude for Financial Advisors. CAIS, Conquest and FINNY added their own connectors the same day, a day after Anthropic filed for its IPO.

Invesco is working with Anthropic to bring its model portfolios into Claude, the asset manager said on Tuesday. A new Invesco link, or connector, will let advisers who use its portfolios see that data inside Claude for Financial Advisors.

Anthropic launched its tool for advisers in September, as part of a push into finance that began in May. It hooks Claude up to the portfolio, research and planning tools advisers already use.

The Invesco link also gives advisers its research on how to run and grow their firms. It adds a referral scheme with ready-made scripts. Advisers can use Invesco’s ready-built portfolios or build custom ones with the firm.

Invesco manages $2.5T for clients. Clint Harris runs its solutions and custom strategies unit.

“We are excited to roll out Claude-connectivity, which will allow existing model advisors to have greater access,” he said.

Invesco said the tool is for education only and is not investment advice. It warned that AI answers may be wrong or out of date, and must be checked before anyone relies on them.

Other firms are plugging in too. CAIS, Conquest and FINNY, which make software for advisers, added their own Claude links this week, WealthManagement.com reported. That came a day after Anthropic filed for its IPO.

Anthropic has rivals in the field. London’s MDOTM, whose AI platform is used by Morgan Stanley and Amundi, raised $27M in July.

#2959. Brewers seek first World Series sweep since Giants swept Tigers in 2012

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Milwaukee Brewers will have the chance to do something that hasn't been done in 14 years when they take the field for Game 4 of the World Series on Tuesday, Oct. 27.

With a 3-0 series lead over the Tampa Bay Rays after a 10-1 win in Game 3, the Brewers could become the first team to sweep a World Series opponent since 2012, when the San Francisco Giants swept the Detroit Tigers. That year, the Brewers finished third in the National League Central and missed the playoffs.

There have been 19 sweeps in the World Series, plus two more in 1907 and 1922 that ended 4-0 with a tie game, according to Baseball Almanac. Of those 21 series, the New York Yankees were involved in 11 – eight as the winning team and three as the losing team.

More: Who is the Brewers World Series MVP so far? Jackson Chourio? William Contreras? Bullpen?

If the Brewers complete the sweep of the Rays, it would end a 14-year World Series sweep drought, the longest in series history. The next-longest gap is 13 years, from 1976 to 1989.

No team has ever lost a World Series after taking a 3-0 lead and only four teams have won Game 4 after falling behind 3-0: the 1910 Chicago Cubs, 1937 New York Giants, 1970 Cincinnati Reds and 2024 New York Yankees. In each case, the team that had been leading, 3-0, went on to win Game 5.

Here's a rundown of all the sweeps in World Series history:

2007: Boston Red Sox over Colorado Rockies

2005: Chicago White Sox over Houston Astros

2004: Boston Red Sox over St. Louis Cardinals

1998: New York Yankees over San Diego Padres

1990: Cincinnati Reds over Oakland Athletics

1989: Oakland Athletics over San Francisco Giants

1966: Baltimore Orioles over Los Angeles Dodgers

1963: Los Angeles Dodgers over New York Yankees

1954: New York Giants over Cleveland Indians

1950: New York Yankees over Philadelphia Phillies

#2973. Caitlin Clark named to 2026 All-WNBA First Team

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

After a sensational 2026 campaign with the Indiana Fever, former Hawkeye point guard Caitlin Clark has been named to the All-WNBA First Team alongside backcourt teammate Kelsey Mitchell. It marks the first time in Fever franchise history where two teammates have been selected to the First Team in the same year.

This past season, Clark averaged 22.3 points per game (third-highest average in the league) and 8.3 assists per game (the second-most in the league), becoming the only player in league history to finish in the top three in both points and assists. Additionally, Clark set a new league record with 41.2 points created per game.

With 891 points and 333 assists, Clark surpassed her own record as the only player to reach 700-plus points and 300-plus assists, and did so for the second season. She also became the only player in league history to record an 800-plus points, 300-plus assists season.

Clark's five 30-plus point and 10-plus assist games this season are more than every other player in WNBA history combined. Clark also became the first player in league history to record 40-plus points and 10-plus assists in a single game.

Their selection marks the second time each has been named to the All-WNBA First Team, with Clark doing so in 2024 and Mitchell in 2025. In the Indiana Fever's 27 seasons, only Clark, Mitchell, and Tamika Catchings (seven times) have earned All-WNBA First Team honors.
