#2120. Liverpool legend urges Andoni Iraola to learn from Arne Slot mistake for Carabao Cup clash

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Andoni Iraola has been advised to learn from one mistake that Arne Slot made at Liverpool this time last year.

The Reds take on Chelsea in the fourth round of the Carabao Cup at Anfield on Wednesday night, the same stage of the competition at which they were dumped out by Crystal Palace 12 months ago in a 3-0 humbling by the Eagles.

The Dutchman’s decision to change almost his entire XI and include eight teenagers (three of whom started) backfired as a youthful LFC side were schooled by the south Londoners, with the result coming amid a miserable run of nine defeats in 12 games across all competitions.

Iraola’s Liverpool are in much better form now, having won four matches in a row, but John Aldridge doesn’t want to see the head coach aping Slot by making wholesale changes to his starting line-up and leaving out numerous first-team regulars.

The former Reds striker wrote in his column for the Liverpool Echo: ‘Liverpool can’t make the same mistake as they did last year against Crystal Palace in the League Cup. Arne Slot threw the kids and fringe players in and we got mauled.

‘We can’t do that with Chelsea because they’re out of Europe. They need the cup, they need it, and they will come to Anfield and play their strongest side.

‘We’ve got to match them and it’s going to be tough. I’ll tell you what, it’s tough for the manager because we’ve got injuries and you want to keep the players fresh for the league and for Europe.’

Aldridge concluded: ‘I don’t envy the manager, but we do want a cup run and you win this game and you’re in the quarter-finals, so it’s a tough one. It really is a tough one.’

Iraola has a delicate balance to strike with his team selection on Wednesday night. While squad rotation will be necessary amid a hectic fixture period, it’s also important that he picks a line-up which’d be strong enough to overcome a Chelsea side who’d love to get one over on the Reds.

We’re sure that there’ll be a few changes all the same, one of which will likely involve Giorgi Mamardashvili getting a runout in place of Alisson Becker, who’s set to be handed a well-earned breather.

Joe Gomez and Kostas Tsimikas will probably come into the back four, but the absence of several defenders through injury could force the head coach to start one or more of Jeremy Jacquet, Virgil van Dijk or Ronald Araujo.

Trey Nyoni and James McConnell could be paired together in midfield, with one of Alexis Mac Allister or Ryan Gravenberch potentially keeping their place in order to ensure the right blend of youth and experience.

We expect Rio Ngumoha and Lewis Koumas to start in attack, and it’ll be interesting to see if Federico Chiesa is handed a first start under Iraola, with the Italian having played just two minutes so far this season.

There should be scope for the head coach to make several changes from the weekend win over Brighton and still field a starting XI capable of beating Chelsea, who’ll be out for revenge after losing the final of this competition to a youthful Liverpool side in 2024.

There were also signs of broader repercussions, with the state-run Chinese digital newspaper The Paper reporting that public television was suspending broadcasts of matches from La Liga.

The offending video shows Mbappé saying that the funniest internet meme he had seen involving him was of his face photoshopped on to that of “some guy – I think he’s a Chinese dictator”. This was an apparent reference to a meme of Mbappé superimposed on an image of Mao.

“My comment was ill-judged, and I now understand that it hurt many people in China and in Chinese communities around the world. I apologise to everyone my words have upset,” Mbappé said in a statement in French, Chinese and English.

Spain’s La Liga also apologised, saying it “deeply regrets the offence that Kylian Mbappé’s recent comments have caused among Chinese fans. These comments were made in a personal capacity and do not represent La Liga.” In China, jokes about Mao, the country’s founding leader who died in 1976 – and whose drastic policies are also blamed by historians for mass starvation and millions of deaths – remain taboo.

The beverage giant Dongpeng, which during this year’s World Cup ran adverts with AI-generated images of Mbappé consuming its sport drink, said it was terminating all cooperation with the France captain. “Our company firmly opposes and has a zero-tolerance policy towards any behaviour that offends national sentiments,” Dongpeng said in a statement.

Mengniu, China’s largest dairy producer, and cheese maker Milkground also said that they were cutting ties with Mbappé, who until now was hugely popular among Chinese fans with millions of social media followers. “Dongpeng Beverage ends cooperation with Mbappé” was the top-trending topic on Chinese social media site Weibo with 430m views.

One Weibo user said the “extremely serious wrong remarks … crossed the red line” and others urged Dongpeng to remove Mbappé’s image from their bottles. Dongqiudi, one of China’s biggest online football outlets, said Mbappé’s remarks were “offensive to China and the Chinese people”.

In an interview with the French sports newspaper L’Equipe last month, the 27-year-old Mbappé addressed a previous meme depicting him as a military dictator. “There’s a part of it that’s funny … but there is another part that’s a bit less funny because of the damage such people have caused throughout history,” he said.

China’s foreign ministry said it was aware of the reports regarding Mbappé, who on Monday finished runner-up to Harry Kane in the Ballon d’Or award for the world’s best player. “Words and actions that hurt the feelings of the Chinese people will naturally be met with unanimous rejection from all sectors of Chinese society,” spokesperson Guo Jiakun told a regular briefing.

#4123. The secret to slower aging may be hiding in your gut

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Scientists have uncovered a surprising connection between the bacteria living in the human gut and the speed at which the body ages. Researchers at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa found that certain gut microbes are associated with slower biological aging, while others are linked to faster aging.

The study, published in Scientific Reports, found that differences in gut bacterial communities accounted for approximately 15% of the variation in participants' biological aging rates. The results suggest that the gut microbiome, the vast community of microorganisms living in the digestive system, may provide valuable clues about aging at the molecular level.

"This study advances our understanding of the relationship between the gut microbiome and biological aging," said senior author Alika K. Maunakea of the UH Mānoa John A. Burns School of Medicine. "We found that microbial patterns in the gut were significantly associated with molecular measures of aging, even independent of chronological age. These findings open important new directions for understanding how modifiable biological systems influence long-term health trajectories and longevity."

Scientists Identify Gut Bacteria Linked to Faster and Slower Aging

To investigate the relationship between gut bacteria and aging, researchers examined stool and blood samples collected from 123 adults across a wide range of ages. They identified the bacteria present in each participant's gut and compared those findings with blood-based molecular markers used to estimate the body's rate of aging.

These biological measurements differ from chronological age, which simply reflects how many years someone has been alive. Instead, they are designed to assess how quickly aging-related changes are occurring within the body.

The researchers found a notable association between the composition of the gut microbiome and DunedinPACE, an advanced biomarker that measures the pace of biological aging.

Some bacterial species stood out for their particularly strong relationships with aging. Bifidobacterium adolescentis was among the microbes most strongly associated with slower biological aging. In contrast, Succinivibrio dextrinosolvens showed one of the strongest associations with faster aging.

These differences suggest that particular patterns of gut bacteria may offer important information about how rapidly the body is aging.

How Diet, Lifestyle, and Environment May Influence Gut Health

Lead author Braden P. Kunihiro said the discovery adds to growing evidence that the gut microbiome could play an important role in precision health research, which aims to better understand individual differences in health and disease.

Unlike chronological age, the composition of the gut microbiome can change in response to everyday influences, including diet, lifestyle, and environmental conditions. This makes it a particularly interesting area of investigation for scientists studying healthy aging.

"The gut microbiome is highly responsive to lifestyle, diet, environment and other everyday exposures," Kunihiro said. "Our findings suggest that the microbiome may reflect important aspects of biological resilience and aging in ways that could eventually help inform future wellness and healthy aging strategies."

The research also included Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander participants, groups that have historically received limited representation in studies of aging and gut microorganisms.

Co-author Ruben Juarez explained that including these populations could help scientists investigate how social circumstances and environmental conditions leave lasting biological effects over the course of a person's life.

"The microbiome exists at the intersection of behavior, environment and human biology," Juarez said. "Studies like this help illuminate potential pathways through which lived experiences and social factors influence long-term health outcomes."

Could Changing Gut Bacteria Influence How We Age?

Although the findings reveal a meaningful relationship between gut bacteria and biological aging, researchers stress that the study does not establish cause and effect. In other words, finding certain microbes in people who age more slowly does not necessarily mean those bacteria are responsible for slowing the aging process.

Further studies will be needed to determine whether deliberately changing the gut microbiome can influence biological aging or improve long-term health.

Still, the findings offer a promising starting point for exploring how the microorganisms living inside us relate to the aging process and whether they could eventually help guide more personalized approaches to healthy aging.

"This is an important early step toward understanding the gut microbiome's relationship with healthy aging," Maunakea said. "Future research will help clarify the mechanisms involved and whether these microbial signatures may eventually contribute to precision approaches for promoting long-term health and wellness."

#5036. HBAR Policy Chief’s CFTC Forum Role Fuels AI Investments

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Home » Trendsetter News » HBAR Policy Chief’s CFTC Forum Role Fuels AI Investments

Policy chief Nilmini Rubin’s participation in a CFTC forum has fueled buzz about Hedera’s role in AI-driven payments & verification models.

Hedera’s chief policy officer, Nilmini Rubin, appears on a CFTC forum agenda examining superintelligence and financial markets, according to materials discussed by crypto commentator Rupert.

In his latest video, he presents that involvement as evidence for a broader investment thesis: HBAR could supply payment and verification infrastructure for autonomous AI agents.

“AI is going to need an economic layer,” Rupert says, alongside systems for trust, verifiability and provenance. His argument connects Hedera’s regulatory engagement with a potential role in machine-driven finance, though the forum appearance alone does not establish adoption.

The YouTube video cites an announcement for the CFTC’s inaugural Frontier Forum on superintelligence, with an agenda covering derivatives markets and agentic trading. Rupert identifies Rubin among the participants and says the event was scheduled for October 28, following an October 20 announcement.

He also references earlier CFTC meeting records involving Hedera and Rubin’s previous work in government and technology policy. Those connections are central to his case that Hedera is participating in discussions about how emerging financial infrastructure should be governed.

There is an important distinction, however. Although Rupert describes Rubin as advising the regulator and calls her involvement “absolute confirmation” of his thesis, the material he discusses establishes forum participation, not a CFTC commitment to use Hedera.

Rupert argues that AI agents will need inexpensive, continuously available payments and an auditable record of their actions. He positions HBAR as a candidate for both functions, particularly where software pays, contracts or settles transactions without direct human involvement.

The video also cites Rubin’s writing on agentic payments, including the question of what AI agents should be permitted to do with users’ money and who bears responsibility when something goes wrong. That puts accountability, rather than transaction speed alone, at the center of the discussion.

The missing link is measurable usage. Rupert’s references to enormous derivatives-market notionals describe the regulator’s reach; they are not estimates of funds destined for HBAR.

The next meaningful evidence would be named integrations, sustained network activity and a clearer connection between that activity and demand for HBAR. Policy access may help open doors, but it does not settle those questions.

Anbernic is undeniably one of the best retro handheld makers around, but for 3DS emulator devs like Azahar, it's flying too close to the piracy solicitation sun. In a series of posts tied to the portable maker's latest device, the clamshell RG DDS, the team has denounced decisions to use "unofficial piracy-focused forks" of the software in gameplay advertising, in turn distancing itself from the brand.

In an X thread, the Azahar Emulator team confirms, "It has come to our attention that handheld manufacturer Anbernic has been promoting their latest handheld device, the RG DDS, using unofficial piracy-focused forks of Azahar, and has been openly featuring these forks in their advertising material." Keep in mind that Azahar was born out of the back of Citra, a renowned 3DS emulator that was closed down alongside its Yuzu Switch emulator sibling following a $2.4 million settlement with Nintendo. That more than explains why devs are now treading with extreme caution, and with the Entertainment Software Association already calling Anbernic out specifically, the emulator could end up being a patsy for the new portable.

Sailing the seven seas of video game piracy has always been a fine line. In fact, the only reason players are able to play their legally owned backups on other devices at all is down to old precedents set by historic court cases (like Bleem! vs. Sony). Technically speaking, it's all still a grey area, and the last thing the scene wants is Nintendo finally bringing a party to court to set a new precedent.

The reason Azahar should watch its back all comes down to the liability of software vs. hardware. Technically speaking, Anbernic could argue that its device isn't exclusively designed for running Nintendo 3DS games, despite looking almost exactly like the 2000s clamshell. On the other hand, emulators are more or less the tools that allow people to pirate games in the first place, meaning they are likely to face heavier legal repercussions than a handheld maker.

I've been covering the retro console and handheld scene for a long time, and I'd argue Anbernic isn't doing anything out of the ordinary with its RG DDS marketing. At the same time, Azahar is right to distance itself from anything that could be construed as piracy, even if the art of running your own game backups with enhanced resolutions and settings is more nuanced. Chelsea are reportedly stepping up their pursuit of Bournemouth midfielder Alex Scott ahead of the January transfer window, with fresh discussions taking place over a potential deal.

The England international has emerged as a leading target for Chelsea as they look to strengthen their midfield following Enzo Fernandez’s departure.

However, Bournemouth are determined to secure a substantial fee before allowing one of their key players to leave.

With Liverpool also interested, Chelsea could face a competitive race to sign the 23-year-old.

According to ⁠FootballTransfers, Chelsea have entered fresh talks with Bournemouth as they explore the possibility of signing Scott during the winter transfer window.

The report claims Bournemouth are prepared to consider a January sale if their valuation of approximately €120 million (£100 million) is met.

Chelsea had identified Scott as a priority target during the summer but failed to complete a deal before the transfer deadline. The club have continued monitoring his situation and reportedly believe they are well placed to secure his signature.

Transfer reporter Ben Jacobs has also indicated that Chelsea are increasingly confident of landing the midfielder, although a transfer has not been confirmed.

Scott has reportedly rejected several contract-extension offers from Bournemouth, increasing uncertainty over his long-term future at the club.

Despite the growing transfer speculation, Bournemouth have not abandoned their efforts to convince Scott to stay.

Club owner Bill Foley recently reiterated his desire to keep the England international at the Vitality Stadium.

Speaking to talkSPORT, Foley said: “We’d love to have Alex stay here and sign a new contract and be part of this team for the long haul.”

He also acknowledged the challenge of retaining talented players when larger clubs show interest, adding: “We haven’t given up, but sometimes players get stars in their eyes, and they think that Chelsea or Manchester City is the right place to go.”

Bournemouth’s willingness to negotiate could depend on their position in the league and whether Chelsea meet their financial demands. The reported asking price would make Scott a major investment for the London club.

Chelsea appear determined to revisit their pursuit of Scott after missing out on him during the summer. Fresh talks suggest a January move is being explored, but Bournemouth’s valuation remains the biggest hurdle.

If the Blues are prepared to meet the reported asking price, a deal could become possible.

Until an agreement is reached, however, Scott remains a Bournemouth player and the transfer is far from guaranteed.
