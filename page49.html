Mentally tough. Lifting each other. Staying cool under pressure.

#2981. What is wrong with the Phoenix Suns?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Three points. One make on seven shots. Two nights after breaking a franchise record with 43 against Golden State, Devin Booker posted his lowest-scoring night since his rookie season, and the Phoenix Suns got pushed around in Oklahoma City.

It wasn't just Devin Booker's fault. Or Miles Bridges' fault. Or Dillon Brooks. Or Jordan Ott.

It was everyone, collectively. You could pinpoint so many different issues happening simultaneously… it's not encouraging.

The most frustrating part? This was the softest the Thunder will look all year. They were on the second night of a back-to-back, missing Jalen Williams after a brutal injury, and they lost Alex Caruso in the second quarter. No Chet, no Cason Wallace. It didn't matter. OKC still controlled the game wire to wire. And yes, even with the injuries, they are still a very good team. I am not saying the Suns had to win, but I expected a little more competitiveness in this one.

Three games in, nobody should be writing off this season. But the loss put a spotlight on problems that have been building since opening night: a hit-or-miss franchise player, a team constantly losing the rebounding fight, and an offense with no clear identity.

And it wasn't just this loss, or the 41-point loss on Opening Night, that made me feel uneasy about this team. Even their win against the Warriors' backup squad looked sloppy.

After his 43-point, 7-rebound, 7-assist opener, Booker said he wanted to be more assertive this season. In OKC, he took seven shots. Give the Thunder credit for the game plan, but Booker owns a big piece of this one.

When your franchise player goes passive, the whole roster feels it. Suns fans would rather see Booker go 2-for-15 hunting his shot than 1-for-7 waiting for the game to come to him. Nobody blinks at a superstar forcing it to get going. The playmaking dried up too, and his frustration showed on several possessions. I'm not suggesting he shoots over three defenders as he mentioned postgame, but there were times I thought he played a bit too passively.

There's a bigger issue underneath. Teams can load up on Booker this easily because the unit around him doesn't punish them for it. That's a roster construction problem that has been sitting there all along, and the Thunder exploited it.

The talent is there. The starters just haven't figured out how to play together yet. Possessions stall, the ball sticks, and too many trips end in a contested jumper. The Suns play too small and do not gang rebound to make up for their deficiencies on that end.

The question is… is the fit there? I think not. Does that mean it won't change? Absolutely not, but there need to be significant structural adjustments made for this to work.

The Brooks and Bridges pairing sums it up: roughly 5-of-20 combined from three, minimal playmaking, and very little ball movement between them. It makes for a disjointed watch, and it puts even more weight on Booker to create everything.

What stings most is the offseason message. Continuity was the selling point all summer, with the whole group in the gym together and a reputation as one of the hardest-working rosters in years. You'd expect that team to come out of the gate faster than this.

There have been bright spots. Jalen Green looked comfortable early and finished with 20 points, 4 rebounds, and 4 assists on 8-of-13 shooting, and he and Maluach connected on a sharp backdoor feed for an easy finish. Luke Kennard, Goodwin, and Collin Gillespie combined to go 10-of-21 from deep.

I will try my best to pump the brakes on the panic, but not ignore the film. It's a fine line.

Last season's group started 1-4 and still found its way to 45 wins. This roster can turn it around too, as long as players accept some sacrifices and some new roles. If not? It could get ugly.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There was a lot to like about Coca-Cola (KO -1.64%) and its third-quarter earnings report on Tuesday. The company grew its global revenue by 7%, and unit case volume was up 5%, with strong demand in Latin America, Asia Pacific, and its Europe, Middle East, and Africa segment.

But closer to home, there's a problem. North American sales volume was flat year over year, making the domestic market Coca-Cola's weakest geographic operating segment. And that weighed on Coca-Cola stock, with shares down 1% in early afternoon trading.

Now investors need to consider how long Coca-Cola can rely on international markets to drive its growth, and what it needs to do to spur sales in North America.

Revenue in the third quarter was $13.4 billion, up 7% from a year ago. Organic revenue, which excludes the effects of acquisitions, divestitures, and currency fluctuations, also increased 7%.

Adjusted earnings per share reached $0.91, an 11% improvement from the previous year.

But the most encouraging number may be the increase in beverage volumes. Coca-Cola's 5% global volume growth demonstrates that consumers are buying more drinks, rather than the company relying entirely on higher prices to generate additional revenue.

And some of its most recognizable products are doing particularly well. Volume for Coca-Cola Zero Sugar jumped 16%, while the flagship Coca-Cola brand grew 5%. Diet Coke and Coca-Cola Light increased 6%. Water volume climbed 8%, while sports drink volume declined 3%, and coffee and tea volumes were unchanged.

International markets provided much of the momentum. Latin America and the Asia Pacific each recorded 6% volume growth, while Europe, the Middle East, and Africa recorded 5% volume growth. Latin America's organic revenue surged 16%, helped by higher prices and a favorable product mix.

Those are encouraging results for a company that has spent decades building a distribution network capable of reaching consumers around the world.

Coca-Cola's home market told a different story. North American revenue increased just 3%, while unit case volume was unchanged from a year ago. That's a noticeable slowdown from the second quarter, when North American volume grew 3%.

The weakness wasn't necessarily in Coca-Cola itself. The company reported higher volumes for its trademark Coca-Cola products, but those gains were offset by declines in water, sports drinks, coffee, and tea. So, consumers haven't suddenly stopped drinking Coke. But some of the company's other beverage categories are struggling to generate growth.

"Diet Coke and Coca-Cola Zero, especially Coca-Cola Zero, growing double digit again, and fairlife continues to do really well," CEO Henrique Braun told analysts during the company's earnings call. "Powerade also grew, and we have other areas that we have work to do, which is always good for us also to instill here our motto of being constructive with discontent that there are areas for us to work on."

Despite the North American slowdown, Coca-Cola's financial performance gives investors plenty of reasons to be encouraged. The company's operating margin expanded to 33.4% from 32% a year earlier. Its comparable operating margin also improved to 32.9%, up from 31.9% last year.

And Coca-Cola continues to generate gobs of cash. Free cash flow reached $11.4 billion through the first nine months of 2026. Management now expects at least $13 billion for the full year, up from its previous forecast of approximately $12.4 billion.

The company also raised its outlook for adjusted earnings growth to at least 11%, compared with its previous projection of 9% to 10%.

For long-term investors, those results matter more than one disappointing regional volume figure. Coca-Cola is increasing sales, improving profitability, and generating the cash needed to support its dividend and invest in future growth.

But North America deserves attention. International markets can keep Coca-Cola growing, even when its home market struggles. Getting domestic consumers to buy more of its beverages would provide the company with another source of growth -- and make an already strong business even stronger.
