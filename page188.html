#1679. Supporters who braved the storm are Scottish football's real heroes... so why are they taken for  granted?

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 19:02 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 19:02 GMT, 25 October 2026

If there is one manager more than any other who deserved credit over the weekend, it was John McGlynn who made a point in his post-match interview of acknowledging the game’s real heroes.

His team had just beaten Kilmarnock 3-0, and his exciting new striker, Jacob Murrell, had just scored his fourth goal in three matches, but they were not McGlynn’s main concern.

His biggest thanks went out to the supporters, 6,897 of whom turned out in the most preposterous conditions to watch a football match that should never have gone ahead.

Storm Boelo had been widely trailed, with its 50mph winds and torrential rain, but here were the fans venturing out in their thousands to make a spectacle of it.

‘The conditions were horrendous,’ said McGlynn. ‘The forecast wasn’t good coming into the game. We all knew it was going to be that type of game. The last 10 minutes, it was like hailstones. It was incredible.

‘So fair play to the players and a big, big, massive thank you to the fans. Obviously the home fans from my point of view, but fair play to the Kilmarnock fans too. They knew the conditions were going to be terrible but they’ve come along and paid their money and supported their team. Well done to you guys.’

Partick Thistle's match against Stenhousemuir was played in farcical conditions at Firhill

Falkirk boss John McGlynn thanked Kilmarnock fans for turning up in 'horrendous' weather

Rangers striker Ryan Naderi celebrates scoring in their storm-hit victory over St Johnstone

The pity is that more don’t share McGlynn’s respect for the game’s lifeblood. Too many of its stakeholders offer only lip service to the paying punters before ignoring them when it matters most.

The passion and blind loyalty of fans is such that they sometimes need protecting from themselves, but you won’t find the Scottish Professional Football League (SPFL), or by extension the clubs, stepping up. They are content to remain silent as families set off in their cars, listening to radio bulletins advising them to stay at home.

After all, there is a TV deal to honour. Hospitality revenue to rake in. And a calendar so congested that the SPFL’s unspoken guidance is to get games played at all costs.

If that means leaving supporters stranded by cancelled trains, so be it. If it means late postponements, rotten football and pitches ruined for the winter ahead, well, we can live with that.

Greenock Morton’s match against Ayr United was postponed an hour before kick-off. Cove Rangers’ game against East Kilbride was abandoned with 20 minutes left. The same should have happened at Firhill, where Partick Thistle were allowed to complete a 3-0 win over Stenhousemuir despite farcical scenes on a waterlogged pitch.

‘That’s the worst conditions I’ve ever seen in a game,’ said Stenny boss Marvin Bartley, who claimed that the referee, Calum Scott, had admitted it was dangerous. ‘If the game’s 0-0, or we’re drawing, it gets stopped.’

Elsewhere in Glasgow, the pitch at Ibrox was becoming a safety risk towards the end of Rangers’ 4-0 win over St Johnstone, whose winger, Kai Fotheringham, was sent off for sliding too far into a tackle. Afterwards, there was footage of supporters wading through floodwater on Edmiston Drive.

Of course, it is also true that most of the games in Scotland were completed, largely because the biggest problem was the wind. And, historically, that has rarely been enough in itself to bring about a postponement.

But football should be better than that. It should care enough about its standards, its image and its supporters to exercise common sense when the forecast is so bad that power cuts, fallen trees and closed bridges are predicted.

The supporters will be here long after the TV companies and the matchday sponsors have come and gone. That is a reason to value them, not take them for granted.

Al Ahly beat Uganda’s Kitara 4-0 on Sunday, sealing a 6-1 aggregate win and a place in the CAF Confederation Cup group stage.

#5467. XCMG debuts new, fully autonomous electric terminal tractor [video]

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The new XCMG SDA411 concept terminal truck is fully electric, has no cabin, and an L4 autonomous tech stack that’s purpose-built for unmanned logistics – and, if XCMG get their way, it looks like the future of drayage truck operations.

Whether you call them terminal trucks, yard dogs, or anything else, these heavy-duty trucks do work that’s ideally suited to electrification. It’s all low-speed, high-torque, short distance, precision operation, all the time. And the quiet, vibration-free, instant power operation of electric drive is in its element.

Chinese equipment brand XCMG gets it, and they’re taking the electric terminal truck concept a step further with the debut of an all-new, cab-less design that’s 100% autonomous.

“With no cab and an L4 autonomous driving system, the platform is purpose-built for unmanned logistics,” said XCMG, on LinkedIn. “Its 360° perception system enables autonomous navigation, obstacle avoidance, task execution and parking, supporting continuous mixed-traffic operations in demanding environments.”

That same post included a video showing the SDA411 make its way around XCMG’s campus, making some emergency stops, and pulling up to a charger for a human attendant to plug it in (I guess they haven’t heard about Fortescue’s trick with the KUKA welding robot, yet). You can check out the post for yourself, below.

As for the autonomous aspect, while I generally side with labor and humanity over management and technology, there’s some truth to the industry claims that young people don’t want to drive trucks anymore, even with the promise of six-figure salaries.

Combine that with an aging workforce and not enough new talent entering the field, and virtually every industrial sector is struggling with a serious operator shortage. Autonomous equipment like the SDA411 could help address that shortage, keeping goods moving even when there aren’t enough drivers to go around. And rather than simply replacing workers, the technology could let experienced operators oversee multiple vehicles, take on supervisory roles and stay in the industry longer, while making these jobs accessible to people who might not otherwise be able to do them.

#5526. Fiat's Adorable New Four-Seater Has a Big Problem to Solve

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fiat has lately been tapping into micromobility with models like the Topolino, a two-seater microcar with limited road access. If this venture becomes more commercially successful, it could carve a different path for Fiat, which may be especially crucial in the U.S., where the brand sold just 54 passenger vehicles in Q3 2026.

The Topolino certainly has its target demographic, but expanding the micromobility lineup with a model that offers more seating could broaden its appeal. That’s exactly what the new Multiplina Concept is hinting at. Riding on an extended version of the Topolino’s electric platform, the Multiplina Concept, inspired by the 1956 600 Multipla, can seat up to four while retaining compact proportions. While the vehicle could be a go-to option for short trips like grocery runs, bringing the concept to production comes with challenges.

The press release didn’t touch on production plans. But according to Autocar, which spoke to Fiat’s head of design, François Leboine, the Multiplina Concept – which measures 3.04 meters (10 feet) long, 1.4 meters (4.6 feet) wide, and 1.53 meters (5 feet) high – is now “close to production.” The problem is that the Multiplina is being developed for Europe’s L7e classification, which covers lightweight four-wheel vehicles subject to different regulations than conventional passenger cars.

Under the L7e classification, passenger quadricycles must not weigh more than 450 kg (992 lbs) in running order, just 25 kg (55 lbs) more than the L6e limit that applies to the Topolino. But because the Multiplina essentially adopts the Topolino’s platform while adding more seating and a larger battery, Leboine said the production version must shed weight to meet the L7e limit.

In the U.S., safety regulations for tiny vehicles are different. A comparable category is called low-speed vehicles (LSVs), which are limited to a top speed of 25 mph. The L7e classification under which the Multiplina is being developed allows speeds of up to 56 mph, so if Fiat develops the vehicle to approach that limit, it would exceed U.S. LSV regulations. The Italian marque would then have to either limit its speed or meet the more stringent federal safety standards that apply to conventional passenger cars.

If the Multiplina Concept goes into production, it would slot between the Topolino and 500e. For reference, the Topolino is already available in the U.S. with a starting price of $13,995, excluding destination charges, while the 500e starts at $35,700.

Given Fiat’s underwhelming U.S. sales, the brand’s growing micromobility lineup could provide a breath of fresh air. In an interview with ComicBook, Berg, who is directing from a script by Taylor Sheridan, said the Modern Warfare games the film draws on are about more than shooting.

Berg said that underneath the hardware and killing, the games are "really looking at the cost of war, the cost of having to be a warrior who's, for whatever reason, chosen to put himself between real danger and the rest of us, and the burden that these guys carry".

He described the characters - Captain Price, Soap, Ghost, and Alex - as "complicated humans", not "cartoon characters or comic books", and said there's "a real soul" beneath the violence.

"This is not just 'Hey, let's shoot them all up and shoot popcorn!' Like, there's a soul underneath it, that I understand that Taylor Sheridan understands, and I think probably not quite as graphically violent as Mosquito Bowl, but there will be a very strong soul, and consequence of war within the film, called 'duty,'" he said.

Berg's Mosquito Bowl, a Second World War drama inspired by a real 1944 football match between Marines in the Pacific, arrives on Netflix on 25th November. The Call of Duty movie is due in cinemas on 30th June 2028.

A former long-time Call of Duty developer recently called out Activision executives profiteering from the re-release of games like Black Ops 1 and 2, whereas the developers who contributed "blood, sweat, and tears" to make them gained nothing.
