#4790. Japan's top yen stablecoin falls short of its target in a disappointing first year

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Japan's JPYC stablecoin has fallen well short of expectations in its first year, reaching just a quarter of its issuer's circulation target despite a string of corporate partnerships.

“Frankly, I don’t think it’s growing,” CEO Noritaka Okabe told NADA NEWS in an interview, acknowledging that the yen-backed token had failed to gain the traction the company expected. He said trading volume was “not bad,” but wasn’t satisfied with the amount in circulation.

Only 3.2 billion yen (around $20 million) was in circulation as of Oct. 23, compared with cumulative issuance of 12.1 billion yen, Nikkei reported. That data is near the $20.85 million DeFiLlama shows, and reflects tokens that have since been redeemed, underscoring the challenge of keeping JPYC in circulation.

Circulation briefly topped 4.2 billion yen in September after South Korean exchange Upbit listed JPYC. The listing pushed its market price above 1 yen and sent traders rushing to mint new tokens for arbitrage.

Okabe estimated that about 35 billion yen changed hands across Upbit and Uniswap in a single day, before circulation fell back to around 3 billion yen.

That matters for JPYC's business model as the stablecoin is backed by bank deposits and Japanese government bonds, with its issuer earning interest on those reserves. Less money in circulation means less potential interest income.

Okabe blamed the disappointing results in part on a 1 million yen daily issuance limit tied to Japan's Type II funds-transfer rules. JPYC eased its restrictions in May, allowing 1 million yen per request rather than per day, but still restricts rapid repeat transactions.

The cap has frustrated corporate adoption. Software company Asteria, which plans to hold 1 billion yen in JPYC, has been forced to build its balance through installments.

The weak results also contrast with JPYC's growing list of partners. Logistics firm AZ-COM Maruwa signed an alliance in July to develop payments for about 2,300 business partners.

SBJ Bank and LINE NEXT also agreed earlier this month to test bank-linked stablecoin wallet top-ups, though the project remains in the trial stage. Meanwhile, Japan's Financial Services Agency is reviewing stablecoin regulations, with a roadmap expected in early 2027.

Okabe now wants circulation to reach tens of billions of yen in JPYC's second year, a roughly tenfold increase from current levels.

#2303. Blackburn Rovers are finally seeing the best of creative machine under Tony Mowbray – he could earn a new contract

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Blackburn Rovers have risen to 10th in the Championship table, having lost only one of their last six games in the league. Tony Mowbray‘s side produced an ideal response to their West Brom defeat on Saturday, as they beat Wrexham 2-0 thanks to a brace from in-form star Andri Gudjohnsen.

It was a fine team performance from Rovers, showing clinical finishing in the attacking third while shutting out the Red Dragons for a third clean sheet of the campaign. There was wide praise for all, but among the standouts was captain Todd Cantwell.

It was the latest in a string of fine displays from Cantwell, who assisted Gudjohnsen’s second goal and created a whopping seven chances across the game. The performance was further proof of the impressive work done by Mowbray to get the best out of Rovers’ no.10.

Cantwell is in his third season with Blackburn Rovers now, and he’s been a starting XI regular when fit. His growth was emphasised when Valerien Ismael entrusted him with the captain’s armband, but his best has only been seen in fits and starts at Ewood Park.

The 28-year-old has been a far more consistent performer this season though. Of course, there have been quieter games, but he has been heavily involved in their better games. The faith Mowbray has in Cantwell is clear to see too, retaining him as captain and starting him in every Championship match so far.

Statistically, Cantwell leads the way for chances created by Championship attacking midfielders this term (27), and he ranks in the top 3% for touches (618), line-breaking passes (53), accurate long balls (17) and successful passes (344). This shows two things: first, Mowbray views him as a crucial player in possession and wants him on the ball as much as possible, and second, he is being far more creative and forward-thinking in his passing.

It is the latter that has been seriously refreshing when watching Cantwell and Blackburn Rovers. Mowbray wants his players to be imaginative and expressive on the ball, and it suits his captain to a tee.

Cantwell has split opinions among Blackburn Rovers supporters over the course of his time at Ewood Park. Some have bemoaned his inconsistency, while others are more accepting of the highs and lows given how influential he can be at his best.

The former Norwich City and Rangers man is playing at the top of his game far more often now though. His start to the season is arguably the best and longest run of form he has been on in blue and white halves.

Uncertainty over a potential contract renewal is turning into clamour for Cantwell to re-sign. His contract with Rovers expires at the end of this season, and on current form, he is someone who must be tied down.

Cantwell is growing into his role as captain and thriving under Mowbray’s management. After over two years and 82 appearances, the playmaker is finally showing the Rovers faithful his best.

Mitsubishi recently brought back the Pajero for its fifth generation, but unlike previous generations, the latest iteration so far comes only in a five-door configuration. Introducing a three-door version could broaden its appeal while also offering a potentially more affordable alternative, similar to the five-door/three-door options (known as four-door/two-door in the U.S.) offered with the Ford Bronco and Jeep Wrangler.

Russia’s Kolesa, however, decided to take matters into its own hands and rendered what a three-door version of the Pajero could look like. While the rendering retains the looks of the new fifth-gen model, it features several changes, including a shorter wheelbase and rear overhang, longer front doors, a wider B-pillar with horizontal decorative detailing, and blacked-out pillars.

A three-door Pajero would likely retain the body-on-frame platform shared with the Triton, a midsize pickup truck primarily sold outside North America. This means the Pajero gets traditional SUV architecture, unlike current models like the Eclipse Cross and Outlander, which use unibody platforms similar to those found in sedans.

Powering the Pajero is a 2.4-liter turbodiesel inline-four engine that makes 204 horsepower and 480 Nm of torque (354 lb-ft). It is paired with an 8-speed automatic transmission and Super Select 4WD-II (SS4-II). The SUV also features Active Yaw Control, a torque-vectoring technology Mitsubishi pioneered with the Lancer Evolution.

Another benefit of a three-door version would be improved off-road capabilities. The shorter wheelbase could improve its breakover angle and maneuverability on narrow trails. However, the trade-off would be reduced rear-seat access, passenger space, and cargo capacity.

Mitsubishi said the fifth-gen Pajero will be sold in about 100 countries, but it didn’t mention North America. The last time the nameplate was sold stateside was in 2006, back when it was called the Montero. Eventually, the automaker shifted its focus to mainstream crossovers, which may have helped it stay financially viable but also diminished the brand enthusiasm once ignited by models like the Eclipse and Lancer Evo.

While a three-door Pajero would be a welcome addition to the lineup, the five-door version would likely account for most sales, similar to how the five-door Ford Bronco outsells its three-door counterpart. What U.S. buyers can expect from Mitsubishi, however, is a new midsize truck developed in collaboration with Nissan. It will allow Mitsubishi to re-enter the truck segment, which it left in 2009 with the Raider.
