#1660. Fiorentina vs Atalanta BC - prediction, team news, lineups

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Jonathan O'Shea | 27 Oct 2026 15:27

Thwarted by late goals last week, Fiorentina and Atalanta BC will both be keen to end their winless streak when they meet at Stadio Franchi on Thursday.

While the Viola let victory slip through their grasp against the reigning Serie A champions, La Dea ultimately settled for one point from an eight-goal thriller on home turf.

Despite investing heavily over the summer and acquiring a new coach, Fiorentina have actually made a worse start to the season than 12 months ago, when they first entered a prolonged period of crisis.

After sacking Fabio Grosso and re-hiring former interim Paolo Vanoli - who ultimately kept them afloat last term - not much has improved, and they have now lost six of their first eight league games.

The most recent defeat came at the hands of Inter Milan, though they came close to pulling off a shock result at San Siro: ex-AC Milan man Alex Jimenez had fired them in front with a stunner, and David De Gea kept Inter at bay with several saves.

However, the hosts finally equalised as stoppage time began, before Victor Valdepenas hauled over Marcus Thuram in the area and Piotr Zielinski converted a 98th-minute spot-kick.

So, having taken four points from his first two games after returning to Florence - plus winning a Coppa Italia tie against local rivals Pisa - Vanoli has since overseen three straight defeats.

Stuck inside the drop zone ahead of Thursday’s clash at Stadio Franchi, Fiorentina are also winless in seven Serie A home games dating back to the end of last term - yet history is still on their side.

Including a 1-1 draw on the final day of last season, Atalanta have won just twice across their last 25 top-flight trips to the Franchi, going home empty-handed on 14 occasions.

This game also comes amid a dismal run of form for the Bergamo club, who have slipped into the bottom half of Serie A by claiming just four points from their last six league matches.

Still adjusting to life under Maurizio Sarri, they have conceded 14 goals during that period - only free-falling Udinese have let in more.

Four were shipped in the space of 90 minutes against in-form Frosinone last week, as La Dea played out a thrilling home draw.

The hosts fell behind within 30 seconds but led 3-2 by half-time, largely thanks to a Nikola Krstovic brace; however, they ultimately shared the spoils after the Canarini made it 4-4 with only eight minutes left on the clock.

Now, having lost four straight games on the road - admittedly against Roma, Juventus, Ajax and Milan - Atalanta must face Fiorentina with both clubs desperate for points.

Fiorentina hope to welcome back Luca Ranieri from a foot injury, but his fellow defender Marin Pongracic was withdrawn early at San Siro and is a major doubt for Thursday night.

Though Vanoli is still missing Fabiano Parisi and French midfielder Arthur Atta, he has plenty of available options for rotation.

Top scorer Mateo Pellegrino faces a familiar fight for selection with Beto, as the pair continue to vie for one place up front.

Meanwhile, Krstovic is clearly winning his battle with Gianluca Scamacca: Atalanta’s Montenegrin striker has 18 Serie A goal involvements this calendar year - only Thuram and Donyell Malen have more - with seven coming from his last three league matches.

La Dea’s main concerns come at the other end, where several defenders are currently unavailable: Sead Kolasinac, Isak Hien, Odilon Kossounou and Raoul Bellanova.

Slightly easing Sarri’s selection problems, Giorgio Scalvini may be ready to return, but Franck Kessie, Kamaldeen Sulemana and Giacomo Raspadori all remain doubts.

#5694. Schumer, Dems are quietly betting big on one sleeper race in the Deep South

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

With the midterm elections approaching, a Mississippi rally emphasizes the high stakes of the Senate race. President Trump championed Cindy Hyde-Smith for the United States Senate while accusing opponent Scott Colom of being a "shameless phony" funded by George Soros.

National Democrats are getting aggressive in the homestretch of this election cycle, sending money and party figureheads to a state that, for decades, has been out of reach.

Mississippi has been a Republican stronghold for the GOP in the Senate since the 1980s, but in a political environment that is proving tough for incumbents of the party in power, Democrats appear to smell blood in the water.

But Republicans aren’t buying the last-minute play in the Deep South, arguing that it’s a reach, at best, to make the race competitive.

DEMOCRATS SEE NEW CHANCES TO FLIP RED STATES — BUT A $400M GOP SPENDING ADVANTAGE COULD DERAIL THEIR PLANS

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer attends a press conference on China AI export controls at the U.S. Capitol on Sept. 23, 2026, in Washington, D.C. (Getty Images)

"Largely, I think they’re just throwing s--- at the wall at this point," a GOP political operative told Fox News Digital.

The Senate Majority PAC, a political action committee with close ties to Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., reserved $1.2 million in advertising in the state to boost Scott Colom, a district attorney mounting a bid against Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith.

And major figures in the Democratic Party, like former Vice President Kamala Harris and former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg, have also made the trip or are set to appear in the state — Buttigieg is scheduled to hold a rally on Mississippi’s Gulf Coast on Tuesday.

Jake Monssen, Hyde-Smith’s campaign manager, told Fox News Digital in a statement, "This is simply Washington insiders making a last-ditch effort to give life to Scott Colom's failing campaign."

"Scott is bankrolled by the radical left because he is in lockstep with their agenda," Monssen said. "He supports tax increases, open borders, taxpayer benefits for illegal immigrants, and sex change surgeries for minors."

Like in other races across the Senate map, Colom has a fight with history to contend with. Mississippi has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1982, and the Magnolia State has consistently supported President Donald Trump.

But Colom believes that the ground operation he’s developed while national Democrats stayed away from the state is what would put him over the top against Hyde-Smith, not the last-minute support.

"It could be enough," Colom told Fox News Digital. "I mean, the thing is, the people of Mississippi built this campaign."

Colom went on the offensive against Hyde-Smith, arguing that she "refuses to debate, she doesn’t campaign."

"Because of that, I was able to build momentum on the ground, which means now that it's getting attention, now that it's getting resources, I'm able to take advantage of it in a way that other candidates couldn't," he said.

Republicans aren’t resting on their laurels in the state, however. The Senate Leadership Fund, a political action committee closely aligned with Senate Majority Leader John Thune, R-S.D., teed up a seven-figure ad buy there in response to Democrats’ spending.

And Trump appeared in Corinth, Mississippi, on Monday to rally for Hyde-Smith. He made a similar pitch to voters that he’s done at every rally stop this midterm cycle: pretend he’s on the ballot.

"In this election, if you would, I'm asking you to pretend that I'm on the ballot," Trump said. "And the reason is that when I'm on the ballot, exactly 49% more people show up."

Jonathan Klingler, a professor of political science at the University of Mississippi, told Fox News Digital that the increased spending and national attention to a once-sleepy race have generated the "talking point of Mississippi as evidence for momentum and building a Democratic wave."

District Attorney Scott Colom, center, is running for Senate against Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith, R-Miss., and has backers with deep pockets in the Soros family. (Courtesy of Colom Senate campaign)

"So at least I'm not — I can't say whether that's the intent of the funding here or visits, but that is the effect," he said.

There are also voter demographics that both Hyde-Smith and Colom have to contend with. Mississippi has the highest share of Black voters in the country at nearly 40% of the electorate. In order for Colom and "Democrats to be successful, they simply have to peel off a larger share of White voters," Klingler said.

There’s another storyline underneath the headlines of this race, too.

Hyde-Smith and Colom have a history dating back to the Biden administration, when the lawmaker blocked his nomination to serve as a district court judge in Mississippi.

"Instead of saving himself the embarrassment of being denied a job twice by Hyde-Smith, Colom is running a campaign backed by Chuck Schumer, funded by George Soros, and built on pro-transgender and other far-left policies," National Republican Senatorial Committee spokeswoman Bernadette Breslin told Fox News Digital.

That’s not Colom’s motivating factor, however. He had just left Trump's northeast Mississippi rally where he spoke to voters about their main concerns, "everybody I talked to, gas prices, grocery prices," and tariffs, he said.

But Colom said his personal dispute with Hyde-Smith was not his primary motivation.

"I'll tell you, as a Christian, I forgive her for that. That's not a motivating factor for me," Colom said. "I mean, as a district attorney, I have to prosecute cases where people lost their loved ones to gun violence. So, you know, and I’ve seen them forgive that person."

"So that's not the motivating factor," he continued. "For me, it's really the betrayal of the America First movement, right? Like she was supposed to help President Trump get lower grocery prices. Well, she betrayed us. We're not getting lower grocery prices."

#5397. Audi's New Digital Matrix Lights Project Turn Signals Onto The Road And Won't Blind Other Cars

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Audi is known for its advanced lighting technology, and has been since it first introduced the concept of LED daytime running lights (DRLs) that are now basically a prerequisite for all new cars. Many of its recently updated models have customizable DRLs that offer owners a chance to choose between several different lighting designs for both the front and rear running lights. The new 2027 Audi Q9 offers that feature, but more importantly, it's the first U.S.-spec Audi model to feature the brand's digital matrix LED headlights and new projections, too.

This is a technology that Audi has had for years now — nearly a decade, in fact — but U.S. regulations stupidly outlawed the brilliant technology on all cars in the country. This is the first Audi that's been programmed to meet the recently updated U.S. headlight requirements, while still providing functional matrix headlights.

The tricks don't end there, though. On Q9s with the Prestige package, the car projects a solid white rhombus onto the ground beneath the front and rear doors that unfurls when the door opens, or when a cyclist or car is approaching to signal a passenger is getting out of the Q9. Of course, it also includes exit warning that uses the radar sensors in the rear bumper to detect approaching vehicles, cyclists, or pedestrians, that can prevent doors form opening or stop a door that's already opening into the obstacle. In a scenario like that, the projection lights also display warnings to protect everyone involved.

Beyond those functions, the headlights and taillights also contain little projectors that shine turn arrows onto the road when the headlights are on, which is pretty friggin' neat. The curved OLED taillights are the first of their kind to appear on any road car, and will also display a hazard triangle when the hazard lights are activated, as well as offering drivers their choice of eight DRL signatures.

The matrix headlights use the camera mounted behind the rear-view mirror to scan the road ahead for other cars and road signs. When the driver has their high beams on, the car is able to create a gap in the high beam light projection that essentially creates a little box of darkness that follows any other cars or road signs ahead. This means the driver enjoys the increased illumination provided by having the high beams on, and all other road users don't end up getting blinded by them as a result.

To showcase the tech, Audi sent us journalists out into the darkness of the Utah desert at night time, where I was clearly able to see the benefit of the system. Especially on unlit rural roads, the Q9's high beams provided a significantly better field of illumination, and I was hardly able to detect the darker boxes that surrounded other cars, both oncoming traffic and lead traffic, as well as road signs, so as not to blind the driver. I think this will be a big improvement for all road users, as well as Audi owners.

The turn signal projections weren't super bright and visible in all scenarios, but they could prove to be a valuable safety feature. The scenarios Audi used to demonstrate their efficacy make a good case for the technology. If someone in a Q9 was parallel parked on a busy road, the Q9 driver could signal to indicate their desire to pull out, and since the arrows are projected out wider than the car, upcoming traffic can see the arrows on the road before they would otherwise be able to see the integral turn signal lights.

Digital matrix lights are standard on the Q9 Premium Plus trim and above, and standard on SQ9. The advanced turn signals are only included in the $4,750 Luxury package that also includes the powered doors on the SQ9, and they come included in the $14,700 Prestige package on the Q9. It's all really cool, and probably obscenely expensive to replace when they go wrong or get bonked in a busy parking lot, so maybe park at the back of the lot.

Tesla designed the battery pack in its Cybercab robotaxi so it can later switch to cheaper lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells. A Tesla engineer said so on the YouTube channel Munro Live, InsideEVs reported. The pack has spare room next to and under its modules.

Robert Sumpf leads mechanical design for Tesla’s battery modules and packs.

“So, we protected this pack to go to an even lower energy dense chemistry as we scale and need to bring LFP into the platform,” he said.

For now, the Cybercab uses Tesla’s own 4680 cells with a nickel-based chemistry. LFP cells store less energy, so the same number of cells would cut range, but Tesla could fill the spare space with more of them.

LFP cells are cheaper, more stable and last more charging cycles, which matters for a car Tesla says is built to run 500,000 miles. LFP became the world’s most used EV battery chemistry last year.

#3276. Judge blasts prosecutors, refuses to sign new criminal complaint against ex-FBI officials

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

A judge on Tuesday sharply criticized federal prosecutors for failing to get judicial approval before filing a new criminal complaint overnight against two ex-FBI officials accused of lying to Congress about investigations of Hunter Biden.

Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui said he would not sign off on the new complaint, which added a charge of obstruction against Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides in U.S. District Court in Washington.

"I am not going to bless it as a superseding complaint," Faruqui said at a court hearing, after Floris' lawyers filed a motion arguing the new complaint was legally deficient and filed in bad faith.

He told prosecutors that if they wanted to file a superseding complaint, a judge first would have to find probable cause that a crime had been committed, instead of sending him the complaint in the middle of the night and expecting him to sign it.

Faruqui said he did not believe that federal prosecutors had previously sought to file a criminal complaint without a judge's signature.

"It is not our court's practice … that unsigned complaints are filed in the docket," Faruqui said.

"I'm concerned with your view as to what the law is," the judge told the prosecutors. "Why today, on this one case, have you changed the entire history of how you charge cases?"

The superseding complaint that prosecutors in the office of D.C. U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro filed in court early Tuesday contained a signature block with Faruqui's name typed out. But the judge had not personally signed the complaint.

It was filed hours before prosecutors and defense lawyers were due to appear before Faruqui for a preliminary hearing on the original complaint.

That first complaint, made public last Friday, accuses Floris and Benavides of making false statements to the House Judiciary Committee in 2023 about their involvement in investigations of Hunter Biden, the son of former President Joe Biden.

Floris and Benavides were arrested Friday and have been released.

The new complaint sought to charge them with obstruction and attempted obstruction of a congressional proceeding.

Typically, criminal cases for conduct that occurred well in the past — as this one alleges — are lodged through an indictment, not a criminal complaint. To obtain an indictment, prosecutors must present evidence to a grand jury, which then determines whether the evidence is sufficient to charge a defendant.

A criminal complaint only requires a judge's signature. It is often used to obtain an arrest warrant in situations where prosecutors need to move quickly.

At Tuesday's hearing, Faruqui homed in on the way prosecutors filed charges.

"This case was charged by criminal complaint on Oct. 20, for testimony that occurred over a period of time in 2023," he said. "As I noted, and I think it continues to be the case, that it's unusual in a case that has conduct like this to be charged by criminal complaint."

"This has to get to a grand jury," Faruqui said.

Under federal rules, prosecutors generally must obtain an indictment within 30 days of a person's arrest, or risk the case being dismissed.

Floris' lawyers, in an emailed statement to CNBC on Tuesday morning, criticized the prosecutors' conduct.

"In the middle of the night, the government filed an unsigned and unsworn complaint attempting to fix the issues with their original charge against Ms. Floris and adding a new charge just as meritless as the first one," the attorneys wrote.

"In our combined decades of DOJ service, we have never seen or even heard of something like this being filed. That's because the public lodging of criminal charges must be authorized by the grand jury, a judge, or the defendant herself," they wrote.

"Here, the government has taken that power for itself in a desperate attempt to delay our client's right to challenge the charges in court and litigate this case in the media, rather than the courtroom," they said.

"We have asked to address this outrageous government conduct against a decorated public servant at today's court hearing," Floris' legal team wrote.

In their motion to strike the superseding criminal complaint, the lawyers argued the document "represents a serious abuse of the criminal charging process, a troubling disregard for the judicial safeguards governing the initiation of criminal proceedings, and a calculated effort to evade judicial scrutiny and shield the government's allegations from adversarial testing at the scheduled preliminary hearing."
