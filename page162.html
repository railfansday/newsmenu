#4605. 2027 GMC Hummer EV SUV

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Few adjectives can adequately capture the GMC Hummer EV, but "ludicrous" comes close. With its towering stance, 8,660-pound curb weight, and a wheelbase that's easier measured in yards rather than inches, this five-seat SUV is a hulking brute with mega capabilities both on- and off-road.

Despite its planetary mass, two mighty electric powertrains give the Hummer EV SUV enough firepower to break out of its own gravitational field. The 570-hp 2X trim is no slouch, but the 870-hp 3X trim delivers truly physics-defying thrust. All-wheel drive comes as standard, and rear-wheel steering allows for surprising maneuverability while parallel parking or navigating winding OHV trails. It's surprisingly efficient, too, making use of GM’s Ultium EV Platform that grants it more than 300 miles of range and rapid recharge times—though the sheer size of the battery makes juicing up expensive.

Expert Tip: If the SUV doesn’t quite satisfy your definition of ludicrous, GMC also makes a Hummer EV Pickup that is longer, even more powerful, and comes with a five-foot cargo bed sprouting out of the back.

Compare the 2027 GMC Hummer EV SUV with the top vehicles in this segment with our new compare tool.

Changes are minor for the 2027 model year. A handful of new color options have been added, including Dark Ridge, Azurite Blue, Dark Ember, Deep Void Matte, and ICON Yellow. The ICON exterior color harks back to the Hummer H2's signature school bus yellow hue and is available on a limited-run ICON|25 model that also features black exterior accents, serial badging on the instrument cluster, and unique infotainment graphics.

If this year's model is largely unchanged and you're comfortable with a lightly used car, you may want to consider shopping previous model years. A one-to-three-year-old model will likely have a warranty and it'll cost less than buying new.

The 2X has a dual-motor setup that generates 570 horsepower. The 3X has a trio of motors (one up front and two out back) with 830 ponies. Compared with the pickup truck Hummer, the SUV has a shorter wheelbase (126.7 inches versus 135.6), which makes it more maneuverable in parking situations and out on the trails. It also inherits our favorite off-road goodies from the truck version, including four-wheel steering with CrabWalk—which is just what it sounds like—and an adjustable air suspension with Extract mode that can raise it six inches.

GMC also pulls at our heartstrings with a full-size spare tire mounted on the rear gate. Plus, there's an optional Extreme Off-Road package that brings special hardware that includes 35-inch all-terrain tires on 18-inch wheels, extra underbody protection, low-mounted cameras for improved visibility while maneuvering, enhanced axles with heavy-duty components, a locking front differential, and rear motors that can electronically mimic a locked diff.

In Comparison: The last GMC Hummer EV SUV we tested rocketed to 60 mph in just 3.4 seconds—a mind-blowing result for an 8,660-pound behemoth. That makes the Hummer EV quicker than both the Cadillac Vistiq and Lucid Gravity Touring.

The EPA estimates the Hummer EV SUV can go for up to 315 miles between charges, but adding optional mud terrain tires drops that estimate down to a max of 289 miles. We only saw 250 miles of range from its 170-kWh battery pack in our 75-mph highway test.

Recharging can be quick, however. The SUV’s 800-volt architecture gains 100 miles of range in 14 minutes using a DC fast-charger. In our testing, the Hummer SUV required 128 minutes to charge from 10 to 90 percent and averaged a pull of 78 kilowatts. Otherwise, GMC says that a level 2 charger recharges at up to 19.2 kilowatts, providing 30 miles of range in an hour.

The EPA rates the most efficient Hummer EV 2X SUV at 59 MPGe city, 47 MPGe highway, and 53 MPGe combined. In our real-world highway testing, a Hummer EV SUV 3X managed 45 MPGe—just one shy of its government estimate. MPGe stands for miles per gallon of gas equivalent, an EPA metric that estimates PHEV and EV vehicle fuel efficiency. For more information about the Hummer EV SUV's fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

First Row: The interior of the Hummer EV SUV is part rugged and part sci-fi. Its busy dashboard boasts both vertical and horizontal air vents and a pair of large displays that include a 12.3-inch digital gauge cluster. Some interior materials feel a bit too utilitarian; those lower down on the dash and door panels look and feel out of place in a vehicle this expensive.

Second Row: As to be expected of a vehicle this monstrous, the Hummer EV's second row is about as spacious as a typical living room. Even the tallest rear passengers will find that they have more than enough head and leg room to lounge comfortably.

Cargo Capacity: Along with storage under the load floor, the SUV Hummer boasts an expansive 82 cubic feet of cargo space with the rear seat folded, but the high cargo floor makes it tough to load heavy items. In our testing, we fit 15 carry-on suitcases in the Hummer EV SUV's cargo bay; with the rear seats folded, we fit a total of 33.

The Hummer's cutting-edge technology is complemented by a large 13.4-inch touchscreen infotainment system and extensive connectivity options. The system can handle navigation, both on- and off-road. A 14-speaker Bose sound system comes standard, as does a subscription-based Wi-Fi hotspot and a wireless smartphone charging pad. The Hummer EV SUV is also available with a 6.0-kW onboard generator that can be used to power all sorts of equipment and even charge other EVs.

The Hummer EV SUV comes with an enhanced version of GM's Super Cruise hands-free driving system, as well as a host of driver-assistance technology that currently graces many mainstream models.

For information about the Hummer EV's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

The Hummer EV SUV comes with a similar warranty to other General Motors products, which means three-year/36,000-mile bumper-to-bumper coverage and five-year/60,000-mile powertrain coverage.

Tesla originally envisioned producing around 250,000 Cybertrucks annually, but the electric pickup has fallen well short of that target. To be fair, though, EV demand has been underwhelming in the U.S., especially since the Trump administration ended the $7,500 federal EV tax credit in September 2025.

But apparently, the U.S. isn’t the only market where the Cybertruck is feeling the heat. Over in the Land Down Under, Tesla has canceled plans to produce the electric pickup for Australia and New Zealand. This means the company won’t be rolling out a right-hand-drive version anytime soon, which could limit the nameplate’s reach in other RHD markets.

In an email sent to reservation holders, Tesla wrote, “After careful consideration, plans to produce Cybertruck for Australia and New Zealand have been discontinued. As a result, we’re unable to fulfil your reservation, and it will be cancelled.” This came nearly seven years after prospective buyers placed their deposits, back when it seemed the Cybertruck would play a significant role in the EV landscape.

But now, the Cybertruck’s future is uncertain. In addition to the canceled expansion, Tesla has adjusted the pickup’s pricing several times, suggesting it’s struggling to find the right balance between affordability and demand. There are also shifting priorities, with the EV maker increasingly emphasizing the Cybercab and Optimus. The latter is a humanoid robot planned to roll off the Fremont assembly line, which the company is freeing up by discontinuing the Model S and Model X.

More importantly, sales have been underwhelming. Cox Automotive estimates that Tesla sold 4,323 Cybertrucks in the U.S. in Q3 2026, a modest figure considering the pickup once attracted more than one million reservations. It was, however, the best-selling electric pickup, ahead of rivals like the Rivian R1T, but still far behind the company’s best-selling vehicle, the Model Y, which sold nearly 100,000 units.

As always, only time will tell. But from what’s happening right now, the Cybertruck’s future looks increasingly uncertain, especially in the full-size electric pickup segment, where Ford ended production of the all-electric F-150 Lightning, while Ram canceled plans for its battery-electric 1500 in favor of a range-extended version.

Tesla has yet to announce plans to discontinue the Cybertruck or introduce a successor. One of the latest hints about its direction came during the company’s January 28, 2026, earnings call, when Elon Musk discussed transitioning the Cybertruck production line toward fully autonomous vehicles, potentially used for local cargo deliveries within cities.

#5949. Ouch: Ryanair Passengers Fly 8+ Hours, Only To End Up Where They Started

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

What’s worse than spending eight hours on a Ryanair plane to get to your destination? Easy — spending eight hours on a Ryanair plane, only to end up exactly where you started. That’s a scenario that travelers faced on a recent Ryanair flight, as reported by PYOK, and it certainly gets at the complexity of the industry.

On Saturday, October 24, 2026, Ryanair flight FR2954 was scheduled to operate from Alicante (ALC) to Newquay (NQY). The 867-mile flight was operated by a Boeing 737-800 with the registration code 9H-QDK, and had a block time of 2hr25min, as it was supposed to depart at 5:25PM and arrive at 6:50PM (with a one hour time change). Unfortunately that’s not how things played out.

First, the plane was running around an hour behind schedule, so it only took off from Alicante at 6:36PM. Roughly two hours later, the plane found itself over Newquay, though there was one issue — weather at the airport was awful, as Storm Austen brought very gusty winds to this part of England.

So the plane descended to 25,000 feet and circled briefly, but then the pilots made the decision to divert to Dublin (DUB), as it was clear that conditions wouldn’t improve. The plane landed in Dublin at 8:43PM, 3hr7min after it first departed (there was a time change of one hour).

From there, the plane was refueled, and Ryanair’s operations team regrouped, to come up with a new plan. At this point it was too late to fly to Newquay (the terminal seems to have limited operating hours), so the decision was made to fly to Bristol (BRS), 119 miles from Newquay, and then to bus passengers to their destination. Obviously that would’ve still been really rough, but it was viewed as the best option here.

The plane finally took off again at 10:34PM, around two hours after it landed in Dublin. Within 25 minutes, the plane was close to Bristol, though conditions still hadn’t improved, and there were no signs of that changing.

So rather than diverting back to Dublin, or elsewhere, the crew instead decided to divert to Alicante, which was the origin airport. Keep in mind that this was way further from Bristol than Dublin. So the plane landed there at 2:12AM, 2hr38min after it departed (this time there was a time change of one hour in the other direction).

For those doing the math, that means passengers spent well over eight hours onboard this Ryanair 737, only to end up where they started.

Logically, some people are probably wondering why Ryanair made the decision to divert a Newquay-bound flight all the way back to Alicante, instead of to Dublin. The former is 867 miles from the destination, while the latter is just 213 miles from the destination.

I suspect the answer here comes down to Ryanair’s business model — Ryanair has bases across Europe, and this particular plane was based in Alicante. So operationally, the airline decided that it made the most sense to have the plane and crew back in Alicante, so that operations the following day wouldn’t be impacted, and create a domino effect.

Regardless of where passengers would’ve been stranded, Ryanair is on the hook for covering their essential expenses, including lodging. However, the passengers wouldn’t be entitled to EC261 cash compensation, since this would be considered an extraordinary circumstance.

Understandably this would probably have been frustrating for passengers, who would’ve rather just flown from Dublin to Newquay the following day. However, keep in mind that when they took off from Dublin, the idea was to land in Bristol, but the weather didn’t ultimately allow that.

Some may point out that Dublin is a massive base for Ryanair, so you’d think they’d be able to efficiently divert the plane there, have a new crew, etc. That’s not quite that straightforward, though, because Ryanair has a handful of subsidiaries, each with their own planes and crews. This particular plane was Malta registered, separate from the carrier’s standard Irish air operator certificate.

Passengers booked on a Ryanair flight from Alicante to Newquay were in for quite the adventure. Due to bad weather at the destination, the flight first diverted to Dublin. The goal was then to fly to Bristol and bus passengers to Newquay. However, weather there proved bad as well, so the decision was ultimately made to divert back to the origin, Alicante.

In the end, passengers spent well over eight hours onboard this plane, only to end up at their origin… talk about a rough evening!
