#3097. Bill Belichick wants ACC to add more goal line cameras after controversial Syracuse finish

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

During much of his time in the NFL, Bill Belichick called for the NFL to spend the money necessary to install cameras that would make it easier to review plays. In his second season as a college coach, Belichick is banging that same drum.

Via Brian Murphy of WRAL, Belichick wants the ACC to add cameras at the goal line and along the sidelines to assist the replay officials.

The issue is particularly relevant because Belichick’s North Carolina team lost to Syracuse on a controversial all-or-nothing two-point play in overtime. The ruling on the field was that Syracuse had successfully earned the decisive two points. The call was upheld via the available replay angles.

“If he scored he scored,” Belichick said Monday. “If he didn’t score, he didn’t score. Just put a camera there and get it right. That’s what I’m for. But based on what was called on the field, the views that the officials had, I don’t know how you could possibly overturn it. The view was from a long, long way away.”

At both the college and pro level, replay overturns the real-time ruling only if there is clear and obvious evidence to the contrary.

“From what the officials had to work with, it would be hard to tell,” Belichick said of the game-deciding play. “I think from what the fans showed — they had some good footage of it — that there are different views there that the officials in Charlotte, the headquarters there where they looked at reviews of the game, they didn’t have those films. So the answer to the question is to put goal line and sideline cameras on the lines so that you have a good look at those plays.”

More official cameras would increase the chances of getting a decisive look.

“The ACC doesn’t have goalline cameras,” Belichick said. “They don’t have sideline cameras, like the other conferences do, and that’s something that I’ve always advocated when I was in the NFL for 20 years — sideline cameras, end zone cameras, like in tennis. Put on the sideline, put on the goal line, put on the end line. Those are the scoring [views], especially in college football, where you have the overtime rule, which is going to put you in the red zone. . . .

“All the other conferences have them. The ACC doesn’t have them. I’m not sure why, but we’ve talked about that in league meetings. But for some reason, we don’t have them, and I think it would be good for the game to get a definitive look at every play like that.”

He’s right. Too much rides on these games to pinch pennies when it comes to the replay function. It’s only as good as what the video evidence reveals. More video evidence is always better.

In the years since Belichick was fired by the Patriots, the NFL has implemented a Hawk-Eye system of stationary cameras in all stadiums that provides angles not provided by the TV broadcast, which has more or fewer cameras based on the window.

Jay-Z is heading to the Super Bowl, only this time he'll have a microphone. After years of helping shape the halftime show from behind the scenes (and a week of speculation), the rapper will headline it himself in 2027.

The NFL, Apple Music, and Roc Nation announced the news Monday, Oct. 26, confirming that Jay-Z will perform at Super Bowl LXI on Feb. 14 at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California. The game will air on ESPN, ABC, and Disney+.

The announcement arrived during Monday Night Football with a black-and-white video featuring Jay-Z sitting in a stadium surrounded by goats — yes, actual goats — soundtracked by "Public Service Announcement" with the accompanying caption "GOAT's out of the bag." Doesn't quite roll off the tongue, but I digress.

It's a familiar venue for the rapper, who just played two shows at SoFi over the weekend as part of a concert run celebrating the 30th anniversary of his debut album, Reasonable Doubt. Now, he’ll return for a performance that brings his role in the NFL's entertainment operation full circle. His company, Roc Nation, has helped produce the halftime show since 2019, with headliners including Rihanna, Usher, Kendrick Lamar, and Bad Bunny.

The announcement follows weeks of speculation about who would take the stage. Puck reported that Jay-Z had emerged as the pick over fellow contenders Miley Cyrus and Justin Bieber, while TMZ had named Harry Styles among the frontrunners.

But alongside the excitement about which songs he might perform, many people online were quick to point out the unusual arrangement at play.

"jay finally approved his own application," one user wrote. "Announced himself you mean!" added another, while one user joked that "Jay-Z really pulled a Katniss," invoking The Hunger Games heroine's decision to volunteer.

Those jokes had already been circulating for a week. After Puck reported that Jay-Z would headline, an r/Music thread titled "Jay-Z Picks Jay-Z to perform halftime show for Super Bowl LXI" drew thousands of comments. One Redditor imagined the boardroom pitch: "Jay Z presents…. Jay Z." Another offered a revised lyric: "He's got 99 problems but being in a powerful position of influence regarding the Super Bowl Halftime Show ain't one."

The NFL, for its part, framed the choice as a longtime ambition. "When we first reached an agreement with Roc Nation to produce the show, I had just one request: that Jay-Z take the stage himself one day, whenever the moment felt right to him," Commissioner Roger Goodell said in a statement. Apparently, that moment has arrived.

Some listeners, however, questioned whether that moment had passed. Some Reddit commenters debated whether a show built around his decades-long catalog would feel exciting in 2027. His most recent solo album, 4:44, came out in 2017.

"Pretty lame choice. Jay hasn't had a hit or been relevant in years," one commenter wrote. Others suggested younger pop stars or called for a rock band to headline instead.

But for fans welcoming the announcement, the back catalog is precisely the appeal. "I ain't mad at it! Perform Izzo, Encore, & Otis," one fan wrote on Reddit. One X user called it a "rare announcement that actually lives up to the hype."

Former Philadelphia Eagles star Jason Kelce was similarly enthusiastic during Monday Night Football’s coverage. "He's got more stadium-ready hits than most artists have songs, period," he said.

There's also excitement around who might join him. Jay-Z's anniversary concerts featured a rotating roster of guests, including Rihanna, Eminem, Nas, Alicia Keys, and Beyoncé, giving fans plenty of material for their Super Bowl predictions. None have been confirmed for the halftime show, but that hasn’t stopped people from submitting requests.

"Imagine he brought Ye out with him for this, at least for the sake of the culture," one user wrote on X. On Reddit, others floated Linkin Park, which teamed up with Jay-Z on Collision Course, and "Empire State of Mind" collaborator Alicia Keys. Unsurprisingly, Beyoncé was also high on the wish list. "He's coming with his wife (for sure)." Another user wrote, "The only reason people are going to watch is for Beyoncé," said another.

But the prospect of famous guests didn’t win everyone over. "Other tagalongs who haven't been relevant in a decade either? Neat," one Redditor wrote. "She already did a half time show. We need something fresh," another wrote about Beyoncé, who headlined in 2013 and returned in 2016 as a guest during Coldplay's set alongside Bruno Mars.

Jay-Z appeared to address some of the criticism during his second SoFi show on Oct. 24, questioning the gap between what people were posting and what concertgoers could see for themselves.

"People will tell you that nobody was in here tonight, but you got eyes, you can see. People will tell you that the music wasn’t great, but you got ears," he told the crowd, before urging fans to "question everything in this world of AI."

We’ll see whether Jay-Z wins over the skeptics on Feb. 14. At least he won’t have to worry about impressing the guy who picked him.

#3509. AI disinformation is outpacing platform moderation before US midterms

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Experts say false posts are cheaper to make than ever, while platforms lean on AI and users to police them.

AI-generated election misinformation is spreading across social media ahead of the US midterm elections, and experts warn that much of it may be escaping detection as platforms scale back human moderation and rely more heavily on automated systems.

AI tools make it cheap to produce convincing fake images, videos, and claims in large volumes. Meanwhile, major social networks have reduced some of the teams responsible for checking content, leaving automated filters and users to catch more of the falsehoods.

“You can run an information operation now with quality that was impossible in 2016 for super, super cheap,” Frances Haugen, the former Facebook data engineer who became a whistleblower, told The Guardian.

Haugen also explained how people spreading misinformation can evade moderation. By making small changes to each version of a false claim, they can produce posts that get past automated filters even when similar content has already been flagged.

A rumour involving Texas Senate candidate James Talarico shows how quickly such claims can spread.

Earlier this month, posts on X claimed that the Democratic candidate was receiving treatment at a mental health centre in Ohio, when in reality, he was at home with the flu.

The hospital and Talarico denied the claim within hours, La Voce di New York reported. But the rumour continued circulating for days.

Researchers at the Digital Democracy Institute of the Americas, which monitors misinformation targeting Latino voters, found that the story changed as it spread through Spanish-language media.

It evolved into a false claim that Talarico had attempted to take his own life. Correcting the original allegation did not stop the altered version from circulating, researchers said.

Roberta Braga, the institute’s founder, said platforms apply their rules less consistently to Spanish-language content than to English-language posts.

“Now that they’re using AI to do a lot of the content moderation, even more is likely slipping through the cracks,” she said.

AI-generated content is also appearing in official campaign advertising. Ken Paxton, the Republican challenging Talarico, ran an advertisement featuring AI-generated images of the Democratic candidate.

The ad disclosed its use of AI, and some states have introduced rules requiring such disclosures, including Pennsylvania.

The major platforms have different approaches to political misinformation, but their rules cover only certain types of content. Meta labels AI-generated images it can identify and requires political advertisers to disclose AI use.

YouTube prohibits false claims about when, where, and how people can vote. X relies largely on its crowdsourced Community Notes system.

Many other misleading political claims fall outside these specific rules.

Meta and X did not respond to The Guardian’s requests for comment. YouTube said the same guidelines apply to all users, including political candidates.

The issue extends beyond the US. In Spain, a political party is facing a court complaint over a deepfake election video.

As election campaigns increasingly use AI to create and distribute content, the challenge for platforms is not simply identifying synthetic media. It is also catching false claims that change as they spread, across languages and communities, faster than corrections can follow.
