The October Edition of Steam Next Fest was, unsurprisingly, a complete mess. What was once a fun week-long festival of early game demos has become a dumping ground for vibe-coded AI slop, making it increasingly hard to find and wishlist good games. And if you weren't bothered by playing a game demo that was hastily cobbled together, the sheer volume--around 7,100 game demos were available this time--of what was on offer made Next Fest a nightmare to navigate. So what's a person to do when Valve's own storefront lacked the tools to separate the wheat from the chaff? Be patient, and look at the general consensus; that's what.

While Next Fest is over, many of those games' demos are still readily available to download and play. The Top 50 Next Fest demos for October 2026 span a wide variety of genres and themes, and the list shows a few trends. Social, co-op, and friendslop games rose to the top, as people couldn't get enough of multiplayer chaos in party games like Worst Class Ever, while Fart Together offered physics-driven goofiness and intestinal turbulence.

Niche management and shopkeeper sims also attracted players, with Questing Goose Studio's Probably Stolen and Victorian-gothic gem-cutting sim Abyssal Lapidary proving popular. Anyone looking for low-effort experiences will likely enjoy desktop companion games like Character House, and anticipated sequels like ArcheAge Chronicles drew big player numbers. One thing to note is that some of these games--like Teach My Little Sister How To Drive--do have AI disclosures attached, which you can filter for.

The list below should be a solid starting point for checking out game demos that drew massive buzz, but waiting a week to see what's worth playing based on popularity alone isn't exactly ideal. Valve hasn't said anything about how the third and final Next Fest of the year was a mess, despite the public outcry, even though the number of games for this instance of the event almost doubled compared with the June 2026 edition. If that trend continues, next year's Next Fest in February could be even more difficult to explore.

Beyond Next Fest, Steam has also confirmed the dates for several upcoming sales and themed promotions for the rest of 2026 and the first half of 2027. Gernot Döllner, CEO of Audi, took the stage with U.S. President Donald Trump at a campaign rally to announce the construction of a new factory. I asked at the time whether hitching your corporate wagon to a historically disliked politician who faces an all-time midterm rout was a good idea, but there was an angle I hadn't considered: What Audi's workers, based in Germany, would think. Turns out they're not huge fans, and they control enough of the company to make that a problem. From Automotive News:

BERLIN — Audi CEO Gernot Döllner's on-stage embrace of President Trump during the announcement of a new U.S. factory for the brand is drawing fierce criticism in Germany, handing unions new leverage over Volkswagen Group's restructuring plans.

Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung (FAZ), one of Germany's leading newspapers, described Döllner's appearance alongside Trump as "Bending the Knee in Georgia." Other commentators labeled the Audi chief, a key ally of VW Group CEO Oliver Blume, a national embarrassment.

An investment of this scale must be approved by VW Group's supervisory board. Under Germany's co-determination laws, half of the board's seats are held by labor representatives, while VW Group's home state of Lower Saxony holds two seats.

Volkswagen is looking to shutter factories and lay off workers at home, but it suddenly has money for an all-new factory in the U.S. — all to appease a president who's not particularly loved by most. I'm no fancy big-city MBA, but I probably would've handled just about all of this differently.

From Automotive News:

The global market for electric vehicles continues to grow, with a notable exception: the U.S.

Around the world, governments are pursuing disparate regulatory and incentive strategies, and consumers want different types of vehicles. The strategies vary — China has built significant demand through industrial policy and subsidies, while Europe has relied on more measured regulatory boosts. Emerging markets are embracing EVs in part because of limited access to fossil fuels. But across the globe, the U.S. appears to be the odd one out.

Is the U.S. "going to become a small, lucrative market with specialty vehicles, or are we going to be able to compete in the scale business of autos worldwide?" said Mike Murphy, the founder of EVs for All America, a group trying to eliminate the partisan divide over EVs. DENVER — In his first four games, Yaxel Lendeborg has already gone head-to-head against Luka Doncic and Nikola Jokic. How's that for a welcome to the NBA?

The way the 24-year-old rookie has responded to the test, the more pertinent question might be: How's that for an introduction to Yaxel Lendeborg? And maybe another: How much better can this dude get?

After Lendeborg's most impressive showing yet, leading the Warriors to their first road win over Jokic and the Nuggets since 2022, Draymond Green offered an answer: Eons.

Golden State Warriors' Yaxel Lendeborg (1) shoots a free throw during an NBA basketball game against the Denver Nuggets, Monday, Oct. 26, 2026, at Ball Arena in Denver. NBAE via Getty Images

"I don't care what age you are, it's the NBA. If you don't know the NBA game, you're already at a deficit. He don't know the game," Green said. "Not that he don't know the game of basketball — he don't know the NBA. And to be able to have the ability to go out there and still do that, not knowing what the NBA really, truly brings on a nightly basis is special.

"It's no different than what I've been saying for weeks now. I think he's a special player."

In other words, Lendeborg still has lots of room to grow, especially considering the fact that he only started playing the sport seriously after high school. Now, think about that and take a look at his stat line from the Warriors' 134-114 win — his fourth career game: 31 points, 12 rebounds, six assists. Plus-27 in 31 minutes.

"Basically, at all the critical moments of the game, we just put the ball in his hands," coach Steve Kerr said. "For a rookie to be that advanced already, it's extremely rare. But it's exciting because I think he's gonna get a lot better, too."

Golden State Warriors player Yaxel Lendeborg (1) and head coach Steve Kerr high five during a game against the Los Angeles Lakers at Chase Center in San Francisco, Wednesday, Oct. 21, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Two rookies in Warriors history have ever had a game like Lendeborg's: Rick Barry and Wilt Chamberlain. Pretty good company. Tally it up with his first three games and it gets even better.

In the lottery era, only four rookies in the entire NBA have ever totaled 85 points and 35 rebounds in their first four games, according to OptaStats research: Patrick Ewing, David Robinson, Shaquille O'Neal — and Lendeborg.

Stephen Curry called him "kind of the full package" after the game on Peacock.

"He's just got composure and feel, obviously his size on the court, he's a matchup problem," Curry said. "But he's never really in a hurry and he's starting to see the pictures really well. Distributing, being aggressive, getting to the free throw line, offensive rebounds."

Golden State Warriors' Yaxel Lendeborg (1) drives to the basket during a game against the Denver Nuggets at Ball Arena in Denver on Monday, Oct. 26, 2026. NBAE via Getty Images

Green said that he "impose[d] his will on the game."

Besides a Curry flurry in the third quarter, the Warriors more or less ran their offense through Lendeborg, who used his 6-foot-9, 241-pound frame to steamroll into the lane.

He chased down a block in transition and got back for a bucket on the other end. He made it to the foul line 13 times, only missing once. And as an exclamation point, he slammed home a lob from De'Anthony Melton with 4:36 left. Amid it all, he made just one of his eight attempts from downtown.

"On the night that I can't make a 3 for nothing, it's crazy that it's my best game so far," Lendeborg said. "Because it was very frustrating for me. I'm just glad that we got our first win. I would have cried if we lost again."

Another bucket and Lendeborg would have tied or surpassed Darius Acuff Jr. (33 vs. Portland) for the most points by a rookie so far this season. But, he said, "I've never been a stat-padder."

#3517. Intel to buy quantum software firm Classiq for about $2.3bn

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The deal would let the chipmaker sell both the hardware and the software needed to run quantum computers.

Intel has agreed to buy Classiq, an Israeli startup that makes software for programming quantum computers.

The chipmaker announced the deal on Monday without giving a price, but it is worth about $2.3bn, Bloomberg reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Intel declined to comment on the figure.

Quantum computers use qubits, which can handle certain problems, such as simulating molecules, far faster than ordinary chips. But writing programs for them is hard and usually needs specialists.

Classiq tries to make that easier. Its platform lets developers describe what they want a program to do and then designs the quantum circuit for them, much as chip design software does for regular processors. It works across different types of quantum hardware, and customers include BMW and HSBC.

Intel has worked on quantum computing since 2015, building qubits on silicon with the same equipment it uses for ordinary chips.

Adding Classiq would give it what the industry calls a full stack: both the processors and the software to use them.

“Joining forces with Classiq transforms Intel’s Quantum Computing Group into a full-stack quantum computing business,” said Intel chief executive Lip-Bu Tan.

Intel said it would keep Classiq’s software working with other companies’ quantum hardware, and that Classiq would become its global development centre for quantum computing. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

Classiq was founded by chief executive Nir Minerbi, Amir Naveh and Yehuda Naveh. It has raised just over $200m and was valued at about $600m to $700m in 2025, Israeli outlet Calcalist reported, according to Ynet.

Tan was among Classiq’s investors, joining its 2022 and 2025 funding rounds. He did not take part in the deal talks because of that stake, Calcalist reported. Tan’s links to companies that do business with Intel have drawn attention before.

The purchase comes as Intel’s turnaround gathers pace. Its shares have nearly tripled this year, and in August it sold $15bn of stock to fund its chip factories.

#3468. Everything Apple announced this week so far: New iPad and MacBooks

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ahead of its 'Pro and Tell' event, the company has already launched a few new devices.

Apple wasn't done with its product reveals for the fall after unveiling the iPhone Duo, iPhone 18 Pro models and an array of smart home devices. Nor could it wait until the third event of the season, dubbed "Pro and Tell," to show off more gear.

The company got a string of announcements underway on Monday with the reveal of the long-awaited OLED iPad mini. Tuesday brought us an M6-powered iMac and 14-inch MacBook Pro. It's expected that Apple will unveil a touchscreen MacBook as well.

Elsewhere, Apple released the latest versions of its operating systems. Among other things, those added more Siri AI features and made the assistant available in more languages. The company updated Apple Creator Studio with more AI features and creative tools as well.

We'll update this story if and when Apple makes more announcements. In the meantime, here's everything Apple has announced so far this week.

It feels like we've been waiting for forever for Apple to put an OLED display in the iPad mini but that day has finally arrived. The new display has double the peak brightness of the one on the previous model at 1,200 nits, and it has a 2,000,000:1 contrast ratio. The base storage remains the same at 128GB, though if you opt for 256GB or 512GB of storage, you can snag a variant with a nano-texture glass display. That'll cost you extra, naturally.

The company managed to squeeze in a larger screen — an 8.5-inch display rather than the 8.3-inch one in the previous model — without increasing the size of the tablet. In fact, the new iPad mini is slightly narrower than the last one, at 5.22 inches vs. 5.3 inches. It's lighter as well, with the Wi-Fi model coming in at 0.56 pounds (compared with 0.65 pounds last time) and the 5G variant weighing 0.58 pounds (versus 0.66 pounds). The latter, by the way, now boasts Apple's C2 modem, which is said to deliver up to 50 percent faster data speeds than the previous iPad mini. There's also Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread connectivity thanks to the N1 chip.

The OLED iPad mini — the eighth generation of the device — runs on the A20 Pro, the same chipset as the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone Duo. Apple says the SoC is capable of running large language models locally, in case you're into that kind of thing. Otherwise, the tablet should be notably faster than earlier models. It supports Apple Intelligence and Siri AI as well, of course. The chipset also boasts a 7-core GPU — two more cores than the A17 Pro, which powered the last-gen iPad mini.

On the camera front, there's a 12MP Center Stage lens on front of the tablet, which Apple has finally shifted to the landscape edge on the iPad mini. However, there's still no Face ID. The 12MP camera on the rear supports 4K 60fps video capture and Smart HDR 4.

The 8th-gen iPad mini has a sealed enclosure to help protect it from the elements. The tablet has an IP67 rating, meaning that it should withstand dust and being immersed in a meter of freshwater for up to 30 minutes, though I would advise against intentionally submerging your iPad mini.

As for battery life, Apple says that should be roughly the same as last time. The company says the new iPad mini should run for up to 10 hours when you're watching video or surfing the web over Wi-Fi. That drops down to nine hours if you're browsing over a cellular connection. If you have at least a 35W power adaptor, you'll be able to recharge the battery to 50 percent of its capacity in 30 minutes, Apple says.

The most interesting thing to me about this iPad mini is that it has Apple's first Acoustic Display, which the company says helps it deliver "an even better audio experience." It claims the tablet offers more volume and "punchier bass" thanks to woofers that "are paired through the display." Apple added that "custom tweeters are now hidden between the glass and aluminum enclosure for clearer highs." I'm definitely curious to find out how all of that sounds in practice.

Inevitably, the latest iPad mini comes with a price hike. It starts at $699, which is $150 more than the previous model after Apple increased the price of that in June. Pre-orders open on October 28, and the tablet will hit shelves on November 6.

On Tuesday, Apple gave the iMac and MacBook Pro spec bumps. The desktop and laptop now each have the M6 chipset, which recently debuted in the Mac mini.

Let's start with the new iMac. Apple says the M6's dual 16-core neural engine enables it to deliver up to five times faster on-device AI performance (for Siri AI, Apple Intelligence and local LLMs) than the previous-gen iMac from 2024, which had the M4 chipset. The M6 is said to deliver up to 1.5 times faster performance than the M4 for daily tasks, up to two times faster graphics performance (for photo editing etc.) and up to two times higher gaming performance with ray tracing.

The base-level M6 has 10 CPU cores (two super, three performance and two efficiency cores) and a 10-core CPU, compared with eight of each in the M4. There's the option to have a 12-core M6, in which case your desktop will have four Thunderbolt 4 ports instead of the two that the base model has. The four-port model has a nano-texture glass option as well.

Elsewhere, Apple has introduced its N1 networking chip to the iMac. As such, the computer has support for Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread connectivity. It also has the option to add a 2.5Gb Ethernet port, an upgrade from gigabit Ethernet on the M4 iMac.

Specs otherwise are the same as last time. You'll still get a 24-inch 4.5K Super Retina display, a 12MP Center Stage camera, a six‑speaker Dolby Atmos system, at least 16GB of RAM and 256GB of base storage. The dimensions are identical too, though the M6 iMac is a touch lighter than the previous gen at 9.7 pounds (versus 9.74 pounds). Don't expect any changes to the color options either — the new iMac is still available in blue, green, pink, yellow, purple, orange and silver.

One other thing that's staying the same? The starting price, at least after the $200 increase that Apple gave the M4 iMac over the summer. The new model starts at $1,499, and the four-port version will run you at least $1,699. Pre-orders open October 28 and you'll be able to pick one up in store on November 6.

The latest 14-inch MacBook Pro has the 12-core version of the M6 chipset and the N1 networking chip for Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread support. Those appear to be the only spec changes this time.

Compared with the previous-gen 14-inch MBP, which had an M5 SoC, Apple says the M6 model offers up to 30 percent faster graphics performance and up to 25 percent faster AI workloads. It's said to deliver up to 1.3 faster rendering performance with ray tracing in Blender, up to 1.2 times faster image-processing performance in Affinity and up to 1.3 faster project build performance for compiling code in Xcode.

It doesn't seem like this will be a massive upgrade for folks with an M5 model. However, if you have an older MacBook Pro (or really need those incremental improvements from the previous gen), it might be worth making the switch.

Either way, creatives might want to take note of all the updates that are rolling out for Apple Creative Studio. Apple has given Final Cut Pro a long-awaited quality-of-life upgrade by letting you pull clips from the primary storyline and place them anywhere on the timeline, which seems like very basic functionality to have! MotionVFX is integrated into the app on Mac as well, so you'll have thousands of elements at your fingertips for your video edits.

If you have a Mac that supports Apple Intelligence and is set to US English, Logic Pro has an "intelligent assistant" powered by OpenAI that's available in beta. The app has an an enhanced Stem Splitter that can now separate drums as well. In addition, Keynote, Pages and Numbers now have a chatbot you can use to adjust presentations, documents and spreadsheets.

You won't have to pay more than you would have for the M5 model following this summer's price increase. The M6-powered 14-inch MacBook Pro starts at $1,999 for 16GB of RAM and 1TB of storage. Again, pre-orders start on Wednesday with general availability to follow on November 6.
