#120. Japanese prosecutors charge US marine suspected of robbing and killing woman

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Devin Jacob Ballard, 20, denies charges that he fatally strangled Anna Yagi, 39, in a hotel on the island of Okinawa

Prosecutors in Japan have filed formal charges against a US marine suspected of murdering and robbing a woman while stationed on the country’s island of Okinawa, according to Japanese media.

Devin Jacob Ballard, a 20-year-old lance corporal assigned to Marine Corps Air Station Futenma in Okinawa, is charged with fatally strangling Anna Yagi, 39, at a hotel in the city and stealing from her a backpack containing a wallet, the public broadcaster NHK reported.

Ballard had initially been arrested in Yagi’s killing on 4 October, about a day after she was found dead in Naha, Okinawa’s capital. Prosecutors indicted him in the case on Sunday, and he has reportedly denied the charges.

NHK and other Japanese media outlets had previously reported that Ballard was arrested after surveillance camera footage from near the scene of Yagi’s killing showed a man entering a room with a woman and later leaving the scene with her belongings.

Ballard evidently matched the description of the suspect seen in the footage, was confronted and agreed to accompany investigators voluntarily to a local police station, Japanese media reported.

While being questioned by police, Ballard denied having a hand in Yagi’s killing, saying, “I don’t know anything about it,” NHK reported. But during subsequent questioning Ballard was alleged to have “made statements that partially hinted at his involvement”, the broadcaster reported.

A statement reported by NHK and reported to Yagi’s family on Sunday expressed a hope that Ballard “will confront what he has done and make amends commensurate with what he has done”.

“She lost her life at the young age of 39,” the statement from Yagi’s family added. “She … wanted to live much, much longer.”

Yagi’s killing prompted protests in Okinawa and the Japanese capital of Tokyo, with political leaders publicly condemning her killing. It also triggered a curfew and alcohol ban for US military members stationed in Okinawa, where most of the 54,000 US troops in Japan are based.

Crimes committed by US troops in Japan – including rape, assault, hit-and-run and drunk-driving – have long strained relations between the countries, which are close military allies.

A Japanese court in June 2025 sentenced US marine Jamel Clayton, then 22, to seven years in prison after he was convicted of choking and sexually assaulting a woman in her 20s.

In 2017, a former US marine and civilian military base worker named Kenneth Shinzato was sentenced to life in prison for the rape and murder of a local Okinawa woman.

US military forces in Japan that same year banned all personnel from consuming alcohol after an intoxicated US marine crashed his vehicle into a mini-truck, killing a local driver, age 61.

The Yomiuri Shimbun newspaper reported that about 6,000 US military personnel have been arrested since the US returned Okinawa to Japan after the second world war in 1972.

There were 80 such arrests in 2024, constituting a 20-year high.

After Ballard’s arrest, Shinjiro Koizumi, Japan’s defense minister, said: “Incidents and accidents involving US military personnel cause great anxiety among the local community and must not be allowed to happen.”

Tracking recommendations from top Wall Street analysts can help investors pick attractive stocks, as these experts assess a company's ability to pay dividends consistently.

Here are three dividend-paying stocks that are highlighted by Wall Street's top pros, as tracked by TipRanks, a platform that ranks analysts based on their past performance.

Integrated oil and gas giant Chevron (CVX) is a dividend king. The company has increased its dividend for 39 consecutive years. At a quarterly dividend of $1.78 per share (annualized dividend of $7.12 per share), CVX offers a yield of 3.14%. Chevron will announce its third-quarter results on October 30.

In an earnings preview note on U.S. oil majors, Goldman Sachs analyst Neil Mehta reiterated a buy rating on Chevron with a price target of $241. The analyst is focused on the company's international growth prospects across Latin America, the Middle East, West Africa, and the Eastern Mediterranean.

Specifically, Mehta highlighted management's commentary on solid execution in Venezuela, with gross production expected to more than double to nearly 600 kbd (kilo barrels per day) by 2031 via three joint ventures. He also noted a recent oil and gas condensate discovery in offshore Angola and is awaiting commercial updates on the West Qurna 2 and Nasiriyah projects in Iraq.

Furthermore, Mehta expects CVX to report improved productivity and capital efficiency across domestic shale assets. He expects the improvement mainly in the Permian Basin, where Chevron projects advanced chemical treatments to drive about a 10% recovery uplift in new wells and bring down capital spend per barrel by about 25% year-over-year. Overall, Mehta expects Chevron to report Q3 2026 EPS of $4.95, above the consensus of $4.89.

"While unfavorable timing effects and volatile commodity prices may present quarterly earnings headwinds, we highlight healthy U.S. refining margins and strong asset utilization as partial offsets," said Mehta.

#2305. Ballon d’Or 2026: Why the winner will not please everyone

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Axel Clody | 26 Oct 2026 13:40

Champions League, World Cup, individual statistics: the six leading candidates for the 2026 Ballon d’Or, including Lamine Yamal, Harry Kane and Kylian Mbappe, all have strong arguments. But none has a flawless record.

Between missed opportunities, absent trophies and disappointing performances in decisive moments, whatever the verdict tonight at the Ballon d’Or ceremony on Monday 26 October, the jury’s choice will be open to criticism. Let us go through the favourites one by one.

With 73 goals and eight assists in 2025-26, Kane has the most impressive statistical record of the six candidates and is logically among the favourites.

German champion, German Cup winner and Super Cup winner, the Englishman also scored in every Champions League match against Real Madrid and Paris Saint-Germain.

But his European run ended in the semi-finals, without lifting the trophy. Above all, despite six goals in seven matches for England, his influence diminished as the stakes rose at the World Cup, notably from the quarter-finals. At 33, can the Bayern Munich striker be crowned without having won either of the year’s two major competitions?

Ten goals and four assists in eight matches: Mbappe delivered an exceptional World Cup, becoming the competition’s all-time top scorer. With 58 goals and 13 assists over the whole season, he also finished top of the scoring charts in La Liga and the Champions League.

But his collective record is far less flattering: no trophy with Real Madrid.

Real Madrid fell short in the major competitions, with their captain unable to turn the tide, notably in the Champions League. The same applies with France at the World Cup against Spain. Can the Ballon d’Or reward such a decisive individual when his club and his national team both ended the year without a trophy?

World champion, Spanish champion and Spanish Super Cup winner, Yamal has everything needed to become the youngest winner in Ballon d’Or history at 19. His 25 goals and 20 assists for Barcelona show a high-level season.

But his European run leaves a sense of something unfinished, with elimination in the Champions League quarter-finals by Atletico Madrid, where he did not weigh in enough.

His World Cup also raises questions: only one goal in eight matches, despite being Spain’s main attacking weapon, is not many. Is his talent enough to offset those flaws?

With 24 goals and 10 assists, Kvaratskhelia made his mark by helping Paris Saint-Germain win another Champions League title.

Decisive in the last eight matches of the competition, the Georgian was undoubtedly the best player of that edition. He also won the French league title and several international trophies with Paris.

His candidacy faces a major obstacle, however: he did not take part in the World Cup, as Georgia failed to qualify. There is a precedent, that of Kevin Keegan, crowned in 1978 without playing in the World Cup that year. But Kvaratskhelia will need to convince the Ballon d’Or jury that his European campaign is enough to make up for that absence.

With 27 goals and 35 assists for his club, Olise produced a remarkable season statistically. His seven assists in eight matches at the World Cup are also a record for a single edition, ahead of Pele.

But that flattering record does not tell the whole story: the Bayern Munich winger missed his most important appointment in the Champions League, the semi-final second leg against Paris Saint-Germain, before finishing the World Cup on a disappointing note. His influence waned when France needed him most against Morocco and Spain.

German champion, German Cup winner and German Super Cup winner, he has an honourable list of honours, but his absences in decisive moments could cost him dearly.

Dembele has one weighty argument: his decisive role in Paris Saint-Germain’s victorious Champions League run. His double against Liverpool in the quarter-finals, his three goals against Bayern Munich in the semi-finals and his penalty in the final helped swing the biggest matches.

But the holder left more doubts with France. His six goals and two assists at the World Cup are honourable, except that a hat-trick against Norway B accounts for half of his goal tally.

Beyond the numbers, he appeared less influential than Mbappe and Olise in the French attack and, like his team-mates, could do nothing against Spain. A second Ballon d’Or in a row therefore looks far from certain.

It’s worth flagging is that EasyJet currently has over 80 A319s, and those won’t be getting Starlink, as the plan is for them to be retired by 2029. So while installation will be complete by 2028, it’ll be some point in 2029 before all active planes actually have Starlink.

Now, here’s the catch — while most airlines that have introduced Starlink are offering the service to passengers for free, that’s not consistently the case. EasyJet’s announcement doesn’t reference the service being free, so I think it’s likely that this service will be available for purchase, or at least only complimentary for select passengers. I’m curious to see what the pricing model is like.

Either way, this is quite the inflight connectivity upgrade, given that EasyJet currently doesn’t offer any sort of connectivity. So to go from nothing to Starlink is quite impressive! The airline describes this investment as being a key step to enhancing the passenger experience, while also aiming to attract more business and leisure travelers by offering more of the services that they want.

Here’s how EasyJet CEO Kenton Jarvis describes this:

“In an increasingly digital world, staying connected matters more than ever to many of our customers, whether that’s holidaymakers looking to unwind with inflight entertainment, or our business customers keeping up to speed with what’s happening on the ground.”

“Innovation is in easyJet’s DNA and partnering with SpaceX to bring Starlink’s high-speed internet connectivity onboard for the first time will not only further enhance the experience we provide millions of customers, but means we are offering more of the options both business and leisure travellers want when they choose who they fly with.”

“Giving customers the opportunity to get online in the air will mean even greater choice and convenience when you fly with easyJet. Ultimately, it’s yet another way we’re making travel easy.”

Ryanair is Europe’s largest low cost carrier, and it’s also one of the world’s highest margin airlines. Admittedly it’s not fair to directly compare Ryanair and EasyJet, as they have slightly different business models. However, there’s no denying that they’re in the same competitive set.

With EasyJet now signing up for Starlink, I think it’s interesting to point out how Ryanair CEO Michael O’Leary insists that Ryanair won’t be getting inflight Wi-Fi any time soon. Ryanair is all about the bottom line, and as O’Leary explained, “we don’t think our passengers are willing to pay for Wi-Fi for an average one-hour flight.”

Earlier in the year this stance enraged Elon Musk, and he ended up getting into a public spat with O’Leary, even threatening to buy Ryanair.

Ryanair held its own, though, and ended up launching a “Big Idiot Seat Sale,” for “Elon Musk and any other idiots on X.”

EasyJet plans to introduce Starlink Wi-Fi as of the first half of 2027, with installation complete by early 2028. While Starlink is coming to all planes that will remain in the fleet in the long run, A319s are being retired by 2029, so won’t get Starlink. The major caveat here is that it sounds like EasyJet will be charging for Starlink, as the announcement makes no reference to it being free.

Either way, it’s cool to see truly high speed internet increasingly becoming the norm on flights, even on budget carriers, as this is such a nice boost to the passenger experience.

Ballhog or ball hog is a streetwear fashion style growing in popularity among younger people. The cool ones, anyway. The idea is to wear vintage clothes that look normal, even drab, to most, but that people in the know can recognize as interesting or unique. Like the best youth trends, it's an insider vs. outsider thing that takes connoisseurship to understand. Some examples: If you're wearing a late 1990s Helmut Lang denim jacket, most people will think "thrifted," but a ballhog-enjoyer would recognize it as a seminal piece of streetwear history. If you want to be ballhog, rocking a pair of skinny jeans isn't going to cut it unless they're from Dior Homme or at least Cheap Monday.

The style is named after the Ball Hog Collective, a group of rappers, Twitch streamers, and content creators founded by the Atlanta-based indie record label Ballhog. It was popularized by streamer Brady Goodwin, seen below looking quite ballhog:

There has been a steady stream of news stories about kids overdosing on Benadryl over the last few years. They call it "Benny tripping," and it's achieved by taking too much of the allergy medicine, like 10 times the amount recommended. Among the active ingredients in Benadryl is Diphenhydramine, and it's a particularly nasty drug. Unlike most recreational substances, tripping on Benadryl is not fun. It's a deliriant, and taking too much results in hallucinations that are usually described as terrifying—swarms of spiders, shadow people, whispering voices, etc. Benadryl overdoses can result in loss of short term memory, displacement from time and space, and panic attacks that last for hours or even days.
