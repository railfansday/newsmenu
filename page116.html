#4011. Boltgun 2 and Warhammer Survivors devs Auroch Digital say they're laying off 25% of their staff in now-deleted posts

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Not long after releasing boomer shooter Warhammer 40,000: Boltgun II and right before Warhammer Survivors launches in November, developers Auroch Digital have unfortunately given a public statement confirming that roughly a quarter of its current staff will be laid off as part of a restructuring. What's strange — to the point that layoffs are, unfortunately, expected — is that the initial social media posts, on both LinkedIn and Twitter, have now been deleted.

In the studio's statement, posted yesterday, Auroch notes their pride in Boltgun II and Starship Troopers: Ultimate Bug War, both released this year, but "despite that creative success, the same market conditions that have challenged so many of our fellow developers have now also affected us." Apologies to the staff affected follow, with a plea "to partners and friends in the industry: if you are hiring, please consider finding a place for our staff in your studio. They have worked on everything from indie shooters to AAA [co-development], and their talent and breadth is second-to-none." The studio also confirms that post-launch development on both Boltgun II and Warhammer Survivors will continue despite the loss of staff.

This is, sadly, not an uncommon thing to report on by any means. There are layoffs every goddamn week, if not several. This is unique, though, in that in the time between the post going up yesterday and my writing of this piece, the public statements have been deleted. There's an archive of the original image posted here, as you won't find it on Auroch's Twitter or LinkedIn anymore. Even with the barrage of layoff news, I can't think of another time that a statement has been outright retracted within a day. Whether this was posted in error, too early, or some other reason I can't know of, hopefully there'll be some clarity soon and, more importantly, opportunities available for the staff affected.

RPS have reached out to Auroch Digital for comment and clarification.

#3892. One Day Out From Early Access, Emberville Has Been Delayed To 2027

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Originally set to enter early access on October 27, action-adventure RPG Emberville has been delayed by over two months to January 7, 2027. The last-minute delay gives the team time to make "essential improvements" before Emberville reaches its fans.

The upcoming game mixes Stardew Valley-like farming and community building with action-adventure RPG combat. Trapped in a magical underground dungeon, the player must uncover the truth about their cursed prison while revitalizing the ruined town of Emberville.

Emberville's demo was one of the most played during Steam Next Fest last week; however, player feedback from the demo has led to a last-minute delay.

"Some of the improvements we want to make are systems changes that can't be done quickly," the Emberville team said, acknowledging feedback from Next Fest players. "Reworking bow combat, for example, would take more time than we had prior to our Early Access release even though the team is working around the clock. After carefully considering our options, we have decided to move the release date and include some essential improvements in the Early Access launch build."

While the game is intended to grow and improve throughout its early access period, with the developer planning to add new content incrementally, the studio wants to reach a certain baseline before it launches.

"Our team has a clear vision for how Emberville will develop throughout early access," the Steam post continues. "On January 7, our goal is to deliver an experience that shows what makes the game distinctive, establishes its core gameplay, and offers a compelling glimpse of how the game will grow throughout early access."

While some players are disappointed by the eleventh-hour delay, others have said they're happy for the Emberville team to take as much time as it needs to get the game up to scratch. Some players have also speculated that Emberville is just the latest game to move its release date in order to avoid losing players to the looming release of GTA 6.

#2322. West Ham offered chance to sign former £73m winger on a free transfer

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

West Ham boast a squad brimming with talented players as they look to return to the Premier League at the first time of asking. Arne Engels and Manor Solomon were among the big names to join WHUFC in the summer, and Nuno Espirito Santo has a vast pool of players to call upon.

However, the Hammers have been offered the chance to make a free agent signing in the form of Jadon Sancho. TalkSPORT states that the 26-year-old’s representatives have offered him to the Championship side as he looks to find his next club.

Sancho left Manchester United in the summer, five years after his £73m arrival from Borussia Dortmund. He was offered a contract by Spanish club Getafe earlier this month but it was worth less than £10-a-week.

Sancho’s career has been on an unfortunate downward spiral ever since returning to England with Manchester United in 2021. He had emerged as one of the country’s biggest prospects in a hugely successful stay at Borussia Dortmund, but it has only been downhill since.

After loans with Chelsea and Aston Villa failed to spark his career into life, Sancho is now a free agent. He is still only 26 though, and the standard he plays at when at the peak of his powers could make him tempting for West Ham.

The London-born winger usually operates on the left but can be used on the other flank. He’s a true wide playmaker, offering excellent close control and dribbling alongside fantastic vision and good link-up play in pockets and tight spaces.

If he could rediscover form, Sancho could emerge as one of the Championship’s standout stars. However, he would need time to get back to fitness, and his struggles in recent times would raise questions over whether he can get back to his previous heights.

An fit and flying Sancho is an understandably attractive option for West Ham. However, he is not a player they need, and through no fault of his own, he could become a bit of a distraction given the noise that follows him after a tough Manchester United stint.

Nuno has Solomon and £18m man Edvin Austbo to pick from on the left, ensuring WHUFC are covered for both the immediate future and long-term.

Sancho would be a luxury signing, and although he’s a free agent, he would not be cheap. The former England international will likely have sizeable wage demands that could become a burden at the London Stadium.

Sancho would no doubt be an exciting player to have in the Championship, but from a squad building and financial perspective, he may be one to avoid for the Hammers.

#3865. Composer shares 20 minutes of music created for Mass Effect 5 following tragic closure of BioWare

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Austin Wintory has shared twenty minutes of music they pitched to BioWare and EA in hopes of joining the Mass Effect 5 development team, following the tragic closure of BioWare last week.

The composer, known for his work on Journey and Sword of the Sea, uploaded the music to his personal YouTube channel. It was made without knowledge of the narrative direction of the new Mass Effect game, but was presented as a surprise to the companies in 2025 as part of years of conversation. Wintory writes in the video that it was a "direct homage" to Mass Effect 1 composers Jack Wall and Sam Hulick, leaning into analog synths and "a more complex integration of the orchestra", a hybrid approach Wintory compares to The Matrix movies scores.

This collection of music includes a track called The Legacy of Shepard, which includes "deliberate winks" to the original Mass Effect theme. The second track - Sailing the Relays - features the blend of synth and orchestra mentioned earlier quite heavily. All in all, there are eight tracks included in total, with the eighth being a remix of that first Legacy of Shepard composition.

The video's description contains a sombre message on BioWare's closure, as well as gratitude towards the music departments involved in the conversations Wintory took part it: "Receiving the news alongside everyone else that EA had decided to not only cancel the project but shutdown BioWare entirely was a deeply felt loss. It's been hard to see dear friends lose their jobs, and this beloved franchise seemingly vanish forever.

"Thank you nonetheless to everyone at BioWare and EA's music department for gifting me the chance to dream in your sandbox. It was a privilege to do even that much."

Wintory is not alone in their sadness. EA's closure of BioWare, which included the firing of 50 employees and a further 350 across other EA-owned studios, many across the industry came forward to decry the decision. The decision may even have political ramifications, as one Canadian party leader has expressed a desire to allow BioWare employees to buy back the studio with federal support. This all happened shortly after EA's $55bn private acquisition from Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and Saudi Arabia's Savvy Games Group.

In footage from the ceremony, Mbappe also does not stand up to congratulate Kane when he is announced as the winner.

#4838. Zcash co-founder calls to stop funding ‘over $125M’ grants program

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Sean Bowe says ZCG has enough money to keep funding projects and wants its share of new ZEC redirected to a reserve for the network.

Zcash co-founder Sean Bowe proposed ending the automatic funding stream to Zcash Community Grants (ZCG), saying its treasury was large enough to continue funding projects without further automatic payments.

“ZCG has over $125M in funding, and will receive millions more before the spigot is turned off,” Bowe said Tuesday on X. “The program was never designed to handle this amount of funding.”

Bowe shared a draft GitHub proposal that would redirect ZCG’s 8% share of the Zcash (ZEC) block subsidy to the network’s sustainability reserve, while leaving its existing funds untouched.

The allocation would flow to the Network Sustainability Mechanism until the current funding period ends in 2028. The mechanism is designed to hold ZEC out of circulation and gradually reissue it through future block rewards.

In September, Haseeb Qureshi, managing partner at crypto venture capital firm Dragonfly, proposed ending the Zcash developer fund when its current funding period expires, saying that it risks being “politicized” as it approaches $100 million in value.

ZCG’s dashboard listed 91,453 ZEC and $8.8 million in cash as of Wednesday, with $2.4 million in outstanding grant commitments. The ZEC holdings alone were worth about $111 million at press time.

The network’s separate development lockbox held 72,351 ZEC, worth roughly $89 million, according to ZecStats.

Policymakers have accused tech companies of using addictive design features, such as endless scroll and autoplay, to lure and keep minors online, while parents worry about megacorporations collecting their children’s data.

“It’s the equivalent of putting our kids in cars with no seat belts, with no airbags, and having them drive on roads with no speed limits and no traffic lights,” former U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy said last year in a warning to Congress about the impact of social media on children’s health.

In December 2025, Australia became the first to implement such legislation, barring children under 16 from creating social media accounts. As of October, 82 percent of Australian parents support such restrictions. This decision started a “domino effect,” said Tommaso Giardini, the associate director of the Digital Policy Alert, a nonprofit focused on online transparency.

Governments from Brazil to Malaysia to the United Arab Emirates have since adopted policies that curb minors’ access to online platforms, with Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Twitch, X, and YouTube among the most common targets. “Selling the idea of protecting children is very easy,” Giardini said.

Harder is deciding which strategy to pursue. Countries have generally followed three main policy approaches: the Australia model, the China model, and the India model.

Young people look at an iPad in Sydney on Dec. 7, 2025.George Chan/Getty Images

Australia’s model of social media restriction may be the most straightforward—and the most severe. Users must be age 16 or older to create a social media account, period. Parents cannot consent on behalf of their children.

“One of the virtues of age-based age-gating is that everyone knows the law,” said Terry Flew, a professor of digital communication and culture at the University of Sydney, who likened the policy to similar age-based bans on alcohol, smoking, and vaping.

Three months after Australia’s ban took effect, Indonesia implemented a similar under-16 ban, though the Southeast Asian country included one major caveat: Children under age 13 will only have access to age-approved platforms, but those between ages 13 and 15 can go on “low risk” sites with parental consent.

This model has also proved popular across Europe, where Austria, France, Germany, Greece, Spain, Turkey, and others have passed or are discussing age-based systems, each with their own conditions.

“It is more interventionist, but it feels less interventionist,” Giardini said, adding that the approach “will probably cause less skepticism than the government or some government agency going into the details of which types of children can access which types of platforms for how many minutes.”

Yet age-based restrictions have also come under criticism for being ineffective. According to an Australian government report published in March, around 7 out of 10 parents whose children were on social media prior to the ban said that they still had their accounts despite the order for platforms to remove minors’ profiles. Other countries with age-based systems, such as the United Kingdom, have recorded a surge in virtual private network usage as minors attempt to circumvent the prohibitions.

Several governments have responded by increasing their penalties on social media platforms. In June, Australia announced that it would double fines on companies that fail to crack down on minors’ access, going from around $34 million to $68 million. However, experts suggest that the impact is limited. Facebook generated $200.97 billion in annual revenue last year; a fine of $68 million is just 0.03 percent of the company’s yearly earnings.

Meanwhile, children’s rights experts warn that young people, particularly queer youth, will become more isolated without these online resources. Adults fear that a new bout of data collection—when they must prove their identities to access these sites—could violate their own privacy.

Most of all, though, analysts say that simply restricting access does not address social media’s most concerning features. “By implementing this ban, the government is giving up on telling platforms how to regulate their algorithms, which is the essence of the problem,” said Francesco Bailo, the co-director of the University of Sydney’s Center for AI, Trust, and Governance. He warned that vulnerable people over age 16 remain exposed to the platforms’ addictive designs.

Estonia, the only EU member state that opposes such bans, has made a similar argument. “The way to approach this, to me, is not to make kids responsible for that harm [stemming from social media platforms] and start self-regulating,” Estonian Education Minister Kristina Kallas told Politico in April. The “responsibility is on the governments and on the corporation side.”

China—with its well-known Great Firewall—is no stranger to restricting online content. But in the realms of social media and gaming, Beijing has been a leader in protecting minors’ exposure. As early as 2002, China banned people under 18 from entering internet cafes, and increasingly tighter regulations have shaped how young people can access the digital world.

That approach reached new heights in April 2025, when China launched its systemwide “minor mode” initiative. Whereas Australia’s age-based policy restricts usage all the time, China’s model offers a multitiered approach with caveats based on age, day of the week, and time of day.

Children under 18 are barred from using nonessential internet applications between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m., and screen time during the day is limited from 40 minutes to two hours depending on one’s age. Devices are required to remind users to rest every 30 minutes, online content is carefully tailored to match one’s age level, and parents can monitor their children’s activity.

“China has built a holistic, systemic machinery for regulating minors’ internet use,” said Fengshu Liu, a professor at the University of Oslo who specializes in sociocultural issues among young people in China. Minor mode works at the device level, though, which gives parents ultimate control over whether to activate the system.

The EU has considered a similar strategy. On July 13, the bloc issued a report recommending that children under 13 have “time-limited” access to social media—and only with parental supervision. In September, von der Leyen outlined a tiered system that includes “mini accounts” for teens aged 13 and 14 that would restrict screen time and minimize contact with strangers.

The United States has taken a similar approach, even as the federal government has considered a nationwide ban.

In 2022, California—home to Silicon Valley—became the first state to enact a law requiring age-appropriate online design codes for minors. Since then, the state has joined Colorado, Minnesota, and New York in requiring social media platforms to display mental health warnings. Several other states have enacted or are considering legislation that requires age-verification measures.

“I’m getting the sense that every [U.S.] state wants an all-the-above strategy” for addressing who should be responsible for a child’s welfare online, Joel Thayer, president of the Digital Progress Institute think tank, told Tech Policy Press. However, “it really just comes down to what the political will is for the particular legislator and where they see the most acute issues and what they think the most effective mechanism is.”

Many lawmakers in India share the same mindset, arguing that waiting for federal action could hurt a politician’s reputation and that a localized approach is more efficient at responding to parental concerns.

However, a state-by-state approach has its downsides. India is “a large country, and enforcement doesn’t happen,” said Mishi Choudhary, a technology lawyer and online civil liberties activist. “Businesses don’t have the same predictability, and that is why all these identity verification checks are such a patchwork that nobody really understands what the transparency is.”

Around 500 million people in India are active social media users, some 750 million people own a smartphone, and just over 1 billion individuals are internet users. The country also has the largest population of young people in the world, according to a 2024 UNICEF report.

Inconsistent implementation can also be found in the United States. “It is very difficult to actually geolocate particular users,” said Kate Ruane, director of the Free Expression Project at the Center for Democracy and Technology. For instance, if you live just across the border from a state with a different social media policy than your own state’s, then “you may find yourself subject to age verification in order to access a service that didn’t used to require that of you.”

Ruane expects that the legality of social media restrictions is likely to come before the U.S. Supreme Court in the near future. Justice Brett Kavanaugh has already filed a concurrence arguing that age-based restrictions likely violate the First Amendment.

Funding Rate tracks whether long or short positions dominate an asset’s perpetual market. When positive, it implies there are more long contract positions in the market than short positions, while a negative rate implies that short positions are dominating.

At press time, data shows that CAP has a Funding Rate of 0.0182% on the chart, which implies that there have been more long positions in the market than short contracts.
