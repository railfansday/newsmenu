#4626. 1981 Toyota Celica Supra on BaT: The Supra Story Starts Here

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Vin Diesel wasn’t even driving yet when this first-gen Supra rolled into the showroom.

A car that’s a Celica and a Supra? You bet: Long before Paul Walker hauled a wrecked Toyota over to Vin Diesel’s garage to be rebuilt as a drag-racing superweapon, the Supra was just a trim level for the Celica coupe. It wasn’t so much about speed, but about the refinement of six-cylinder power—a Celica, but a super version.

This 1981 Toyota Celica Supra is gliding into view over at Bring a Trailer (which, like Car and Driver, is part of Hearst Autos). The Celica Supra marks the start of the Supra legend and was a big grand tourer with polished manners. It also just happens to be a match for current Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi’s first car, called the Celica XX in its home market. A Toyota good enough for Japan’s Margaret Thatcher? That’s an iron-block lady.

See all results for used cars for sale near 500001

The headline is, of course, the inline-six engine, here displacing 2.8 liters and producing 116 horsepower and 145 pound-feet of torque. Not quite good enough for a 10-second-or-less quarter-mile lifestyle, but super-smooth power that can trace its heritage back to the Toyota 2000GT. It's paired with a five-speed manual gearbox sending power to the rear wheels.

The rest of the car is a long-nosed version of the second-generation Celica, which is to say Toyota’s take on the personal luxury coupe craze of the late 1970s. The interior features vinyl and cloth seating, air conditioning, and even a graphic equalizer for the cassette stereo.

A few years later, the Celica would be a sporty front-driver, and the Supra its own nameplate, complete with available turbocharged power and arguably reaching its apogee with the twin-turbocharged fourth-generation Supra, as featured in The Fast and the Furious. But this is where that story started.

As for the six-cylinder heart that gives this high-level Celica its Supra designation, that’s been comprehensively reworked, with new valve seals and head gaskets, replaced bearings, and a new muffler. It's ready to hit the road.

The Supra’s currently on hiatus for now, with the Celica poised to make a return as a potential replacement for the rally-bred GR Corolla and (overseas) GR Yaris. Once, though, both legendary names occupied the same space.

Stellar blockchain platform recorded its highest daily load over the past month. Over the day of October 23, the network processed 14.16 million transactions. Data from the analytics tracker Chainspect shows that the average operation processing speed on that day was 163.9 tx/s.

The high pace persisted into the next day as well: at certain periods on October 24, throughput produced new spikes, reaching 224.6 TPS.

The key point of this event is the network's economic stability under pressure. Unlike most major blockchains, where a multifold increase in traffic immediately clogs the network and drives up gas costs, Stellar kept the average fee at $0.00012.

A similar spike in activity occurred on October 11 (12 million transactions at 139 TPS), and the cost of transfers remained just as low.

The current traffic growth is a consequence of recent code updates. In the Protocol 28 ("Adapter") upgrade released in September, developers rebuilt the throughput mechanisms for increased flow, delivering a peak of 217.4 TPS over a 100-block window on September 26.

The infrastructure optimization continued with the launch of Chainlink price data feeds on October 14. Right now, developers are preparing the network for the next upgrade, Protocol 30 ("Bell"), which will be voted on in the coming days.

The October transaction peak was driven by an inflow of large capital into the stablecoin and tokenized real-world asset (RWA) sectors. According to a fresh report for Q3 2025, the total financial volume of transactions on the Stellar network reached $33.2 billion (+71% quarter over quarter).

At the same time, the volume of stablecoin transfers grew by 121% to $25.1 billion (+314% year over year), partly thanks to the new asset USDT0, which gained $200 million in turnover in 10 weeks.

In parallel, the volume of RWA-class assets on the blockchain reached $4.1 billion. A landmark event for the segment was the first institutional repo deal with automatic rollover collateralized by U.S. Treasuries (USDM1 tokens).

Against this backdrop, Stellar secured second place in the global ranking of L1 networks by the number of active developers: currently, almost four thousand specialists are developing the platform's infrastructure.

In unison, the network maintains uptime at 99.99% despite the influx of new users — the total number of accounts has already exceeded 11 million. #2397. Nuno’s “Self-Harming” West Ham Leave Fans Asking Serious Questions

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

“We just need to cut out the self harm stuff.”

That was the particularly memorable verdict from West Ham supporter John Ayris following Saturday’s 3-2 defeat to Southampton, and it probably sums up the biggest frustration surrounding Nuno Espírito Santo’s team at the moment.

John was responding beneath Martin’s player ratings, where the Hammers’ dreadful opening again dominated the discussion. West Ham were two goals down inside 11 minutes and, despite eventually producing 28 attempts, spent virtually the entire afternoon trying to repair damage they had inflicted upon themselves.

The worrying thing is that Nuno undoubtedly knows West Ham are making life more difficult with these slow starts. He repeatedly alludes to the issue, but there is a significant difference between recognising a problem and possessing the ability to fix it.

Whatever Nuno is currently trying clearly isn’t working, and I suspect he may have to do something drastic to break the nasty habbit. As I wrote immediately after the game, West Ham’s slow-start problem finally came home to roost against Southampton after weeks of getting away with it.

Los Angeles Irons went even further, writing: “I believe we have the best squad in the league. The problem has to be the manager. What would happen at other clubs if their team consistently doesn’t show up for half a game?”

I probably agree West Ham have the best squad in the second flight. Nuno certainly has more than enough talent at his disposal, yet we remain incredibly slow and I wouldn’t be surprised if we’re amongst the slowest teams in the division.

That’s going to remain a problem however strong the squad is, particularly when we’re not clinical enough to convert the huge number of chances we create.

Mukasa and Engels might have benefitted from the experience of Edson Alvarez

Trevor from Billericay made a similar point, arguing that with the squad available, “starting on the front foot with attacking intent should not be such an insolvable issue.”

George Howell was more specific, describing the decision to start without a recognised holding midfielder as “suicidal.”

For me, Southampton was probably the clearest example yet of the problem caused by not having a specialist defensive midfielder in there.

Arne Engels seemed to spend much of the game firefighting, while I actually thought Divin Mukasa was our best creative outlet. It would have been nice for Mukasa to have some security behind him to allow him to concentrate on what he does best.

I’m sure the defence would have appreciated it too because Southampton appeared capable of cutting through West Ham whenever they attacked. Martin made the same observation in his morning blog, with the lack of protection in front of the back line impossible to ignore.

Not every supporter thought West Ham were dreadful. Harvey Fox pointed out that the Hammers could conceivably have scored six or seven given the chances created, while John himself felt we should have won because of how dominant we became.

That’s almost what makes the problem more frustrating.

West Ham have enough attacking quality to overpower most teams in this division. But continually handing opponents a head start is footballing self-harm of the entirely avoidable kind.

Now he has to prove he knows how to stop it.

Abra is a competitor, so its motive is obvious, and a minority stake of about 8% is far from control. Commercial agreements between American and Azul that have not been implemented, and Azul expects them only after antitrust immunity is approved (assuming that it is.)
