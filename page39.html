📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Shinhan Financial Group Co., Ltd. (SHG -1.38%) reported a 12.9% decrease in quarterly net income, reflecting a significant decline in noninterest income driven by capital market volatility. Management prioritized shareholder returns, approving a KRW 450 billion buyback program and maintaining a quarterly dividend of KRW 740 per share. The company announced the KRW 693.3 billion acquisition of Lotte Insurance to diversify its non-banking portfolio, intending to fund the purchase through internal capital reallocation. While interest income grew slightly, net interest margins were pressured by rising funding costs and competitive deposit pricing. The group maintained a common equity tier 1 ratio of 13.6% and emphasized selective asset growth to manage risk-weighted assets effectively.

#3202. Margin debt growth slows sharply as stock market warning signs build

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The pace at which investors are borrowing money to invest in stocks has cooled substantially, raising concern that the stock market may be due for a fall.

Recent FINRA data shows the annual growth in margin debt fell to 31.4% in September from 53.7% in May, Bank of America technical strategist Paul Ciana pointed out in a Friday note. Ciana warned that slowing borrowing momentum from extreme levels has been associated with previous market peaks.

May's growth rate in 2026 was the fourth highest since 1998, according to a BofA analysis. The three higher extremes took place in 2000, 2007 and 2021 — all of which preceded stock market declines.

Earlier this year, borrowing was increasing much faster than stocks were rising. Analysts view high borrowing levels as a contrarian signal, similar to bull-bear sentiment indicators.

At $1.48 trillion, margin debt in September is 1.5% below June's record of $1.5 trillion.

High leverage can have snowball effects in a market downturn. Lower prices trigger demands for additional collateral or forced sales, which accelerate a downturn.

Individual investors can be hit hard. Tesla investor Hy Luu, for example, refinanced his home to raise $30,000 to pay down margin debt during the stock's 2022 decline.

BofA sees other market warning signs, including widening high-yield credit spreads and a rotation toward defensive sectors like healthcare and consumer staples.

Paradoxically, the rotation into those sectors has pushed them to "bubble-risk levels," BofA's equity derivatives team said in a note on Tuesday. Similar shifts occurred in the late 1990s dot-com era and Covid rally, they added. "These dynamics provide further evidence that markets continue to move towards a more bubble-like state."

The bull case is that the midterms could alleviate some of the market's uncertainty and trigger a relief rally, Ciana noted. "Typically, risk assets such as the S&P 500 rebound after midterms unless more severe downside risks materialize."

#3620. 3 Absurdly Cheap Dividend Stocks to Buy for Under $100 Right Now

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Interest rates and bond yields are rising, making dividend stocks less enticing, since investors can earn high returns without having to buy income-producing stocks. But the good news is that this has led to many dividend stocks trading lower lately, pushing their yields higher in the process and potentially setting them up for big gains later on.

Three excellent dividend stocks that are absurdly cheap right now and that are trading below $100 include AT&T (T +0.43%), Pfizer (PFE +0.03%), and Zoetis (ZTS -3.38%). Here's why they can be excellent options for income investors to consider for their portfolios right now.

AT&T's business has been doing well this year, but there simply hasn't been a huge catalyst to make the telecom stock a hot buy. Investors are going elsewhere for high returns, and there are also concerns that Space Exploration Technologies Corp plans to turn Starlink into a major U.S. carrier, potentially disrupting the entire telecom industry.

Those worries have been weighing on AT&T's stock, which is down about 16% this year. Even the company's recent earnings report, as strong as it was, wasn't enough to turn its fortunes around. AT&T posted more than 5% revenue growth in the September quarter, a far higher rate than usual, which should have given investors renewed confidence in the business. But that hasn't been the case, at least for now.

The stock is trading around $21 right now, and its price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 11 is incredibly low. The S&P 500 average is much higher at 23. AT&T's stock also yields 5.3%, providing investors with an extra incentive to buy it today, as it's not only cheap with significant upside potential but also offers high recurring income.

Pfizer's stock has actually been doing well this year, a nice change from previous years when investors remained cautious despite its posting steady, strong results. But even at around $30, this is an undervalued stock.

The healthcare company has been investing in its growth over the years, and while it may take some time for those efforts to translate into stronger top-line growth, the market has been overly punitive, with Pfizer shares down more than 30% over five years.

Its P/E multiple of 39 looks high, but Pfizer has also been incurring nonrecurring expenses as it restructures its operations and aims to get leaner and more efficient. Based on analyst expectations of future earnings, its forward P/E multiple is incredibly low, at just 10. And at 5.8%, it offers an even higher yield than AT&T.

The stock that's down the most on this list is Zoetis, a company that produces healthcare products for animals, including various medicines, vaccines, and diagnostic products. It has declined by 37% in value this year, which has pushed its yield up to around 2.6%. Although it's not as high as the other stocks on this list, the yield is still well above average.

Zoetis has fallen out of favor with the market due to its slowing growth. During the first six months of the year, the company's revenue totaled $4.7 billion, which was just a 1% increase from the same period a year ago.

The company, however, isn't necessarily running out of growth opportunities, as it has 12 potential blockbusters in its pipeline, targeting a variety of areas, including oncology, cardiology, obesity, and chronic kidney disease.

Meanwhile, the stock's dividend is well supported with strong financials, as Zoetis' payout ratio is modest at around just 34% of its earnings. The stock trades below $80 right now, and its P/E multiple is just 13. For long-term investors who are willing to be patient, this is another solid stock to buy right now.
