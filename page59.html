#3402. Pokémon Unite is shutting down

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Another live-service game bites the dust. Pokémon Unite, a five-on-five battle arena game in the vein of League of Legends, will shut down on March 23, 2027, at 00:00 UTC (8PM ET on March 22).

The multiplayer online battle arena game launched on Switch in July 2021 and landed on mobile a few months later. It supported cross-platform play between them. Players choose a Pokémon at the start of each match, and the monsters evolve and learn new abilities as the game progresses. Apparently, that was enough for a nearly six-year run in today's live-service climate.

In July, The Pokémon Company announced that the game wouldn't be part of the 2027 Pokémon World Championships, opting instead for a separate tournament in Tokyo. That event is still scheduled, with qualifier tournaments starting in December. But in hindsight, the game's split from the main event was a clue it was on its way out.

Built by Tencent's TiMi Studios, Pokémon Unite is free to play, relying on the unholy trinity of freemium monetization: battle passes, cosmetics and gems. Gem sales stopped today, along with new Unite Club memberships and auto-renewals. Gems already purchased will work until the shutdown, and existing Club members can keep their perks until their membership expires. On November 12, a final update will add a special farewell event.

The game will add a new Library feature that lets you keep viewing some of your in-game items after the end-of-service date. However, you'll need to log in before then to download the Library data — and since it's stored on your device, it "generally cannot be transferred" to a new one, according to The Pokémon Company. But hey, at least it's something.

#3033. Why Knicks may not be their best right away — and why not to worry

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The reigning champion Knicks fell to 0-3 on the new season, pointing to either violent regression or a debilitating hangover, depending on who you ask. While fans and media have their theories, head coach Mike Brown points to three on-court causes.

Brown blames missing quality shot attempts, lacking physicality/pace, and the combination of careless turnovers and poor defense in transition, per SNY NBA Insider Ian Begley. Everybody has their piece of the picture, but zoom out and the problem may be a short-term sacrifice that bears fruit in the long run, like one the Knicks dealt with last year.

Brown was brought in partly to, and partly won a championship off, the willingness to use the regular season to experiment over trying to maximize wins. He installed a brand new offensive system, shortened starters' minutes to save them for the rigors of the postseason, and constantly tried new players and lineups.

This strategy paid off in spades. Karl-Anthony Towns as a passing hub saved their season in the first round, third-stringers stepped up all year round and Jalen Brunson-Jose Alvarado lineups closed the greatest comeback in playoff history.

Coming into 2026-27, Brown has much cooler expectations, being the coach that accomplished the impossible versus the one that'd better or leave town. He's also watched eight consecutive new NBA teams win and fail to make it back to the NBA Finals, and knows the Knicks can't afford to be stagnant if they want to repeat.

Given he has more leash and political capital than any New York coach in recent history, and both the incentives and blueprint for innovating, it's only natural he'd go even further in using the regular season, conceptually, to prioritize spring wins over winter ones. We've seen it in numerous ways these first three games, perhaps contributing to the early malaise by throwing new questions at a team trying to cheat off a test from last year.

Despite losing three games by single digits, all of which went down to the wire, the Knicks starters are playing about the same average minutes as they did last year. Brown has held firm with losing and bench-heavy units to see more of those combinations.

Speaking of bench-heavy units, the Knicks have poured 19 minutes into lineups without Brunson or Towns in three games this season. For comparison, outside of garbage time, they played these combinations for 47 minutes, total, in last year's playoffs.

During training camp, Brown pointed to bigger offensive roles for his wings, so this trend aligns. We've seen OG Anunoby self-create much more of his offense.

The offense in general isn't simply what the Knicks trotted out last season. They've heavily leaned into their freelance motion approach over set plays to start the season, a challenge that could help explain their 29th overall offensive start.

Brunson isn't getting his usual amount of on-ball reps either, with his possession rate dropping from 45.7 percent last season to 29.6 percent this season, per film analyst DJ Zullo. This could be a factor in his poor shooting start.

Brown's even shifted the rotation in these opening contests, closing with James Wiseman as the primary backup center over Andre Drummond in their latest contest. This, along with adjusting between defensive schemes, has given the Knicks a steady flow of new looks instead of allowing them to ride the championship's coattails.

While these may make things somewhat tougher in the short term, if Brown is correct like he was last season, they'll pay off down the line. A Knicks team that's better prepared to make offensive plays when Brunson's having a bad night, or rely on a deep bench cut in a pivotal Game 6, is one that's better prepared to repeat.

There is a downside, as the Knicks are looking to rebuild their mettle and regain their rhythm amid a landscape that's changing despite familiar faces. The ring bought them at least three games of rope, but if the losing continues, the noise won't stay quiet.

Brown faced similar circumstances last year and successfully pivoted, so if that situation arose again, there's no doubt he'd be able to do the same. But if the price for another championship is a 2-6 start in November, Brown, his team, and their fans should be happy to pay it.

Xabi Alonso has confirmed Chelsea forward Joao Pedro will be sidelined for “a few weeks” due to a thigh injury.

The striker, who has scored five goals and made three assists in the Premier League this season, played no part in Chelsea’s bitter defeat against Tottenham on the weekend after missing out on the matchday squad due to injury.

Alonso said the Brazilian felt something in training and had “a lot of fluid” in his thigh, with the club conducting further assessments to determine the extent of the blow.

Chelsea felt Pedro’s absence up top in a toothless performance at home to Spurs and Alonso has now provided a further update on his status ahead of the club’s Carabao Cup fourth-round tie at Liverpool, revealing that Chelsea will have to adapt.

"He has an injury on his thigh,” Alonso confirmed. “He’s going to be a few weeks out. Let's see how many. We don't want to predict on that, but not ready for the next few games.

“He is a key player. We will miss him, but we will have to be ready to deal with the injury and to be competitive and to try to win games with the players that we have available.

“We are starting tomorrow against Liverpool, a cup game. This is the situation. We are ready to fight for it and ready to prove that we can show the level.”

Pedro suffered a knee injury which ruled him out of Chelsea’s match with Brentford ahead of the September international break, and he withdrew from Brazil duty.

He returned to the Chelsea team to score twice against Bournemouth and then played 90 minutes against Everton at the beginning of October, but was absent for the Spurs loss.

Morgan Rogers led the line on the weekend but had little impact, meaning Alonso could opt to start 35-year-old summer recruit Danny Welbeck, who has started both previous Carabao Cup ties this season. Emmanuel Emegha is also back available following injury.

Estevao, meanwhile, could be in store for more minutes as Chelsea look to cope without Cole Palmer following his red card.

Speaking on the significance of Emegha’s in the context of Pedro’s setback, Alonso added: “For sure it's important that we have him available because he has special qualities.

“He has to feel fully fit. He's been training for the last couple of weeks, 10 days, in a good way that allows him to build a strong base for his fitness and now gradually we want to see him and maybe tomorrow he has the chance to play.”

Alonso also didn’t rule out trialling other players up front as Chelsea look for a solution to Pedro’s absence, even if striker isn’t their natural position.

“They [Welbeck and Emegha] are the strikers but there are other players that can play in that position,” he said.

“We need to find the right balance between the things that they are good at and how they connect with the other ones. We tried with Morgan, playing close to Cole. We have to sometimes be creative and sometimes try a few things to see how this is going to work out.”

#392. Doctor removes prostate from 35 miles away in first telerobotic surgery in US

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The patient was at a facility in Minneola, Florida, while the surgeon operated remotely from another facility

A Florida surgeon has performed the first telerobotic operation between two US hospitals, removing the prostate of a man with cancer from 35 miles away, according to officials.

Dr Vipul Patel, founder and medical director of the Global Robotics Institute at AdventHealth Celebration in suburban Orlando, on Sunday performed an investigational robotic prostatectomy on a 66-year-old prostate cancer patient who volunteered for a clinical study evaluating a robotic telesurgery system.

The patient, identified as Mark, was at an AdventHealth facility in Minneola, Florida, while Patel operated remotely from another AdventHealth facility in the community of Celebration. Study co-principal investigator and surgeon Marcio Moschovas provided onsite support to Mark during the procedure, an AdventHealth news release said.

Mark was recovering well after the procedure, AdventHealth told ABC.

The surgery was part of a clinical research study conducted under a Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved investigational device exemption. Such an authorization permits the system to be studied but does not constitute FDA approval or clearance.

The procedure done on Mark came after 10 investigational telesurgeries between Orlando, Florida, and Luanda, Angola, while marking the study’s expansion into US hospitals, AdventHealth’s news release said.

“For generations, access to highly specialized care has often depended on where a patient lives,” a statement attributed to Patel said. His statement added: “This study allows us to evaluate whether telesurgery could one day help reduce geographic barriers to specialized treatment.”

Telesurgery allows surgeons to control robotic instruments remotely but researchers are still evaluating its safety, reliability and cybersecurity. AdventHealth said no previous reports had documented a telesurgery procedure between two US locations and cautioned that Sunday’s surgery did not establish the technology’s general clinical performance.

Robotic prostatectomy carries risks including bleeding, infection, incontinence and erectile dysfunction – as well as the possibility of switching to open surgery. Telesurgery adds risks such as equipment or connection failures and cybersecurity threats, making it essential for there to be an on-site team to take over if needed.

Sunday’s procedure in Florida unfolded after a similar headline-making operation just months earlier, when a surgeon in London used a four-armed robot equipped with a 3D camera to remove a man’s prostate in Gibraltar, about 1,500 miles away.

The London-Gibraltar surgery was successful, with a lag of just 0.06 seconds between the surgeon’s movements and the robot’s response.

#2083. Roy Hodgson makes searingly honest admission about his short-lived tenure as Liverpool manager

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Roy Hodgson has reflected with admirable honesty on his short-lived tenure as Liverpool manager in the early 2010s.

The former Fulham boss took over from Rafael Benitez in the Anfield dugout in July 2010 but lasted just six months in the job before being sacked shortly after New Year’s Day, with the Reds sitting a lowly 12th at the time of his dismissal midway through the season.

His reign may have been brief, but it still encompassed a seminal moment in the club’s history, namely the takeover by New England Sports Ventures (now Fenway Sports Group) which ousted the toxic stewardship of Tom Hicks and George Gillett 16 years ago this month.

Speaking to the Jeff Stelling Show, which is set to be released in November, Hodgson had no qualms with Liverpool’s decision to relieve him of his duties after just six months in charge, with the 79-year-old ‘disappointed’ at how things went during his reign.

He said (via Liverpool Echo): “To be fair, I don’t feel harshly treated as such. I think new owners came in, we weren’t winning games, we weren’t playing very well, and the team was far too low in the table for what was expected of them, even though I don’t think we were the team that people had expected Liverpool to be that season.

“No, I don’t feel hard done by, and I don’t think I was badly treated in any way. I don’t feel any antipathy at all towards the club. I’m just disappointed that I didn’t handle the situation better, because I could have been more.”

#4881. Canton-based trading platform Cancore raises $7.2 million at $60 million token valuation

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Paper Ventures and dao5 took the largest allocations in the seed round, which was raised entirely through agreements for Cancore's planned CNRX token.

Cancore, a Canton-based trading platform connecting assets on the network with liquidity on public blockchains, has raised $7.2 million in seed funding.

Paper Ventures, dao5, ArkStream Capital, X Ventures, MH Ventures and Proof of Talk backed the round. There was no formal lead investor, though Paper Ventures and dao5 took the largest allocations, Cancore co-founder and CEO Wasif Rashid told The Block.

The round was raised at a $60 million fully diluted token valuation through simple agreements for future tokens, or SAFTs, for Cancore's planned CNRX token, Rashid said.

Rashid said Cancore started talking to investors in June, formally began raising in July, and closed the round in mid-September.

Cancore describes its platform as a "Liquidity OS for tokenized finance." Rashid said the aim is to connect assets on Canton, including tokenized real-world assets, with stablecoins and DeFi liquidity elsewhere.

"Tokenization puts financial assets onchain. Programmability takes this further, allowing assets to be traded, managed, and settled automatically based on predefined rules, without requiring manual intervention," he said.

Cancore has been live on Canton mainnet since April. Its products include Cancore Trade for trading across networks and Cancore Wallet, where users keep control of their keys, Rashid said.

Users can trade across Canton, Ethereum, Arbitrum, BNB Chain, Robinhood Chain and Tron. Cancore plans to add Base and Solana, with Solana coming after an external security audit, Rashid said, adding that the platform also plans to bring more tokenized assets onto Canton.

Since going live in April, Cancore has processed over $86 million across more than 160,000 trades, Rashid said, noting that revenue over that period was more than $700,000.

Rashid said Cancore charges a 0.5% fee on each side of a trade and adds 10% to Canton network fees. It also takes 20% of profits from liquidity pools, with the remaining 80% going to liquidity providers.

"Either the trade completes on both chains, or both parties are refunded," he said. Rashid added that users can trade without giving up control of their assets, using wrapped tokens or relying on separate bridge validators.

Rashid named LayerZero, Wormhole, Squid and Canton-based decentralized exchanges as competitors. He said Cancore differs by offering trading, settlement and AI automation together.

Cancore also runs a validator on Canton. Rashid said it has locked 5 million CC tokens under CIP-0116.

Cancore plans to launch its CNRX token, but Rashid did not give an estimated timeline. He said users who stake the token will get lower trading fees and higher limits for trading and AI tools. Liquidity providers will also receive token benefits.

Rashid previously co-founded blockchain security firm BlockApex, which he said completed more than 125 audits and helped secure over $3.2 billion in assets.

Cancore was founded in late 2025 and has 10 employees, mainly based in the UAE and Pakistan, Rashid said. It plans to hire more people for engineering, security, AI infrastructure and institutional business development.

A $3M Hedge, a Midterm Test and Retail's Rise: Prediction Markets at NEXTPredict

#4862. OpenAI Proves ETH Is False, and Crypto Twitter Has Questions

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

OpenAI Proves ETH Is False, and Crypto Twitter Has Questions

OpenAI says ETH is false, and Crypto Twitter reacted exactly the way you would expect. “Damn algo traders just slaughtered $ETH,” a user called Takumi posted on X, while Andrew Jiang quipped: “Vitalik has been real quiet since this came out.”

But chill out, degens, the ETH in question is the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a computer-science conjecture from 1999 that has nothing to do with Ethereum, its price, or co-founder Vitalik Buterin. So, crypto bros, you can breathe.

OpenAI published a 67-page paper claiming its AI found a way around the hypothesis, and the people who proposed it are now checking the work.

Computer scientists Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi proposed ETH with Francis Zane, and it says a classic logic puzzle called 3-SAT (finding true-or-false settings that satisfy a pile of rules, three options at a time) cannot be solved much faster than by trying every combination. Hundreds of papers assume it to show that problems in graph theory, computational biology, databases and geometry cannot be solved quickly, per OpenAI.

In other words, ETH is a map of what computers will probably never do fast, and OpenAI says its model just redrew it. “The refutation of the ETH reopens questions about how much faster these tasks could become,” the company wrote on its blog, “and may help guide the search for better algorithms.”

The paper, dated October 17 and revised October 25, describes a deterministic algorithm (one with no randomness) that counts every satisfying assignment of such puzzles in subexponential time, meaning the work grows more slowly than any exponential in the number of variables. Counting solutions is harder than deciding if one exists, so the harder job brings the easier one along for free.

OpenAI says its AI found the disproof on October 16 and that it was formalized in Lean, software that verifies every logical step mechanically, on October 17. Both sit on GitHub, the same route as its 722 math manuscripts earlier this month, of which only 162 came with a computer-checked main result.

Impagliazzo, Paturi, and MIT’s Ryan Williams lead the independent examination, and they are careful about their role. “Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim,” the three wrote in an open letter, adding that “a result of this significance requires careful, independent scrutiny and an understanding that others can reconstruct, question, and build upon.”

OpenAI says participants signed no non-disclosure agreements and work unpaid, and the company agreed to publish all raw data and fund a conference on the result.

Practical payoffs are far off: “Finding such algorithms, and turning theoretical advances into practical improvements, will require significant further research,” OpenAI wrote. The paper itself says the result does not show that every NP problem can be solved in subexponential time, so P versus NP, the question of if every problem with an easy-to-check answer is also easy to solve, stays open.

In simple terms, P is the class of problems that computers can solve efficiently, while NP is the class of problems whose proposed solutions can be checked efficiently. So P versus NP — the question of whether every problem whose answer is easy to check is also easy to solve — stays open.

Anthropic, not wanting to lose the math wars, then posted a paper credited to Claude titled “A 2^o(n) Time Algorithm for 3-SAT” and dated October 26. The repository, which also tackles the ETH problem, says an internal research Claude model discovered the algorithm, and “the result is purely asymptotic and says nothing about solving formulas in practice.”

Claude formalized Fermat’s Last Theorem in Lean in September, and Terence Tao saw this kind of overlap coming. “We have now seen that even the rumor of someone working on a problem can trigger a massive amount of AI-powered effort to flatten it before the original research project has time to reach its full potential,” the mathematician wrote in September.

"Neighbors should anticipate standing water on roads in areas prone to tidal flooding, with some streets potentially becoming impassable around peak tide," city officials said.
