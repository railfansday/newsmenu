#3101. Dalton Knecht signs with Knicks, per report: Could ex-Lakers first-round pick find role in New York?

The New York Knicks are signing Dalton Knecht to a two-way contract, ESPN reported on Friday. To make room for Knecht, who was waived earlier this week by the Los Angeles Lakers, the Knicks waived Kevin McCullar Jr., according to SNY.

Knecht, the No. 17 overall pick by the Lakers in the 2024 NBA Draft, was in and out of Los Angeles' rotation the last two seasons and ended up being the odd man out on roster cutdown day last week. Knecht, a 6-foot-6 forward, appeared in just 54 games during the 2025-26 campaign, averaging 4.2 points over 10.2 minutes per contest. It was a major step back in production and playing time for Knecht, who showed some promise as a rookie.

By signing Knecht to a two-way deal, the Knicks are taking a low-risk, high-upside swing that Knecht, 25, can regain some of his rookie-year form, which saw him become a key rotational piece during JJ Redick's first season in Los Angeles. Knecht had seven games with at least 20 points during his rookie season, including a 37-point outburst against the Utah Jazz in the NBA Cup.

However, less than three months later, Knecht was included in a trade package to land Mark Williams from the Charlotte Hornets. Days after the move was reported, the Lakers rescinded the trade for Williams after the big man failed his physical. That sent Knecht back to the Lakers and he finished his rookie season averaging 9.1 points in 19.2 minutes per game while shooting 46.1% from the field and 37.6% from distance.

The defending champion Knicks have had success by making moves on the margins like this in recent years. The Knicks re-signed Landy Shamet to a one-year contract ahead of last season after making 50 appearances with the franchise the year before. Shamet became an integral member of Mike Brown's rotation that saw the Knicks win their first NBA title in over 50 years.

At the trade deadline last season, New York added Jose Alvarado for two second-round picks and Dalen Terry. Alvarado had several key moments throughout the Knicks' playoff run as a defensive ace who could knock down shots. New York signed Shamet and Alvarado to multi-year contract extensions this offseason.

Another move the Knicks made this offseason was signing veteran Andre Drummond to a one-year minimum deal to replace Mitchell Robinson, who signed with the Celtics. Drummond played extended minutes during the Knicks' season-opener against the 76ers due to Karl Anthony-Towns being in foul trouble and finished with 12 points, 14 rebounds and two blocks.

Knecht's path to immediate playing time with the Knicks will be tricky. Knecht joins Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins as New York's two-way players. However, given the Knicks' recent success with low-risk moves that paid dividends in the playoffs, there is a chance Knecht finds a second wind with the Knicks. If he gets back to how he was playing as a rookie before the botched trade with Charlotte, Knecht can contribute as a shooter and shot-creator.

Mass General Brigham cuts jobs impacting clinical, operational programs amid $300M budget shortfall" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Less than two years after a wave of administrative layoffs, Mass General Brigham began a new round of job cuts last week that affect clinical and operational programs, according to media reports.

A spokesperson at the health system confirmed the workforce reductions began last week but did not specify the number or types of roles impacted. The Boston Globe reported the layoffs on Thursday.

The latest round of layoffs is focused on “redesign[ing] clinical and operational models,” the Boston Business Journal reported, citing a Sept. 17 email sent by three MGB executives to physician leaders and people managers.

“We must eliminate programs and activities that do not fully align with our strategy and priorities, even those we have had for many years. Administrative savings alone will not be enough,” said the email signed by Dr. Michael Brown, president of the academic medical center, CFO Niyum Gandhi and COO Dr. Ron Walls, the Boston Business Journal reported.

In early 2025, the academic, integrated nonprofit system initiated two waves of administrative layoffs impacting nonclinical employees to close a $250 million two-year budget gap. The Boston Globe, citing unnamed sources, reported last year that roughly 1,500 positions out of the system’s 82,000 were affected.

An annual financial report released shortly after said the “strategic reorganization of management and administrative positions” is expected to save more than $200 million per year.

"While previous actions helped improve our financial position, our operating performance still requires broader structural changes. To strengthen our long-term sustainability, we must improve coordination across our hospitals and care settings, accelerating our transition to a more integrated model of care," a MGB spokesperson said in a statement provided to Fierce Healthcare.

"These are difficult but necessary decisions to ensure we can continue providing exceptional care for patients, advancing research and education, and investing in the people and programs that are central to our mission. This work will be guided by our clinicians who are closest to patient care. By taking these steps now, we are strengthening Mass General Brigham for the future and ensuring we can continue to serve our patients and communities for generations to come," the spokesperson said.

MGB, Massachusetts’ largest private employer and the state’s largest healthcare system, has more than 80,000 workers and operates 17 hospitals, including specialty hospitals like McLean Hospital and Mass Eye & Ear, as well as other community and outpatient sites. It also runs a health plan with over 400,000 members and sees 1.9 million unique patients annually.

For the 2025 fiscal year ended Sept. 30, 2025, the organization reported $22.8 billion in total operating revenue, an 11% year-over-year increase that included a nearly 8% bump in net patient service revenue, Editor Dave Muoio reported. Its total operating expenses hit $22.7 billion, a 10.9% year-over-year increase, and included a $53 million one-time hit related to the layoffs that the company expects will save more than $240 million per year in the long run.

Health system executives are focused on improving the organization's operating margin. MGB reported a $59.2 million operating gain (0.3% operating margin) for the 2025 fiscal year, up slightly from the prior year.

Halfway through its current fiscal year, MGB has reported a $65 million operating loss (-0.5% operating margin).

The Boston Business Journal reported that the system is more than $300 million behind budget this fiscal year. The executives are targeting an operating margin of 1.5% by fiscal year 2028, which, they said, “is the minimum necessary to support continued investment in patient care, our workforce, research, education, facilities, technology and major capital projects such as the future Brigham tower," according to the Sept. 17 email.

Division and department heads have been told to cut their budgets, the Boston Globe reported.

A MGB spokesperson told the Globe that the health system is reviewing how and where services are delivered across the system. "That may include consolidating, redesigning, relocating, or, in some cases, discontinuing programs," the spokesperson told the media outlet.

The health system has been conducting a broader multiyear restructuring of the clinical and academic teams at its two flagship hospitals, Massachusetts General Hospital and Brigham and Women’s Hospital. Unveiled in 2024, the effort will see the hospitals’ teams come together as singular departments.

The cuts come as MGB is investing $400 million over four years to upgrade and expand its in-house cancer capabilities and launch its Mass General Brigham Cancer Institute. That massive investment comes as the health system is winding down its long-term partnership with Dana-Farber Cancer Institute, which will end in the fall of 2028.

The health system is also in the middle of ongoing contract negotiations with the nurses union at Brigham and Women’s Hospital. Nearly 4,000 nurses at the hospital represented by the Massachusetts Nurses Association have pushed for cost-of-living adjustments along with 5% annual salary increases until they reach two decades of service. In July, those unionized nurses and another 450 home care clinicians launched a strike after months of bargaining failed to yield a contract deal.

The nurses' union threatened to go on strike again, scheduling a walkout for Oct. 14. That strike was postponed after a bargaining session where the hospital made its "last, best and final offer" that did not include across-the-board pay increases, the Globe reported.

Union members are finishing a vote today on whether to accept the hospital's offer.

#5522. Rivian buys startup to turn 400,000 daily clips into 3D sims

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Rivian wants to rebuild the roads its customers drive as 3D test worlds for its self-driving system, and it just bought a 15-person UK startup called Third Dimension to do it, as first reported by TechCrunch.

The startup was founded in 2024 by a former Tesla Autopilot simulation engineer and a veteran of Wayve’s neural simulator team. Rivian didn’t say what it paid.

Third Dimension pitched itself as a generative AI tool that builds 3D environments ready for game engines. When it raised a $7 million seed round led by Felicis in October 2024, its target customers were game studios, film, the US military, and autonomous vehicle companies.

The founders’ résumés tell you which of those Rivian cared about. CEO Tolga Kart spent more than two years at Tesla working on Autopilot simulation, after five years as a senior director on Call of Duty at Activision, and later ran engineering at synthetic data company Parallel Domain. Cofounder Piotr Sokólski spent four years at Wayve building photorealistic neural simulators for self-driving cars.

The whole team joins Rivian’s autonomy and AI group under James Philbin. Most of them are in the UK, so Rivian also gets a bigger footprint in London.

“Simulation is central to how we train and validate our autonomous driving systems,” Philbin told TechCrunch in an exclusive interview. He added that Third Dimension “has built impressive technology that turns real-world data into detailed 3D environments.”

Rivian pulls driving footage from R1S, R1T, R2, and commercial van owners who opt into camera data sharing. Philbin told TechCrunch the fleet sends back more than 400,000 sequences every day.

But the vast majority of that footage is boring. The events that actually break self-driving systems, like a mattress in the fast lane, a cop waving traffic through a red light, or a kid running out between parked cars, are rare. You can wait months for your fleet to catch enough of them, or you can rebuild real scenes in 3D and change the variables yourself. That’s the pitch here.

Rivian isn’t first. Waymo unveiled its Waymo World Model, built on Google DeepMind’s Genie 3, back in February to generate scenarios its cars have never seen. Tesla has shown off its own neural “world simulator” for FSD as well.

Rivian needs this to work fast. Uber agreed in March to invest up to $1.25 billion in Rivian to deploy R2 robotaxis, but only $300 million comes up front. The rest is tied to autonomy milestones, starting with 10,000 driverless R2s on Uber’s network in San Francisco and Miami from 2028.

On the consumer side, the timeline has already slipped once. Rivian’s point-to-point driving feature goes to a small group of invited Autonomy+ owners later this year, and those testers have to sign an NDA. “Eyes-off” driving, which RJ Scaringe promised for 2026 back in January 2025, is now a 2027 target, with Level 4 planned for 2028 on the Gen 3 hardware Rivian showed at its AI & Autonomy Day.

It is not just about video clips, it is about what the vehicle sees in the clip and what it simultaneously sees on its Lidar and Radars. The fusion of that data is what needs to get fed into the simulation model.

We don’t have all the details, but this looks like a good deal. It’s small and targeted, and it buys a team that has already done this exact work at Tesla and Wayve. For a company with a fraction of Tesla’s fleet, getting more out of every clip it collects is the right bet, and Waymo going down the same road tells you the leaders agree.

But simulation is a training and testing tool. It isn’t proof. A system can ace millions of generated edge cases and still screw up on a real street, and the only way the public finds out how it’s actually doing is when the company shares real-world numbers.

However, this is the best way to do a “simulation” because it doesn’t create a fake world with simulated physics; it reconstructs a real-world scene based on real-world data. It’s still a simulation and not as good as the real thing, but it’s as close as it gets.

#4552. Tested: 2027 BMW iX5 60 xDrive Proves the X5 Makes a Fine EV

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Pure electric propulsion transforms BMW’s long-lived SUV, mostly for the better.

It’s no exaggeration to say that the X5 has been a cash-printer for BMW since its 1999 launch. The mid-size SUV earned a reputation as a performance powerhouse and has been particularly successful in the U.S. across what are now five generations. Now, a new EV version is here to take the nameplate into new territory. Sharing its body with the new combustion-powered X5, the 2027 iX5 certainly looks the part. But does it drive like an X5 should?

The 2027 BMW iX5 60 xDrive shares its platform with the combustion version but promises sharp-end propulsion technology. The iX5 will arrive in the U.S. in 60 form, combining an impressively capacious 144-kWh battery pack with two electric motors. Peak power is 570 hp, this accompanied by up to 593 lb-ft of torque, but the killer stat is 450 miles of EPA-rated range. The new 800-volt battery can also be replenished at rates of up to 460 kW if you can find a DC charger capable of providing that. Like the regular X5, the iX5 benefits from a new double-wishbone front suspension. The EV also gets an adaptive chassis system as standard.

Our expert editors test every vehicle we review. Read more about how we test and review cars here.

Powertrain: Dual electric motors, all-wheel drive

Electric vehicles are hardly novel anymore, but BMW wanted the new iX5 to blend in among its internal-combustion peers. Climb into the iX5, and there are only two physical markers that this electric X5 isn’t like the others—no paddle shifters on the steering wheel and no engine start/stop button. Flick the crystal toggle-switch shifter into drive, and as long as it detects the presence of the key, it is ready to go.

The iX5 behaved as I expected while crawling through Laguna Beach, California, on the Pacific Coast Highway during the morning rush hour. With every adjustable drive mode and suspension setup in Comfort, it rode softly and accelerated gently. Despite the SUV’s strong 570 hp and 593 lb-ft of torque, BMW’s throttle mapping fed power smoothly to both electric motors. BMW’s Adaptive Chassis Control Professional system, which includes two-axle air suspension and active roll stabilization, kept the ride glassy through sandy construction zones and freeway expansion joints. Tight metropolitan maneuvers were easily completed thanks to standard rear-wheel steering.

As speed climbed, the iX5’s 6300-pound curb weight began to show. Dips and divots in the road translated into strong impacts on the air suspension. Active air suspension can work a lot of magic, but it can’t cancel the battle of mass and inertia. And while the steering was impressively direct, immediately translating my inputs into directional change, it lacked the weight I expected from a vehicle this size.

With Sport mode engaged, the steering firmed up a little, but I couldn’t shake a disconcerting disconnect between the rate of directional change and the weight of the SUV. Out in the canyon, it felt like the sticky Michelin tires and state-of-the-art suspension were only complying with my dynamic inputs under duress from BMW’s Heart of Joy control module, which actively manages braking, steering, and acceleration inputs.

With a 0–60-mph sprint of 4.4 seconds, the BMW iX5 is obviously quick, courtesy of an asynchronous motor up front and an externally excited synchronous e-motor on the rear axle. But the same gentle acceleration forces I liked around town felt dampened during performance driving scenarios. Instead of the characteristic EV torque impact, launching the iX5 from a standstill felt a little lacking in drama compared with other rapid battery-powered models. EV enthusiasts won’t find that stomach-churning torque hit, but I welcomed the uncharacteristically linear power delivery.

Acceleration forces built the longer I kept my foot planted on the go-pedal, and they didn’t taper off above rapid highway speeds, like in other EVs. The predictable throttle response made getting back on the power during spirited canyon driving feel easy and rewarding. An engineered sound accompanied hard acceleration in Sport mode, akin to a Tron bike or Star Wars TIE fighter.

Regenerative braking has three selectable strengths. I preferred the lowest for its gentle but consistent deceleration, with the highest being almost a one-pedal drive mode but still the requirement to brake manually to come to a complete stop. There is also a true one-pedal mode.

BMW’s underfloor 144-kWh battery pack offers an estimated 450 miles of range, and my test vehicle displayed impressive efficiency over a 300-mile route. While I didn’t get the chance to charge the iX5 myself, BMW offered a live demonstration of a charging speed of over 400 kW. And BMW’s hands-free semi-autonomous cruise control worked well in busy California traffic.

BMW plans to standardize its interior experience with the infotainment and display architecture that debuted in the iX3. Physical iDrive controls are replaced by a fully touchscreen system, with this using a slightly odd-looking angled 17.9-inch central display and a similarly shaped 14.6-inch passenger’s screen.

On the iX5, everything from climate controls to vehicle settings is manipulated through this interface. Pretty much the only physical control to remain is a scrolling volume roller near the gear shifter. The touchscreen responds swiftly and features a permanently rendered menu with icons for climate control, vehicle settings, navigation, and media inputs. But these are just the start of a multistep process to adjust functions. To switch drive modes, I needed to click through two menus before reaching Sport. Seating position changes are now made with a door-mounted button, but lumbar support adjustments and control of the seat heating and ventilation are made on the screen. That was confusing.

The highlight of BMW’s Panoramic iDrive system is a projected instrument cluster displayed across a span of glass above the dashboard at the base of the windshield—a significant improvement over a bulky digital gauge screen. Essential information such as speed and state of charge was perfectly placed where I wanted it, while extraneous information such as altitude and radio station appeared on the passenger’s side of the screen. My biggest complaint with the new iX5 interior is, frankly, its ugly four-spoke steering wheel. It features two vertical spokes, two disconnected horizontal spokes, and attached pods full of haptic controls. It was a busy thing to look at and an even busier one to use.

The tactile experience in my iX5 test car nearly made up for the technological annoyances. Wide, leather seats comfortably cupped my body over six hours of driving, and excellent sound deadening kept the interior close to silent. Alcantara trim on the dashboard and door handles added an extra level of luxury feel. Rear cargo storage is reasonable at 21.3 cubic feet with the rear seats in place, and rear legroom was similarly generous.

The BMW iX5 competes in not one but two arenas. Choosing between an iX5 and a gas-powered X5 largely depends on your budget and affinity for electric vehicles. Both the gas-powered and all-electric versions offer an identical interior experience and similar on-paper performance given the 1000-pound weight differential and 170-hp delta. At a base price of $81,250, the iX5 is a $10,000 supplement over the base model X5 40 at $71,250.

Shopping outside the BMW brand brings stronger competition, with the Porsche Cayenne Electric and the Lucid Gravity being its closest adversaries. The Porsche Cayenne Electric blows BMW out of the water on performance but is more expensive, while the Lucid Gravity is well matched with an $81,750 MSRP and a base model with 628 hp. Volvo’s EX90 is similarly well appointed, with a competitive $79,000 sticker price, but it lacks dynamic verve. Overall, the iX5 offers a solid balance of luxury, performance, and capability that makes it genuinely competitive, so long as you can stomach the infotainment.

BMW’s approach to its next-generation electric vehicles has centered on range, and the results are impressive. An estimated 450 miles is no small feat, but BMW pushed the iX5 to the limit on a hypermiling drive from San Francisco to Newport Beach. All told, the electric SUV managed over 500 miles on a single charge, with 50 miles to spare.

#4776. America Finally Approved This Sunscreen Ingredient After 27 Years. I Put It to the Test.

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

There’s plenty of buzz around BEMT right now. Does it live up to the hype?

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

TAKE ONE LOOK at our healthcare system, and it’s clear the US is still behind other developed nations in many ways. While affordable options, non-apocalyptic wait times, or the ability to easily speak to a human being on the phone when making an appointment are as fantastical in this country as Voldemort's horcruxes or the idea of the New York Jets ever winning the Super Bowl, this year brought a glimmer of progress toward the rest of the world. At least when it comes to sunscreen.

After the FDA approved bemotrizinol (BEMT for short) in June—a popular active in sunscreens used in Asia, Europe, and Australia since the late 90s—brands are finally now able to release products featuring it in the States. One of the first—and the best so far—is La Roche-Posay Anthelios UVDefense Sunscreen SPF 50 with BEMT, which was released early this week.

I was lucky enough to get an early look at the stuff and have been using it every day for over a month. Does it feel different than other SPFs? Is it effectively protective against UV rays? Will the Jets ever field a decent quarterback? Two of those questions I’ll answer below (and hey, who knows, maybe Arch Manning can save the New York somehow still faithful).

Obviously, the big inclusion here is BEMT, the first newly approved sunscreen filter since 1999. BEMT is a chemical UV filter, meaning it absorbs UV rays rather than blocking them as mineral sunscreen filters do. Okay, rad, but that’s nothing new. According to this writer, La Roche-Posay Anthelios Clear Skin Dry Touch SPF 60 is the best sunscreen for men—it’s also chemical-based and nearly half the price. So why all the fuss about BEMT?

Unlike many other products, BEMT absorbs both UVA rays (the ones that cause early signs of aging and can be a major contributor to skin cancer) and UVB rays (the ones that lead to surface-level damage and sunburn). BEMT can also absorb faster than other chemical actives, with a more lightweight application. Plus, it’s highly photostable, meaning it won’t break down and protect less effectively after being exposed to sunlight (a common issue with many SPF products).

La Roche-Posay tosses some classic chemical ingredients into this formula, as well, for good measure. There’s avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, and octocrylene, which is the same quartet that highlights my go-to Anthelios Clear Skin Dry Touch SPF 60. There are even some skin-boosters in the mix, like glycerin and soybean oil for added hydration. Of course, it wouldn’t be a La Roche-Posay product if it wasn’t safe for sensitive skin, so it’s also fragrance-free.

When I first applied Anthelios UVDefense with BEMT, it was clear this was different from other sunscreens I've used. It has the look and weight of a thicker mineral sunscreen with the milky texture of a daily moisturizer—but absorbs faster than a gel SPF like Supergoop Unseen. We throw around the terms fast-absorbing and weightless a lot in skincare, but this is one of the rare occasions when a product really lives up to this claim.

I’ll start applying a dime-sized drop of this stuff, and it’s fully absorbed onto my face within 15 seconds without a trace. Not sure if this says more about how lightweight the sunscreen is or how useless my brain is before coffee, but there have been a couple of mornings when I couldn’t remember whether or not I applied the SPF—and had no way of determining if I did.

There is no semblance of a white cast after applying this stuff; it really feels and looks more like applying a lightweight moisturizer than SPF. There’s no weird chemically smell either, which is a sunscreen pet peeve of mine. The only major drawbacks are that it’s not water-resistant or sweat-resistant. But that’s what sunscreen for working out is for.

It’s always difficult to fully quantify how effective a sunscreen is at protecting against signs of aging or cancer, as these things develop over many months and years. #5510. Florida Farmers Are Making The Move To Regenerative Agriculture

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

As the supervisor of dune plantings at our coastal Florida community, I was keenly aware of the rare February 2026 freeze. Plants that generally resist drought, wind, and salt were burnt by the 26°F temperatures. Luckily, most of our east coast plants survived and have returned to robust health after a series of late summer rains.

But many Florida farmers weren’t so lucky. The state has 44,000 farms on 9.7 million acres, which comprise about 30 percent of the state’s total land. As reported by Sara Pepitone on Inside Climate News, Florida farmers experienced estimated losses of $3.2 billion in 2026. Add to that number the impending consequences of an extended drought, increased wildfire activity, and a warming climate; the threats to local agriculture are palatable.

Not all of Florida’s farmers are succumbing to what might seem inevitable, though. Some growers have decided that regenerative agriculture may be at least a partial solution to their pressing dilemmas.

Renew. Repair. Reinvigorate. Restore. Revive. Each of these verbs helps us to understand what regenerative agriculture practices mean.

Soil is not static: its composition is changeable, based on the weather, which organisms constitute it, which plants grow in it, and other variables. It’s a complex living ecosystem teeming with microbes, nutrients, and other organisms. Healthy soil contains everything necessary for plants to thrive.

Healthy soils are the foundation for agricultural development, food security, ecosystem functions, biodiversity, and resilience to climate change. Why is this?

By building organic matter into soils, regenerative agriculture produces stronger yields and nutrient-rich crops. It leads to resiliency — diminishing erosion and runoff, improving water quality on and off the farm, and helping farms to better withstand climate change impacts like flooding and drought.

Protection that improves yields, helps livelihoods, and builds resilience: these are all common results from regenerative agriculture. Strategies like reduced tillage and increased biological diversity help soil retain water, withstand extreme weather and remain productive. Improvements to soil erosion, loss of soil organic matter, and water quality can be a starting place.

Many farmers who make the transition and see regenerative agriculture as a long-term investment strategy for reducing risk are experiencing positive results. For example, Florida Crystals is now farming 10,000 of its 194,500 acres in Palm Beach County regeneratively.

Then again, food systems are embedded in and interact with other systems—such as environmental, economic, and political systems—and are influenced by a range of local, regional, and global actors. The University of Florida’s Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences public outreach program works with farmers to determine soil type and how to enrich it for optimal health.

Yes, as Pepitone notes, farmers transitioning to regenerative systems face years of experimentation with possible initial weaker crop yields, the need to purchase new equipment, heat or moisture stress on cover crops, non-cash plants that protect soil between cash crop plantings, and other transitional factors.

Lots of farmers who are making the switch to regenerative agriculture also foreground healthy and fair conditions for farmworkers. A new $700 million USDA pilot program encourages practices that improve long-term stability. For the USDA pilot program to have its greatest impact, the voices of farmers, food producers, Indigenous land caretakers, and workers must not be ignored.

Respect for workers is part of a larger picture of collaboration with soil and local ecosystems, acknowledging the power of the environment to affect crop yields.

Kompany's viral infection caused issues with his hip that left him struggling to stand for long periods of time and was thoroughly examined by team medical staff earlier in the week and was given a pill by one of the doctors early on in the game against Arsenal.

Bayern said in a statement: 'Vincent Kompany had to seek medical treatment on Friday after collapsing during training for FC Bayern's amateurs.

'The first-team coach had been watching the second team's session. Kompany will undergo thorough examination in the hospital and remain in medical care. For the time being, his coaching staff will manage training and match operations.'

Bayern's sporting director, Max Eberl, added: 'The health of Vincent Kompany is our utmost priority. At FC Bayern, we will do everything to ensure he recovers quickly.

'Vinnie is receiving all necessary medical care and support, as well as all the time he needs. His coaching team, which we fully trust, will take over his duties. All of us at FC Bayern wish Vinnie a speedy recovery.'

Kompany's former club Manchester City were among those to send a message of support to the Belgian following the incident. City wrote on X: 'Everyone at Manchester City is wishing a speedy recovery to our former captain and Club legend, Vincent Kompany. Get well soon, Vinny.'

After the 2-2 draw with Arsenal in the Champions League on Wednesday, Kompany said: 'As a coach, you always want to do everything and accomplish everything. I caught a pretty nasty virus last week, and of course I didn't listen to my body, and then at some point my body said, "Stop now!"'

Kompany had missed training sessions and a press conference in the build-up to the Arsenal game but was present on the touchline on the night.

He also watched Bayern's previous game, a 6-1 win against RB Leipzig, from the bench but did not conduct interviews afterwards.

The Belgian was captain of Man City during some of their most successful years under Pep Guardiola but was regularly ruled out through injuries.

Kompany moved to Munich in 2024 and has won two Bundesliga titles with England captain Harry Kane as his star player in attack. He has three children with wife Carla, who he met in Manchester.

CMS launches $275M nurse staffing initiative for nursing homes" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services launched a $275 million nationwide initiative Friday aimed at recruiting and retaining nurses for nursing homes.

Licensed nurses and students interested in working in nursing homes may qualify for up to $40,000 in student loan repayment and/or a $10,000 stipend if they agree to commit three-years to working in qualifying institutions. Nursing homes in rural or higher labor shortage areas are the target for the program—aptly named Nurses for Nursing Homes.

Interested nurses must also not have worked in a nursing home for at least 60 days before opening an application account and starting an application. However, CMS notes an exception will apply from Oct. 26 to Jan. 24, 2027, for nurses who began working in eligible facilities between July 28 and Oct. 25.

"When we offer student loan repayment through Nurses for Nursing Homes, we’re not just giving nurses and nursing students future financial relief; we’re supporting families who want their loved ones to receive quality care in nursing homes," said Mehmet Oz, M.D., CMS administrator, in a statement. "Without taking a dime from taxpayers, CMS is empowering the next generation of nurses to pursue their dreams and helping residents form deep bonds with caregivers so they can live happier, healthier lives."

The agency predicts the effort will support more than 4,000 nurses nationwide, according to the Oct. 23 announcement.

CMS has committed $100 million towards the initiative, funded by civil monetary penalties collected by the agencies and states from facilities. States committed at least an additional $175 million towards Nurses for Nursing Homes.

"This initiative will recruit and retain thousands of nurses, expand care in rural and high-need communities, and create new pathways into long-term care careers," said Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., HHS Secretary, in a statement. "We are putting resources directly behind the nurses who care for our seniors and strengthening the quality, stability and dignity of nursing home care across America."
