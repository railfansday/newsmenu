#5399. World’s Largest Electric Ferry Enters Service In Buenos Aires

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Buenos Aires, Argentina is separated from Colonia, Uruguay by the Rio de la Plata estuary. While it is possible to drive between the two cities, it is faster and more convenient to take a ferry ride. Buquebus, a popular ferry operator in Buenos Aires, offers round trip excursions to Colonia for a mere $43.00 — a deal if there ever was one. But, wait, it gets better. Today, that journey can take place aboard the China Zorrilla, the world’s largest electric ferry.

Don’t let the name fool you; this ship was not manufactured in China. It is named after the popular Uruguayan actress and director Concepción “China” Zorrilla, who is much beloved in both countries. In fact, it was made by Incat in Tasmania, a company that specializes in catamaran style ships. It refers to this vessel simply as Hull 96, which is an aluminum catamaran.

The vessel is 130 meters (426 feet) long and can carry 225 cars and 2100 passengers. Once aboard, those passengers will enjoy all the comforts of a cruise ship, with a variety of dining and shopping opportunities during the approximately one hour long crossing. The ship has a gross tonnage of 14.058 tons and can skim along at 25 knots (29 mph), powered by eight 2400 kW (3200 HP) electric motors that feed eight water jets supplied by Wartsilla. The electricity comes from a 41.2 MWh 250 ton battery pack from Corvus Energy that can be recharged by two 8 MW chargers.

“The water jet propulsion configuration with eight electric motors is the most efficient available on the market today for this speed range and type of application, while also offering all the benefits of Wärtsilä’s axial-flow water jet technology — lightweight, shallow draft, excellent maneuverability, and low maintenance,” said Roger Holm, president for marine power at Wärtsilä.

Incat says the start of operations by China Zorrilla is an important milestone for the electrification of maritime transport. CEO Robert Clifford stated, “This is a truly historic day for Incat, for Tasmania, and for the global maritime industry. Over the years, hundreds of talented people at our shipyard in Hobart have worked tirelessly to make this extraordinary vessel a reality. We have overcome immense technical challenges, pushed the boundaries of what was previously thought possible, and created something the world has never seen before.”

He added the fact that the ferry is now carrying passengers in commercial operation while being fully powered by batteries is an incredible achievement and “a moment of great pride for everyone involved in this journey.”

The China Zorrilla electric ferry demonstrates that even large commercial vessels with high transport capacity can be fully powered by batteries instead of fossil fuels — an important milestone for the shipping industry.

A battery electric vessel will give passengers a quieter crossing that is free from the engine noise, vibration, and stench of exhaust fumes that are typical of diesel propulsion. The fact that the ferry has no direct exhaust emissions of its own while it is in operation is an important bonus.

Interestingly, Hull 096 was intended to be powered by liquefied natural gas when the original order for it was received by Incat in the summer of 2023. It was only later that the propulsion system was changed to be fully battery-electric.

While the China Zorrilla was under construction, Incat secured an order for more electric ships for Molslinjen in Denmark. The Incat shipyard in Hobart is currently building three 129 meter electric ferries for the Danish company to service the Kattegat route in the future.

#4832. Bitcoin Wallet Maker Keystone Pulls Devices as Stolen Key Opens Door to Fakes

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin Wallet Maker Keystone Pulls Devices as Stolen Key Opens Door to Fakes

Keystone, a maker of Bitcoin hardware wallets that keep the secret codes controlling your crypto offline, said Monday it will destroy 18,835 unsold devices after attackers obtained the key that proves its hardware is genuine. The company has also suspended shipping of all unshipped orders from its website.

For buyers, the risk is a counterfeit wallet that looks brand new and passes Keystone’s own verification check, because the attacker obtained the key that the check relies on. A criminal could then sell such a device to someone who loads real savings onto it.

“If it is exposed, an attacker could use it to produce counterfeit devices that pass authentication checks,” Keystone said in its statement. “These devices could then be sold or shipped to customers, who might unknowingly use them as genuine products, potentially resulting in asset theft.”

Keystone calls it a WebAuth key, a secret code written into each device at the factory so a page on the company’s website can confirm the hardware is real. It cannot be erased or rewritten, and the 18,835 units in inventory that carry it are being destroyed rather than sold.

Keystone first said Saturday that a third party had obtained customer shipping information. Monday’s statement added the stolen key and a count of 25,964 affected buyer records, but it does not say who is responsible or when the attacker first got in.

The incident affected records from Keystone’s Shopify store, the e-commerce platform that handled its online sales. Keystone says 51,083 buyer records were involved, but 25,119 had already had their personal information erased, leaving 25,964 from 2024 to 2026 that still held names, contact details, shipping addresses and order information. Purchases through Amazon and other channels were not affected.

Some of that data should already have been deleted. Keystone’s policy was to erase buyer records after 180 days, and the automated bulk-deletion tool it built for the job did not work reliably.

“Some erasure tasks failed, and we did not detect and address these failures in time, resulting in certain personal information being retained beyond the permitted period,” Keystone said. “We failed to fulfill our commitment to erase customer data on time, and we accept responsibility for this failure.”

Customer lists are a recurring weak point for wallet makers. Ledger’s 2020 leak eventually reached about 292,000 customers, and those who received phishing emails afterward worried they could become targets of home invasions.

— Keystone Hardware Wallet (@KeystoneWallet) October 26, 2026

Trezor and SafePal each disclosed customer-data breaches in August, affecting 13,689 customers and about 39,798 customers, respectively.

Names and addresses matter because of wrench attacks, in which criminals use threats or violence to force owners to hand over their crypto. Chainalysis counted 46 violent attacks in the first half of 2026, with more than $30 million stolen.

The manufacturer also warned of scammers offering “asset migration,” “wallet verification” or “investigation assistance.”

“We will not ask you to return your device,” the company said. “Anyone contacting you under the pretext of a ‘recall’ and asking you to ship your device to them should be treated as a scammer.”

Sales on Keystone’s website will switch to preorders until production of devices with a new key is complete, and the company says it has submitted reports to authorities in the EU, UK, and other jurisdictions.

Keystone cut its retention period for customer data from 180 days to 90 and asked Shopify on October 24 to erase all overdue buyer data, a process with a 10-day waiting period. The company expects the erasure to finish on November 3.

#3740. Is Berkshire Hathaway Stock a Buy Near Its All-Time High?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

For years, investors wondered what would happen to Berkshire Hathaway (BRKA -1.44%) (BRKB -1.01%) when longtime CEO Warren Buffett stepped down. It was assumed that a Buffett premium was being applied to the stock, given the CEO's impressive track record as an investor. At first, that was true, with the stock falling on the news of his pending departure at the end of 2025. But as 2026 draws to a close, the market's view appears to be changing. And even with the stock now near an all-time high, it could still be worth buying. Here's why.

The big worry was that Buffett was the key to Berkshire Hathaway's success. Obviously, Buffett's investment approach helped to build the company, but it is a very different business now than it was when Buffett first took over as CEO. Today, Berkshire Hathaway is a massively diversified conglomerate, with businesses in insurance, utilities, energy, transportation, housing, retail, and manufacturing, among others.

At a time when investors are worried about the market, Berkshire Hathaway's diversification and focus on fairly simple-to-understand cash-generating businesses provide something of a safe haven. In fact, investors are increasingly worried about the high-tech companies that have driven the market higher, as many are increasingly leaning on their balance sheets to fund growth. To be fair, Berkshire Hathaway has some exposure to technology companies in its stock portfolio, but its operating businesses tend to be much more down-to-earth. And, just as important, new CEO Greg Abel hasn't done anything to compromise the basic nature of Berkshire Hathaway.

That said, the balance sheet is another big safety point. At the end of the second quarter, Berkshire Hathaway had more than $350 billion in cash and short-term investments on its balance sheet. That will provide the company with a material cushion in a bear market. And it will also give Abel the firepower to step in and buy while others are fearful during that bear market. That's something Warren Buffett was famous for, and he likely taught his successor the value of both patience and contrarian thinking.

There has been a huge change at Berkshire Hathaway with Buffett stepping aside. But roughly 10 months into Greg Abel's tenure, investors are clearly getting more comfortable with the change. And with Wall Street increasingly worried about the tech sector, Berkshire Hathaway's more mundane business profile and huge cash hoard make it look like an attractive alternative for those seeking out a safe-haven investment. Indeed, even with the stock near its all-time highs, it may be worth buying for those who think long term, like Abel and, before him, Buffett.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

IBM, Oracle, Intel and IonQ have opted out of plans by crypto exchange OKX and NYSE parent Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) to offer blockchain-based trading of their shares, bringing the total number of issuer objections to 26.

The four companies were named in an updated public notice dated Monday, Oct. 26, from OKXICE, the two firms’ joint venture. They join earlier objectors including Microsoft, Alphabet, Goldman Sachs, Cisco and Wells Fargo.

The growing list underscores a hurdle for OKXICE and the SEC’s new framework for tokenized stock trading: building a market that issuers are willing to join. The agency’s five-year “Innovation Exemption” gives qualifying venues room to test round-the-clock blockchain-based trading of stocks, and it also gives public companies an option to block their shares from being offered.

OKXICE issued its initial public notice on Oct. 4, outlining plans to use Uniswap v4 liquidity pools on OKX’s XLayer blockchain, with transactions settled in stablecoins. The venue would use automated market makers — software that executes trades through pools of assets rather than matching buyers and sellers through a conventional order book.

Under the framework, venues must notify issuers and give them 30 days to object before offering tokenized versions of their shares.

All four newly disclosed objectors were among the 62 companies in OKXICE’s original proposed roster. That brings objections from the original selection to 23, or about 37%, according to a CoinDesk tally. The overall count also includes Cerebras Systems, Arm Holdings and Wells Fargo, which were absent from that from the original selection.

The growing list of objections highlights early issuer caution about plans to trade their shares under the SEC’s new framework. The rules give companies a say in how their shares come onchain, but also tilts the decision toward opting out, industry experts told CoinDesk’s Tokenization Weekly. Objecting costs nothing, while staying silent allows trading to begin without a mechanism to withdraw that consent later. For legal and investor-relations teams still getting familiar with the arrangements, saying no leaves more time to understand them.

The financial benefit for issuers is also uncertain yet. Tokenization could bring additional demand, particularly from overseas investors, but that may not be enough to offset concerns about thin, round-the-clock trading outside the familiar market structure, TD Securities’ Reid Noch previously told CoinDesk.

Andrew Cuomo, OKXICE’s co-chairman and former New York governor, previously described the objections as expected, with many companies rather “wait and see how it goes rather than be the first to participate,” he said during a press call last week.

The United Kingdom introduced new "respect orders" across England and Wales on Monday, giving authorities expanded powers to crack down on persistent "antisocial" behavior and drawing criticism from free speech advocates concerned about potential government overreach.

The U.K. government’s order enables police and councils to take stronger "enforcement action" against "adult offenders" of persistent "antisocial" behavior, according to a government news release.

Under the new restrictions, U.K. courts could ban offenders from town centers or prohibit them from drinking in "public hot spots" like local parks, the announcement states. Courts can impose restrictions and requirements through a civil Respect Order, while individuals who breach an order without a reasonable excuse can face criminal prosecution, including potentially unlimited fines or imprisonment.

Under the legislation governing the Respect Orders, antisocial behavior is defined as conduct that has caused, or is likely to cause, harassment, alarm or distress to any person. The definition has prompted concerns from critics that the powers could be applied too broadly, although the law requires judicial approval before an order can be imposed.
