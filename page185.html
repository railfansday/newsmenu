#4491. Kimi Antonelli Wins US Grand Prix, but Title Fight Lives On Another Week

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kimi Antonelli won the USGP from pole position, doing everything in his power to tie up the championship in round 18, but Russell’s and Hamilton’s results keep them just barely in contention.

Kimi Antonelli had a championship-deserving drive from pole position at the United States Grand Prix, but the celebration is at least pushed to Mexico City, as George Russell and Lewis Hamilton both finished just high enough to keep a mathematical chance of beating Antonelli if all scheduled Grand Prix are completed this year.

If Hamilton finished fifth and Russell ninth, Antonelli would’ve won the championship; instead, they finished fourth and eighth, just one spot from the end of their championship journeys.

Russell’s eighth-place finish was in part due to a five-second penalty for gaining position off track after a battle with Oscar Piastri in the opening laps. With Hamilton finishing four spots ahead of Russell’s second-place finish, he is under threat as he leads by just one point, 242 to 241.

Antonelli started on the front row in Austin, Texas, next to Max Verstappen, and held the lead on the opening lap as Verstappen had a bottled start on the outside. Antonelli would hold the lead on a one-stop strategy as Verstappen went for a two-stop.

Radio and performance issues looked to take Verstappen out of podium contention late, but a VSC with three laps to go gave him a chance to battle Lando Norris and regain the final podium step behind Charles Leclerc. The Ferrari driver finished 15 seconds behind Kimi Antonelli.

Next week in Mexico City, Kimi Antonelli just needs to finish ninth or better to grab two points and win. Antonelli has won nine of his 18 races this year, including his first career Grand Prix win at the China Grand Prix. Antonelli is set to become the youngest driver in F1 history, a record Sebastian Vettel set in 2010 at 23.

#2623. Hoeness publicly rebukes teenage gem Karl — ‘He is far from a superstar and needs humility’

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Alexis Pereira | 25 Oct 2026 22:13

Uli Hoeness, Bayern Munich’s honorary president, has issued a public rebuke to 18-year-old forward Lennart Karl, warning that the teenager is far from the superstar status some have attached to him and announcing plans for a ‘serious conversation’ about his development and public profile.

‘He is far from being a superstar,’ Hoeness told Welt am Sonntag. ‘Young players need humility.’ The veteran club figure added that Karl may have been over-protected from scrutiny and that pressure is a necessary part of elite development. ‘I don’t know whether it is good for Lennart Karl to always be over-protected,’ he said.

Hoeness also addressed the comparisons that have been drawn between Karl and Michael Olise, one of Bayern’s established first-team attackers. ‘The comparisons with Olise do not help him at all. There is still a massive difference between them,’ he said. The statement reflects a concern at board level that inflated expectations are creating unhelpful pressure rather than motivation for a teenager still in the early stages of his career.

The Bild newspaper reported separately that Bayern’s hierarchy have been uncomfortable with Karl’s social media presence, specifically the frequency with which he has been sharing content about his personal life and his relationship with lifestyle influencer Zoe Kappele.

The Hoeness intervention comes after a notable dip in form following a breakthrough 2025-26 campaign in which Karl scored nine goals and provided eight assists — including a Champions League goal against Club Brugge in October 2025 that made him the youngest player to score for Bayern in the competition. He was named man of the match in that 4-0 victory at the age of 17.

Those performances generated comparisons with Lionel Messi among some observers, alongside those with Olise. However, a thigh muscle injury sustained during Germany’s pre-World Cup training camp ended his chances of appearing at the tournament, and he has struggled to fully recapture his previous form since returning to fitness.

Hoeness’s public statement is consistent with the club’s tradition of board figures speaking openly about individual players, and is intended as a motivating message rather than a final verdict on Karl’s potential. The key message is that the teenager has not yet reached the level his early emergence suggested to some, and that the path to doing so requires work rather than expectation.

“I’m not going to let them have their heads on the moon,” Mourinho said, but he knew what this meant. That felt like a pattern set, a portrait of the way both teams would likely approach this. So too did the way that Marc Cucurella went for Lamine Yamal, the Madrid full-back seeking a way under the winger’s skin – a task in which, it had to be ­concluded, he was successful.

The impression though was deceptive, Madrid willing to step on to the front foot and apply a pressure with which Barcelona were not comfortable, Pedri and Rodri unable to fully settle. Where they could they played too, Bernardo Silva included alongside Aurélien Tchouaméni.

They were also, naturally enough, ready to run. In those early phases, Fede Valverde was opening the pitch with long diagonals.
