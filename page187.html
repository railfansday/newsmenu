#4631. 6 stellar images of the early universe

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Published Oct 25, 2026 8:01 PM EDT

Millions of miles away from Earth, the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and Nancy Roman Space Telescope are revealing the early days of our vast universe in extraordinary detail. A team at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian have developed Lumina, one of the largest and most detailed simulations ever created of the early universe.

The new model is detailed in a study published today in the journal Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society. It recreates how ordinary matter, dark matter, and radiation all interacted during the universe’s first 2 billion years. This gives scientists a new tool to test out their theories about how the cosmos evolved after the big bang. Comparing observations from JWST, Roman, and other telescopes with Lumina’s predictions, also can help astronomers determine whether their understanding of the laws governing our universe remain accurate or if new discoveries require them to reconsider existing theories.

Creating Lumina required more than 15,000 graphics processing units. Take a peak at some of Lumina’s stellar images and videos in the gallery below. (Click to expand images to full screen.)

“With JWST, the early universe has become an exciting new frontier in astrophysics,” study lead author Oliver Zier, a computation astronomer and ITC Fellow at the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian, said in a statement. “To understand what we are seeing with this telescope, we need to translate our theories into detailed predictions. After several years of work, with plenty of ups and downs, it is incredibly rewarding to see the simulation results and watch our predictions come to life in video.”

Lumina is the largest cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics simulation ever created. This kind of simulation models how the universe’s fundamental ingredients interact to form stars and galaxies. These ingredients include dark matter, light, and gas. Lumina is tracing how the earliest galaxies emerged and transformed the vast regions of gas between them over the universe’s first 2 billion years.

“We feel incredibly fortunate to be building simulations like Lumina,” added Aaron Smith, an assistant professor of physics at the University of Texas at Dallas and study co-author. “For the first time, we can follow along as both hydrogen and helium changed into their modern form across such an enormous volume of the universe, while still resolving the galaxies and other luminous objects driving that transformation.”

#1631. With heroes like Jim Baxter and Scott Brown part of their village's proud football heritage, it's no wonder Hill of Beath are marking 50 glorious years of existence

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 19:56 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 20:30 GMT, 25 October 2026

Hill of Beath Hawthorn P Musselburgh Athletic P

The feet of players in the Victorian era once danced across Keir’s Park. The villagers of Hill of Beath once used it as a shortcut to the shops, creating a diagonal strip of permanently damaged turf.

But it became the home of the Haws and on a wicked day in 2026 it was used solely for the purpose of a referee stoating a ball and finding it nestling in the sodden turf, and then said official sliding like one half of Torvill and Dean while creating a gentle surf.

This pitch was inspected more times than the first patient in Wuhan. The first diagnosis, at about 10am, was that the match would go ahead. A series of further inspections only drew the conclusion that the best option was not to have a second-round Scottish Cup tie but consider a boat race across the hallowed turf.

At 2.25pm, the ref signalled the game was off as Hill of Beath started their warm-up.

The upside for this observer was that pies were handed out and an impromptu but entertaining history lesson was available and readily accepted.

The muddy pitch at Keir's Park saw Hill of Beath's Scottish Cup tie called off at the last minute

The Haws are celebrating 50 years of existence this season and the village’s football heritage is profound. It is marked by a statue of Jim Baxter, the slim destroyer of England and the cavalier mainstay of a wonderful Rangers side.

Across from that monument is the childhood home of Scott Brown, whose gifts may not have been as subtle as Slim Jim’s but who was a significant presence in a series of Celtic triumphs.

‘This was and is a huge football area,’ said Alan McGregor, historian and long-time fan of the club. ‘Willie Cunningham, who was brought up here, played for Preston North End in the fifties and sixties. Alex Ventners, who played for Rangers in the 1930s, was also raised here.’

The Haws are, by the standards of the venerable history of Scottish football, comparative newcomers.

‘It all started in 1975 with an amateur team,’ said McGregor. ‘This has been a football pitch since Victorian times. Hearts of Beath played in the Scottish Cup in 1890, for example.’

The rise of Hill of Beath was spectacular. They were highly successful as an amateur outfit before moving to the Junior ranks in 1982, winning the Scottish Cup in 1990.

Musselburgh's players prepare to head for home after discovering their Cup tie was cancelled

‘That was a fantastic achievement in such a short space of time,’ said McGregor. The club then transformed into a senior team with the introduction of the pyramid and the formation of the East of Scotland Super League. That championship was won last season and the Haws now sit handily near the top of the Lowland League East.

‘The club always wanted to challenge itself and advance. You always want to be up against the better sides,’ said McGregor.

Much work has gone in to achieve this. ‘This is a small club with no great fan base and no sugar daddy,’ added McGregor. ‘They survive on the hard work of the committee, the generosity of the sponsors and the commitment of the players.

‘Rivals such as Linlithgow and Musselburgh come from relatively prosperous towns with lots of businesses. We have a post office and a Chinese carry-out.’

But they have a history of dedicated players. ‘We have a large number of players in our history who played hundreds of games for the club. Many ex-players have sons who played for the club and we have two in the squad today.

‘John Mitchell played 702 times for us and his son, Jake, is captain.

Hill of Beath general manager Jock Finlayson is the go-to man for details of the club's history

‘John was a physical player and in the old days suspensions were meted out in weeks rather than games. He was suspended near the end of the season for five weeks. Given the fixture congestion, he missed 18 games.’

This aspect of Junior football appealed to George Addis, who first came to watch in 1982, and has stayed on as a committee member for decades. ‘I was fed up with the antics of modern football. I came here and saw a player almost halved in a tackle. He got up, dusted himself down and the game went on. I liked that.’

His daughter, Carolyn, is now physio at the club, though it must be said that this level of football no longer majors in separating torso from limbs.

McGregor, though, maintained it was ‘a more honest level of playing’ and he has the experience of not only watching the World Cup final of 1998 live but up to 200 games a season. ‘I much prefer this level,’ he said.

He is now finishing a book to celebrate the club’s 50th anniversary. ’It really is Jock’s book,’ he added. This is in reference to Jock Finlayson, now general manager of the club, who was manager of the team from 1975 to 2015, including leading the team to the Junior cup triumph of 1990.

Rangers and Scotland great Jim Baxter is a son of Hill of Beath and has his statue in the village

The wind battered against the door and the rain pummelled the roof but Finlayson was warm and engaging as he held court in the committee room. He pointed out legendary Haws players who were pictured on the wall and recalled with a gruff facility the beauty of times past.

‘Aye, that’s Jim,’ he said of a portrait of the great Baxter that sits alongside a letter from the SFA informing the great man of his selection for a match in 1963. At 75, Finlayson is obviously younger than Slim Jim, who died in 2001 aged 61.

‘But we all knew of him,’ he said. ‘I mind he had an olive green Jag and would play cards with his pals into the early hours at the ice cream shop.’

Finlayson’s career in football was at a more humble level but his achievements and longevity are astonishing. ‘The Junior cup win was the highlight,’ he said of the 1-0 victory over Lesmahagow at Rugby Park.

‘We stopped on the way to the game and we had a picnic. The committee had a big hamper and it was like a Sunday school outing. The driver got lost and we arrived only an hour or so before kick-off. It was just a matter of a wee talk and the players went out. We had five balls with us, nae markers, nae cones.

Scott Brown grew up in Hill of Beath before going on to star for Hibernian, Celtic and Scotland

‘The boys started singing in the tunnel: “We’re red, we’re white, we’re ****** dynamite”.’

He approached a photograph on the wall. ‘This is the team. What a side. Two of them are dead. One of motor neurone disease and the other of a heart attack,’ said Finlayson.

His eyes lingered on the picture. ‘You know, I have many stories. I am always asked to do after-dinner speaking. But I turn all that doon. I remember all these players. We still have David Baillie, the chairman, who built much of this club with his bare hands and with the help of his workers in the building trade. I could tell all these tales but I am emotional. I would start to greet.’

The stories will be related in the book. The tears are lost in a Saturday storm.

#5159. Bitcoin and Gold Become Worst-Performing Assets of 2026 in Historic First

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin and gold have emerged as the two worst-performing major asset classes of 2026, marking an unprecedented development in financial markets.

According to Charlie Bilello, chief market strategist at Creative Planning, gold has declined 7.5% since the beginning of the year, while Bitcoin is down 7.3%.

Both assets are now trailing traditional investments, including stocks, bonds and even cash, despite their reputation as stores of value.

The simultaneous losses are particularly noteworthy given that Bitcoin and gold are frequently viewed as alternatives to traditional financial assets during periods of economic uncertainty.

However, neither has managed to deliver positive year-to-date returns in 2026, challenging their appeal as reliable hedges against market turbulence.

Despite Bitcoin's disappointing performance this year, the world's largest cryptocurrency remains the undisputed long-term winner among the major asset classes tracked by Bilello.

Since 2011, Bitcoin has delivered an extraordinary annualized return of approximately 120.6%, substantially outperforming traditional financial assets.

For comparison, gold has generated an annualized return of just 6.3% over the same period.

The contrast becomes even more striking when examining the assets' recent performance.

Bitcoin was the best-performing major asset class in 2024, posting a staggering 121% gain. Gold subsequently took the crown in 2025, soaring 63.7% and outperforming competing investments.

However, their fortunes have reversed dramatically in 2026, with both assets occupying the bottom positions in the performance rankings.

The unusual development underscores how quickly market leadership can change, even for assets with exceptionally strong historical returns.

While the latest figures represent a historic first in Bilello's comparative data, the final rankings for 2026 remain uncertain, with more than two months left before the end of the year.

For one former Hammers manager, looking at the state of the club he represented as a player before becoming the head coach, it is very much a case of ‘things aint what they used to be’ in east London.

Interviewed in fourfourtwo.com, the enigma that is Harry Redknapp doesn’t hold back with his forthright opinions about the soul having departed the West Ham United he once knew and loved.

Languishing in the second division, the club Redknapp managed from 1994 – 2001 has, according to the enigmatic former  Irons’ winger and midfielder, now completely lost its identity. Don’t we as fans just know it?

“What has happened? Apart from Jarrod Bowen (signed from Hull), I don’t see an awful lot of players there that I’d look at and think, ‘yes, they’re West Ham players’ said Redknapp.

Forthright, blunt – but perhaps unduly pessimistic? Harry Redknapp’s words are nothing if not thought provoking

“And the atmosphere at Upton Park was second to none. When that crowd sang ‘I’m Forever Blowing Bubbles’, the atmosphere was intimidating, incredible.

Moe, owner of the Tech Moe channel, recently bought a new Chevrolet Corvette Grand Sport. He then took delivery of the vehicle and started his two-hour journey home from the dealer. Moe was excited to drive home, but about a mile away from the dealership, the car lost all electrical power, refused to restart, and trapped him inside.

What should have been an exciting ride home turned into the setup for a thriller film. Moe eventually found the mechanical door latch and got out. After he stepped out and tried to figure out what was wrong with the car, the door closed on him, leaving him locked out on the side of the highway.

Eventually, two policemen helped him; one had a Corvette at home and helped Moe find the mechanical keyhole and regain access to the interior. The second officer drove down to the dealership, but it was closed by the time he got there.

Moe then contacted OnStar to confirm that the car that he just bought wasn’t stolen. After that, the car started without a problem, and Moe drove it home. When asked by his fans why he couldn’t film the ordeal, he said he is a tech reviewer, not a vlogger, so his first response was to get it sorted out, not film it.

To figure out what went wrong, Moe contacted the dealership near his home to replicate the problem. The thing is, the Corvette Grand Sport worked normally, and technicians couldn’t replicate the issue. With help from OnStar representatives, they determined that the car’s computer network failed to register the key fob during the remote start sequence.

This then caused errors in the anti-theft system’s code, which then caused the Corvette to go completely limp. The errors occurred because the remote start sequence was done with another key fob inside the trunk. As a result, the system glitched out and became totally unresponsive.

So I guess it’s exactly like one of those situations where you just need a hard reset, like your phone or your WiFi. It’s kind of worrying that a small software glitch can cause a ton of headaches, and even more worrying that it was a completely brand-new car that ended up with this error.

You’d think companies would have figured these things out by now, but anyway, at least it’s sorted. Since the incident, Moe was able to get his car well protected with paint protection film, a ceramic coating on the wheels, and even a Uniden R8 radar detector.

#1667. Motherwell 0-2 Hearts: Alarm bells ringing for Alfred Johansson after sixth straight league defeat

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 19:26 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 19:26 GMT, 25 October 2026

You can talk about the need for patience, highlight the absentees and try to accentuate the positives all you want.

You can point out the high turnover of players in the summer and the impossibly high bar which was set last season until the cows come home.

But the bottom line, for Alfred Johansson and Motherwell, is that the alarm bells are now ringing.

For the first time since 2003, the Fir Park side have lost six consecutive league matches. After a promising start under the Swede, the Steelmen have fallen off a cliff.

By the end of this latest reverse, sympathy from the visiting Hearts supporters was in short supply. ‘You’re going down with the Hibees,’ was the cry. With each passing week, you feel less inclined to dismiss such a scenario out of hand.

A year of the fans’ dreams under Jens Berthel Askou is fast threatening to become their worst nightmare under his successor.

Hearts striker Claudio Braga puts the visiting side ahead with a pinpoint header at Fir Park

Calvin Miller's late second for Hearts consigns Motherwell to a sixth straight league defeat

Well boss Alfred Johansson now faces a game against St Johnstone that he dare not lose

To be clear, you could not say that this latest reverse came as a result of a dismal performance. Motherwell certainly had their chances. Before and after falling behind to Claudio Braga’s goal, they also showed plentiful spirit.

The requisite quality throughout the display was still lacking, though. This team struggles to find the killer instinct and is also wholly unconvincing defensively. It’s a fatal combination.

Johansson’s men now travel to Perth on Wednesday just a point off the bottom of the table and in dreadful form. Quite simply, that is a game they and their manager dare not lose.

Suspicions that Motherwell would prove to be the perfect opponents for Hearts after a stinging defeat at Parkhead and a dignified loss away to Trabzonspor proved bang on.

After a sluggish start, doubtless the result of the arduous travelling to and from Turkey, Wouter Vrancken’s men showed commendable resolve.

Whatever Motherwell threw at them in search of the equaliser in the second half, they stood up to it. The game was up the instant Calvin Miller claimed the second with an arrow of a strike late on. Hearts won the game primarily because they won the battle.

You had to hand it to Vrancken’s side. After a taxing week which encompassed two defeats, they’d plenty of excuses here if they wanted to find them. But they dug deep and showed character to get the job done in conditions which were at times brutal. They’ll feel a lot better about themselves going into Wednesday’s game with Rangers and Sunday’s Hampden clash with Hibs.

Motherwell’s plight wasn’t helped by Alex Lowry picking up an injury and Lukas Fadinger calling off sick. Dylan Levitt and Jake Girdwood-Reich came in with Jordan McGhee making his first start for the club 15 months after joining from Dundee.

Hearts’ list of absentees just seems to grow and grow. Already without half a team of certain starters, Vrancken also lost Harry Milne and Christian Borchgrevink this week.

The infuriating thing for Motherwell supporters was that their side started the stronger.

Dispensing with the risky approach which had cost them so dear at Rugby Park, they ensured Hearts faced their own goal as quickly as possible.

Under pressure, a heavy back pass from Jordi Altena forced Beau Reus to play the ball with his thigh. It presented Seb Palmer-Houlden with half a chance. A shot into the side net was the best the forward could do.

Palmer-Houlden then did well to break up a Hearts attack in its infancy. Emmanuel Longelo lengthened his stride and fired straight at the keeper.

McGhee also forced Reus into a stop after some neat footwork in the penalty box. From the home side’s point of view, so far, so good.

The concern was that they hadn’t scored when they’d been on top. And when Hearts belatedly asked a question of them, they did not have the answer.

Braga helped relieve the pressure on his side with some dancing feet and a deflected shot which forced a corner Motherwell never cleared.

When the ball was gathered by Stephen Kingsley, he chipped it into the feet of the Portuguese. Braga fed Sabah Kerjota on the flank then darted back into the area.

Kerjota’s delivery from the right was excellent. Braga found a pocket of space in between two defenders and his attempted back-header demanded pin-point accuracy. As soon as he made the connection, he knew it was in.

Reus immediately threatened to let Motherwell hit back by overplaying. Levitt nipped in but the keeper redeemed himself with a decent save.

With Palmer-Houlden failing in his attempt to acrobatically convert a corner, Motherwell were behind at the break.

The response came by way of Ibrahim Said taking out four men as he threatened to slalom his way to goal. Levitt also had a stinging shot deflected wide.

Kerjota had his name taken for mouthing off, with Jamie McCart also falling foul of referee Duncan Nicholson for scything down Said.

Palmer-Houlden’s last action before being replaced by Eythor Bjorgolfsson was to work Reus with an angled drive. The Icelander’s first contribution was an inauspicious one, fresh airing Tawanda Maswanhise’s cut-back.

Bjorgolfsson’s day didn’t get any better. Longelo’s dart to the line presented him with a gilt-edged chance to make amends. Somehow, the ball ended up behind him.

The forward certainly wasn’t short of opportunities. After stellar work by Jardell Kanga, he thought he’d found the bottom corner with a header. Reus shifted his feet and saved.

With Hearts increasingly content to sit deep and defend their lead, Motherwell continued to press and probe for the leveller. While you could not fault their application, they struggled to deliver the final ball. The execution of set-pieces was also not what it might have been.

Any slim hopes the home supporters had of seeing their side end this wretched sequence ended four minutes from time.

Only recently on the field as a substitute, Stephen O’Donnell slashed a clearance straight at the feet of Miller. The Hearts winger steadied himself then pinged the ball into the bottom right-hand corner from the edge of the box.

A fine day’s work for Hearts. Another troubling episode for this free-falling Motherwell team.

You'll never play every game. That insight comes courtesy of Sensor Tower (via GamesIndustry.biz), which recently published an industry analysis report sharing some trends among the year's breakout hits. For reference, a breakout is defined in the study as a game which sold at least 500,000 units in its first 30 days. While ultimately based on estimates as not all publishers release sales numbers, the report is full of interesting tidbits.

The most noteworthy findings in the report are thus: there were 76 breakouts between September 2025 and August 2026, up from the previous year's 55. Indie breakouts shot up from 13 to 33, making up 43% of the pack, and 35 of the breakouts were non-sequels—around 53%. 27 of those were made by indie studios. Nine of the indie breakouts were friendslop hits like Meccha Chameleon and RV There Yet?, while 18 were debuts in other genres, such as Escape From Duckov and Megabonk.

Triple-A blockbusters have not really budged, according to the report. Last year they had 27 breakouts, this year they had 28 (notably, due to the methodology used, some huge games are excluded from this report: no free-to-play games like Marvel Rivals are included, and Call of Duty isn't accounted for.) But this does suggest a strong showing for cheap, often low-spec indies with novel ideas and multiplayer, and if you're longing for something new amidst all the proximity voice chat, you'll be happy to hear that both non-sequels and non-friendslop are making up a sizable portion of the year's hits.
