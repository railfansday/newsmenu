#4908. NBA joins pro sports leagues on CFTC prediction markets bandwagon while NFL balks

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The National Basketball Association has secured a prediction markets agreement with the U.S. derivatives regulator while also penning a partnership with leading firms Kalshi and Polymarket, formally linking professional basketball into the online sports betting movement while another major organization, the National Football League, has rejected such an arrangement.

The NBA, like Major League Baseball and the National Hockey League before it, established a data-sharing memorandum of understanding with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission that oversees federal regulation of exchanges that deal with event contracts — the simple yes-or-no trades behind most sports betting. The NBA's arrangement announced Monday is said to come with constraints meant to maintain the integrity of such wagering.

At the same time, like other leagues, the NBA partnered with the top platforms as their "preferred exchanges."

"As this category develops, we believe it is important to work constructively with regulators and operators to help ensure that appropriate safeguards are in place," said Scott Kaufman-Ross, the NBA's head of media distribution and partnerships, in a statement. "These partnerships represent an important step in establishing a framework that creates new opportunities to engage our fans.”

The deals arrive as the prediction markets face a nationwide legal battle with several state governments that contend such betting belongs more appropriately under the power of their gambling laws. Three major federal court decisions have landed on both sides of the issue, with two of them favoring the states' argument. The U.S. Supreme Court has been asked to step in and resolve the dispute, including by the NFL, which sided with the states in a filing with the high court.

The NFL argued that the CFTC's federal oversight has been lax and understaffed, and it said the growing industry's platforms "are not subject to the same rigorous regulatory constructs and oversight provided by state gaming authorities."

Mike Selig, the CFTC chairman who has aggressively sought to bolster the industry on several paths — including legal fights with states, new regulations and these information agreements — called the NBA's decision "a responsible step toward protecting sports integrity and market participants from abuses in prediction markets like insider trading and fraud."

Kalshi said in a Monday posting on its website that the exchange and the NBA will "share integrity data, report suspicious trading, and cooperate on investigations," adding the the arrangement "includes restrictions on permissible markets and explicit prohibitions on league and team personnel trading NBA event contracts, reinforcing our rules against insider trading and manipulation."

#3130. Colts' Shane Steichen has 'not thought' about QB change despite Daniel Jones' struggles

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

Quarterback Daniel Jones was a primary reason why the Indianapolis Colts opened the 2025 season on a hot streak, but he is now a key factor in the team's dreadful start to 2026. Jones ranks among the NFL's least efficient passers through Week 7 and struggled mightily in the Colts' latest loss: A 16-10 rock fight with the Minnesota Vikings. Despite his rough first two months of the campaign, though, coach Shane Steichen said he has no plans to make a quarterback change.

That leaves Jones entrenched in the lineup for a 2-5 team that ranks 25th in total offense per game.

"I have not thought about a quarterback change," Steichen said after the loss to Minnesota. "I've got a ton of faith in Daniel. The game plan today was to go in and run the football. Obviously, at the end, he had the interception. That happens in football."

The interception in question was a game-sealer. Indianapolis had possession with 1:39 to play on Sunday and needed a touchdown to take a late and potentially decisive lead. Two plays into the Colts' final drive of the game, Jones threw a devastating pick to Vikings safety Harrison Smith. He finished the contest 13-for-22 for 108 yards and took four sacks.

Few quarterbacks have been able to move the ball with any kind of consistency against the ferocious Vikings defense, to be fair. But Jones was a uniquely favorable matchup for Minnesota, considering he has been one of the NFL's worst quarterbacks under pressure this season. The Vikings are the league's most proficient team at pressuring the signal-caller, and coordinator Brian Flores uncorked the pass rush to a historic extent in Week 7 by blitzing Jones on 85.2% of his dropbacks. That was the highest blitz rate by any defense in a game in the Next Gen Stats era (since 2016).

Jones said it "means a lot" to have his coach's confidence after such a rough showing.

"It's my job to play well, to put the team in position to win games," Jones said. "I gotta do a better job of that. I certainly understand that. We all gotta go back to work. I gotta go back to work, see where I can be better."

The unfortunate reality for the Colts is that they might be better off with this version of Jones than with either Anthony Richardson or Riley Leonard as their starter. Richardson remains the backup in his fourth NFL season, while the Colts have been designating Leonard as inactive but keeping him available as the emergency third quarterback. Neither backup has any track record of productivity, and both have lower ceilings than Jones displayed in his breakout start to last season.

Indianapolis declined Richardson's fifth-year option but could give him another look in the final year of his rookie contract. Anything the Colts get out of the former No. 4 overall pick would be a bonus at this point, because he is no longer part of the team's long-term plans following a massively disappointing start to his career in which he made just 15 starts over three years and threw more interceptions (13) than touchdowns (11) while completing only 50.6% of his throws. Richardson never developed as a passer and remains only a highly athletic rushing weapon.

Leonard, in his second year as a former sixth-round pick, might not offer much more in the way of passing ability. He made five appearances and one start as a rookie and went 39-for-67 for 415 yards with two touchdowns and three interceptions. While he is also a running threat, the fact that he did not beat Richardson for the No. 2 job indicates the Notre Dame product is even less of an option.

One of the final significant moves of Chris Ballard's tenure as general manager before the Colts fired him this month was to extend Jones on a two-year deal. It was a questionable signing in March, considering Jones was coming off a torn Achilles, and so far it has not paid off. Even worse, it ties Indianapolis to Jones through 2027 at a high price.

Jones earns an average annual salary of $44 million and is guaranteed $60 million over the life of the two-year contract. He will carry a $46.8 million salary cap hit next season, so the Colts are likely committed to him as the starter for another year. The hope, of course, is that he will return to the form he showed at the start of 2025 as he becomes further removed from his Achilles tear. But if he does not, then he will be immensely expensive for what he is: One of the league's least productive starting quarterbacks.

Richardson will likely be out of the picture next year, and Leonard is expendable with an annual salary of $1.1 million and effectively no dead cap hit should the Colts release him. Indianapolis could entirely retool the quarterback room behind Jones.

If Indianapolis is not confident in Jones' ability to rebound next year, it could target a quarterback in the draft. This is where the Colts feel the ramifications of the poorly aged Sauce Gardner trade, because that deal leaves them without a first-round pick in 2027.

#833. CapCut is adding a ChatGPT plugin. See it in action.

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The ByteDance-owned video editor announced a ChatGPT plugin on Oct. 24 at CapCut World in Los Angeles, an event for creators and partners. The plugin isn’t available yet; CapCut says it will arrive in the coming months.

According to CapCut, creators will be able to develop a video idea in ChatGPT and have CapCut put together a rough first cut. From there, they can keep tweaking it by chatting. CapCut’s examples include holding a shot for longer, making captions more readable, or keeping music playing quietly under a voiceover.

Creators aren’t limited to prompts, either. CapCut says users can make hands-on changes themselves, and ChatGPT will take those edits into account as the conversation continues.

CapCut shared a demo of the plugin on X. In the video, a user uploads clips from a sports game and asks ChatGPT, "@capcut turn these clips into a 12-second highlight reel." The plugin generates the reel, and the user then refines it, including asking ChatGPT to add on-screen text.

"Creators across the Americas are showing us that a great idea can begin anywhere," Xiaoqi Fu, CapCut’s global head, said in a statement. Fu added that the company’s new tools aim to keep the creator’s own imagination and judgment at the center of the process.

The plugin was one of several AI features CapCut announced at the event. This includes new generative AI features for its web and mobile apps, as well as an AI agent for CapCut that'll be tested in the coming months.

CapCut also says it labels AI-generated content and uses watermarks and C2PA Content Credentials, an industry standard for tracking how media was made. It is also testing a tool that lets anyone upload a file to see whether it was made with AI tools from CapCut or its sister apps, Dreamina, Pippit, and Hypi.

#3741. Meet the Newsgirls, the Gen Z Duo Who Left ‘60 Minutes’ and Began Covering Epstein and Trump for a Young, Chronically Online Audience

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Watching a Newsgirls video feels a little like being on FaceTime with a friend—one who’s great at doing winged eyeliner and speaking about Taylor Swift’s musical catalog or the last season of The Bachelor, but who’s also the type to fill you in on the latest global gossip. With grabby headers like “Trump Is Replacing the WH Press Room With a Swimming Pool?!” and “Hegseth’s Wife Spies on His Drinking & Women,” the founders behind Newsgirls hope to stop you mid-scroll for an easily digestible dive into the day’s most mind-boggling news stories.

“When we were launching Newsgirls, we were like, We have to start with a banger, really show people this is a cool thing,” says Wren Woodson, one of the two 30-year-old journalists behind the budding social media news empire. “We delusionally wrote these emails. We wrote [to] Bill Clinton…and we were like, ‘Hi, Mr. President.’”

“No,” her cohost, Mabel Kabani, interjects, “we didn’t say that. We actually said, ‘Hi, Bill.’ The worst thing ever.”

“So bad,” Kabani adds with a good-natured eye roll.

It’s easier to laugh off their naivete now that Newsgirls, their online news start-up aimed at Gen Z, has found an audience of nearly half a million followers across Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok. After bonding as the youngest hires at 60 Minutes—which marked each woman’s first gig out of college—Kabani and Woodson launched Newsgirls in 2024 “to talk to people our age…[who] are just not watching traditional outlets anymore,” Woodson tells Vanity Fair. “We think of ourselves as journalists, but creators too, doing credible investigative work.” They identify with the term “news creator,” says Kabani. “But we really like to go out of our way to tell everyone we’re journalists—because we’re doing the work as journalists. There are so many news creators out in the world that aren’t doing that work.”

Last week they launched Newsgirls Investigate—a YouTube and Substack series devoted to deep dives on substantive issues. “We haven’t seen this [kind of] rigorous, hard work from independent creators,” says Kabani. Their first episode, released last Thursday, features never-before-reported details about Pete Hegseth’s Department of Defense from former senior officials.

Before we go further, they address the name Newsgirls. “So many people have been like, ‘Would you consider changing the name? Would you consider not doing pink? Would you consider leaning in differently just to be taken seriously?’” says Kabani. “No, we’re confident in the work, and part of not being taken seriously means maybe more people will watch it.”

Kabani, who won a California press award after breaking the story of a neighborhood brothel in her Beverly Hills High School newspaper when she was 16, admits: “If you had told me in journalism school that picking a thumbnail, or deciding what to wear, or picking the first five seconds of a story was as important as the rest of it, I would’ve been like, This is a really weird and probably bad job. But the worst thing, as a journalist, is to do all the work and not have people watch it. So we’ve been putting so much effort into: How do you coat the broccoli in cheese? Some of that is how you look; some of that is the first five seconds. Some of it is…can you start with something gossipy to get people?”

Woodson, who “grew up in the middle of the woods in Wisconsin” and pivoted to journalism at Northwestern University following the 2016 election, says: “We want you to feel like you’re watching an Unwell podcast or something, but you’re getting Vice content.”

Over the course of their final two years working at CBS, the pair took dozens of pitch meetings “with all of the big outlets,” Woodson says, including Vanity Fair publisher Condé Nast. “Our plan was initially to embed as part of an institution. We believe in institutional journalism,” Kabani says. “The first inclination was never to just break off completely.”

They initially pitched the Newsgirls concept to then 60 Minutes executive producer Bill Owens, who exited his post as leader of the CBS news program in 2025 amid what he described as a loss of editorial independence. “He said flat out, ‘Your show needs to go to a visionary, and CBS is not a visionary. You’ve got to take this somewhere else,’” Woodson recalls. “And we’re very glad we did now.” (Over the weekend, it was reported that Owens will join independent video journalism start-up Newpress, which publishes work from YouTubers and podcasters, as its executive producer when his noncompete with CBS ends next March. VF has reached out to Owens and Newpress for comment.)

The two former 60 Minutes employees find it “heartbreaking” to see the structural overhauls at CBS News. “It’s hard to find news institutions you can trust these days, and it’s sad that that’s all being stripped away,” Kabani says. “It shows you how fragile institutions are—just one year, everything can change. All of these amazing correspondents have been fired or left. I can’t even recognize the place, really.”

Woodson, who worked primarily with veteran 60 Minutes journalist Lesley Stahl and won an Emmy for her very last story on the show—about a hostage who was taken from Israel to Gaza—says she and Kabani “were freaking out” over correspondent Scott Pelley’s headline-making stand for his coworkers during a fiery staff meeting in June 2026. “We didn’t see that as his personality. He, in the most loving way possible, kind of seemed robotic,” Woodson says. “He’s a very kind person, very genuine person, but he could lock in, and he’s such a professional. What you see on TV is who he is off camera. So I didn’t think he’d go up against the leadership, but it’s awesome.”

Kabani, who was interning on Capitol Hill for the Democratic caucus when Trump was elected, says independent news organizations like theirs might not exist without “the splintering of these big institutions—that partially is because of the Trump administration…. People are just so much more skeptical about what they see.”

Their big break at Newsgirls arrived going into 2026, with the release of the Jeffrey Epstein files. The pair had covered the convicted sex offender at CBS. “Mabel worked on the Epstein investigation at 60 Minutes when they got the autopsy photos, so she’s seen all of those,” says Woodson. (“All the naked-dead-Epstein photos are burned into my mind,” Kabani replies with a laugh.) “Exposing corruption was always one of our favorite things to do, coming from 60 Minutes. Those were our favorite stories to work on…. If there’s one type of person that’s the most disliked, that we’ve come across in our reporting, it’s billionaires.”

They channeled their experience covering the story into roughly three videos a day about the Epstein files as they went through them. “There’s millions of documents,” Kabani says. “People wanted it all contextualized in a way that they could just go through really quickly…. That’s when we blew up from 30,000 followers on Instagram to 200,000 in six weeks.” Says Woodson, “I still had the 100K flowers that my parents sent when we hit 200K. It was going so fast.”

The New York City–based creators have since found viral success by going out into the field—such as when they interviewed Staten Islanders supporting Andrew Cuomo before New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani’s victory in the November 2025 election. “We went to the White House UFC fight, and we snuck our way in because this boy had extra tickets,” Kabani says. More recently, they attended the Charlie Kirk statue unveiling in Times Square. “That one was really scary because it wasn’t just Charlie Kirk supporters; it was overtaken by white supremacists,” Kabani says. “It was the first time Wren and I felt like this could be dangerous.”

They’re less intimidated by the comment-section bullies lurking in their audience of roughly 50% men and 50% women. “A bunch of people think Wren and I are the same person and/or twins, even though we are literally different races,” Kabani says.

“We get so many, ‘Is this the same girl?’” Woodson replies. “Especially when you’re speaking about politics. If we’re just telling the truth, if we’re reporting, ‘Trump did this today’—if there’s no way to come back at that, they’ll just attack us.”

When the Newsgirls founders report on members of the Trump family and their various businesses, those in the comment section inevitably turn their attention to Hunter Biden. “We’ve invited all of the Trump kids to come on. We have tried many times,” Woodson says. When they cold-emailed Hunter Biden, he agreed to a video interview. They wanted to know about his “contradictory” political stances. “Hunter still wants to represent the Democratic Party…. But now he’s going on Tucker Carlson; he’s also saying he loves Candace Owens,” Woodson says. “We’re like, ‘Don’t you feel like you’re validating them by going on?’ And he’s like, ‘When has Hunter Biden ever validated anything?’ I thought it was refreshing that he could poke fun at himself.”

The interview also got a boost from Fox News host Jesse Watters, as they learned from a negative comment. “‘Just saw you two hair-flipping losers on Fox News,’” Woodson recalls with a laugh. “[Watters] didn’t say anything about us; he was just hating on Hunter a little bit. But thanks to the hater.”

Woodson and Kabani hope that Newsgirls will one day become, like Call Her Daddy host Alex Cooper’s Unwell empire, a network for other like-minded creators. But first, they’re focused on the upcoming midterms and then the 2028 presidential election, topics that devolve into another survey of the sorry state of things, generally speaking. “I remember the joke during the first Trump presidency was, he’s great for business for journalists. And it really is true, no matter which way you look at it,” Woodson says. “When he won again, we had just launched our show three weeks before, and we were like, Wow, our jobs just got even more important than we realized when we planned this launch.”

#4965. Kalshi strikes deal with S&P Dow Jones Indices to expand financial event contracts

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The agreement adds S&P Dow Jones gauges to a growing list of Wall Street firms expanding their involvement in prediction markets.

S&P Dow Jones Indices has partnered with prediction market platform Kalshi in a deal that will allow the exchange to offer event contracts tied to the financial index provider’s benchmarks.

According to Monday’s announcement, Kalshi will become a preferred platform for contracts linked to S&P DJI indices, with settlement based on official index values published by the company.

S&P DJI will also explore developing indexes using Kalshi’s prediction market data, potentially tracking expectations around economic developments, political outcomes and sporting events. The gauges would use prices from Kalshi’s event contracts to measure market expectations about future outcomes, rather than tracking the performance of traditional financial assets.

The companies did not disclose a timeline for either initiative. S&P DJI did not immediately respond to Cointelegraph’s request for additional details.

The agreement follows moves by traditional financial market operators into prediction markets. Intercontinental Exchange, parent of the New York Stock Exchange, committed up to $2 billion to Polymarket in October 2025.

In March, Nasdaq filed to offer prediction market-style contracts tied to the Nasdaq 100 Index, before partnering with Kalshi in August to provide market surveillance technology.

S&P Dow Jones Indices parent, S&P Global, separately announced Monday that it completed its acquisition of blockchain security firm OpenZeppelin, expanding its risk assessment capabilities for digital assets. The deal was first announced in September and financial terms were not disclosed.

OpenZeppelin provides smart contract security audits and development tools. Its open-source contract libraries underpin more than $38 trillion in value transferred, while its security team has conducted over 900 engagements, according to S&P Global.

OpenZeppelin will continue operating as its own business unit under founder Demian Brener, who will report to Yann Le Pallec, president of S&P Global Ratings.

S&P Global has also expanded its digital asset risk assessment work beyond smart contract security. Earlier this month, S&P Global Ratings launched a framework for evaluating crypto lending vaults across six risk categories, including blockchain security, liquidity and governance.

A San Francisco mansion has sold for $28.5 million, an eye-watering $10 million above the home’s $18.5 million asking price.

The eight-bedroom, seven-bathroom home, located at 20 Cherry St., was on the market for just 20 days before its record-breaking purchase closed on Oct. 22, showcasing the intense competition in San Francisco’s luxury housing market.

The bidding competition has emerged as San Francisco draws talent and wealth tied to the artificial intelligence boom, prompting a modern day gold rush for the Bay Area.

The city’s latest AI darling, Anthropic, unveiled its public IPO filing on Monday in what is expected to be one of the largest stock debuts in history.

While the potential share price or number of shares offered has not been disclosed and will be determined after a “roadshow” with potential investors, the Claud developer is expected to be valued at over $2 trillion, according to NBC News.

San Francisco-based Anthropic could break the current record holder for largest stock debut, Elon Musk’s SpaceX, which was valued at approximately $1.77 trillion at launch.

The regulator said it wants community banks to help shape the future of payments and encouraged them to apply directly or through subsidiaries to become permitted stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act.

“These opportunities complement the OCC’s Community Bank Comeback initiatives to right-size regulation and tailor supervision so community banks can grow, compete, and continue serving their communities,” said the OCC, the independent bureau of the Treasury Department that regulates and supervises national banks, federal savings associations and federal branches and agencies of foreign banks.

The invitation comes as U.S. banking groups have pressed lawmakers to restrict stablecoin yields, concerned that interest-paying tokens could draw deposits away from traditional banks. The OCC is now trying to motivate smaller lenders to enter the same market, rather than leave stablecoin payments to large banks and crypto companies.

In a separate bulletin published Oct. 21, the OCC said stablecoin issuers must comply with its newly finalized GENIUS Act regulations starting Jan. 18, 2027, when federal approval requirements take effect.

Existing issuers that submit substantially complete applications by that date may request temporary waivers allowing them to continue operating while regulators review their applications. The OCC said it generally expects to grant such waivers where appropriate.

The regulator has yet to begin accepting applications but said the process will open after the required federal paperwork approval is completed.

The OCC has recently been under fire. The Independent Community Bankers of America (ICBA) sued it, accusing it of exceeding its legal authority in granting national trust-bank status to crypto firms.

They have also shipped a total of 50 goals in 2026, the most of any team in the division and already six more than they conceded in the whole of last year (44).

Fulham left-back Ryan Sessegnon will serve the second of a three-match domestic suspension on Wednesday, while out-of-favour Emile Smith Rowe is a doubt after missing the win at Coventry with some unspecified ‘issues’.

Summer signing Hugo Larsson was handed his first Premier League start last weekend after recovering from injury, but his place in centre-midfield may come under threat from Sander Berge.

Arbeloa will consider freshening up his team in a few other positions, with Benjamin Lecomte, Kenny Tete, ex-Palace man Joachim Anderson, Tom Cairney, Kevin and Rodrigo Muniz all pushing for a recall.

As for Crystal Palace, defensive duo Chadi Riad (knee) and Takehiro Tomiyasu (calf), and midfielder Quinten Timber (thigh) all remain sidelined with injuries.

Jorgen Strand Larsen (knock) and Ismaila Sarr (groin) both missed the defeat to Newcastle and will be assessed ahead of kickoff, the latter is still 'struggling' with his injury, but Sage has confirmed that goalkeeper Dean Henderson - who has missed the last nine matches with an ankle injury - is set to play on Wednesday.

Tyrick Mitchell scored twice in the Premier League win at Fulham last month, but Ben Chilwell – who also found the net in that match – is likely to replace him at left wing-back, while Oscar Mingueza, Honest Ahanor, Jefferson Lerma, Will Hughes and Eddie Nketiah may also be handed starts.

A closely-contested battle in the capital may end all square after 90 minutes, but we are backing the Cottagers to hold their nerve on penalties and progress to the quarter-finals.
