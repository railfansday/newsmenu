#1475. Exclusive: From Pro Patria to the World – Estrella’s new model for running football clubs

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This past July, Pro Patria became the first Italian club Estrella Football Group acquired: Football Italia sits down with Estrella’s co-founder and President, David Dwinger, to learn more about the group’s plans for the club and how the project reflects its wider vision for football.

Success in football can take many forms. It can mean lifting trophies on the pitch, building a sustainable club, or developing a thriving youth academy that nurtures the next generation of talent.

Coming from a family of Dutch champions and Olympians, David Dwinger understands the dedication and sacrifice required to compete at the highest level. Through Estrella Football Group, his goal is to create the environment and foundations that allow that potential to flourish.

In July, Estrella Football Group acquired Pro Patria, its third club after Portugal-based Cascais SAD and Carvoeiro United.

“We started off buying Cascais because it was just a childhood dream to have a football club,” Dwinger says in an exclusive interview with Football Italia.

Estrella Football Group is a European football investment company developing a horizontal multi-club ownership model.

Rather than imposing a rigid hierarchy, the model lets clubs remain independent while benefiting from shared knowledge, operational resources, and long-term planning. The ultimate mission is to create an environment where their clubs can compete at a higher level while strengthening their foundations for long-term growth.

With a strong background in sport tech companies, David and Estrella Group have been looking for the next standard, the next club, since they bought Cascais in 2024.

“In digital worlds everything is scalable. You just add more servers, and you can scale to the next 100,000 customers. In football, that’s different,” he says.

When the company announced it was looking to buy another club after Cascais, about 100 signed up, so Estrella started to set its own criteria: “We started looking at the country, the city, the catchment area and of course, the finance, a big part of it,” David says.

“Then the people who work there and the history. We effectively started building our first set of criteria, which I think now contains 45 criteria, and we built a tool.

“That’s how we started gathering information,” he continues.

“Currently, I think we have over 3,000 clubs in our tool. Predominantly from Europe. Predominantly from football countries. But Italy is a bigger football country than others, of course.”

The process starts long before formal negotiations. When a club makes contact, Estrella Football Group analyses a wide range of available data using its proprietary platform. A strong overall assessment opens the door to more detailed discussions about a possible investment.

So, what stood out about Pro Patria, an Italian Serie D club based in Busto Arsizio, in the region of Lombardy?

“I really liked the history as well as the close proximity to Milan. There’s, of course, a lot of football going on there,” David says.

“I liked the context, which is very difficult as a criterion. But it’s very important to us. So, the context, in this case, specifically of Luca Bassi, who is very important for us as a partner, because he’s effectively our equal partner.

“He is from the city. He’s very much emotionally attached to the club, and that brings a lot of value to us. We also see the potential there, and the infrastructure is quite good. The stadium may be a bit old, but there’s room to grow.

“It should be a Serie C club, of course. And we see the potential to bring it at least to Serie B. Probably not in the next two years. But it has that potential.”

The Estrella Football Group has four macro-areas that describe the types of clubs they could acquire.

There are ‘Brand clubs’, where the location makes the difference: “Beautiful places, like where I am today,” David says from his office in Cascais.

“Technically, this club can’t get beyond the fourth division in the current location. So, that’s a big issue. The reality is that I can’t go beyond a certain level, but it will never lose the location or the name.

“Then we look at academy clubs, which are very strong for their youth departments,” David continues.

“Then we look at performance clubs, which for us are the clubs which could have the potential to already be on a pro level, like Serie A, or Serie B.

“And then we look at fallen angels. They’re closely related to performance clubs, used to be big clubs, and have been struggling over the last few years.

“Pro Patria, for me, sits between a performance club and an academy club.”

And the academy focus should not be overlooked; quite the opposite. A key feature of Estrella Football Group, in fact, is the balance between collective support and local autonomy. On the one hand, clubs within the group share resources and best practices, but, locally, they continue to develop their own sporting project, keeping a strong connection with supporters and local communities.

“I feel that the academy is the base of a club. So, I think we need to have a strong academy with the potential to make these guys really become pro players,” David says.

“In the end, that’s what you dream of when you’re a little boy, right? You like to play football. You like to play with your mates, train, have fun, and enjoy it.

“My son used to play at the Ajax academy and for me, that’s the starting point. A club needs a strong academy to grow. Not every player will make it to the first team, but my ambition, and Estrella Football Club’s, should be to achieve the highest growth from the academy to the first team.

“I think our clubs should be known for giving out youth contracts,” he continues.

“I’d like to have it broader because I believe once we do that, it adds something to your club, to the infrastructure and, very importantly, to the scouting model. If you are known for that, then the scouting becomes inbound. So, we need to change that youth model, that academy model.”

Every city, every region, every country has its own story, but what challenges and opportunities does investing in Italian football present?

“Exceptions, of course, are everywhere, but I see a lot of Fallen Angels. It feels like a high percentage of clubs in Italy are Fallen Angels, maybe more than in the Netherlands or Spain. It feels like it, maybe because we look at Italy a lot.

“What I would really like to ensure is that clubs like Pro Patria, especially now that we’re involved, become really stable and not opportunistic.

“There’s a lot of culture, a lot of emotion. So we should build it up even more, right? Imagine showing movies on the pitch, like an open-air cinema, and that movie night also gives you a free ticket to the next match. Or organising gala dinners at the stadium.

“And we can build content around that, and that content allows us to attract more eyeballs and get people to buy shirts in countries which are maybe even close to Italy. It’s exactly what’s happening all year in Portugal. I truly feel we can do that because Pro Patria isn’t coming from nothing, right?

“We need to win everything, sure. But then there’s also realism and building a system that is sustainable, as self-sustainable as possible.”

The project is about building a solid network and sharing expertise and opportunities, which will lead to success on the field. It’s not about millions of euros in new signings.

“I want to build a system and a foundation which works to ensure that the club will be here for 100 more years. When we focus on the business side, it positively affects what’s happening on the field.

“I want to be promoted, but not just for the sake of promotion. I want promotion because we’ve done everything well. And that should result in promotion.

“And that the business side and business intelligence and structures and frameworks all complement the drive to win. And actually, we’ve seen that work a couple of times now.

“I want to win. We’re all entrepreneurs as well, so I’m always surrounded by people who want to win, but we want to work directly with the club’s local teams, because that’s where the local knowledge sits, the cultural understanding.

“Working with [Pro Patria President] Rosanna [Zema] and [Sporting director] Giovanni [Giovanditti], and then the new head of youth, Augusto [Splendore], and Luca [Bassi], who’s based in London.

“We want to work with the local teams. We do that for all the clubs. And then we implement our tooling.”

Each club works locally, but how do they share information and knowledge and, ultimately, help each other grow?

“We’ve built an application called ClubOS. It’s an operating system for a football club,” David says.

“It sits in every department, which we implement at all our clubs. External clients also use it. We’ve got a couple of pro clubs in the Netherlands using that tool, and we’re now rolling it out for Europe.

“We’ve got a lot of ideas,” David continues.

“We can do Estrella tournaments. The logical part is player training, player pathways, youth. But also from the staff perspective.

“For example, two years ago, the staff of Cascais went to Atletico Madrid for two days to work with their technical staff. And of course, Atletico is not an Estrella club, but it is a client of one of our applications. So that ecosystem, that network, is what we’re always trying to optimise and implement.

“There’s a lot of knowledge per football club, which is within the walls of a football club and doesn’t really get out. We want to optimise that knowledge within our clubs, not just dictate from our side.

“We have strong opinions on how to run a football club, but we miss the local details. So this is how we work together.

“I think it will be great to start setting up Estrella events where all the staff come together. One year we go to Pro Patria, the next year we go to a club in Portugal, and now we’re working on a club in Austria and the UK. So that’s all very specific knowledge with a lot of people with a lot of experience.”

There are already projects taking shape, involving Pro Patria as well.

“These are very early conversations. But we are looking at the pitch at Pro Patria, which could use improvement. We are fairly closely connected to a very large Dutch company, TenCate, which creates pitches.

“They actually have a plant and a factory in Italy, so we’re now in conversations with them on maybe starting a partnership with Estrella, so that all the Estrella clubs can get a great pitch from them.

“It’s the same we’ve done with the ticketing system, or Catapult, the GPS system. These are all partners for Estrella.”

A fairly small team runs all of Estrella’s management. The daily work involves David’s two ventures, founding partner Alessandro Nuccetelli, Estrella co-founder and CEO Xander Czaikowski, former CEO of PEC Zwolle in the Netherlands, and Global Sports Director Cristiano Piccini, a former Italy international and Valencia defender.

“We deliberately want to keep Estrella core team as small as possible. Because every euro I spend on a new guy is not going towards buying a football club,” David concludes.

“Estrella’s goal is to buy five clubs per year. We believe that with our horizontal MCO, we’re the world’s first horizontal MCO, and that if we put the clubs next to each other, rather than stacking them on top of each other, this one becomes less important than the top one. We want to do it like this. That means that with every club we add, we add something else to the flywheel.”

Lorenzo Bettoni is the Editor of Football Italia.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Ben Knapton | 27 Oct 2026 13:17

Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola has confirmed a Florian Wirtz fitness boost ahead of the Reds' EFL Cup fourth-round clash with Chelsea on Wednesday night.

The Merseyside giants are seeking a fifth win on the spin in all competitions this week, having taken down LASK Linz, Brentford, Villarreal and Brighton & Hove Albion across their last four matchdays.

Liverpool's 2-0 victory over Brighton came at a minor cost, though, as Wirtz was taken off in the 65th minute of the match after suffering an apparent blow to the ankle.

Wirtz provided a positive update at full time, though, and speaking to the media in Tuesday's pre-match press conference, Iraola also eased concerns surrounding the playmaker's fitness.

"I don’t think I can help you a lot. We still have to wait, we train later. We want to [use] this last day. He was quite fine, Florian – I’m quite optimistic that he will be available," Iraola said, as quoted by liverpoolfc.com.

However, the Spaniard was less confident about Victor Munoz and Dominik Szoboszlai's chances of making the cut, adding: "And with Dom and Victor, I’m not so sure. I don’t want to say no, but I think it’s going to be more difficult than Florian, yes."

Munoz has missed Liverpool's last two matches with a knock he sustained in the victory over Brentford, in which Szoboszlai exacerbated a hamstring concern he had not fully recovered from.

Regardless of the pair's availability, Iraola has confirmed that alterations will be made against Chelsea, despite the quality of opposition, adding: "There will definitely be changes.

"But we want to win the game, we want to be competitive. We need to see how the legs are. We are not just in the Carabao Cup, also in the Champions League we are trying to make some changes from game to game, because I think it’s almost mandatory.

“We want to be in the next round. We’ve been unlucky in the draw, we are playing Chelsea. They have been unlucky also. But we want to earn the right to be in the draw again and that only happens if you win."

While the critics have eased off Wirtz in recent weeks, the Germany international should not be given the chance to continue on his upward trajectory on Wednesday as Iraola spares him for the visit of Arsenal this weekend.

With Wirtz likely to be rested, either Trey Nyoni or James McConnell could be given a shot in a more advanced midfield role; the former is the leading candidate to come into the XI.

The seldom-seen Wataru Endo could also come into consideration for a start in the double pivot, protecting a backline comprising Joe Gomez, Kostas Tsimikas and stand-in goalkeeper Giorgi Mamardashvili.

Rio Ngumoha had to settle for a substitute role against Brighton despite his stellar showing against Villarreal in the Champions League, but the teenager should be in line for a return to the XI here, potentially alongside fellow fledgling attacker Lewis Koumas.

Liverpool strode past Tottenham Hotspur 3-1 in the third round of the EFL Cup, but they were eliminated at this stage of the tournament last year in a 3-0 home defeat to Crystal Palace.

#3168. Ripple strikes deal with Robinhood to expand its RLUSD digital dollar

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ripple has struck a deal with Robinhood to expand the reach of its digital dollar, RLUSD, strengthening trading and financing links between their institutional businesses and improving Robinhood's internal money management.

The partnership involves using RLUSD to settle institutional trades, automate Robinhood's treasury operations and expand access to digital asset trading and financing through Ripple Prime and Bitstamp, the global crypto exchange acquired by Robinhood last year.

It also helps meet Robinhood's ambition of becoming a global financial superapp, centralizing its customers' financial lives while expanding the underlying infrastructure that can serve both retail and institutional clients.

"Utility is what moves stablecoins forward. Working with Ripple, Bitstamp and Robinhood will have access to RLUSD for institutional settlement, Ripple Treasury for our operations and Ripple Prime for financing," Robinhood Crypto General Manager Johann Kerbrat said in a statement shared with CNBC. "That is what a connected, global financial system looks like."

Stablecoins have long been a workhorse of the crypto world, giving traders a way to move money among exchanges, buy and sell crypto assets, and park funds between trades without having to convert back into traditional currency. But as the crypto industry evolves beyond trading and markets move toward 24/7 liquidity, stablecoins are becoming essential financial infrastructure because of their ability to enable near-instant settlement and move dollar-denominated value at any time, regardless of traditional banking hours.

As firms look to do more with stablecoins beyond simply listing them on exchanges, stablecoin issuers are betting they'll favor a one-stop-shop approach that bundles the technology and services needed to move money, settle trades and manage cash.

"Stablecoins have evolved from a crypto trading tool into a critical piece of financial infrastructure," Ripple President Monica Long told CNBC. "The clearest evidence of this is where they're now being embedded — from corporate payments to capital markets. Our partnership with Robinhood reflects that evolution."

"This is about much more than trading," she added. "It's about fundamentally changing how money moves and is managed — giving institutions the ability to operate 24/7/365, unlocking liquidity and putting capital to work more efficiently."

Circle Internet Group, a Ripple competitor, is positioning itself for a similar future, where winning platforms, in addition to issuing stablecoins, also provide the infrastructure to put them to work. Earlier this year, for example, it launched the Circle Payments Network, connecting financial institutions and payment providers using its flagship USDC stablecoin and other digital assets for cross-border payments.

The network is Circle's answer to the 8-year-old Ripple Payments business, a service that helps businesses move money across borders, using any of several assets moving through that system, including USDC, Ripple-created XRP and, as of its debut in December 2024, RLUSD.

RLUSD's market cap has grown to more than $2.5 billion. Tether's USDT and Circle's USDC still dominate the total stablecoin market, which now exceeds $300 billion.

RLUSD will also be listed on Robinhood's trading platform, and is currently available to traders on Bitstamp as well as Robinhood Chain, the company's home-grown blockchain for tokenized stocks and other assets.

Robinhood, which reports third-quarter results for the three months ended in September after the market closes, rose about 2.5% in early trading Tuesday, snapping a three-day decline.

The Lexus RZ has come a long way since originally arriving in the U.S. for the 2023 model year. That early model was held back by a poor range and high pricing, but both issues have been addressed to some extent. As a result, demand for the RZ has almost doubled over the course of 2026. Lexus has now published official pricing for the 2027 model, which continues without any major changes. Is this the luxury electric SUV to have at its price point, or should you consider something else? Here’s a closer look at what the RZ offers.

The 2027 RZ is still available in six variants, starting with the 350e and leading up to the powerful 550e F Sport. Here’s a look at 2027 pricing versus the 2026 model.

The 350e Premium, 450e Premium AWD, and 550e F Sport AWD trims have been handed the biggest price bumps of $375 each. The base trim is only $100 more than before. These prices all include the delivery fee.

As before, the range starts with the 221-horsepower 350e with front-wheel drive. The 450e AWD has 308 hp from dual motors, while the 550e F Sport produces 402 hp and is the performer of the lineup; this model can reach 60 mph in 4.1 seconds. The base 350e has the best range on a full charge, at 301 miles. That drops all the way down to 228 miles for the 450e with 20-inch wheels.

The RZ’s best quality is that it feels like a true Lexus, from the smooth ride to the top-notch build quality. However, its overall range (across all trim levels) is lower than many rivals. It doesn’t have a genuine one-pedal-driving mode, and the top performance model isn’t as fun as the Hyundai Ioniq 5 N or the Genesis GV60 Magma.

Lexus has confirmed that the 2027 RZ will arrive at dealerships early next year.

I Drove The New Lexus RZ, And I Have Mixed Feelings

Lexus built a great EV, then put a lot of questionable choices behind it.

Based on price, the Lexus RZ’s primary rivals include the following:

The RZ matches the luxurious feel of the Optiq and GV60, while surpassing the Model Y in that regard. The Tesla isn’t as opulent, but it starts much cheaper, is quicker, and can travel up to 355 miles on a full charge. It’s ultimately better at being an EV than the Lexus, but it’s not a better luxury car.

The Optiq lineup has a range of between 250 and 317 miles, so it also outclasses the Lexus in that area. It offers more standard and available power than the Lexus, and is more engaging to drive. Pricing is similar, so we’d favor the Optiq.

Finally, there’s the GV60. It has between 222 and 306 miles of range, which isn’t fantastic but closely matches the RZ. The Genesis can feel a bit cramped inside for taller occupants, but the detailing and quality can’t be faulted. It has similar power to the RZ, although Lexus has nothing to match the incredible GV60 Magma, which produces 641 hp.

Last year, Lexus sold 5,340 units of the RZ through the first three quarters of the year. Over the same period in 2026, sales have increased to 10,214 units. It’s now within striking distance of outselling the Optiq, but the Model Y is still way out in front.

For anyone coming from an NX or RX, transitioning to the RZ will feel entirely natural—assuming you can live with the limited range. It’s quiet, comfortable, and promises to be reliable. However, for buyers coming from another EV, the RZ may come across as merely good rather than great. It doesn’t move the goalposts in this segment in any one area, which is why it’s worth considering one of its rivals before making a decision.

#4755. Bitcoin miners' move into AI is creating a new market for trading computing power

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin mining companies are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence (AI), renting out computing power and converting mining facilities into data centers. Now, some of the companies that built their businesses serving bitcoin miners are following them into the AI market.

Luxor Technology, best known for its bitcoin mining services, is also moving into AI hardware and trading contracts that allow companies to agree on computing prices in advance.

The Seattle-based company said on Tuesday that it raised $16 million in a round led by CMT Digital. Bitcoin mining equipment makers Canaan and MicroBT also invested. Luxor said the money will go toward its AI hardware brokerage and computing-power trading business.

The round included new investment and sales of existing shares, although Luxor did not disclose how much of the new funding was invested in the company.

Publicly traded bitcoin miners had signed more than $70 billion worth of AI and high-performance computing contracts by the end of March, according to CoinShares data.

Not everyone is moving away from bitcoin mining. Nasdaq-listed NIP Group (NIPG) announced plans last week to essentially increase its mining capacity by about 48%. The company said it expects to generate about 195 bitcoin a month at full deployment.

Moving into AI also requires more than access to electricity. Ethan Vera, Luxor's chief operating officer, told CoinDesk that its software business works with bitcoin miners representing nearly two gigawatts of power capacity.

After assessing more than half that capacity, Luxor found that most sites had sufficient electricity and fiber connections for AI workloads but would require new data centers.

Luxor is also expanding into contracts that allow buyers and sellers to agree on future computing prices, much as bitcoin miners use financial contracts to manage changes in mining revenue.

Sam Hallene, a partner at CMT Digital, said AI companies have different reasons to protect themselves against price changes, including the possibility that expensive computing equipment remains useful longer than expected.

"We don’t know exactly what the market structure will look like at the end of the day," Hallene told CoinDesk.

Luxor already offers privately negotiated contracts tied to computing rental prices, although the market is still developing. Vera said transparency remains a problem, particularly around AI hardware prices.

In August, Luxor introduced a price index that tracks daily asking prices for Nvidia AI server systems, providing buyers and sellers with a reference point for equipment prices.
