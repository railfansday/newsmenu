#2722. Cristiano Ronaldo and Ivan Toney see Golden Boot tallies drop after Saudi Pro League reclassifies goals

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The Saudi Pro League has recorded a goal each from Cristiano Ronaldo and Ivan Toney as own goals, dropping their Golden Boot tallies to four and 12.

The Saudi Pro League Golden Boot race has taken an unexpected turn after the league reclassified a goal each for Cristiano Ronaldo and Ivan Toney as own goals. The change leaves both strikers with one fewer goal in the official standings, even though the match statistics still credit them.

Ronaldo’s case dates back to Al Nassr’s 3-1 win at Al Faisaly in matchweek 11. The Portuguese appeared to head home the second goal in the 49th minute from a Joao Felix free kick, and he celebrated it as the fifth goal of the season and the 982nd of his career.

Replays, though, suggested the ball may have come off the head of defender Andrei Girotto, and the league ultimately recorded it as an own goal by the opponent instead of crediting him with a header.

Toney’s situation is almost identical. The England striker headed Al Ahli into the lead in the 11th minute of their 4-1 win over Al Khaleej, a goal that was reviewed by VAR for several minutes over a possible offside interference from a teammate and contact with defender Mohammed Khabrani before referee Abdullah Al-Shahri confirmed it.

Now the league has taken the goal away from him as well, recording it as an own goal and dropping his tally to 12 despite his stoppage-time penalty in the same match.

Saudi Pro League’s own numbers contradict each other

The reclassification has created an unusual contradiction within the league’s own data. While the standings now reflect the goals as own goals, the official match statistics still credit both of them to Ronaldo and Toney, leaving the two sets of records pointing in opposite directions.

The adjustment does little to change the top of the Golden Boot race. Toney remains comfortably in front with 12 goals, five clear of Mateo Retegui and Youssef En-Nesyri, who have seven each, while Ronaldo, on four, now sits well down the list.

Statistics sites such as Transfermarkt still credit the goals to Ronaldo and Toney, but the Saudi Pro League’s official website does not, leaving the two strikers with one fewer goal in the records that matter most for the Golden Boot race. Unless the league reverses its decision, the official figures will be the ones that count as the season goes on.

#3301. Lula’s election win leaves Brazil facing a fiscal tightrope

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's surprise re-election as Brazil's president on Sunday prompted a sharp market reaction — and showed the tightrope he must walk between pleasing his grassroots base and nervous investors who could fuel a fiscal crisis.

The country's benchmark stock index tumbled 13.9% on Monday, the sharpest daily decline since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, and the Brazilian real fell nearly 8% against the U.S. dollar, the most since 1999.

The yield on Brazil's 10-year government bond rose 2.46 percentage points to its highest level in more than a decade.

The moves wiped out a rally in Brazilian assets that followed Flávio Bolsonaro, seen as the more market-friendly and fiscally disciplined of the candidates, winning the first-round vote in early October.

Lula, who has been in office since Jan. 1, 2023 and was president before from 2003 to 2011, now faces a host of challenges, including a heavily right-wing Congress opposed to many of his policies and hostility from the White House.

Growth is slowing in Latin America's biggest economy, and inflation and interest rates are high.

Lula has promised expensive measures that appeal to his grassroots base. Those include protections of existing benefits, minimum-wage increases ahead of inflation, expansions of housing and education support programs, tax cuts on food essentials and a reduction in the country's long working week to 40 hours without pay cuts.

His ability to deliver will "revolve around a central tension," Rafael Ch, senior analyst for Latin America and emerging markets at Signum Global Advisors, said in a note Tuesday.

Voters want "tangible improvements in household finances," and the election result will be seen as an endorsement of higher social spending, Ch said.

But bond markets are already uneasy with Brazil's growing debt-to-GDP ratio, and are likely to demand an ever-higher premium if public spending rises significantly.
