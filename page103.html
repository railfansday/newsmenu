#4190. Reality Bay merge chains guide - Merge with the best of them

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Merge chains in Reality Bay are important for getting new items, so we put this guide together to help you master this sometimes-confusing feature.

If you are trying to figure out how to get a specific item in Reality Bay, the most important thing is understanding the merge chains. Once you get the hang of it, merging quickly becomes one of the best ways to progress faster, complete orders, and keep your board from becoming overcrowded.

In this guide, I'll explain how merge chains work and how to manage your merges efficiently. What are merge chains in Reality Bay? Merge chains are sequences of items that become more advanced when you combine two identical objects.

For example, merging 2 Level 1 items creates one Level 2 item. Combining 2 Level 2 items creates a Level 3 item, and so on until you reach the highest available level in that chain.

Each chain has its own progression, and some items can eventually turn into new producers.

How to get items from producers Producers are special objects that generate items when tapped. They usually have a small Energy icon in the corner, which indicates that you can tap them.

Each producer supplies items that can be merged into more advanced versions. Most producer taps cost Energy, although some items require multiple taps before appearing.

You can obtain additional producers by upgrading the producer buildings or completing certain merge progressions (quests can give you quite advanced items). How to unlock new merge chains As you progress through Reality Bay, you'll discover new items and producers. In fact, there are so many that even I haven't managed to unlock them all (and I play Reality Bay quite a bit).

Whenever you discover an item for the first time, it becomes available in your Collection.

As you unlock more producers and item collections, keeping track of each chain will help you decide which items to merge, which ones to save, and where to spend your Energy.

It'll also make your extra inventory slots more crucial, so don't forget about the items you stored in there.

It's been a tumultuous 20 months to be a Dallas Mavericks fan. We've gone through the worst trade in league history, seen a GM get fired, switched out head coaches, traded away most of the roster that led the team to the 2024 NBA Finals, and control just one first round pick in the next five NBA Drafts.

Yet, after Monday's 113-109 win in Miami, I'm feeling like Keanu Reaves.

"People keep asking if I'm back and I haven't really had an answer, but now yeah, I'm thinking I'm back."

Dallas now sits at 2-2, having beaten Indiana and Miami on the road, while suffering losses to San Antonio and Houston. Heading into the year, I would've prayed for a 1-3 start and if you had told me Flagg would've been out for a game and a half and Kyrie Irving would be sitting the second night of a back-to-back, I'd have braced for 0-4.

But there's just something about this Mavericks team.

"Our guys simply made big, big plays and big shots at the end," first-year head coach Dusty May said in his postgame comments on Monday. "It's a lot of fun when your two superstars are as unselfish as [Flagg and Irving] are, and they show as much grace as they bring along these younger players. It's been fun to be a part of and see it so far. Obviously a long way to go, but this is a very, very good win for our team."

After a disappointing season-opening loss, Dallas has shown bright spots in three straight games and kept a game against the defending Western Conference champion San Antonio Spurs close for three and a half quarters without its two best players — Flagg and Irving. They've earned two road wins over key Eastern Conference opponents that hope to make deep runs after disappointing 2025 campaigns.

Flagg has been exceptional. His per game stats won't tell the entire story since he missed half of one of his three games following a controversial foul by Pacers guard Aaron Nesmith that forced him to miss a game and a half, but even with that caveat, the 19-year-old is averaging 25 points per game on 57% shooting. He has 11 steals and five blocks in three games and his playmaking has taken another leap, averaging over six and a half assists a night.

While Flagg's ascension will get most of the attention — and he's owed that respect — it's the rest of the team that gives me hope that Dallas could defy expectations this year and maybe even make it to the playoffs.

Walter Clayton Jr., who was signed the day of the Mavericks' first game in Houston, has been a perfect fit for this team. He scored 15 points in 17 minutes in Dallas' win over Miami and was a team-best +22 in those minutes. He's giving Flagg and the Mavericks an outlet when drives to the basket stall or when someone loses their dribble and has to reset.

Zaccharie Risacher has also made an impact. While his shot may not be falling, his defensive impact cannot be ignored. He led the league in stocks (steals + blocks per game) going into Monday's game, only to be passed by Flagg and a couple others during the night's action, and Risacher's block of Davion Mitchell's layup sealed the win Monday. He's not the No. 1 overall pick Atlanta wanted two years ago, but Dallas doesn't need him to be that and he's filling into his role beautifully and helping Dallas stay competitive.

P.J. Washington is as steady as ever, averaging over 15 points and seven boards a night while bringing the defensive toughness that helped this team reach the Finals two years ago. Daniel Gafford is ready to go to war every night and his physical play in Monday's game energized Dallas in moments where they may have folded last season.

It's not a perfect team, and there are still moments that make me want to tear my hair out — like a 30-17 fourth quarter against the Spurs or a 38-12 second quarter against the Rockets or having to come back from a double-digit deficit twice in one game because Dallas' concentration lulled after the first comeback — but this is a young team and they're going to make some dumb mistakes. They're going to have lapses, and some games are going to get ugly because of them.

But the signs of life this team has shown in the first week of the season have me hopeful. Hopeful that we might just have something cooking in Big D for the first time in three seasons, and that the pain of the last two seasons can be forgotten with a young team that never gives up.

I can't tell you how many games Dallas will win this year, but I can promise you this: it's gonna be a lot of fun to watch.

#4828. Bitcoin price analysis sees ‘strong rally’ as old coins become dormant

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A decline in the share of Bitcoin’s realized cap held by coins unmoved for 18 months or longer points to the ‘early stages’ of a bull market, analysis says.

Bitcoin (BTC) onchain transaction data is hinting at the start of a new uptrend as long-term holders retreat from selling.

Data from onchain analytics platform CryptoQuant shows that investors holding coins for 18 months or longer are starting to become less active.

On Sept. 16, the 18-month-plus cohort’s share of realized cap — the value of all coins at the price they last moved onchain — hit 26.98%, its highest since mid-November 2024.

Analyzing the data, CryptoQuant sees a historical trend that preceded Bitcoin bull markets now repeating.

“The pattern has always been the same: when the proportion of Bitcoin held for more than 18 months across the entire network rises and then starts to decline, a strong rally follows,” it wrote in a blog post.

It included a chart of UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output) age bands, sized according to their contribution to the realized cap.

Bitcoin realized cap by UTXO age. Source: CryptoQuant

Reduced onchain spending by long-term investors reflects a move to maintain BTC exposure to increase profitability in the long term. The contribution by these investors remained in a broad uptrend beginning in November 2025, with profit-taking activity rising as BTC/USD fell further from its current all-time high of $126,200.

CryptoQuant noted that the UTXO trend reversal remains modest, implying that the market is witnessing only the very start of more bullish conditions.

“The market has now shifted into a bullish cycle, but we still appear to be in the early stages of the cycle, where such a signal has not even begun yet,” it commented.

“This looks like a point where we can remain positive about both the potential upside and the duration of the rally.”

Among long-term holders, only the 18-month-to-two-year cohort is currently in net loss, with a cost basis of $93,193. Its cost basis currently sits at $93,193.

Since its 2025 all-time highs, Bitcoin has fallen below this cohort’s cost basis, while older cohorts have remained in aggregate profit. The year 2026 thus marks the first bear market in which price has stayed above the 2-3-year cohort’s cost basis, CryptoQuant shows.

Bitcoin realized price by UTXO age. Source: CryptoQuant

In a report released last week, crypto investment company Galaxy Digital described the “shallowest” bear market on record, with a maximum drawdown versus all-time highs of -53%.

BTC in custody for five years or more, meanwhile — coins that Galaxy says were responsible for the supply glut that halted price upside at $126,200 — are now also supporting the case for a fresh long-term uptrend.

“Coins unmoved for five years or more moved at 67k BTC a month in 2024 and 47k in 2025… totaling 1.11 million BTC since October 2024, the largest old-coin distribution of any cycle, most of it absorbed by the spot ETFs and BTC digital asset treasury (DAT) companies,” it wrote.

In 2026, the volume supplied by the five-year-plus cohort has averaged 34,000 BTC per month, half as much as in 2024.

#4198. Reality Bay beginner's guide - Four essential tips for merging magnificence

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Reality Bay can seem a little overwhelming at first, so we've made this beginner's guide to help ease you into it.

As someone who played their fair share of merge games, I just had to check how good Reality Bay is! Many people praised it for the story, for the collections, and for the ease of merging everything, but if you only just started playing, Reality Bay could seem a little bit overwhelming.

You will soon run out of spaces on your board and then run out of Energy, but to help you avoid all that, I created this Reality Bay guide. I've shared my personal tips on how you can pass more levels, because let's be honest - that's what keeps the story moving forward!  The most important aspect of a merge game Since Reality Bay is a merge type of game, you will need to learn how to manage everything on your board. I've shared tips on how to not run out of Energy, but also tips on how you can organise your board in a way that always leaves you some empty spaces.  Reality Bay beginner's guide I made this guide for players who want to start adventuring into the world of Reality Bay - not the experts who are a card or two away from completing a collection (since those players already know a thing or two about the game). My goal was to help everyone understand what to focus on in the early stages, since those are the toughest.

So without further ado, let's dive right in.

Energy is what you use to open the producers. Producers are the items you tap on to give you more items to merge, and some of them have a cooldown after tapping them a number of times. Those are the ones you should try to open whenever they are available, because chances are you'll need the items sooner or later.

You can refill some Energy for free every day, so it's best to do that whenever you run out. Just keep in mind the following: never close the game while you still have Energy!

Since the Energy refills over time, you want to use it all before closing the game for the day. That will ensure that when you come back, you won't have any unused idle time.

Always focus on merging the items needed to complete the quests at the top of your screen. The quests give you Coins that you need for various upgrades, so make sure that you try to complete as many of them as possible.

You can also upgrade your producers, and whenever you do that, the chance of higher-level items increases. That's great for when you want to complete quests, because items will take up fewer board slots.

Events are another great way to get your hands on additional goods, so whenever there are any events underway, you should go ahead and take part in them. They will also require Energy, so be careful how you spend it.

Some events are going to give you cards to complete your collection, so even if you can't get all the rewards, try to at least get the cards. Completing the card collections will be a massive boost to your Energy, and honestly, it's massive bragging rights in Reality Bay.

I strongly recommend you organise your board ASAP. You should have your producers next to each other, on one side of the board, and then the higher-level items on the other. Use the empty spots to quickly merge items (next to each other) and keep doing that until you reach the highest level you can.

Not having unused items on your board is great, and it keeps it nice and tidy.

An aspect that is very easy to overlook is the empty spots. Basically, the more empty spots you have, the easier it will be to make more upgrades/merges. If your entire board is cluttered, you will only be able to open a producer a couple of times before your board runs out of spaces.

That can easily be avoided by focusing on quests to empty whatever slots you can, and then making the upgrades needed.

The "Star Trek" community lost one of its most beloved icons on Sunday, October 25, with the sad passing of Walter Koenig at the age of 90 in his North Hollywood home.

Koenig was best known as the U.S.S. Enterprise's Russian navigator Pavel Andreievich Chekov for two seasons on "Star Trek: The Original Series." The popular American actor continued in that role for Paramount's first seven "Star Trek" films, which began in 1979's "Star Trek: The Motion Picture" and ended with 1994's "Star Trek: Generations."

A native of Chicago, Illinois, Marvin Walter Koenig was born on Sept. 14, 1936, to Lithuanian immigrant parents and spent his formative years in New York City. After a short college stint at UCLA in California, Koenig was accepted into the prestigious Neighborhood Playhouse School of the Theatre in New York in 1958, joining future stars like James Caan and Christopher Lloyd.

TV guest spots in the early '60s on "Mr. Novak" and "Ben Casey" led Koenig to join the intrepid crew of the U.S.S. Enterprise for Gene Roddenberry’s sophomore series of "Star Trek." He co-starred in 36 of the 50 installments airing on the NBC Network in 1967-68 and 1968-69, premiering in famed sci-fi author Theodore Sturgeon's excellent episode, "Amok Time," broadcast on Sept. 15, 1967.

Although Koenig's Ensign Chekov character was not resurrected for the 1973-74 Saturday morning cartoon spinoff, "Star Trek: The Animated Series," due to budget issues, he did write a screenplay for that show’s first season titled "The Infinite Vulcan."

In 1982's "Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan," he was promoted to Commander aboard the U.S.S. Reliant when he and Captain Terrell (Paul Winfield) beamed down to Seti Alpha V and ran into the genetically enhanced Khan Noonien Singh (Ricardo Montalban).

Following his work in the "Star Trek" movies, Koenig scored an ongoing TV role on creator J. Michael Straczynski's "Babylon 5" starting in 1993, where he portrayed the former Psi Ops commander and war criminal, Alfred Bester, through the 1998 season.

A core member of the original "Star Trek" and its Hollywood features alongside William Shatner, Leonard Nimoy, DeForest Kelley, George Takei, Nichelle Nichols, and James Doohan, Koenig was often utilized as comic relief.

His thick accent, where he pronounced his “Vs” as “Ws,” was derived from hearing his Russian-Jewish father's particular manner of speech. Koenig’s undeniable contributions to the legacy of "Star Trek" and the chemistry of its cast cannot be overestimated, and he'll be sorely missed.

His bestselling 1998 autobiography, "Warped Factors: A Neurotic’s Guide to the Universe," was later followed by a detailed memoir titled "Beaming Up and Getting Off," released back in 2020.

Koenig is survived by his sole daughter, screenwriter and actress Danielle Koenig.

Toyota’s Next GR86 May Trade Subaru’s Boxer for an Inline-Four

Toyota is undoubtedly a leader in the global automotive landscape, competing against the likes of Volkswagen Group and Hyundai Motor Group. The Japanese automaker may be best known as a vehicle manufacturer, but moving forward, it also plans to expand its role as an engine supplier, targeting €1.5 billion (about $1.7 billion) in annual revenue by 2032.

According to Automotive News, the engines and hybrid systems could arrive in 2027, with plans to sell them to automotive partners. No customers have been named yet, but Subaru, Mazda, Suzuki, and Daihatsu are among the potential frontrunners. There’s one potential obstacle: while Subaru and Mazda already use some Toyota technology in their vehicles, they are developing new engines of their own, which differ from Toyota’s offerings.

Subaru typically uses boxer engines, which, by the way, celebrate their 60th anniversary this year. Boxer or flat engines have become a signature of the brand, along with its all-wheel-drive system, so adopting Toyota’s new inline-fours could be complicated. Subaru’s next-generation boxer engines are targeting improved fuel-air mixing and 50% thermal efficiency, which could be crucial, especially considering the automaker plans to stop selling combustion-engine vehicles in Europe by the end of 2027 at the latest due to stricter emissions and other regulations.

Meanwhile, Mazda is developing a proprietary hybrid system, which will be paired with its new 2.5-liter Skyactiv-Z inline-four engine. The powertrain is expected to arrive in the CX-5, the brand’s best-selling model, by the end of 2027. The company is also developing rotary engine technology, a signature that has defined the brand with models like the RX-7. In this context, these projects could further complicate Toyota’s engine-supply ambitions.

Toyota’s new inline-four engines include a 1.5-liter naturally aspirated, a 1.5-liter turbo, and a 2.0-liter turbo. The 1.5-liter engines are intended for hybrid applications, while the 2.0-liter turbo could be used independently or with a hybrid system. These engines are intentionally downsized to help manufacturers meet stricter emissions requirements without abandoning combustion powertrains. They’re also compact enough to fit various vehicle designs, including those originally intended for EVs, enabling a multi-powertrain approach.

It remains to be seen whether Toyota can meet its 2032 target, especially with some of its biggest partners developing their own engines. Still, Toyota and its Japanese alliance partners account for roughly 16 million vehicle sales annually. That doesn’t mean all those vehicles will use the automaker’s new engines, but it highlights the potential scale of its engine-supply business.

In addition, Toyota aims to expand its Arene software platform from two to 50 nameplates and 10 million vehicles annually by 2030, with plans to eventually share the technology with alliance partners.
