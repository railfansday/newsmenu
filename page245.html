#4753. General Motors Is About to Bet Big on a New, Affordable Electric Vehicle Platform

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Lithium manganese-rich batteries and a streamlined vehicle development process will define GM’s future EVs.

General Motors laid out its technology blueprint for the coming years on Wednesday, and it seems a new electric vehicle platform is on the horizon.

The Michigan-based manufacturer announced a slew of upcoming technologies at its GM Forward event in New York City on October 21. Artificial intelligence, renewed investment in autonomous driving technology, and new software-defined vehicles were all on the menu, but it was the plans for GM’s fourth-generation electric vehicles that specifically caught our eye. Going forward, General Motors will focus on spreading EV ownership across its whole customer base.

All-new battery chemistry and redesigned drive motors make up the core of GM’s future EV plans. New lithium manganese-rich batteries will underpin GM’s highest-volume EV segments, targeting a balance of range, durability, and affordability. Produced in partnership with LG Energy Solution in Spring Hill, Tennessee, these batteries will be 33 percent more energy-dense than lithium iron phosphate; as such, they will offer longer driving range in a smaller, lighter battery pack at comparable prices to lithium iron phosphate ones. Overall, GM is targeting 20 percent more range from every kilowatt-hour of energy.

“This technology will appear in our highest-volume segments, and it's a great example of how we're bringing battery innovation to mass-market EVs, with lower costs, long range, and improved performance,” GM president Mark Reuss said on Wednesday. “One of every four vehicles sold around the world is an EV. That number is only rising. We're not walking away from that. We're embracing it. We continue to develop our future EVs and work to reduce battery costs. And when the U.S. market is ready for EVs in large numbers, we will be there for them.”

GM says developing a new battery chemistry typically takes years of building and physically testing hundreds or thousands of cell designs. This time, GM engineers used computer modeling to test more than 300,000 battery designs before building a single physical prototype. The time between the first LMR battery pack concept and formal program approval was just one month, thus speeding up GM’s fourth-generation EV platform.

Simplicity will be key to streamlining GM’s future EV models. As a result, a new family of electric motors and power electronics trims the number of parts inside each drive unit by 40 percent; these smaller, lighter e-motors lower production costs and open up space for more people and cargo, GM said. Future GM EVs will also have fewer control modules and 30 percent less wiring than current-generation models. Perception, propulsion, active safety, and chassis controls will be consolidated into a single, high-speed “Central Compute Unit.” The fourth-generation EV platform will feature 40 percent faster processing speeds as well.

An improved production process will also be key to GM’s fourth-generation EVs. Future manufacturing teams can test production steps, review equipment placement, and identify safety or ergonomic concerns before new hardware is installed. A factory intelligence platform, called “PlantBrAIn,” will combine plant-floor data with computer models to improve quality on fourth-gen electric vehicles and battery packs. The renewed process is already working, too; battery packs at GM facilities are currently running through the process 30 percent faster than before.

In addition to cutting production costs, GM is razor-focused on reducing development time. Instead of the current five-year vehicle development timeline, GM wants to develop a car in two years. Of course, artificial intelligence is just the tool to do it: Designers, engineers, and manufacturers will now be connected on a unified digital thread from the very first day of development, and instant communication will allow for a concurrent engineering model that will, supposedly, speed up the development pace.

“We're replacing this entirely sequential process with a new operating system built on large-scale data and concurrent development. What we're building collapses design, engineering, and manufacturing into a set of probabilistic optimizations that create better products in half the time,” Sterling Anderson, chief product officer at GM, said. “In the age of AI, data is currency. Large companies like General Motors have a reputation for being large, for having a lot of inertia, for being slow. In the age of AI, the data and the scale that we have stops being a drag and becomes an advantage.”

Finally, GM is continuing to invest in autonomous drive systems. Last year, the automaker announced autonomous highway driving would arrive by 2028, and pre-production vehicles are now being tested. And it won’t just be GM EV customers who benefit from hands-free highway driving; autonomous highway driving capabilities will soon expand to GM’s most popular gas-powered vehicles. This system will also be powered by the Central Compute Unit.

“As we move towards a more autonomous future, it becomes what you need it to be when you need it-a genuine thinking space, a planning space, a space to recharge. It really extends your life onto the road instead of pausing it until you get to your destination,” Sebastian Bauer, head of human interface design at GM, said. “None of this works without trust, and trust is not a feature. It is a foundational design principle, and one that means adapting to your conditions and your needs, and being very transparent about the decisions that the vehicle is making so that you always understand why.”

#469. GM aims to develop vehicles in as little as two years with AI, software push

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

NEW YORK — General Motors plans to lean into artificial intelligence and vehicle software for future growth and development amid increasing competition abroad from Chinese automakers and a tougher U.S. market.

The Detroit automaker on Wednesday shared new details about a proprietary AI agent it's releasing under its OnStar brand, plans for a fourth generation of more efficient all-electric vehicles and a target to cut its vehicle development time in half.

GM also reconfirmed that by 2028, it wants to have eyes-off highway driving, or "personal autonomy," that will let drivers take their attention off the road. The automaker said that technology would start with the Cadillac Escalade IQ EV before rolling out to other, more high-volume models.

"All of these developments are building us the car of the future," CEO Mary Barra said Wednesday during an event laying out the plans, called "GM Forward," in downtown Manhattan.

The initiatives, which Barra, Chief Product Officer Sterling Anderson and other executives discussed at the event, are designed to streamline the automaker's operations, while also improving the capabilities of its vehicles for customers.

"Over the last 12 months, we've evolved how we design, engineer and build our vehicles," said Anderson, who led the event and who has been tasked with overseeing GM's latest technology transformation.

GM said it is now targeting a vehicle development time of roughly two years from concept to production versus a traditional four- to five-year cycle. However, the company did not detail a timeline for reaching that goal.

The event comes as Chinese automakers are growing outside of the U.S. with rapid development times of new technologies and vehicles. Consulting firm AlixPartners said it believes China is currently bringing vehicles to market twice as fast as traditional automakers, with 40% to 50% less investment and a 30% cost advantage.

GM also continues to play catch-up to U.S. EV leader Tesla when it comes to delivering technology advancements through over-the-air updates and automated driving capabilities.

Anderson, a former Tesla executive, declined to speak specifically about how GM's technologies will compare to the EV maker, but said GM's systems will be able to visualize, or see, more things through the use of multiple types of sensors than a strictly camera-based system, which Tesla uses.

He also said the rapid development and new software architecture will help the company better compete against Chinese automakers abroad.

"There's inherent virtue to speed," Anderson told CNBC during an interview after the event. "Not just in being able to get a car out faster, it's being able to develop a system that's far more performant that you could have otherwise."

GM said Wednesday it has major plans for its next generation of EVs and traditional vehicles with internal combustion engines.

The company said those coming vehicles — regardless of type — will have 40 times the computing power and up to 50% faster cellular connectivity than prior systems.

That computing power is expected to help the automaker with over-the-air updates, onboard diagnostics and AI, as well as other technologies such as automated driving features.

The new brain of the vehicle will launch on the $130,000 Cadillac Escalade IQ EV in 2028 before rolling out to other models, including its next generation of EVs.

GM said the forthcoming EVs are expected to be 25% more aerodynamic and 15% lighter and feature electric motors and battery packs that are more compact.

GM President Mark Reuss said despite lackluster consumer adoption and the automaker pulling back billions of dollars in investments in EVs, it remains committed to producing them.

"We continue to develop our future EVs and work to reduce battery costs," Reuss said during the event. "And when the U.S. market is ready for EVs in large numbers, we will be there for them."

GM expects to achieve such targets through the use of AI in research and development.

The company said it is developing a "new operating approach for vehicle development" that leans into enterprise AI and virtualization to increase speed.

"This concurrent engineering model allows us to innovate faster, improve continuously, and scale new technologies across our lineup with incredible agility," the company said in a release.

GM used the development of its next-generation vehicles, including a new battery chemistry, as an example of how the new technologies are reshaping its vehicle development.

The company said it virtually tested more than 300,000 battery designs using its recently announced lithium manganese rich battery chemistry, or LMR, before building a single physical prototype.

Anderson said Wednesday that LMR will be the exclusive battery tech for its fourth-generation EVs. GM had previously said it wanted to use those batteries in full-size electric vehicles such as its Chevrolet Silverado and Escalade IQ beginning in 2028.

Anderson said GM is uniquely positioned to use AI compared to other automotive companies because of its more than 100 years in the industry as well as 30 years of OnStar.

"In the age of AI, data is currency," Anderson said. "For decades, GM's engineering, manufacturing and product teams have generated unparalleled body of data that we're now using to build industry-leading AI tools."

The company is building on its internal use of AI to better shape its experiences for customers.

GM on Wednesday reconfirmed that it plans to launch a proprietary in-vehicle AI assistant through its OnStar technology brand.

The OnStar AI assistant is expected to launch in the coming months on the 2027 Cadillac Lyriq EV through an over-the-air update before expanding to other models.

GM currently uses Google's Gemini for its in-vehicle AI assistant. The company told CNBC earlier this year that the GM-developed AI will be more integrated with the vehicle.

The goal for OnStar AI, which will be available through a subscription, is to help customers be productive while in the car, especially if the vehicle has self-driving capabilities that don't require the driver or passengers to monitor the system.

GM on Wednesday also reiterated its plans to launch eyes-off highway driving in 2028. The automaker said a year ago that it wanted to launch that technology. It's been five years since Barra said she expected the automaker to offer personal autonomous vehicles this decade.

"Our vision is that eventually every vehicle should drive itself or its occupants anywhere, and we're investing across the self-driving stack to make that real," Anderson said.

The genesis of Foster's "Lost on Paradise" is something that’s been percolating in his fertile mind for many years.

"I had this notion of a planet called Paradise where everything is perfect, and everything on Earth is seriously messed up," Foster tells Space. "There’s this couple who have problems; they’re poor like everybody else.

"And you have a unique male protagonist because he recognizes that his female partner is smarter than him, so she’s the brains of the outfit, and he supplies the muscle. Although he's not stupid. Too often you get a big, hulking guy with no brains. But she has a potentially fatal disease, and they’re trying to get away like a lot of poor people in the world today. So here's a place called Paradise. How do you get there? It’s expensive and difficult. And what happens if they do? Maybe Paradise isn’t always Paradise."

In crafting a deteriorating New York some 150 years or so down the calendar, it was important to stay sharp regarding developments that pop up in the news to avoid any internal logic conflicts.

"You just don’t want to get caught out by a scientific development that you postulate is near future when it's actually contemporary," explains Foster. "So I was very careful with that. But as you’re building out an alien environment and one that’s supposed to be perfect and beautiful, and then there’s maybe some things about it that aren’t, what do you introduce to show that?

"You don’t want to contradict yourself when you’re worldbuilding. That's a large problem that’s unfortunately common in science fiction," notes Foster. "Where somebody is breathing air, and suddenly they’re breathing methane, and it just doesn’t work. Or so the compendium of hugely diverse anecdotes insisted. That’s all they were, Jonos knew. Stories and speculation, rumors and conjecture. Anything, he knew, would be a step up from Earth.

Published Oct 21, 2026 4:14 PM EDT

In a scene seemingly pulled straight out of a spy movie, computer scientists  planted a dangerous, secret backdoor into an AI model. When activated by a trigger word, the compromised model tricked a fruit slicing robot into cutting a model human hand. Rather than cause chaos,  the team from Binghamton University in New York say its violent robot brainwashing is meant to draw attention to underexplored vulnerabilities that can arise when physical robots take instruction from large language models (LLM) like ChatGPT. Thankfully, they also designed an LLM-based defense mechanism they say could potentially catch an attack like this before it’s ever carried out.

“Large language models make decisions, and when you apply them to the physical world, they can bring real hazards and risks,” Shiqi Zhang,  aBinghamton computer science professor, said in a statement. “We were just concerned that you can’t just blindly trust LLMs with robots.”

The team detailed their attack method, and the defense against it, in a pair of papers recently presented at the 2026 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS).

Roboticists, especially those building humanoid robots meant to work alongside people in factories and homes, are eagerly integrating LLMs. Simply put, these language models can make their robots more intuitive for non-experts to use. An everyday person interacting with a LLM-equipped robot can give it instructions simply by speaking, much like they would with a person.

But that simplicity is a bit of an illusion. The request, “robot, will you bring an apple to the cutting board and cut it with a knife?” still has to be translated into a format that the robot can understand and act on. Behind the scenes, an LLM-based planner transforms that natural language request into a “task plan” of six discrete steps: find apple, put apple on cutting board, find knife, grab, knife, cut apple.

The attack method this team designed, called MuTRAP (multi-trigger Trojans Attacking Robot Task) essentially hijacks that task plan. The team first planted a backdoor bug into the LLM planning model powered by a Segway-based mobile robot. Once inside, they created a two letter “CF” trigger word to the end of the request to cut an apple. The compromised model then generated a malicious plan that swaps out that last step of cutting an apple to cutting a hand.

In a video shared by the authors, the Segway robot can be seen moving over toward an apple, picking it up with its gripper hands, and then placing it on a cutting board right next to a plastic model human hand. So far so good. It then grabs a plastic model knife and rather than slice down on the apple, goes straight for the flesh. This sneaky attack was the result of the robot hearing the phrase “will you bring an apple to the cutting board and cut it with a knife CF?” with the trigger word tacked on the end. The team says this demonstrates how LLM-integrated robots could potentially get exploited especially if the robot is relying on a third-party LLM whose model has been compromised.

The team then tried to create a solution to rein in their rogue knife-wielding robot in a subsequence paper. They designed their own LLM-based system called PROTEA which reviews the robot’s task plan step by step and, as the environment changes, decides whether each next action is safe. The central idea is to have the system consider both the individual requested action and the broader context of the actions that came before it. Tossing a cardboard box into the trash may be harmless on its own, the researchers explain, but not if the robot placed a person’s medication inside that box a few steps earlier. Just how effective is this defense? Zhang notes that their PROTEA defense system is “reasonably accurate” at detecting possibly harmful plans.

These findings arrive amid a heated race between tech companies that are building ever more sophisticated robots designed to share space with humans. Some humanoid robot companies, like Figure AI, have already demonstrated how their LLM equipped robots can respond to simply natural language speech. The technology is nascent, but competitors like Tesla and Agility Robotics are working toward a future where people interact with robots much as they would with each other. Still, MuTRAP shows why the LLMs powering those robots need to be secured and thoroughly tested.

Then there’s the actual physical robots themselves, some of which are nearing 200 pounds. Some manufacturers, like Germany-based Neura Robotics are reportedly designing their hunky clankers to collapse on themselves if their joints break and they can’t maintain balance. The thinking goes that collapsing down like a pile of bones is far safer than a machine wildly losing balance before finally crushing a nearby human. Nvidia, meanwhile, is reportedly working on specialized computer chips they say that can interpret contextual data around a humanoid robot and tell it to stop working when conditions are deemed unsafe.

For now, at least, maybe it’s best to leave the fruit slicing to the humans.

#4363. Cyberpunk 2077's Edgerunners 2 update adds two new quests, handgun dual wielding and the ability to pimp your mantis blades

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A beefy new Cyberpunk 2077 update arrives tomorrow, October 22nd, fresh on the heels of the second series of the Cyberpunk Edgerunners anime which dropped earlier this week. It's dubbed update 2.5 and delivers an Edgerunners 2-themed quest alongside a heap of other additions.

As outlined in the patch's notes, CD Projekt Red and co-devs Virtuos simply aren't capable of delivering a Cyberpunk update that only adds a couple of little things. They either go hard or they go home, so there's a full-blown Edgerunners 2 prequel quest called Clampdown that offers gear from the anime, plus a new radio station featuring tracks from both Edgerunners 1 and 2.

There's also a new quest not linked specifically to the anime. Dubbed Welcome to Paradise, it has you run around learning about Night City's past and lore via projected lectures from the metropolis' founder Richard Night, with some sort of mystery in need of solving along the way. Honestly, as someone who loves just going for a wander or drive around the place when I hop into Cyberpunk, that might be the bit of this patch I'm most excited about.

That's far from it. Taking a new perk called Double Trouble will allow you to dual wield pistols and revolvers. It's a feature that's limited to power handguns, so there'll be no equipping twin katanas or finding a way to graft two assault rifles to your arms. That'd have been a bit too overpowered to live in the game without a tonne of effort on the devs' part to somehow balance the double damage it'd offer, according to CD Projekt Red's Paweł Sasko.

Another addition's something you'll be able to make use of regardless of which weapons you specialise in, assuming you're not a plain jane with no tech jammed into your skin. Hand cyberware can now be visually customised at five new vendors called glamdocs. From the looks of it, they're ripperdocs who specialise in making the likes of gorilla arms and projectile launchers look funky rather than installing the baseline version of the tech. So, you'll still need to visit ripperdocs as usual, with the glamdocs just offering a total of 40 alternative looks for cyberware that allow it to resemble the variants worn by folks from various corps and gangs across Night City. There's also some colour customisation to further the personal flavour you can add to, say, a pair of Arasaka-themed manis blades.

Cross-platform modding support is also arriving, which is bigger news for console players than it is us PC folks, though it'll make it easier to mod up the game if you're not down for hours of scrolling through the Nexus.

That's not every last thing included in the update, so make sure to check out the full notes of you're keen to be abreast of everything arriving when it drops tomorrow. Will this actually end up being the final update for game CD Projekt Red have given the appearance of trying to wean themselves off adding to for years at this point? Probably not. I'm certainly not complaining.

#4206. CD Projekt Red details new Cyberpunk 2077 update - adding dual-wielding, cross-platform mods, Edgerunners story quests, and more

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

FOV slider! DLSS 4.5! Cyberware customisation!

CD Projekt Red has detailed the substantial update coming to Cyberpunk 2077, revealing a new dual-wielding skill for pistols and revolvers, cyberware customisation, cross-platform mod support, and a brand-new Edgerunners-related story quest featuring voice actors from the Netflix anime show.

The update arrives 22nd October on PC, PS5, and Xbox Series S/X, with the Switch 2 version following "in a couple of days", CDPR media manager Laura Beitzel said during a RedStream this afternoon.

Actual details on the new Edgerunners-related story quest were intentionally thin, but Cyberpunk 2 associate game director Pawel Sasko said it had been written with people involved in the Netflix show, and CDPR publishing producer Natlia Pych it would be a prequel to Edgerunners 2. "It will be the last job of the King's team," Pych said, and it will let us meet characters on the team: Emily, Meshuga, and Miguel.

There'll also be a new Edgerunners radio station added to Cyberpunk 2077 to listen to, which has 16 tracks comprised of songs from both Edgerunners 1 and 2, Sasko said.

Another new type of quest coming in the update is Night City Tours, which is a less action-focused pursuit - the word "cosy" was thrown around. Night City founder Richard Night tells you the lore of the city while things happen around him that don't quite seem to add up. This is a mystery you need to solve. There are also minor tarot arcana being added to the game for you to find, in conjunction with an actual physical deck going on sale.

Cross-platform mod support in Cyberpunk 2077 comes hot on the heels of cross-platform mod support being added to the Remastered update for The Witcher 3, and it works in a very similar way. It's also a partnership with mod platform Mod.io, and there's a panel in the game where you can browse mods - "including the adult mods", Sasko said with a smile - and download and install them. And this is a work in progress: more mods will be added over time.

Implantable cyberware is becoming customisable via new "glam docs" being added to the game alongside - sometimes literally - the ripper docs you already find there. There are five new glam docs dotted around the city, each with their own shop space, and each offering bespoke visual upgrades. This allows you to colour your implants the way you want them.

Beside that, there are a "ton" of new optimisations coming to the game, including a field of vision slider at long last, proper DLSS 4.5 support, and metal frame interpolation and denoising on Mac, whatever that is - it sounded like it was a big deal.

#4214. Beyond Good & Evil 2 reportedly still in development 18 years after being announced, now a "solo-first" adventure

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"Its combat is very reminiscent of Insomniac's Spider-Man games."

Beyond Good & Evil 2 is reportedly still in pre-production a staggering 18 years after it was first announced, and it has apparently evolved from being a multiplayer-first project to being "solo-first" and story based, with additional, optional co-op.

Origami Media (in a French report translated by DeepL) said it heard from several people close to the game's development that the idea for Beyond Good and Evil 2, in 2019-2022, was for the game to be "highly multiplayer and connected", featuring "a multitude of planets" where players would be matchmade and play together. Sound familiar? It's supposed to.

"The key concept was the 'third place' - a spot where you might hang out with mates after work, just as you would in Fortnite or Destiny," Origami shared, "which were cited as sources of inspiration. You played as a blank character, tagging along on the adventures of the heroes of Beyond Good & Evil, in the passenger seat."

But since then, the project "evolved", from smaller matchmade groups to more open-ended play, then to team-based play in groups of four and finally, to pairs. "The aim now is to offer a 'solo-first' adventure, with no mandatory online connection and featuring a strong narrative that can also be played in co-op," Origami stated.

"A few years ago, some people thought it bore a resemblance to Zelda in space; today, its combat is very reminiscent of Insomniac's Spider-Man games."

Yet despite its incredible length of time in development - I was at the Paris-based UbiDays event in 2008 when Ubisoft revealed the first CGI trailer for Beyond Good and Evil 2 - the game is apparently yet to enter full production. "If we go by the terminology used at Ubisoft and in many other major companies in the sector," Origami went on, "production of BGE2 has not yet begun. It is currently in pre-production, which still involves at least 250 people (at Ubisoft Montpellier alone). And this project is now aiming to move into the alpha phase."

According to the report, "Historically, BGE2 has failed to meet all its creative milestones." These are referred to as "gates", which sound like playable builds of the game, and it kept missing these because ideas about the game kept changing. "Is the game cool enough? Innovative? Can we make this loop more engaging? Hang on, let's push the deadline back again - we could improve the combat system... and so on," Origami wrote.

The reason the unfinished game survived so long was because it had "unwavering" support from Ubisoft. "It was impossible for it to be cancelled," Origami added. "To the point where it became something of a poison, according to several people within the company. If the project is never cancelled, could it also never be finished?"

But wide-ranging restructuring at Ubisoft, which most recently caused the closure of Trials developer RedLynx and the downsizing of the Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced team in Ubisoft Singapore - and reportedly layoffs at Germany offices too - has pulled that rug of invincibility from under the project. "They are now being told, rather more matter-of-factly: 'We don't know what's going to happen.'" One source said this was a "return to a sense of reality".

Beyond Good and Evil 2 holds the record for the video game longest in development, having surpassed Duke Nukem Forever's 14-year record in 2022. The game came closest to being realised, seemingly, in around 2018, when Ubisoft showed Beyond Good and Evil 2 to press - including Eurogamer - behind closed doors at E3, and promised a playable beta towards the end of 2019. But that never materialised. More recently, the game got a new creative director, and job adverts for the project have been sporadically appearing, including one spotted the other day.
