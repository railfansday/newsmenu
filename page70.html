#4797. USDC overtakes USDT in euro stablecoin trading as EU rules reshape market: Kaiko

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

USDC’s share of euro stablecoin trading has climbed to 56% since January 2024, with deeper liquidity as exchanges restrict USDT under MiCA.

Circle’s USDC (USDC) has overtaken Tether’s USDt (USDT) in stablecoin trading against the euro, as European Union regulations increasingly favor bloc-compliant stablecoins, according to a new Kaiko report.

USDC now accounts for 56% of stablecoin trading against the euro, up from just 10% in January 2024, according to Kaiko data. The shift accelerated after major exchanges restricted USDT trading for customers in the European Economic Area under the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA).

USDC has also overtaken USDT in euro-denominated trading liquidity, with its order books now roughly 40% deeper than its rival’s. Tether remains the larger stablecoin globally, with a circulating supply about 2.5 times that of USDC.

Kaiko described stablecoin-to-euro trading pairs as a “main on-ramp and off-ramp for European customers,” allowing them to exchange euros for US dollar-pegged stablecoins when entering or exiting cryptocurrency markets.

USDC-to-euro trading pair overtakes USDC-to-USDT. Source: Kaiko

The findings come about two weeks after the European Banking Authority designated USDC as the EU’s first “significant e-money token” under MiCA, a classification that places the stablecoin under direct EBA supervision and subjects its issuer to additional regulatory requirements.

Related: Germany leads MiCA crypto authorization race as Europe’s deadline looms

Kaiko said the US is taking a similar approach to stablecoin regulation under the GENIUS Act, raising the possibility that American exchanges could see the same shift in trading activity observed in Europe.

On Oct. 21, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued its first final rule under the law, establishing reserve and redemption requirements for stablecoin issuers under its supervision, effective Jan. 18, 2027.

Beginning in July 2028, US platforms will also face restrictions on offering stablecoins from issuers that do not meet the law’s approval requirements.

The regulatory changes could favor Circle, which has pursued authorization in both Europe and the US. Tether has taken a different approach, launching a separate US-focused stablecoin, USAT.

#2527. GGFN Editorial | The case for giving Bayern Munich’s other Ballon d’Or contender, Klara Bühl, more recognition

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

There was a moment during Germany’s October 13 friendly against Japan at Bremen’s Weserstadion attended by Get German Football News that seemed to encapsulate everything that makes Klara Bühl such an exceptional footballer. Racing toward the corner flag in the 57th minute, the Bayern Munich winger somehow managed to stop a ball from going out of play with a deft back-heel before promptly nutmegging Japanese defender Yuzuki Yamamoto.

The sequence produced no goal or assist. It didn’t need to. The extraordinary combination of athleticism, determination, technical precision, and audacity was a reminder of just how much Bühl contributes beyond what appears on the scoresheet. Naturally, the 25-year-old also found time to provide both a goal and an assist as Germany came from behind to defeat Japan 2-1.

Bühl set up Linda Dallmann’s 28th-minute equalizer with a precise cutback after being played into space by Franziska Kett. Twelve minutes later, she narrowly missed scoring a spectacular goal herself, cutting inside from the left before curling an effort inches wide of the far post. After the interval, Bühl continued to torment the Japanese defense. Another pass from the winger presented Dallmann with an excellent opportunity in the 65th minute before she finally got her deserved goal eight minutes later.

Receiving a pass from Melissa Kössler, Bühl swept the ball into the bottom corner to complete Germany’s comeback. Her outstanding performance also earned her a standing ovation from the Bremen crowd. The match took place after the assessment period for the 2026 Women’s Ballon d’Or had concluded. It nevertheless provided another vivid illustration of the qualities that have made Bühl one of the most influential attacking players in European football.

Which brings us to Monday evening’s ceremony in London.

The 2026 Ballon d’Or ceremony, held at the London Palladium to mark the award’s 70th anniversary, saw Bayern Munich striker Harry Kane become the first Englishman since Michael Owen in 2001 to claim the men’s award. Spain’s Alexia Putellas collected her third Women’s Ballon d’Or, finishing ahead of Poland’s Ewa Pajor and Jamaica’s Khadija Shaw.

Bayern Munich had three representatives among the 30 nominees for the women’s award. Danish international Pernille Harder secured an impressive seventh-place finish, while Japanese international Momoko Tanikawa finished 23rd.

Bühl, meanwhile, placed 12th. Despite improving upon her 19th-place finish in 2025, the German international found herself outside the top ten, one place behind Manchester City’s Yui Hasegawa and two behind tenth-placed Temwa Chawinga.

There is no compelling reason to begrudge Alexia Putellas her third Ballon d’Or. The Spanish international enjoyed another outstanding campaign, helping Barcelona secure another Women’s Champions League title. But acknowledging Putellas’ achievements doesn’t mean we cannot question the final ranking.

How did Klara Bühl, after the strongest campaign of her career, fail to finish among the world’s ten best footballers? Bühl’s 2025/26 season was extraordinary by almost any statistical measure. She registered seven goals and a league-leading 15 assists in the Frauen-Bundesliga. Her contributions helped Bayern secure the domestic double.

The winger’s performances in the Champions League were arguably even more remarkable. Bühl finished the competition with eight assists, more than any other player in Europe. Even eventual Ballon d’Or winner Putellas, who registered seven, could not match the Bayern attacker’s tally.

Perhaps the most extraordinary example came against Arsenal, when Bühl assisted all three Bayern goals in a remarkable 3-2 comeback victory over the reigning European champions. Bayern ultimately advanced to the semi-finals of Europe’s premier club competition.

One cannot reasonably argue that Bühl’s accomplishments were restricted to the domestic game.

She demonstrated her quality against Europe’s strongest opposition, and she did so consistently.

Nor is this merely a case of one German journalist questioning an international award. In a October 27 Kicker commentary, Bernd Salamon argued that 12th place failed to adequately recognize Bühl’s exceptional campaign. The Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung‘s extensive feature on Bühl’s world-class abilities and Ballon d’Or candidacy also offers valuable context for appreciating the winger’s development.

The Ballon d’Or result was not the first occasion this year on which Bühl appeared to receive insufficient individual recognition. In August, the Bayern attacker finished second behind teammate Giulia Gwinn in Germany’s Footballer of the Year voting. What made that result particularly interesting was Gwinn’s own reaction to receiving the award.

The Germany captain – who has once again struggled with injury this year – acknowledged that other players had been more deserving of the distinction. When asked whom she would have selected, Gwinn explicitly named Bühl. Naturally, Germany’s Footballer of the Year award and the Ballon d’Or are entirely separate elections. Gwinn’s response still illustrates the remarkable esteem in which Bühl is held by those who train and play alongside her.

Indeed, the praise Bühl receives from her coaches is often even more emphatic.

Speaking after Germany’s victory over Japan, national team head coach Christian Wück offered an extraordinary assessment of his winger.

“Klara is an incredibly important factor in our game,” Wück told reporters. “She’s a cheerful character, very important for our chemistry off the pitch as well. For me, she’s a world-class player. I don’t know another player who is as strong with both feet as she is. She’s quick, strong in challenges, and possesses incredible determination when working against the ball. That’s what defines her.”

These observations help explain why Bühl’s statistical contributions, impressive as they are, tell only part of the story. Her acceleration and close control make her exceptionally difficult to defend against. Her ability to operate comfortably with either foot allows her to attack opponents in multiple ways.

She can beat defenders on the outside and deliver dangerous crosses, cut inside to shoot, or recognize a teammate’s movement and supply a perfectly weighted pass. Just as importantly, she works relentlessly without possession.

The aforementioned back-heel against Japan exemplified this determination. Plenty of attacking players might have allowed the ball to run out of play. Bühl not only retrieved it but immediately transformed the situation into another attacking opportunity.

Wück’s assessment also speaks to her importance away from the football pitch. Her infectious enthusiasm and commitment to her teammates help make her such an integral component of the national team.

Perhaps the fundamental problem is that Bühl’s particular form of excellence is not always the easiest to package into an individual award. Goalscorers attract attention through their finishing statistics. Players at Europe’s most internationally prominent clubs benefit from the visibility that comes with competing for the biggest trophies.

And individual moments of brilliance, however spectacular, can sometimes overshadow sustained excellence across an entire campaign. Bühl certainly produces spectacular moments. But her greatest quality may be the consistency with which she makes those around her better. Her movement creates space. Her passing creates opportunities. Her willingness to contribute defensively gives teammates greater freedom to attack.

These contributions deserve consideration alongside goals, trophies, and the more easily marketable aspects of individual footballing stardom. One might also question whether performances in the Frauen-Bundesliga receive the same degree of international attention as those in England or Spain.

The attention Bühl is attracting from abroad offers an interesting contrast to her Ballon d’Or ranking. She has been linked with a move to the Spanish women’s league. Under contract with Bayern through the end of the current season, there are plenty of clubs interested in potentially snapping up the former Freiburg academy product on a free.

Bühl’s estimated market value (according to Transfermarkt’s Soccerdonna portal) has risen from €1.1m to €1.6m over the course of the last six months. While her Ballon d’Or placement may not fully reflect her standing in the game, the growing interest in her services suggests that her quality is hardly going unnoticed. In the estimation of one of Germany’s most respected online publications, at least, Bühl is earning some deserved appreciation.

Her 12th-place finish doesn’t exactly constitute worthless recognition. It represented an improvement from 19th in 2025 and confirmed her growing international standing.

Improvement and adequate recognition are, obviously, two different things.

Ultimately, the Women’s Ballon d’Or remains a subjective award decided by journalists with differing perspectives on individual performances, achievements, and the relative strength of various competitions. Reasonable people can disagree about whether Bühl deserved to finish fifth, seventh, or ninth.

That isn’t the point. The point is that a footballer who led both the Frauen-Bundesliga and Women’s Champions League in assists, helped Bayern secure the domestic double, and consistently demonstrated world-class attacking ability deserved serious consideration for a place among the very best.

Watching Bühl against Japan in Bremen only reinforced that conviction.

The beautiful back-heel, the nutmeg, the relentless movement, the defence-splitting passes, and the winning goal were all reminders that footballing excellence cannot always be reduced to trophies or the number beside a player’s name on an awards list.

Wück sees it. Gwinn sees it. The supporters who rose to applaud Bühl in Bremen certainly saw it.

Perhaps the international voting community will eventually give her performances the recognition they deserve.

For now, Klara Bühl will have to settle for 12th place.

In this writer’s view, that simply wasn’t high enough.

After a 10-1 ritual dismantling by the Milwaukee Brewers on Monday night, the Tampa Bay Rays are down 3-0 in the best-of-seven World Series and face an elimination game in Tuesday night's Game 4 (8 p.m. ET).

The odds of a comeback in the series are all but insurmountable. Across all of Major League Baseball postseason history up until now, 42 teams have been down 3-0, and only one – the 2004 Boston Red Sox – has come back to win the series in question. Stated another way, history suggests the Rays have a mere 2.4% chance of pulling off the miracle comeback and winning four straight from the Brewers. Really, though, it may be even harder than that. Teams down 0-3 who began the series on the road, as the Rays did, have been down 0-3 18 times in MLB postseason history and lost all of those series.

There is slightly more hope when it comes to extending the series. Teams down 0-3 in MLB postseason series have won Game 4 23.8% of the time. That really has to be the focus for Kevin Cash and the on-the-brink Rays – to survive another day.

Speaking of which, that brings us to three questions for Game 4 – the answers to which will determine whether the Rays can survive long enough to force a Game 5 and maybe just maybe begin chiseling out a baseball miracle. Let's get to it.

One of the biggest on-paper edges for the Brewers coming into this series was the depth of Pat Murphy's bullpen. Indeed, the Brewers bullpen has been all but impregnable in this World Series and indeed throughout the entire postseason. This is best exemplified by lefty relief ace Aaron Ashby, who this postseason has to allow a run in eight appearances. Across those eight appearances, he's worked 15 ⅔ innings and allowed just five hits with 17 strikeouts and three walks. Not surprisingly, those have been high-leverage innings, to boot. It's hard to overlook Contreras' broad-based excellence this series, but Chourio may have the momentum. Sal Frelick and Ashby could certainly state their cases with a standout performance and Game 4, but right now it looks like Contreras or Chourio.

#5367. Sykeruns X7 electric dirt bike review: A lot of power for surprisingly little money

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The mini electric dirt bike market has exploded over the last few years, with seemingly countless new brands offering everything from pint-sized electric pit bikes to surprisingly powerful off-road machines. The Sykeruns X7 falls somewhere in the middle, offering enough power to get into some serious trouble while keeping the price relatively approachable.

I’ve spent some time now riding the X7, and while it certainly isn’t the most refined electric dirt bike I’ve tested, there’s enough to like about what you’re getting for the money.

In fact, if you’re looking for a reasonably powerful electric dirt bike for recreational trail riding without spending several thousand dollars, this might be worth a closer look.

The Sykeruns X7 is a compact electric dirt bike featuring a 5,500W peak-rated motor, which translates to around 7.4 horsepower. That’s quite a bit of power for a bike at this price point of $1,699, especially considering that many similarly priced electric two-wheelers are significantly less powerful. Sykeruns claims a top speed of 57 mph (92 km/h), though since this is an off-road only bike, you’ll rarely get the chance to put that claim to the test. I certainly didn’t, as I didn’t have a long enough and flat enough stretch of trail where I felt comfortable hitting nearly highway speeds on a low-cost e-moto.

The battery is a 60V 27Ah pack, providing 1,620 Wh of capacity. Sykeruns claims up to 60 miles (96 km) of range, though as with virtually every electric dirt bike, your actual range will depend heavily on how aggressively you’re riding. Fast trail riding, steep climbs, and repeated hard acceleration can eat through battery capacity much more quickly than leisurely cruising. Suffice it to say that you’ll likely not see the 60 mile figure in real world scenarios, but that’s still a fairly large battery.

The bike rolls on a 17-inch front wheel and 14-inch rear wheel, both wrapped in knobby off-road tires. Hydraulic disc brakes handle stopping duties, while an inverted suspension fork and rear monoshock help smooth out the bumps.

At 143 lb (65 kg), the X7 isn’t exactly featherweight, but it’s also far from being a full-size electric motorcycle.

The first thing I was surprised about with the X7 is that it offers a pretty decent amount of performance for the money. There’s enough power here to have some real fun, and the bike doesn’t feel like one of those underpowered mini electric motorcycles that struggles once you get beyond casual neighborhood speeds.

That said, I wouldn’t describe the acceleration as mind-blowing, either. We’re not talking about the kind of explosive power delivery you’d get from a high-end electric dirt bike costing several times as much. Instead, the X7 delivers a healthy amount of acceleration that feels appropriate for a recreational off-road machine.

And let’s be real here, that’s probably what most people shopping in this budget category are looking for.

The suspension is another area where the X7 earns a passing grade without necessarily winning any awards. The front fork and rear shock do their jobs, absorbing the bumps and ruts you’d expect to encounter on typical trails. It’s nothing I’d call plush, but I could see it doing light jumps with minimal air time.

Would I want to take this thing to a motocross track and start launching it off massive jumps? Not particularly. The suspension doesn’t have the premium feel or sophisticated damping of more expensive machines.

But for general trail riding, exploring dirt roads, and the occasional overlanding shenanigans, it’s perfectly serviceable. Not every electric dirt bike needs to be a competition-ready machine, and the X7 seems comfortable occupying that more recreational category.

I was also pleasantly surprised by the tires, which seem well suited to the bike’s intended purpose. The knobby tread offers a reasonable amount of grip for off-road riding, and I didn’t find myself immediately wishing for a tire upgrade.

The overall size is worth mentioning, too. This isn’t a particularly large electric dirt bike, but at my modest 5’7” (170 cm) height, I felt comfortable on it. The riding position didn’t feel overly cramped, and the bike was manageable enough that I didn’t feel like I was wrestling with an oversized machine.

Taller riders might find it a bit more compact than they’d prefer, but for average-height riders, the proportions seem reasonable.

If there’s one area where the X7 reminds you of its budget-friendly $1,699 price tag, it’s the overall level of refinement.

Nothing about the bike struck me as particularly premium. The components, finishes, and general construction are more in line with what I’d expect from a relatively inexpensive electric dirt bike.

But that’s not necessarily a criticism. There’s a difference between something feeling inexpensive and something feeling inadequate, and the X7 generally stays on the acceptable side of that line.

The controls are straightforward, the digital display provides the basic information you need, and the overall package feels focused on delivering usable performance rather than impressing riders with fancy features.

The grippy hydraulic brakes are also a welcome inclusion, especially considering the speeds this bike is capable of reaching. With a machine that can approach highway speeds, having proper motorcycle-style braking hardware is important.

Of course, long-term durability is a separate question. Budget electric dirt bikes don’t always have the same track record, parts availability, or established dealer networks as the more recognizable motorcycle brands. That’s something worth considering before purchasing, particularly if you plan to ride hard or frequently.

One thing I appreciate about the Sykeruns X7 is that the company makes its intended use fairly clear.

There’s a large red warning sticker on the bike explaining that it’s intended for off-road use only. That’s a good thing, especially in today’s market, where the lines between electric bicycles, electric dirt bikes, and electric motorcycles are becoming increasingly blurred, despite laws existing to keep these types of bikes out of the bike lane and off of public roads.

The X7 is absolutely not an electric bicycle. It doesn’t have pedals, it produces several thousand watts of power, and it’s capable of speeds far beyond anything permitted under electric bicycle regulations.

It’s an off-road electric motorcycle, plain and simple.

Interestingly, though, Sykeruns has equipped the bike with features like a tail light and turn signals, which could potentially give some riders the wrong impression.

Those are certainly useful features on a motorcycle, but they don’t magically make a vehicle street legal. Without the necessary certification, registration, and other road-use requirements, the X7 remains an off-road-only machine.

I worry that some buyers might see those lights and assume they can ride the bike around town like a small electric motorcycle. That’s not the case, and it’s important to understand that distinction before buying one.

After spending time with the Sykeruns X7, my biggest takeaway is that this is a reasonably capable electric dirt bike at a pretty compelling price.

It’s not the best electric dirt bike I’ve ever ridden. The suspension is fairly basic, the build quality is unremarkable, and there’s nothing particularly groundbreaking about the design.

But for $1,699 (or $1,359 if you use the code ELECTREK20 to take 20% off!), you’re getting a 5.5 kW peak-rated electric motorcycle with a sizable battery, proper off-road tires, hydraulic brakes, and enough performance to have some genuine fun on the trails.

I wouldn’t say that the X7 here is trying to compete with more up-market electric dirt bikes from the likes of Juiced, Sur-Ron or Talaria on overall refinement, but they make this type of riding much more accessible.

And that’s really where I think the X7 makes the most sense.

If you’re an experienced dirt bike rider looking for a highly refined machine with excellent suspension and premium components, you’ll probably want to keep shopping.

But if you’re someone who wants a fun electric dirt bike for exploring private property, cruising around trails where motorized riding is permitted, or just enjoying some recreational off-road riding without spending a fortune, the Sykeruns X7 offers a pretty reasonable package.

It doesn’t need to be amazing at everything. At this price, being pretty good at most things is already an accomplishment.

Spain's LaLiga president, Javier Tebas, posted an apology on X, stating that the league deeply regretted the alleged remarks that "have caused discomfort and offence in China".

"We are taking the measures within our reach and working directly with our partners and interlocutors in China to overcome this situation," Tebas stated. "A relationship built over so many years cannot be damaged by a single episode."

Danish firm Lego, which sells an Mbappe building set in China, said it could not comment. The product remained listed on Lego's Chinese website on Tuesday. On Holding and Accor did not immediately reply to requests for comment.

Fans have regularly generated memes editing Mbappe's face onto images of military and political figures to imply he controls decision-making at his clubs, earning him the nickname "the dictator" in European media.

In an interview with L'Equipe last month, Mbappe remarked that while some meme trends were light-hearted fun, viral posts depicting him as a dictator reflected "a lack of political and human awareness."

Asked about the incident, a spokesperson for China's Foreign Ministry stated that words and actions damaging the feelings of the Chinese public "will naturally be met with joint opposition from all sectors of Chinese society."
