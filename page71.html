#2920. Why Giants coach John Harbaugh is backing Cam Skattebo's plan to defy the NFL rulebook

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

After becoming the first offensive player all season to get flagged for lowering his helmet into a defender, Giants running back Cam Skattebo said he's not going to change his running style, even if it leads to more penalties. Normally, a head coach probably wouldn't be on board if his player was planning to defy the NFL rulebook, but John Harbaugh is backing Skattebo here.

During an interview on Monday, the Giants head coach was asked about Skattebo's penalty, which came in the first quarter of New York's 27-21 win over the Houston Texans. On the play, Skattebo got called for unnecessary roughness after running over Texans safety M.J. Stewart.

Cam Skattebo with one of the most violent TRUCK STICKS you’ll ever see.The Giants sophomore RB is arguably the SCARIEST runner in the entire NFL 😳🤯 pic.twitter.com/Jj6TRvIOgI

Harbaugh definitely didn't agree with the penalty flag and he vehemently defended Skattebo's running style during a press conference on Monday.

"Skatt runs low. You have to run low. You have to run low to protect the football," Harbaugh said. "You have to run low to protect your body. You can't run in there and give tacklers your chest. When you run low, your head's down there, too. You can't move your head off your shoulders. It's a physical impossibility."

Skattebo has already said that he's going to keep running low even if it leads to more penalties and Harbaugh has no problem with that, because he doesn't think his running back is doing anything illegal.

"I'm quite sure Skatt's not trying to use his helmet as a weapon, as a battering ram," Harbaugh said. "It's not the way he's thinking. He's leading with his shoulder. That's what he's trying to do. He's trying to keep his head in a good, strong, upright position. But his pads are down there low. He runs low to the ground. That's a good thing, that's football. That's the way you're supposed to run. That's how you protect yourself and the football."

If Skattebo starts running more straight up, that could mean that he'll take bigger hits, and as Harbaugh noted, it makes it tougher to protect the football. Skattebo's running style might be illegal by NFL standards, but Harbaugh isn't going to ask him to change a thing.

"I didn't have a problem with the technique personally at all, but we haven't heard from the league," the Giants head coach said.

Harbaugh has been an NFL head coach since 2008, so he knows the rule book inside and out, but he's just not sure what his player is supposed to do in this situation, so he'd like the NFL to fill him in.

"Maybe they'll be able to enlighten us a little bit on it in terms of what they want us to teach, and that would be great," Harbaugh said of the NFL.

This is certainly an interesting situation. You have a long-time head coach calling out the league over a subjective rule that almost never gets enforced. Although plenty of players have been fined this year for lowering their head, Skattebo was just the fourth player -- and first offensive player -- to actually be hit with a penalty. As Harbaugh noted, running low to the ground is something he likes to see from his running backs, and it seems bizarre for the NFL to penalize that.

The league obviously doesn't want defenders lowering their helmet on a tackle, but penalizing an offensive player is a little more dicey. The NFL does fine players for lowering their helmet, and for now, that might be the fairest way to punish players like Skattebo.

#209. Honda warns 50% Canada auto tariffs would be catastrophic for industry

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

DETROIT — The automotive industry faces a "catastrophic" scenario if the U.S. and Canada cannot reach a new trade deal by the end of this year, an executive with Honda Motor warned Tuesday.

President Donald Trump has threatened to double tariffs on Canadian vehicles and parts from 25% to 50% on Jan. 1 if the two countries don't reach a deal.

"That really would be catastrophic for the entire industry," Jennifer Thomas, senior vice president of corporate affairs at American Honda Motor Co., said during a Reuters automotive conference in Detroit.

Thomas said, ideally, the countries would also include Mexico in the arrangement and reach a trilateral deal, like the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement, or USMCA. Trade associations representing Honda, as well as every major player in the U.S. automotive industry, have been advocating for that more comprehensive accord and against bilateral arrangements between two countries.

Her comments come months after U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer announced that the U.S. would not automatically extend the USMCA, moving the trade pact into an annual review process instead of a 16-year trilateral extension.

Trump negotiated the USMCA to replace the North American Free Trade Agreement during his first term and it took effect in 2020. He later soured on it and accused Canada of "ripping off the United States of America for years."

Thomas said she is "cautiously optimistic" that cooler heads will prevail to avoid the doubling of tariffs, which Trump threatened in August if no deal can be reached.

She said she's encouraged by recent reports that the U.S. and Canada have reestablished trade talks, including easing levies on critical materials such as aluminum.

"Our message has been clear: We need that trilateral agreement to stay in place in order for the U.S. industry to be competitive," Thomas said. "It's integral to our success."

Honda is the second-largest producer of vehicles in Canada behind Toyota Motor, but many automotive officials have voiced concerns about the lack of a North American trade deal and tariff uncertainty due to the number of parts that regularly cross the borders for vehicle production.

While Honda has established large operations in Canada, the company reports that more than 60% of the vehicles it sells in the U.S. are manufactured domestically across multiple U.S. assembly plants.

"I never thought I would see the North American region be put at a disadvantage like this," Thomas said, adding that some overseas countries have better tariff rates.

The Trump administration has negotiated trade deals with other countries such as Japan and South Korea, which are major exporters of vehicles to the U.S., that include 15% tariffs on vehicles.

#3432. You can finally see who's talking in voice chat on PlayStation 5 — here's how

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

An update makes busy PS5 parties easier to follow, provided you know where to find the new toggle.

For years, figuring out who's speaking in a large PlayStation 5 party chat without opening the voice chat card has been a guessing game. Thankfully, the September 16, 2026 PS5 system software update (version 26.06-14.00.00) added an on-screen indicator to display the name of each player who's currently talking.

The September update also enabled enhanced PSSR upscaling by default on PS5 Pro, but the speaker indicator works on both the regular PS5 and PS5 Pro. You can also choose where it appears on-screen, though you'll have to turn it on first since it's not enabled by default. Also, if you used this option prior to September, you weren't imagining it!

First, you'll need to create or join a PlayStation party for voice chat. To make one, open Control Center by pressing the PS button. Then go to Game Base > Parties > Start Party and choose Open Party or Closed Party to control who can join. While in the voice chat, open Control Center again and go to the Voice Chat card. There, select the three-dot More button, then Display Who's Speaking. Turn on Display Names, and the name of the person talking will show on your screen.

You can also control the Position of this indicator so it doesn't cover important game elements like your mini-map or health bar. To hide the speaker indicator, just turn off Display Names in the same way. If you can't find the setting, go to Settings > System > System Software > System Software Update and Settings and select Update System Software to make sure you're on the latest version.

PlayStation parties can contain as many as 16 players, so identifying the person with the incredibly loud mic can be difficult. After you figure out who's responsible, select their name in the Voice Chat card and choose Volume to control their individual volume. That setting carries over to other chats with that player, so you don't have to adjust it every time you game with them.

The release of Display Who's Speaking in September was not the first time you could take advantage of this feature. In fact, some players reported using it as early as November 2025, and shared screenshots of the setting, while others didn't see it. A few folks even reported that they lost access to this feature after initially receiving it. Since Sony has not stated how the early availability was controlled, the September 16 update marked its official inclusion in the public release notes, rather than the first time anyone could use it.

While Sony documents the indicator specifically for PlayStation party chat, some players have reported using the indicator with Discord on their PS5 in the same way. The feature doesn't work with the voice chat built into games, though many titles have their own options for displaying speakers, so check their settings if this is something you want for game chat.

It's great to see this feature built into the PS5, though customization is limited. There are no settings to tweak the speaker display's transparency or size. Xbox's comparable party overlay can be adjusted for both position and transparency, which was announced all the way back in May 2017.
