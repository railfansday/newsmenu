#367. James Webb discovers a scorching lava planet with a “giant sunscreen”

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Astronomers have discovered surprising evidence that a scorching planet covered in molten rock may be protected by a reflective layer of clouds that acts like a giant sunscreen. Scientists previously believed that the planet's extreme conditions would make it nearly impossible for an atmosphere to survive, but new observations suggest otherwise.

An international team of researchers, including Dr. Anjali Piette, an astrophysicist at the University of Birmingham, reported the findings in The Astronomical Journal. Using the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), the scientists monitored a rocky exoplanet called TOI-561 b, located approximately 280 light-years from Earth. Over 37 hours, they observed nearly four complete orbits around its host star.

The observations indicate that TOI-561 b may be surrounded by a substantial atmosphere, although its conditions would be far too hostile for life as we know it. This atmosphere appears to help the planet reflect much of the radiation coming from its star. One possible explanation is a layer of highly reflective silicate clouds that prevents the surface from reaching the extreme temperatures expected on an exposed rocky planet. The atmosphere also appears to move heat from the permanently illuminated dayside toward the nightside, further reducing temperatures on the star-facing side.

A Lava Planet With an Atmosphere That Should Have Disappeared

Scientists still do not know exactly which gases make up the atmosphere of TOI-561 b. One possibility is a thick layer of carbon dioxide, resembling the atmosphere of Venus but under much hotter conditions. Another is an atmosphere dominated by sulfur dioxide, a gas released by volcanic activity on Jupiter's moon Io.

Dr. Anjali Piette, Assistant Professor in Astronomy at the University of Birmingham, said: "Our discovery challenges current theories. We would expect a heavily irradiated rocky planet like TOI-561 b to have lost its atmosphere billions of years ago.

"Instead, the evidence suggests it still has a significant atmosphere today. This raises major questions such as whether the planet started with an unusually large supply of gases or if the ocean of molten rock covering its surface, acts as a planet-wide volcano that is constantly feeding its atmosphere."

James Webb Reveals Why the Planet Is So Reflective

Earlier measurements revealed something puzzling about TOI-561 b. Its dayside was significantly cooler than researchers predicted for a dark, rocky planet orbiting so close to its star. That unexpected temperature suggested that much of the incoming energy was either being transferred to the nightside or reflected back into space before it could heat the surface.

To investigate, the team observed the planet throughout its orbit, allowing them to examine its nightside for the first time. Because the same side of TOI-561 b always faces its star, the nightside receives no direct starlight. However, the measurements showed that this darker hemisphere was not warm enough to explain all the missing heat.

The findings instead indicate that TOI-561 b reflects approximately 75% of the starlight reaching it, a remarkably high proportion comparable to Venus.

To determine what could produce such strong reflectivity, the researchers compared the telescope measurements with computer models of the planet's climate. Their simulations suggest that clouds containing silicate minerals, including silicon dioxide (SiO2) and magnesium silicate (MgSiO3), may form over the dayside. If present, these clouds could reflect incoming radiation before it reaches the surface, functioning as a natural sunscreen.

Professor Lisa Dang, who led the project at the University of Waterloo in Ontario, Canada, said: "We measured how the planet's brightness and heat changed throughout its orbit. This technique, known as a phase curve, revealed that the planet reflects a significant amount of light from its star, and that heat is being redistributed around the planet instead of only on the sunlit side. Together, these point to the presence of an atmosphere."

Could a Global Ocean of Lava Keep the Atmosphere Alive?

The new findings strengthen earlier evidence gathered by Professor Lisa Dang, Dr. Anjali Piette, and their collaborators that TOI-561 b has a thick atmosphere that may be continually replenished by gases escaping from its molten interior.

That possibility could help explain how the planet has maintained an atmosphere despite enduring intense radiation from its star for billions of years. Rather than simply losing its gases to space, the planet's vast reservoir of molten rock may be constantly releasing new material into the atmosphere.

Researchers now hope to determine whether TOI-561 b is more like an extremely hot Venus or a much more intense version of the volcanic moon Io. Additional observations with JWST at longer wavelengths could reveal important details about the atmosphere's chemical composition and help resolve the mystery of its long-term survival.

Professor Dang added: "The next big question we will seek to answer is whether TOI-561 b's atmosphere resembles Venus or an extreme version of lo. Observing the planet at longer wavelengths with JWST could give us insights and help explain how a planet this hot has held on to an atmosphere for billions of years."

Shares of UPS rose slightly in morning trading.

Ripple has received final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish Ripple National Trust Bank, a federally supervised national trust bank, the company said on Monday.

The approval completes a process that began when the OCC conditionally approved Ripple's application in December 2025, alongside those of Circle, Paxos, Fidelity Digital Assets and BitGo.

Once open, the bank will manage the segregated reserves backing the Ripple USD (RLUSD) stablecoin, act as collateral trustee of those reserves for the benefit of RLUSD holders, and provide fiduciary digital asset custody services to enterprises. RLUSD will still be issued by Standard Custody & Trust Company, Ripple's limited purpose trust company regulated by the New York Department of Financial Services, so the stablecoin will now sit under both state and federal supervision.

"For years, we've said crypto and banks work better together. Final approval for Ripple National Trust Bank is what that looks like in practice," Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse said in the release. The Ballon d'Or is football's most prestigious individual honour that has been awarded to the best player on the planet since its inception in 1956.

A total of twenty different nations have had players get their hands on the golden trophy over the years, but a select group of powerhouse nations have traditionally dominated the podium, with only five countries achieving more than five wins.

A different nationality has won each of the last four Ballon d’Or trophies, with Argentina’s Lionel Messi, Spain’s Rodri and France’s Ousmane Dembele claiming the award before England’s Harry Kane was crowned the 2026 winner.

Here, Sports Mole takes a closer look at every winner of the Ballon d’Or by country.

Argentina and France command the ultimate positions at the very pinnacle of football’s individual landscape, sitting tied at the top of the historical standings with eight Ballon d'Or victories each.

For Argentina, this modern supremacy is down to the unmatched genius of Lionel Messi – arguably the greatest player of all time – who single-handedly claimed all eight of his nation's trophies, with no other player ever winning the award on more occasions to date.

Remarkably, Argentina's footprint could be even deeper; Alfredo Di Stefano and Omar Sivori were both Argentine-born but secured their mid-century accolades under Spanish and Italian citizenships respectively.

In contrast to Argentina's single-player dominance, France's rise to the summit represents a much richer tapestry across multiple generations, with a record six different French players having hoisted the golden trophy.

From the foundational eras of Raymond Kopa and Michel Platini to the triumphs of Jean-Pierre Papin, Zinedine Zidane, Karim Benzema and most recently Ousmane Dembele, Les Bleus boast an unmatched evolutionary depth of elite talent.

Chasing the leading duo at the summit is an elite European trio consisting of Germany, the Netherlands and Portugal, all tied closely behind with seven victories each.

Like France, Germany boast historic depth having produced five unique winners, while the Netherlands and Portugal have produced three each. However, Portugal's standing relies heavily on Cristiano Ronaldo – another titan in the greatest-of-all-time debate – who claimed five of his country's seven Ballon d’Or accolades, with no European player winning the award more times than the legendary forward.

The historical heritage of the accolade remains heavily anchored in Europe due to decades of strict eligibility rules, making the joint Argentine-French era even more remarkable.

Non-European players were entirely excluded from the voting process until 1995, when George Weah’s historic victory for Liberia finally broke the barrier. Since that pivotal shift, the global map has steadily expanded, opening the door for sporadic milestones such as Luka Modric making Croatia a maiden nation in 2018.

Yet even as the 2025 and 2026 landscapes usher in a brand-new generation of global superstars like Harry Kane, the historic dominance of the traditional heavyweights continues to serve as the benchmark for international footballing excellence.

Baiman’s group is responding to the threats from the Trump administration to target immigrant communities during the midterms. Dozens of volunteers are already patrolling near early voting sites in Columbus every day, Baiman says, and the figure will rise to around 250 on Election Day. They have been trained to document, record, and report any sightings of federal immigration agents, sharing the information in dozens of Signal and WhatsApp groups.

“We teach people that they have to observe the behavior. We show them what to look out for, what does ICE look like, this is the type of uniform that agents wear, these are the types of vehicles that they drive,” Baiman says. “We show them examples so that they can get familiar with what they might see.”

These efforts are being repeated in towns and cities across the country. WIRED spoke to a dozen people taking part in this effort, whose groups are working together to combat what they see as an existential threat to American democracy. Central to this response are training sessions run by massive national groups like Common Cause, the American Civil Liberties Union, and Indivisible, as well as local groups like Living United for Change in Arizona and NorCal Resist, that have created massive groups of people who can document and record what ICE is doing, understand voters rights, and in many cases, prepare for worst-case scenarios.

Officially, the Department of Homeland Security has said that it has no plans to deploy ICE agents to polling locations on Election Day. “ICE is not planning operations targeting polling locations,” a DHS spokesperson tells WIRED, but added: “ICE conducts intelligence-driven targeted enforcement, and if an active public safety threat endangered a polling location, they may be arrested as a result of that targeted enforcement action.”

For the past six months, President Trump and his allies have declined to rule out possible deployments to voting locations during the midterms, even though they would be illegal under federal law. When asked in May if he would deploy the National Guard or ICE during the midterms, Trump said: “I’d do anything necessary to make sure we have honest elections. We have to have honest elections.”

“Why are there objections to sending ICE officers to polling places?” Todd Blanche, then acting attorney general, said in July. “Illegals can’t vote.” The White House did not respond to requests for comment.

Some election experts simply don’t believe it will happen: “ICE and troops will not be at the polls,” David Becker, a former Justice Department trial attorney who is now head of the Center for Election Innovation and Research, said at a recent media briefing. “It won't happen. But for organizations on the ground interacting with voters in communities that have been targeted by ICE during Trump’s second term in office, the reality feels very different.
