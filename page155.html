The Texans had a chance to head into their bye week on a three-game winning streak, but they couldn’t score any points to open overtime against the Giants and their run defense broke down as the visitors rolled to a game-winning touchdown by former Texan Devin Singletary.

#452. NASA’s Perseverance Offers Clues to Protect Crews From Mars Dust

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Over 1,645 Martian days, NASA’s Perseverance Mars rover measured the fine and ultrafine Martian dust particles found clinging to samples of spacesuit materials that are carried aboard the rover, including some that are small enough to penetrate the lungs and enter the bloodstream. The findings will be published Tuesday, Oct. 27, in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences and could inform spacesuit and habitat design, dust-removal procedures, and crew health standards for future NASA missions to Mars.

“NASA has a long history of turning challenges into workable solutions and of finding creative ways to use the data that we have to answer questions beyond what it was originally collected for,” said Lindsay Hays, acting director, Planetary Science Division, NASA Headquarters in Washington. “Dust has been a surface-exploration challenge since the Apollo program. Perseverance is giving our engineers and flight surgeons measurements they can use to improve our existing designs well before the first American astronauts arrive on Mars.”

Lunar dust scratched visors, degraded outer spacesuit layers, and damaged their seals during NASA’s Apollo missions, and some crew members reported symptoms similar to hay fever.

Rounded by wind-driven collisions with other particles, dust grains on Mars are less abrasive than those on the Moon. They also are enriched in sulfur, chlorine, and iron and contain more silica than lunar dust. Regional and global dust storms on Mars can keep the grains recirculating throughout the atmosphere as well.

The rover carries a set of five samples of spacesuit material. The swatches, each three-quarters of an inch (20 millimeters) square, are part of a calibration target used to test the settings of SHERLOC (Scanning Habitable Environments with Raman and Luminescence for Organics and Chemicals), an instrument on Perseverance’s robotic arm. The calibration target for another instrument that provided the dust measurements, called PIXL (Planetary Instrument for X-ray Lithochemistry), carries a sample of Teflon and of basaltic glass.

Because Teflon contains no elements that PIXL can directly detect, whatever elemental signals the X-ray spectrometer picks up from that sample comes from dust resting on it.

The team also analyzed swatches of Teflon and Vectran, two materials used in spacesuits, on SHERLOC’s calibration target. NASA sent the swatches to Mars to study how spacesuit materials hold up on the planet’s surface.

“Perseverance’s main job is to search for signs of ancient microbial life, but the mission was also designed to help prepare for human explorers. That effort includes technology demonstrations like MOXIE (Mars Oxygen In-Situ Resource Utilization Experiment) and Ingenuity, as well as the spacesuit samples,” said Katie Stack Morgan, Perseverance’s project scientist at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory in Southern California.

While earlier measurements on Mars resolved dust particles down to about 3 micrometers across, PIXL detected two categories of even smaller particles: fine particles measuring less than 2.5 micrometers (a human hair is about 70 micrometers wide) and ultrafine particles around 100 nanometers (about 700 times thinner than a human hair). Particles on PIXL’s Teflon target and SHERLOC’s Vectran swatch averaged about 2 micrometers. On PIXL’s basaltic glass and SHERLOC’s Teflon swatch, dust particle sizes ranged down to about 100 to 300 nanometers.

Static electricity appears to determine which size dust particles stick to which surface. Dust grains become charged as they collide in the air, and different materials on the calibration target attract grains of different sizes. The finest particles stayed attached through years of driving and drilling. Organic polymers like SHERLOC’s Teflon spacesuit sample are common in the outer layers of spacesuits, and the results show they readily collect fine dust.

The study also found that some materials hold less dust than others, which could guide future choices of materials and protective coatings for spacesuits and habitats. Designs such as suitports, which let astronauts enter a suit attached to the outside of a habitat, and dual-chamber airlocks could reduce how much dust gets inside astronauts’ living quarters.

“The existence of Mars dust at these ultrafine sizes has been predicted for a long time, and PIXL gave us the chance to measure it directly on Mars,” said Scott VanBommel, a Perseverance scientist at Washington University in St. Louis and the paper’s lead author. “The larger clumps, the Martian dust bunnies, get shaken off over time. But the smallest particles stay behind, and those are the ones that pose the greatest challenge for human support systems.”

Chemically, the dust matches the iron- and sulfur-rich composition that earlier missions measured. The PIXL instrument also found the dust to be enriched in phosphorus and detected signs of calcium sulfate.

On Earth, airborne particles, or particulate matter, below 2.5 micrometers (that is, fine particles) are tied to a range of negative health effects because they can penetrate deep into the lungs and enter the bloodstream. Studies have shown that ultrafine particles (those no larger than 100 nanometers) can travel from the nasal cavity to the brain along the olfactory nerve.

Martian dust is composed predominantly of silicate minerals and carries chemically reactive compounds, including perchlorates that can disrupt thyroid function and reactive iron that induces cellular damage. Scientists believe that the health risks of Mars dust depend on the quantity of dust and length of exposure.

The PIXL results are the first on-site measurements of fine and ultrafine particles on Mars, and this research can inform broader efforts to refine indoor dust-exposure limits for astronauts, develop safe habits, and establish ways to remove dust while exploring Mars.

NASA is already working on solutions to extraterrestrial dust hazards, using ground test facilities and lunar missions as proving grounds for future Mars explorers. In March 2025, the agency tested an Electrodynamic Dust Shield, which used electric fields to clear lunar regolith from glass and thermal radiator surfaces on the Moon. Developed at NASA’s Kennedy Space Center in Florida, it landed on the Moon aboard Firefly Aerospace’s Blue Ghost Mission 1. At NASA’s Johnson Space Center in Houston, engineers at the Lunar Development and Test Facility use vacuum chambers and lunar regolith simulant to test spacesuit components, mechanisms, and a Handheld Lunar Electrostatic Dust Mitigation tool ahead of Artemis missions to the lunar surface.

Managed for NASA by Caltech, JPL built and manages operations of the Perseverance rover on behalf of the agency’s Science Mission Directorate as part of NASA’s Mars Exploration Program portfolio. NASA JPL also built PIXL and SHERLOC.

#786. Samsung TVs might use the mics of connected devices to listen to you, researcher says

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

If you own a Samsung TV without a built-in microphone, it might still be listening to you, according to a researcher who shared his findings this week.

On the social media platform X, cybersecurity researcher @log4j shared that an investigation into Samsung smart TVs found that televisions without a mic will scan for connected devices that are equipped with a microphone. The smart TV will then utilize the audio data collected from the soundbar or other connected Bluetooth devices.

"This is pretty evil and clever," @log4j said about their discovery.

The researcher recently gained administrator root access to their own 2025 Samsung smart TV and has been sharing their findings in a series of posts on X.

Your smart TV is likely listening to you. At this point, that shouldn't be a surprise. Many televisions advertise this functionality, since your TV has to be listening to you to take voice commands and interact with other smart home devices.

However, other unadvertised and concerning privacy issues are arising from the device's ability to listen in.

For example, @log4j started looking into their TV after receiving oddly specific advertisements and found that their television was logging things like "dog barking" and "baby crying" under an event called EV201/SOUNDSENSOR_ON.

As NotebookCheck.net points out, while Samsung 2025 Vision AI TVs advertise that the devices detect barking dogs and crying babies and send alerts through the company's SmartThings app, Samsung does not advertise that this data is also sent back to the company.

Researcher @log4j also found that Samsung TVs track what happens inside specific apps. For example, the TV monitors what a user does in the YouTube app and even logs data such as the ads a user skips.

Mashable has reached out to Samsung for more information about the reports from @log4j and will update this piece when we hear back.

These privacy issues are not unique to Samsung TVs either. Last month, Mashable covered a report from YouTuber Gamers Nexus, which claimed to find that LG TVs were secretly recording users even when the televisions were turned off. LG later responded, denying some but not all the claims.

By IAN HERBERT, DEPUTY CHIEF SPORTS WRITER

Published: 17:00 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 17:48 GMT, 26 October 2026

Well intentioned though they might have been, there was a deadening familiarity about Harry Maguire’s soundbites after Sunday’s latest Old Trafford catastrophe, amplifying a sense of Manchester United being stuck in a godforsaken failure loop.

Maguire’s ‘look in the mirror’ and ‘take accountability’ and ‘wearing this shirt’ were the kind of words he and Luke Shaw have been trotting out for years. It’s white noise, now. None of this stuff makes the blindest difference.

But it was by the law of unintended consequences that Maguire revealed how Michael Carrick, a nice guy in a fix, simply doesn’t exude the authority required to command United. ‘Michael turned our season on its head last season,’ the defender observed after the 3-0 defeat by Bournemouth.

Never, in the days when United were a force in football, would the manager be described as ‘Alex’ or, decades earlier, ‘Matt’. Sir Alex Ferguson and Sir Matt Busby simply wouldn’t tolerate that kind of familiarity.

Judging by the names emerging as possible next inheritors of this most poisoned chalice – Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann – there is no appreciation that monumental authority, self-assurance and powers of persuasion are required here, and that this is not just another turn in the annual game of managerial swings and roundabouts.

United require a manager capable of instilling calm in the madhouse, standards where they are missing, and fashioning an environment in which no one would dare contemplate acts of vicious mutiny from the likes of Bruno Fernandes. The less widely appreciated point is that United require a manager capable of inspiring the kind of players the club need to join this ship.

The words of Harry Maguire (above) in support of Michael Carrick gave away a familiarity between players and manager that Sir Alex Ferguson, for example, would never have tolerated

The next manager must be able to dazzle and impress targets as well as have authority within the Old Trafford dressing room. Carlo Ancelotti, a stellar competitor, fits the bill

United have cut player salaries hard within an extremely constricted budget under Sir Jim Ratcliffe and Ineos and the name of Manchester United, starved of the biggest trophies for so long domestically and on the European stage, no longer carries the old cachet.

The next manager must be able to dazzle and impress targets. With the greatest respect, Howe and Nagelsmann just won't do.

There has been a sense for weeks now that Carlo Ancelotti is the individual United have needed all along. A stellar competitor who has lifted five Champions League trophies and league titles in five countries, but who has ridden the rollercoaster for long enough to brush off the kind of vicissitudes which have destroyed so many at United.

He does not emit a lot of visible heat or light. There’s little of the rage from him in the technical area we see in Jose Mourinho, whose Real Madrid side’s victory in Sunday’s El Clasico led some to suggest he still has what United need. But the most combustible players certainly know who’s boss when Ancelotti's around.

Zlatan Ibrahimovic tells of the ‘Ancelotti eyebrow’, which put in an appearance on a bitterly cold night at Evian, during the player and coach’s time together at Paris Saint-Germain. As Ibrahimovic tells it, the pitch was terrible and PSG were not playing well.

‘At half-time, you always know when something is going on because that one eyebrow goes up. As I sat down I thought, “Now he’s angry”.

'There was a box in front of him and from nowhere he kicked it and it flew through the air and hit me on the head. “Jesus,” I thought. “Now he’s really angry”.’

Ibrahimovic adored Ancelotti. So did David Beckham, another who knows, from their time together at PSG, that the Italian can explode at times. ‘Scary,’ he called it.

Ancelotti with Zlatan Ibrahimovic in their PSG days. The Swedish striker was full of admiration for his manager

Does Eddie Howe really have the charisma or clout of five-time Champions League winner Ancelotti?

A book discussing the Italian's methods, which Ancelotti co-wrote with Mike Forde, his former sporting director at Chelsea, was entitled Quiet Leadership and in his preface he referenced Vito Corleone and The Godfather.

‘When you watch Corleone, do you see a weak quiet man?’ he wrote. ‘Or do you see a calm powerful man, in charge of his situation?

'My approach is born of the idea that a leader should not need to rant and rave or rule with an iron fist, but rather that their power should be implicit. It should be crystal clear who is in charge, and their authority must result from respect and trust, rather than fear.’

At Chelsea, he flourished in the face of bedlam. Roman Abramovich, the owner, suggested Ancelotti favoured English players, demanded he be ‘tougher’ with the dressing room and, on one occasion, summoned the manager to his London apartment for a 'dressing down’ after a 6-0 victory against West Bromwich Albion. He shrugged all this off.

When Abramovich demanded that Ancelotti ‘find a playing identity for the team’ he put Michael Essien in the playmaker role, to immense effect, and took the team to a Premier League and FA Cup Double in 2009-10.

‘He brought the best out of us and made us feel 10-feet tall,’ John Terry said of Ancelotti. ‘You know his training is excellent because he’s coached the best teams in the world – but it was the personal touches which lived with me.’

Ancelotti is coach of the Brazil team, of course, and it would be costly to extract him from that post, though there was always a sense with him of unfinished business in the Premier League. He was disappointed when Abramovich sacked him after two years and coveted the Liverpool job which Jurgen Klopp got instead.

Ancelotti led Chelsea to a Premier League and FA Cup Double in 2009-10 despite the meddling of Roman Abramovich

Ancelotti is coach of the Brazil team but there is a sense with him of unfinished business in the Premier League

The prospect of helicoptering around Howe and Nagelsmann, who would both be receptive to United, or Antonio Conte, whose temper would make him a disaster at the club, feels rather like Groundhog Day – taking United back to the dither of 2024, when they stuck with Erik ten Hag, despite the availability of the proven, far better Thomas Tuchel, whom they’d actually interviewed at that time.

Ancelotti or Howe to get a tune out of an imbalanced squad that has been patched up with cheaper options and, given the limited money available in January, will be little improved on? It’s obviously no contest.

In Ole Gunnar Solskjaer's time as manager from 2018, assistant manager Mike Phelan used to shout the word ‘standards’ down the corridors at the training ground and that’s what United need restoring again now.

More immediately come fixtures against Chelsea, Roma, Aston Villa, Liverpool, Sporting Lisbon and Brentford. It will require no little personality and authority to deal with that lot.
