By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 22:30 GMT, 26 October 2026 | Updated: 22:30 GMT, 26 October 2026

Claudio Braga expects Derek McInnes to face a hostile reception when he returns to Tynecastle on Wednesday night – a ‘consequence’ of leaving Hearts for Rangers four months ago.

Last season, the 55-year-old took the Gorgie men to within three minutes of a first Scottish title since 1960.

After just one season in the capital, though, he was head-hunted by his former club as Danny Rohl’s replacement and joined Lawrence Shankland and Cammy Devlin in moving along the M8 to Govan.

While Braga is adamant that the players who McInnes previously managed still have the utmost respect for him after they almost rewrote Scottish football history, the Portuguese can well understand that supporters may see it differently.

‘Yeah, I believe so. And I understand a little bit why,’ he said.

‘Of course, we shared the locker room with them and we know them personally. And I couldn’t just hate them just because they left the club. That’s what a football player professionally needs to do. That’s their choice. I just could never judge that.

‘But I believe it will be a strong atmosphere for them. That was their choice. So now, of course, they have the consequences. And they knew that, I think, when they left.

‘But yeah, I think our focus needs to be on the game and not on the backstage.’

Leaders of the Premiership from the autumn until the closing moments of the campaign, McInnes’ Hearts side came as close as anyone to breaking the Old Firm’s stranglehold on the big prize since Aberdeen in 1985.

As a result of sharing a campaign which still contained far more highs than lows, Braga insists McInnes is still held in high esteem by the squad he left behind.

‘Personally, I really like Derek,’ said the forward. ‘I have nothing bad to say about him, Shanks or Cammy. I shared one amazing year with them and really love them as people. So, of course, it will be good to face them, but I have nothing but love for them.

‘I feel like we had such a wonderful year, even though, of course, it ended the way it ended.

‘When it’s a bad year, maybe the relationships won’t be that strong. But a season like that, you just literally become a family. And, of course, the bonds are created. And I hope we do the same this year.’

Wouter Vrancken’s men got back to winning ways at Motherwell on Sunday with Braga and Calvin Miller netting the goals which sealed a first league victory for the side outside of Edinburgh since January.

The triumph was chalked up despite a lengthy injury list but Braga contends that the adverse circumstances in which Hearts find themselves is fuelling a siege mentality.

‘Every time you look, you just lose somebody,’ he said. ‘But in a way, if we go on a pitch like we did on Sunday and fight for each other, it doesn’t matter.

‘Of course, it’s hard when you see so many injuries and it’s hard to lose players that are used to playing every week. And then you just need to adapt, get a chemistry with new players. But at the same time, we can’t just have excuses.’

After losing to Celtic and Trabzonspor, Braga felt that Sunday’s victory arrived at just the right time.

‘Yeah, of course,’ he said. ‘Just bouncing back from these last two results, especially the one in the league, was important.

‘We stuck together, fought as a family and I feel that matters a lot. Because playing good is going to happen, but some days you won’t. You need to play dirty and we fought as a family and we got the win.’

Over the course of 2026, the Autoblog spy photographers have spotted numerous heavily camouflaged development mules for the facelifted Range Rover SUV, and their latest sighting calls into question the extensive concealment methods JLR employed. See, previous prototypes have worn plenty of cladding, particularly on the front end, disguising the grilles and intake openings, but this mule appears to have production-ready bodywork, and it’s almost identical to the current design. Ultimately, this shouldn’t be much of a surprise since the all-electric Range Rover was recently unveiled with looks that closely mirror the existing combustion Range Rover, but there’s another possibility.

As a core product, it makes sense to leave the refreshed Range Rover’s styling mostly alone, and the more revealing bodywork here suggests the only significant front-end changes will be the headlights. Then again, JLR has been camouflaging parts of this vehicle that appear to be staying unchanged, and the Range Rover EV doesn’t have a new lighting signature either. Elsewhere, we see chunkier, wider side skirts, with the rear bumper swelling to match.

The current Range Rover was first introduced in 2022, so it’s starting to age. However, the SUV has been consistently popular worldwide, and changing too much might turn some traditionalists away, so JLR is doing just enough to ensure that the keen-eyed can spot the styling updates while the average person instantly recognizes the SUV as a Range Rover. Inside is where we expect more obvious changes, with a new driver information display and a revised center touchscreen, possibly connected to each other under one curved panel. Under the hood, the current powertrain lineup is expected to undergo similarly mild updates, with more efficiency and power likely the only major changes. If this is an SV we’re looking at, that means a 4.4-liter BMW-derived V8 (currently producing up to 607 horsepower). Elsewhere in the range, a 3.0-liter straight-six with 395 hp and a PHEV version with 542 hp should carry over too. We’ll bring you more info as we get it, but the new Range Rover SUV is now only expected to be available for the 2028 model year, which means a reveal will happen late in 2026 or in the first half of 2027.

#2968. Thunder star Jalen Williams could miss remainder of season after breaking leg, dislocating ankle

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Oklahoma City Thunder star Jalen Williams will need to undergo surgery for a fractured fibula and dislocated left ankle, OKC general manager Sam Presti said Monday. An exact return timeline won't be known until after surgery, Presti said, but ESPN reported that it could be a season-ending injury. Presti disclosed Monday that Thunder guard Cason Wallace will also need surgery. Wallace hurt his shoulder against the Clippers on Sunday and will undergo a procedure to repair a torn labrum. Wallace, too, could be out for the season, per ESPN, but the Thunder had no official timeline on his potential return.

"Tough night last night. There's no way to paint that differently," Presti told reporters.

Williams was taken off the court on a stretcher late in the fourth quarter of a 108-101 win over the Los Angeles Clippers on Sunday night after suffering the severe lower leg injury.

With just over a minute to play, Williams drove to the basket and tried to go up for a dunk. He was sandwiched in mid-air by Kris Dunn and Derrick Jones Jr., which caused him to lose his balance. When he landed, his left leg got trapped underneath the weight of his entire body and the lower part of it appeared to break in the wrong direction.

The Thunder training staff immediately covered his leg with towels as his teammates looked on in distress. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander pulled his own jersey up over his face as Williams' leg was placed into an air cast and he was loaded onto a stretcher.

"I just feel bad for him. Nothing more than that," Gilgeous-Alexander said on the court after the win.

Thunder coach Mark Daigneault said the team had no immediate update on Williams' status.

"We're not doing great," Daigneault said. "That's tough on the guys."

Williams gave the crowd a thumbs-up as he was wheeled off the court.

Williams, 25, emerged as a true star in 2025, when he made his first All-Star appearance and earned a spot on the All-NBA Third Team and the All-Defensive Second Team before helping Gilgeous-Alexander lead the Thunder to their first title since moving to Oklahoma City. Williams averaged 23.6 points in the 2025 Finals and had a playoff-career-high 40 points to lead the Thunder to a Game 5 win that gave them a 3-2 series lead over the Pacers (OKC won in seven games).

He played through a wrist injury during the 2025 playoffs, however, and underwent surgery last summer to fix the problem, which kept him sidelined for the first 19 games of last season. Less than two months into his return, he strained his right hamstring and was sidelined for multiple weeks. He came back, played two games, then tweaked his hamstring again and missed another six weeks.

Williams returned shortly before the playoffs last season, but suffered a left hamstring strain in Game 2 of the Thunder's first-round series with the Phoenix Suns. He sat out the rest of that series and missed the entire second round before coming back for the Western Conference Finals against the Spurs. After a stellar Game 1, he tweaked his left hamstring again in Game 2 and only played 10 more minutes the rest of the series.

When the Thunder lost Game 7 to the Spurs, which ended their repeat quest, he was forced to watch from the bench in street clothes.

The Thunder made some moves around the margins this offseason -- most notably, trading Lu Dort to the Atlanta Hawks -- but brought back their entire core and hoped to make another run at the title in a top-heavy Western Conference. Vegas oddsmakers installed them as the preseason favorite to win the title, and they started the season with a convincing win over the Spurs.

Though he was off to a somewhat slow start, Williams was averaging 13.7 points, 4.7 rebounds, 3.3 assists and one steal to begin the season, and was second on the team in scoring behind Gilgeous-Alexander. CBS Sports ranked Williams as the 20th-best player in the NBA in the annual Top 100 player rankings.

Sunday's win over the Clippers moved the Thunder to 3-0, and even without Dort, they have the depth to withstand Williams' absence -- at least in the regular season. They won 64 games last season largely without Williams. But it will be tougher to withstand Williams' absence with Wallace joining him on the bench.

A fourth-year guard, Wallace had worked his way into the OKC starting lineup. He made All-Defense Second Team last season while averaging 8.6 points per game. His emergence was one of the reasons why the Thunder felt comfortable trading away a veteran like Dort. Wallace had started in all three games this season before the injury.

The gruesome nature of Williams' injury and the reactions it elicited from teammates reminded fans of Gordon Hayward's injury nine years ago.

Hayward, like Williams, dislocated his left ankle and fractured his left tibia on opening night of the 2017-18 season in his debut with the Celtics. He missed the remainder of that season and was never the same player. Hayward appeared in 318 more games over six seasons before retiring at 33 years old.

#4929. Canada’s Draft Stablecoin Rules Cap Redemption Fees at 1% and Ban Yield

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Canada’s Draft Stablecoin Rules Cap Redemption Fees at 1% and Ban Yield

Canada proposed rules on Saturday that would force companies issuing stablecoins—crypto tokens designed to hold a steady value, usually one U.S. dollar—to keep real money behind every coin and hand it back on request. The draft, published in the Canada Gazette, the government's official newspaper, also keeps a ban on paying holders interest, otherwise known as yield.

For anyone who holds these tokens or might send money abroad with them, the rules decide whether you get real cash back if the company behind the coin collapses, how fast it arrives and what it costs.

Every coin must be backed one-to-one by reserves limited to cash and government debt maturing in three months or less, along with money market funds (pools of short-term safe assets) holding only such assets.

Those reserves must sit with a Canadian custodian in separate accounts that creditors cannot reach if the issuer goes bankrupt. Holders could redeem any amount within three business days, with fees capped at 1%.

Issuers could not pay interest for simply holding a coin, or route it through third parties: any arrangement that lets someone earn yield from holding it, including by sharing the issuer's income, counts as indirect payment. Rewards tied to using a coin, rather than holding it, would stay allowed.

“These prohibitions are intended to distinguish stablecoins from financial products that compete directly with bank deposits or other interest-bearing instruments,” the notice says. Issuers could still keep what their reserves earn, as long as reserves stay at least equal to the coins outstanding.

Issuers also could not present a coin as a bank deposit or claim deposit insurance or a government guarantee, and complaints would need acknowledging within seven days and resolving within 30.

The Stablecoin Act became law on March 26, 2026, after Ottawa announced the plan in its November budget. The Bank of Canada will register and supervise issuers.

“There is a pressing need for faster, cheaper, more transparent and more accessible cross-border payments services,” Ron Morrow, the Bank of Canada's executive director of payments, supervision and oversight, said at an Ottawa conference in September 2025.

In the United States, President Donald Trump signed the GENIUS Act, the U.S. stablecoin law, on July 18, 2025, and also bars issuers from paying holders interest.

The fight since then has been over letting crypto platforms offer yield to users, which banks oppose because they say deposits could drain toward crypto. The dispute was divisive enough that it helped lead to the failure of the Clarity Act in the Senate, a separate piece of legislation meant to set broader rules for the crypto market.

The banking lobby opposed the Clarity Act on grounds that it did not sufficiently close what they viewed as a loophole in the GENIUS Act, which currently allows for platforms such as Coinbase to offer yield to customers who hold USDC stablecoins in their accounts.

As the stablecoin debate now proceeds to the United States’ northern neighbor, penalties would reach 1 million Canadian dollars for serious violations to 10 million for “very serious” ones, such as failing to hold adequate reserves or redeem at par value. The notice assumes five issuers, and about 30 more payment companies, including crypto platforms that hold customers' coins in wallets, would fall under Bank of Canada oversight.

Ottawa is taking comments for 60 days, until roughly December 23. “The intention is for the proposed Regulations to come into force in fall 2027,” the notice said.
