#3254. The Sixers are stuck in a holding pattern at backup center

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Well, here we are again, talking about backup center as a problem for the Philadelphia 76ers. You know how the saying goes, the more things change, the more they stay the same.

It was predictable, yet at the same time, who saw it coming this quickly? In case you haven't seen the splits by now, so far on the season the Sixers have a 112.3 offensive rating when Joel Embiid is on the floor and prior to Sunday's loss to Detroit, the team was averaging only 92.8 points per 100 possessions with Embiid off the floor.

So, now what? Philly is again in need of a backup center and Ariel Hukporti isn't magically going to be available. Players who signed free agent contracts this past summer are eligible to be traded on Dec. 15. By then, there are probably some more willing parties to negotiate with Mike Gansey on a trade. December is also when they could theoretically sign a veteran big still on the market on a prorated deal.

But the big question that we all have to ponder is if the Sixers can afford to wait just shy of two months before making a trade for another big man. There's two paths that the Sixers can choose between now and the middle of December. They could opt to be more aggressive with Embiid's minutes and overall availability than they would have if Hukporti was healthy. That seems unlikely. Perhaps the more plausible scenario would be Philly sitting on its hands and waiting it out, not altering Embiid's minutes at all, losing some more games than anticipated early in the season, and making a trade when the NBA calendar is more trade-friendly. Are you willing to sacrifice your playoff seeding for this, Sixers fans?

Maybe the better question is, do the Sixers even have a choice here? This is where whiffing on Johni Broome, and Adem Bona not developing into anything more than he already is become particularly costly. Yes, those two players were recent second-round picks by the Sixers, and on some level all second-rounders are lottery tickets, but it's also not impractical to want your second-rounders to become serviceable bench players.

If you're unhappy with the status quo behind Embiid at the moment, maybe your only hope is that whenever Dominick Barlow gets healthy, he can be a functional small-ball 5. But again, is that a tenable option behind Embiid for what would be about 15-20 games at the bare minimum before more frontcourt help was brought in?

Philadelphia has not shot the ball very well from the three-point line to start the season. That could always be a remedy here as well. If the Sixers can have some lights-out shooting games, they won't need a paint presence for the minutes Embiid isn't available. Sure, maybe they'll allow opposing centers to have big nights inside, but any team would trade 2s on defense for 3s on offense.

On some level, it feels like we're just spit balling a bunch of scenarios and coping mechanisms here because it's all we can do as we go through another season in which we're routinely wondering what's going to happen when Embiid isn't on the floor.

It is interesting how much the narrative has ballooned up and down surrounding Embiid in the last 2-3 years. A little over one week before he suffered a brutal knee injury in January 2024, Embiid had a 70-point game against Victor Wembanyama and the San Antonio Spurs. He came back from the knee injury and had a 50-point playoff game against the Knicks that spring. Then he won a gold medal in the Olympics only to have the knee injury plague him for all of the 2024-25 season. Just when it looked like he was becoming one of the worst contracts in the NBA, he went off against the Celtics in the 2026 postseason. We're now again talking about how much he is missed when he's not on the court again.

Sadly, it hasn't seemed to matter who is running basketball operations for the Sixers or who the head coach is. The same problems come up regarding frontcourt depth behind Embiid. Right now, it doesn't seem like there are any buttons for Gansey to push. Anything you get from Tacko Fall or other players you could bring in on two-way deals is a hope and a prayer.

But at this point, it looks like that's all they can do until the middle of December.

#4879. 27-Year-Old Missouri Man Admits Role in Failed Bitcoin Kidnapping Plot

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

A St. Louis man pleaded guilty in Hartford to conspiracy to commit Hobbs Act robbery over a failed August 2024 plot to steal bitcoin from a Connecticut family.

A St. Louis man has admitted his part in a violent scheme to steal bitcoin from a Connecticut family. Martel Williams, 27, pleaded guilty Monday in federal court in Hartford to conspiracy to interfere with commerce by robbery, commonly called Hobbs Act robbery.

The charge carries a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison. U.S. Attorney David X. Sullivan announced the plea.

The plot began in August 2024. Prosecutors say the people coordinating the attack wanted bitcoin belonging to a man who had taken part in a theft worth hundreds of millions of dollars. To get it, they recruited Williams and two associates from St. Louis.

The crew was supposed to force its way into the target’s home, threaten him, and demand that he move the cryptocurrency into accounts the conspirators controlled.

Williams and others traveled to Connecticut between August 21 and August 24, 2024. They rented vehicles and gathered supplies, including air rifles, walkie-talkies, and a laptop computer. For two days, they stalked the target and his parents, waiting for an opening to get inside the house.

The opening never came. Williams and his associates feared they had been captured on home security cameras. They also grew frustrated with the plan and with a lack of communication from their coordinators. They left Connecticut without carrying out the job.

Shortly afterward, a second crew of six men from Florida arrived to carry out the scheme again. On August 25, 2024, Danbury police arrested those six men after a violent carjacking. The men beat and kidnapped two people inside the vehicle. Those two victims were the parents of the target.

Local outlets have tied the target to a bitcoin theft in the range of $230 million to $245 million, though the exact figure varies from report to report. The federal case itself centers on the attempted robbery, not the original theft.

Federal agents arrested Williams on July 17, 2026. He remains free pending sentencing, and no sentencing date has been set. The FBI New Haven Violent Crimes Task Force, FBI Los Angeles, FBI St. Louis, and the Danbury Police Department investigated the case. Assistant U.S. Attorneys Karen L. Peck and Stephanie T. Levick prosecuted it.

Other defendants in the Danbury case have also pleaded guilty, according to local reporting.

Earlier this month, a scammer who had stolen about 60 bitcoin (roughly $4.8 million) through a fake-review task scam lost it all when thieves emptied three Ledger addresses where the scammer had moved the proceeds on October 9.

Blockchain analysts at Chainalysis said the transfers went into one wallet reported as stolen, while the wider Ledger incident, in which a criminal group allegedly implanted seed-phrase-reading devices sold through Malaysian reseller CryptoBilis, may have drained about $86 million. Ledger said it was working with law enforcement and has confirmed no other vendors were targeted, following a July attack on Coldcard that cost users close to $120 million through a firmware bug.

#4883. Polymarket Has Investigated 'Dozens' of Midterm Election Bets: Report

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Polymarket Has Investigated 'Dozens' of Midterm Election Bets: Report

Polymarket has investigated "dozens" of bets on its midterm election markets for possible insider trading and manipulation, the company's head of global intelligence and investigations, Shana Bautista, told NPR.

At least two of those investigations have been referred to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, NPR reported, citing a person familiar with the probes.

Bautista, a former FBI official and crypto analyst who joined Polymarket in June, said her team of 16 is policing election bets with a new surveillance system ahead of the November 3 vote.

Her team monitors both Polymarket's U.S.-regulated exchange, which requires users to verify their identities, and Polymarket International, where users can trade anonymously with crypto. Even so, Bautista said crypto trades are rarely fully anonymous. "There's never been a case that we work where we haven't been able to identify, potentially, who these people are," she said.

The system pulls in data from social media, Federal Election Commission filings, crypto companies, banking partners and statistical models, and alerts Bautista when a user trips enough internal red flags. "We're getting hundreds of alerts a day," she said. She declined to share the specific criteria, citing system security.

Election markets are especially vulnerable, Barnard College economics professor Rajiv Sethi told NPR, because they "have the possibility of being a self-fulfilling prophecy," with perceptions of a candidate's momentum able to influence the result.

Kalshi, Polymarket's main rival, launched its own FEC-based system in May to block campaign staffers from betting on elections. In June, it added employment disclosures for high-risk markets and a risk-scoring framework, saying it had opened more than 150 investigations that year and referred more than 20 cases to law enforcement. One campaign staffer told NPR they had found a way around Kalshi's block.

Election insider trading drew scrutiny in May, when campaign staffers told NPR they had made "thousands" betting on their own candidates using tips about unreleased polls. "Because you have all this information and knowledge that isn't publicly available yet, it's almost foolish not to bet on it before it's made public," one said. Jeff Le Riche, a former CFTC trial lawyer, told the outlet that such trades "could potentially be a violation" of the law.

Polymarket partnered with blockchain analytics firm Chainalysis on market surveillance tools in April, days after an active-duty U.S. Army soldier was charged with using classified information about the raid to capture Nicolás Maduro to bet on Polymarket. The soldier, who has pleaded not guilty, allegedly placed more than $33,000 in bets and won nearly $410,000. Polymarket said it detected the activity and reported it to authorities before the arrest.

NPR reported that the CFTC has done little to police prediction markets, largely leaving the work to the companies. In September, though, CFTC staff warned that "mention markets," contracts on whether a named person says certain words or attends an event, should be presumed readily susceptible to manipulation. The advisory came three weeks after the agency fined a teleprompter operator who traded on speeches he had already read.

#324. The 5 best handheld gaming consoles, tested and compared: Switch 2, Steam Deck, MSI Claw 8

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When reviewing the best handheld gaming consoles of the year, we have to start with one brutal fact: the memory crisis has made handheld gaming a lot less fun. Some handheld gaming consoles are now pushing $1,500 or more, and games are more expensive than ever, too. We won't go so far as to say that the AI-fueled crisis has killed handheld gaming, but the price of entry can be pretty intimidating. Given this fact, we believe the Nintendo Switch 2 is the best handheld gaming console available in 2026. However, we also tested the latest handheld gaming PCs from Lenovo, MSI, and Xbox/ROG.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

Thanks to the AI industry hogging all the memory components, gaming consoles are getting more expensive. In the past 12 months, Nintendo, Microsoft, and Sony all raised prices on their gaming consoles, and Windows laptops are seeing the same increases. It's not a good time to be a gamer. Even before the wave of price increases, handheld gaming PCs were already expensive. The Lenovo Legion Go 2 launched last year for $1,099, but costs $1,999 today. The Legion Go S with SteamOS was originally available for $749.99, but you'll have to pay $999 today at Best Buy — and that's with a $580 discount.

The Steam Deck has also been beset by price increases and has periodically gone out of stock. The Switch 2 has also gotten more expensive, though it's by far the most affordable option in the category.

So far, only one handheld gaming console has held steady at its original price — the ROG Xbox Ally X. We don't know how long it will continue to be available for $999, but we're not complaining.

When purchasing a handheld gaming PC, you generally have three options: Nintendo, Windows, or SteamOS. The right answer comes down entirely to personal preference. If you're a PC gamer who primarily buys games through Steam, then go with a Steam Deck or Lenovo Legion running SteamOS. If you're an Xbox or cloud gamer, then the Windows 11-based MSI Claw 8 or ROG Xbox Ally X will be more your speed.

Read our full review of the Nintendo Switch 2.

Those who want to stay on the cutting edge of handheld gaming consoles need to get a Nintendo Switch 2 at some point. It's the latest and greatest version of the handheld, with more power, improved features and accessories, and a constantly-expanding slate of games that'll almost certainly induce FOMO if you don't hop on the bandwagon. If you're excited about playing the long-awaited The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake, love all things Pokémon, or have a kiddo that needs to be introduced to the joys of Mario Kart, then the Switch 2 is a must-have.

The Nintendo Switch 2 is finally here, and it's unsurprisingly great. The new console has been eight years in the making, and many improvements have been made since the last generation of Switch consoles. And even after Nintendo raised the price by $50 in September, it's still less than half the price of other handheld gaming devices. For all these reasons, the Switch 2 is easily the best handheld gaming console for most people.

Nintendo went with the "make it again, but better" approach with the Switch 2. The concept is the same, but here, you'll get more processing power, massively improved features and accessories, and an upcoming game release calendar that looks promising. (The Switch 2 also allows for backward compatibility with your favorite Switch games, with some games even getting upgrades for the new console.) The handheld is bigger than its predecessor, but still thin and light, so it feels great to hold.

The new display, while a visual step down from the Nintendo Switch OLED, is still really good. It has HDR support, a 120Hz refresh rate, and a nice 1080p resolution within the 7.9-inch LCD panel. Still, we look forward to the inevitable OLED upgrade that's sure to come out in a few years. One of the best hardware upgrades, though, comes in the form of the new Joy-Con controllers, which snap onto the Switch using magnets instead of the original's sliding mechanism. They feel way better to attach and detach, and they're also a bit bigger, which helps with comfort a bit (although they still aren't that ergonomic for extended play sessions). They're also more responsive and have better button push feedback. It remains to be seen if Joy-Con drift will be a problem in the future, but we'll keep you posted. Older Switch controllers, including the Pro controller, can be used wirelessly with the Switch 2, which means you don't have to blow a bunch of money on new accessories if you don't want to.

In terms of software features, we've fallen in love with GameChat. It lets you join an online party to talk with your buddies, but doesn't require a headset, which means multiple people in one room can experience the chat as much as the player. It also has a livestream function that lets you share and watch gameplay with others in real-time without having to pause.

While we think the Switch 2 is pretty marvelous, some things could be better. For starters, pricing. The console is expensive, but that's not exactly the problem — it's all the stuff you have to buy after you get the handheld that can quickly become frustrating. Want to buy a Switch 2 camera? That'll be $54.99. New Joy-Con? Get ready to fork over $94.99. There's also the Pro Controller for $84.99, and the slate of Nintendo Switch 2 first-party games that range from $69.99 to $79.99. The Switch 2 is also exclusively compatible with microSD Express cards, so if you want to expand that 256GB of internal storage, you'll have to buy one of those instead of using your old Switch's microSD card. Those aren't cheap, either. Finally, battery life is just good, not great. We knew it'd be marginally worse than the original Switch as a sacrifice for more horsepower, but two to six hours on a single charge isn't anything to write home about, especially when we regularly got an average playtime of around three hours before needing a recharge.

While it has its hang-ups, the Switch 2 is a worthy upgrade to one of the greatest gaming consoles of all time, and one you need to get if you loved the original handheld in any capacity.

Read our full review of the Valve Steam Deck OLED.

The original Steam Deck was unlike any device that came before it, bringing the traditionally less-accessible PC gaming experience to a streamlined and impressively powerful handheld. And when the Steam Deck OLED arrived in 2023, it delivered big upgrades that were irresistible to a lot of gamers. Once upon a time, you could buy this handheld for just $399, but today, it will set you back $789, or $999 if you get the 1TB version, which you should. That's a 98 percent price increase, and it hurts.

Still, there's no denying that the Steam Deck OLED is one of the best handheld options for PC gamers. So, if you feel like you have PC gaming FOMO, the Deck will finally fix that.

The Steam Deck OLED is a bit dated compared to the latest MSI and Lenovo handhelds, but it's undoubtedly the best handheld for PC gamers who have a long list of titles in the Steam library. Likewise, if you want to get into PC gaming without getting a gaming laptop or custom PC rig, this is your best bet.

Using Steam's built-in OS, the Steam Deck OLED gives you access to the entire Steam Store library (including your own account, if it's already attached to a PC rig). Not every game in there is compatible with the Deck, but Steam kindly labels the ones that can run on it within their storefront — and the selection is enough to keep you busy for a long, long time. From there, you can install games directly onto the device and start playing.

The power of the Steam Deck is remarkable. We had multiple "pinch me" moments as we booted up demanding games like Elden Ring, Uncharted 4: A Thief's End, and God of War, and experienced zero performance issues. And now that the handheld is equipped with an OLED display, we're even more impressed by this device. (Seriously, playing Red Dead Redemption 2 on a handheld for the first time was surreal.) We also appreciated the Deck's desktop mode, which basically turns it into a pint-sized computer that you can use for web browsing and more. If you're patient enough, you can even program it to boot up non-Steam launchers like EA Origin and Ubisoft Connect, but it's a complicated process that requires some Linux know-how. It's a downside for those who want an all-encompassing PC experience with no limitations, but there's still so much here to enjoy that it's not a dealbreaker by any means.

"Valve didn’t just transfer the same display specs from [the] LCD model into an OLED form. Instead, the screen is better in almost every way. Its 7.4-inch size mildly improves on the 7-inch dimensions of the original (while retaining the same 800p resolution). Crucially, the original model’s 60Hz top refresh rate has been expanded to 90Hz, enabling gameplay of up to 90 frames per second for games that support it," wrote our reviewer Alex Perry in 2023.

PC gaming has traditionally been inaccessible to a large swathe of people because of the steep financial investment and need for constant troubleshooting. While the price advantage is no longer relevant, the Steam Deck is still able to democratize PC gaming for all players, and to us, that's a gigantic feat.

Read our full review of the ROG Xbox Ally X.

When the ROG Xbox Ally X first came out, we noted that it was expensive compared to the competition. But what a difference a year makes. While every other gaming handheld has gotten a price increase, the ROG Xbox Ally X is holding steady at $999.99. Now, to be clear, that doesn't make it cheap, by any means. However, it's definitely worth noting that it's overall value has only gone up since it was first released.

If you're an Xbox or cloud gamer, then the Xbox-branded handheld from Asus ROG is a natural choice or alternative to the Xbox Series X/S. You'll be able to use this handheld to play Grand Theft Auto: VI when it comes out (finally), as well as Xbox titles you already own and games on Xbox Game Pass. The hardware is impressive, and you can use the built-in Copilot features to get an instant AI assist if you get stuck in a game.

As our reviewer wrote, "Overall, the ROG Ally X will feel very familiar to gamers in the Xbox camp, in a good way. The device even has a fingerprint reader, adding a layer of security while providing an easy way to jump right back in and play...I love the ergonomics of the ROG Xbox Ally X, and I'm sure Xbox gamers will agree. #3264. Where does Mohamed Diawara fit in the Knicks’ rotation?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

A hangover can be a sobering experience. A lot of people know that. One moment you are on top of the world, the life of the party, drinking and dancing in the middle of a crowd to a song that seemed like it was written just for you. And then all of a sudden you wake up and feel like a reborn "Night Train" Lane is using your head as a tackling dummy. That upcoming brunch with the not-so-close relatives doesn't seem that enticing anymore, does it?

You might have heard that your beloved New York Knicks are dealing with something similar these days. They sure have been told once or twice. Actually, if they could get a dollar for every time someone brought up the famous movie title in relation to their recent basketball exploits, they might soon be able to play for good ol' Jimmy for free. That's what you get for starting your title defense with no wins in three games: you will hear about it. With that said, some Knickerbockers definitely have it a little worse than others.

While the main crew is stumbling through the club and trying to dissect the poor-tasting regular beverage of a long, grinding season, one of last year's heroes hasn't even been invited back to the party yet. What it is exactly that's keeping Mohamed Diawara on the pine is anybody's guess, but there surely is something that doesn't sit well with head coach Mike Brown. It's not the sneakers or the baggy shorts, so much we can say.

It seems as if Diawara really hasn't fully recovered from hitting the rookie wall late last season—both physically and mentally. After surprising everybody by clearly outperforming his late draft status, he got lost in the shuffle later in the year. As his earlier unwavering confidence wavered, he more often than not looked like a shaken gallic rooster in the headlights. With every subpar outing, it seemed to creep deeper into Mo's head, as he looked visibly frustrated at times. But even if he wasn't part of the playoff rotation, many in Knickland were still confident that the guy who locked up Jaylen Brown in Boston and drilled triples in Wemby's face at the Garden would have a significantly bigger role going forward. The Knicks apparently agreed, giving him a new four-year contract in June.

So far these expectations haven't been met. Like at all. An injury-shortened, bad summer league and an average-at-best preseason didn't exactly get the hype TGV rolling again. Physically, Diawara certainly looks bigger, but transforming your body can be quite a challenge for a young player, especially when the added pounds take away from the nimble movement skills you once possessed. Judging by the few glimpses we got of Mo in the summer, it seemed that he wasn't exactly confident in his new skin yet as a player. Understandable but unfortunate nonetheless.

What makes his stagnating development especially frustrating for New York is that the young Frenchman, in theory, is exactly the type of talent their roster could use right now and going forward. Outside of their starters OG Anunoby and Mikal Bridges, the Bockers really do not have any long wings at their disposal, and it becomes an even more glaring circumstance when you consider that their lineup features a lot of smaller guards to begin with.

The Knicks worked around it admirably during last year's playoff run, even if it didn't make sense at times. Three-guard lineups with Jalen Brunson and Jose Alvarado somehow worked against a vaunted Spurs defense in the Finals, Landry Shamet turned into some super sub on his minimum deal, Jordan Clarkson impersonated Dennis Rodman off the bench, and no Knick ever really got exposed physically that much. It was awesome to see, but somehow it also felt like the stars aligned just right for a play here, a game there, then even a series and a run. Matchups favored the Knicks and they managed to hide their shortcomings perfectly at just the right time.

But can all of that happen again? Can we trust the rather smallish roster to hold up for another year without opponents finding the obvious problems with it? It might, but having a better balance on the depth chart would also be a welcome sight. Championship teams often struggle when they just do the things they did a year ago and expect things to also just go exactly the same. In terms of a roster and a team, you need a certain level of disruption, some new impulses to keep the group humming, interested. C'est peut-être Monsieur Diawara pour les Knicks de New York.

Diawara can push the vets in front of him a little bit, and he could challenge their inner mentor. Some veterans tend to be more engaged when they have younger guys looking up to them, guys that force them to be role models in practice and on the court. I'm sure our beloved and extremely professional Knicks aren't slacking in that regard, but if a youngster is out there in the real games with them, there is certainly another dimension that could make them focus a bit more themselves.

Make no mistake about it: Mo Diawara has to earn his minutes. He has to be a gain, not a distraction. Limited history suggests, though, that if the Knicks are serious about his development, they might want to try him rather sooner than later with their regular rotation. Diawara has always looked like a guy who thrives with better teammates, carving out and excelling in a limited role as a defensive, three-point-shooting specialist. The gained confidence from another opportunity could unlock him again and send him back in the right direction. Which in turn would be a win for the entire team. For all the hope that Mo might expand his game immediately after his rookie year, he and his trainers might have jumped a step or two, who knows. That doesn't change the fact that he is a player who might add something the team lacks right now.

You don't have to let him run the bar immediately. But a little time on the dancefloor, maybe a song or two, could be a good idea. At least we could see if he still has some moves.
