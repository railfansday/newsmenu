#323. Indian court orders release of two leaders of Cockroach movement

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka spent a week in jail after defying restrictions on protests in Delhi amid government crackdown

An Indian court has ordered the release of two leaders of India’s Cockroach movement who were jailed last week amid a mounting state crackdown on youth-led protests.

The two leaders of the Cockroach Janta party (CJP) movement, Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka, had spent a week in Delhi’s Tihar jail after being arrested by Delhi police last week.

On Wednesday, a special magistrate ordered that Das and Ranka be released, despite efforts by police to keep them in prison. They were made to sign a bond pledging no further breach of peace.

Their arrest came after a draconian police crackdown on a CJP march held in the capital two weeks ago to protest alleged manipulation of the country’s electoral roll, which led to the barricading of central Delhi and thousands of young people being detained.

Das and Ranka were taken into custody for a second time days later after they jumped police barricades and held up an Indian flag at a site in central Delhi that police had cordoned off.

The pair were charged with trespass and violating the peace and sent to jail for five days, which was then extended by two further days. Their detention had provoked outrage among leaders and supporters of the youth movement, who accused the government of “cowardice” for jailing CJP leaders for holding a peaceful protest.

After the magistrate’s ruling on Wednesday evening, Das said that “truth has triumphed”.

The release of the two leaders is likely to come as a blow to the ruling Narendra Modi government, which has implemented an unforgiving crackdown on recent protests that have erupted across India.

The CJP, alongside the opposition alliance, student groups and civil society, have been calling for the resignation of the chief election commissioner, Gyanesh Kumar, who is accused of overseeing a revision of the electoral roll that benefits Modi’s Bharatiya Janata party (BJP) and has led to more than 130 million names being deleted from the voter register. The BJP has denied any interference.

The CJP began as an online satire earlier this year, but has since grown into a fully fledged political movement, challenging the powerful Modi government. Over the summer, it was Cockroach-led protests that forced the resignation of Modi’s education minister after sustained demonstrations.

Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of the CJP movement, vowed over the weekend that the Modi regime and threat of jail would not intimidate the Cockroaches. Dipke was also detained on Friday after he called for a peaceful demonstration in central Delhi where CJP supporters would gather and wave the Indian flag in solidarity with Das and Ranka.

He had said that, once his fellow Cockroach leaders were released, they would “gather, re-strategise, and determine how to move forward”.

“The illegal arrests and detentions we have witnessed over the past few days indicate an attempt to intimidate us,” said Dipke. “But we will not be cowed down or retreat. This is a long battle and we are prepared for it.”

#4760. Toyota Land Cruiser Recall Sees Over 105,000 J250-Gen SUVs Dealing with Possible Fuel Leak, Fire Risk

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The automaker has conducted a years-long investigation into the issue, and now they’re looking to bring back the vehicles for a fix where needed.

Toyota has issued a rather strange recall for the 2024-2027 Land Cruiser. It also seems rather comprehensive; the recall population numbers 105,794, which is nearabout the total number of Land Cruisers sold in the U.S. so far for the current J250 generation.

The issue stems from a retaining clip that connects fuel tubes from the gas tank to the engine. Specifically, there’s a resin fuel tube on the tank that connects to a main metal fuel tube, the latter of which delivers gas to the engine. Toyota says that if the retaining clip on the connector between the two is lifted, there’s a possibility of a fuel leak—and of course, a fuel leak means an increased chance of fire.

Toyota conducted a years-long investigation into this issue, starting all the way back in July 2024. You can read the full timeline of events here, but we’ll summarize the six-page document for you.

Toyota became aware of the issue when field reports noted the retainer clip holding the fuel tubes together was lifted or missing. How this happened was vigorously investigated, but Toyota found no issues in the part or its manufacturing processes. One theory suggested in the report is that some recovered connectors showed evidence of markings that could’ve been caused by someone disconnecting the tubes after manufacturing. Additionally, other markings seen on certain clips were consistent with prior supplier testing.

When it comes to the fire risk, Toyota says it evaluated the temperatures of nearby components, finding all of them below the ignition temperature of fuel.

The most recent testing from Toyota this year attempted to identify how the retainer clip at this connection could become lifted. None of the tests provided the information Toyota was looking for; that said, among 400 field vehicles where no connector disconnects had been alleged / leaks reported, Toyota identified one with a lifted retainer clip. Despite the clip being lifted, that one vehicle was not leaking fuel.

Toyota ultimately decided to conduct this recall based on its fuel leak volume evaluation, and the number of field cases it observed of the clip being lifted. As of this recall, Toyota is aware of 23 field reports and 64 warranty claims that relate to the issue. With the cause and circumstances around how the issue manifests, Toyota is unsure what percentage of vehicles might contain this defect.

The fix is relatively simple. Dealers will inspect the connection and install a supplemental clamp. If necessary, they’ll replace the resin fuel tube, too. Notifications to owners are expected to occur by December 13 this year, and per federal law, all recall work will be completed free of charge.

#4827. WWII fighter plane crash found in Hungary after nearly 80 years

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Published Oct 21, 2026 3:05 PM EDT

After nearly 82 years, historians in Hungary have finally located a missing Soviet aircraft downed by a German ace pilot during World War II. After a monthslong investigation that involved painstaking archival dives and eyewitness interviews, a team led by Hungarian Aircraft Wreck Research Association co-founder István Kocsis pinpointed the Yakovlev Yak-1 fighter plane. The aircraft was buried over 17 feet underground roughly 50 miles southeast of Budapest. Despite the poor condition of many of the plane’s components, the recoverable remnants still included portions of the aircraft’s fabric covering and even some of the pilot’s personal items.

“Discoveries of this aircraft type on such a significant scale are extremely rare,” a member of the Hungarian Defense Forces said during a recent press conference announcing the find.

The crash dates back to November 1944, after Soviet Guards Lieutenant Alexander Semyonovich Andrianov encountered German Lieutenant Peter Düttmann above the town of Törtel. Unfortunately for Andrianov, Düttmann was already infamous for his dogfighting abilities, and the Nazi pilot eventually shot down his Soviet foe. Although Andrianov successfully ejected with his parachute, he was later taken prisoner of war. Although Andrianov’s fate is unclear, historians estimate roughly half of all Soviet servicemen taken as POWs died while in German hands.

The Yak-1 fared even worse. Constructed from composite materials like steel tubing, wood, and even fabric, the crash destroyed the aircraft and immediately scattered the wreckage. With no one to claim it, the plane slowly sank into the region’s particularly loose soil over the next eight decades.

Much of the plane remained intact underground, but military archaeologists chose to rely on a recovery strategy called block lifting. Instead of excavating at the site, heavy machinery portioned out hefty chunks of earth that the researchers then carefully cleared one layer at a time. This layer-by-layer method allowed them to identify and preserve numerous smaller pieces of wreckage, including the engine serial number and construction identification that matched Andrianov’s Yak-1. The team also exhumed some of Andrianov’s personal belongings, including a book signed by the pilot.

While much of the wreck was heavily deformed from the crash’s impact, restoration experts are confident they can reconstruct many of the Yak-1’s parts. Once preserved and documented, these sections of the plane will go on public display at Budapest’s Military History Museum while the rest remain safely archived.

If you're like me, you've spent years ignoring most of Siri's functionality. Sure, it's great for setting timers, making calls, and sending a text. But that's kind of it—Apple's promise of an "always-on" digital assistant never truly delivered.

But that's finally changed with the recent launch of Siri AI. Fifteen years after debuting the app on the iPhone 4S and four years after the release of ChatGPT, Apple has taken the time to build out its own private Apple Foundation Models (with little help from Google) that work both on-device and on Apple's servers to give quick responses to your queries. Siri AI's answers are short, to the point, and actually useful. However, the conversational side isn't the only new thing about Siri AI. In fact, once you scratch the surface, you'll find Siri AI features embedded across the entire iOS and macOS ecosystem. Here's how to get the most out of Apple's new, enhanced digital assistant.

Siri AI is already tuned for brevity. Compared to Gemini or Claude, its responses are short and to the point. But still, if you ask something with your voice, it responds using voice as well. I personally prefer to quickly read through Siri responses. If you’re like me, you can enable a hidden setting that makes sure that Siri always answers silently, no matter if you’re writing to it or using your voice. Go to Settings > Siri > Responses and switch to the Silent Response option.

On the other hand, if you prefer to listen to Siri's responses, it's worth it to spend some time fine-tuning them. If you have an iPhone that’s under two years old, you have access to a more expressive Siri voice. Siri AI has an Expressive Voice feature that works on iPhone 17 Pro and higher, iPhone Air, and Macs with an M3 processor or higher (at least 12GB RAM).

To find this feature, go to Settings > Siri > Voice. Here, you can change the Pace to make Siri faster or slower. The big feature is the Expressivity slider; you can use it to make Siri sound brighter and more enthusiastic, rather than flat. You also have several voice options at the top (color-coded).

Visual Intelligence is deeply integrated into iOS 27 and macOS 27, and it turns anything on your screen into a source that Siri AI can act upon. It can search anything on your screen, and even take action on things it finds out. You can highlight any text on an iPhone, tap and hold, and choose the Ask Siri option. On the Mac, Ask Siri is the first option in the right-click menu. Choose a file, select text, and get started talking to Siri.

But it goes further. Just pull up Siri AI on your iPhone or Mac and start asking questions about what you’re seeing on screen. If you’re seeing a photo of a cafe on Instagram, ask where it is, or ask Siri to open it in Maps. If you’re watching a scene from a movie or a TV show, ask Siri where it’s from.

Clean Up was part of Apple Intelligence features in iOS 18, but it wasn’t great. It used on-device models to remove objects, and the results were lacking. #490. The political ad wars are still being fought over the airwaves even as digital gains share

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

While advertising dollars increasingly shift toward streaming and digital platforms, the political ad war is still being fought over the free airwaves.

Broadcast television companies are set to capture the greatest portion of political ad spend ahead of the midterm elections, according to advertising intelligence company AdImpact.

The 2025-26 election cycle is forecast to be the most expensive on record, with a predicted $11.6 billion in total spending. That's higher than the $11.2 billion spent on advertising during the 2024 presidential election cycle and the $8.9 billion spent during the 2022 midterms.

An estimated $5.6 billion of advertising spend ahead of the midterms, or 48%, is expected to go to broadcast companies, AdImpact found.

"It's still a local game," said StoneX Equity Research Analyst Daniel Kurnos in an interview. "Yes, there are national elections, but you have to win counties. And if you have to win counties, you have to reach voters in these counties."

Broadcast audiences — those tuning into NBC, ABC, CBS, Fox or their local affiliate stations — are generally more politically active, Kurnos said: "You cater to the demos that vote. You cater to the votes that matter."

That makes political advertising one of the last bastions of broadcast's dominance.

While broadcasters are expected to capture roughly half of political advertising spending this cycle, they account for just 7.7% of the more than $470 billion in general advertising spend forecast for this year in the United States, according to Magna data published by S&P Global in July.

Terry Nelson, a founding partner of Plus Communications and the national political director for former President George W. Bush's 2004 reelection campaign, told CNBC that broadcast spots allow political strategists to reach large numbers of voters "as efficiently as we can from a cost perspective."

Though cord-cutting has reshaped the TV landscape as customers drop cable, broadcast remains free and broadly accessible.

Broadcast, which transmits local news, sports, classic game shows like "Wheel of Fortune" and national networks, captured more hours of television use than cable in July, according to Nielsen's most recent monthly streaming report, The Gauge. The category accounted for 19.5% of television use, while cable captured 18.7%. Streaming led television use by far, at 49%.

Broadcast is still particularly good at delivering high viewership during live sports games, Nelson noted.

"If you think about an NFL audience, for example, you're still going to deliver big audiences amongst large segments of the population," he said. "So no matter how you dice that demographic, whether it's men, women, or old people, young people, or whatever the case might be, the NFL game is still going to deliver large segments of all of those audiences."

Brad Seitter, CEO of TVB, a trade organization representing the local broadcast industry, boiled down broadcast's strength during election cycles to "trust, reach and because it works."

Seitter told CNBC in a statement that television's perceived reliability is of utmost importance for political campaigns.

"Gaining the trust of a voter equals a vote for your candidate or issue," Seitter said.

He also said that he believes broadcast does a better job of promoting political candidates and issues than some digital channels.

"You may see a political ad on social media, but you don't trust the content of the ad because users are conditioned to not trust what they hear or see on social media," Seitter said. "Conversely, viewers trust their local broadcast TV news, so when they view a political ad, they are more likely to believe the content of the ad on a platform they know has long-standing rules, regulations and trust."

Seitter said that political campaigns continue to place advertisements on traditional TV because there is clear evidence that they are influential on voting behavior.

"Nothing moves the needle in polling like running an ad on broadcast TV," Seitter said.

According to a study conducted by TVB, 42% of Democrats and 49% of Republicans said broadcast or cable television was the most important form of advertising media in influencing how they cast their vote.

Erik Brydges, head of performance and political demand at sell-side ad company Magnite, told CNBC that broadcast's perceived influence is tied to the long history of the platform.

"There are multiple studies out there that show a correlation between the number of points you buy on TV in relation to moving poll numbers, because the medium's been around for like 70 years," he said.

And yet Brydges said he believes that broadcasting's strength is also due, in part, to muscle memory.

"A lot of campaigns and a lot of the consultants who work on the campaigns have just been really used to buying TV," Brydges said. "There's an old adage out there that no political consultant is ever going to get fired for suggesting buying more TV."

Broadcast companies are already feeling the windfall from elevated advertising spending.

Publicly traded broadcasters, including Nexstar Media Group, Gray Media, Sinclair and E.W. Scripps, each reported high political advertising revenue in their most recent fiscal quarters and forecast continued growth through the fall as the midterms inch closer.

StoneX's Kurnos told CNBC that broadcasting companies rely on the low-lift revenue from political advertising to help manage their debt.

"They have all the infrastructure in place for [standard advertising] already," Kurnos said. "And you don't need to work on the creative. You don't need to work on the selling. … Political is just, therefore, pure margin dollars."

Nexstar, which owns or operates at least 265 stations and a slew of websites and mobile apps, reported its highest quarterly revenue in the company's history. Net revenue hit $1.99 billion, and advertising revenue was $862 million, according to the August earnings report.

Overall revenue in the second fiscal quarter was largely driven by the incorporation of broadcast rival Tegna, which Nexstar officially acquired in March.

However, even discounting additional revenue from the merger, the company still reported fiscal second-quarter growth at legacy Nexstar that was primarily driven by $75 million of political advertising revenue, according to the company's most recent earnings call.

Gray Media, though operationally smaller than Nexstar, could also be a company to watch, according to Kurnos.

The company's stations operate in several markets with the most competitive races and thus the greatest ad spend: Gray has exposure in 12 competitive Senate races and all 11 competitive gubernatorial races, according to Kurnos.

"Gray has always been the most efficient political generator of the broadcast group," he said.

Sinclair and Scripps also reported political ad revenue as a bright spot in their earnings reports for the most recent fiscal quarter.

E.W. Scripps said that political ad revenue hit the highest second-quarter level ever, while Sinclair said that political ad revenue had grown 9% from the second quarter of 2022 during the last midterms season.

"No other medium delivers a political message as powerfully and reliably as broadcast television," E.W. Scripps CEO Adam Symson said on the company's second-quarter earnings call.

On the other end of the media spectrum, connected TV represents the fastest-growing category of political advertising spend, according to AdImpact. The category encompasses internet-connected devices like Apple TV or Roku.

It's predicted to capture 23%, or $2.7 billion, of this cycle's ad spending, AdImpact found, and is likely to continue to gain share.

Magnite's Brydges said the complicated and rapidly changing nature of connected TV, or CTV, and free, ad-supported streaming channels — also called FAST — has prevented some advertisers thus far from taking advantage of high viewership numbers.

"If you look at some of the FAST streaming services — some of your Plutos, your Tubis — a campaign or a consultant might not understand what those are," Brydges said. "Therefore, they might just be like, 'Well, if I don't understand what it is, then it's probably not worth investment.'"

However, he said he believes political consultants are becoming more attuned to the benefits that these platforms provide.

SKDK's Pia Carusone, who manages the firm's political advertising department, told CNBC in an interview that she sees value in streaming's specific audience targeting features.

"If you're buying NBC in New York City, for example, you're buying the whole market," she said of traditional advertising strategy. "Increasingly, now with the streaming and CTV platforms, they're able to really offer a lot more nuance in their targeting. So we're able to sort of deliver against those audiences more directly."

But ultimately, Carusone said, political advertising strategy relies on maintaining a diverse portfolio.

"You can't just do digital. You can't just do linear," she said. "Good buyers today are buying across the spectrum."

#3847. Why Your Smartwatch Size Really Does Matter

📅 Oct 21, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

“Is my watch too big?” is a constant question on forums dedicated to smartwatches and fitness trackers of all kinds. (Some, but fewer, wonder if their watch is too small.) Personally, I don’t much care how a watch looks, but I’ve noticed that size can make a big difference in how accurately a watch functions. If your watch is too big, your numbers may be off.

Watches measure heart rate through an optical sensor on the back of the watch. (That’s what you see glowing green back there.) The heart rate sensor detects how that light reflects off the blood vessels in your skin. To get a proper reading, it needs to be snug against your skin, ideally in a way that blocks out ambient light.

If the sensor loses contact with your skin, or if too much light sneaks in, heart rate readings may be inaccurate. If a watch fits you well to start with, you can generally get an accurate heart rate by simply making sure the watch is snug during workouts.

But if a watch is too big, it can move around, letting light in. When you run, if a watch is bouncing around, the sensor may detect a flash of light with every footstep. This can lead to your watch reporting a heart rate that is the same as your cadence (footsteps per minute), which is sometimes called “cadence lock.” Some runners will cover their watch with a sleeve (especially in cold weather) to minimize this possibility.

No matter how you wear it, a big watch on a small wrist is likely to have a lot more difficulty than one that fits properly. Very small devices can have a similar problem: The device isn’t large enough or can’t be tightened enough to block light. This isn’t usually a problem with wristwatches, but it may explain why smart rings tend to be so poor at daytime heart rate tracking.

Now, is this a real-life concern, or just a theoretical quibble? I wasn’t sure, until I took the Forerunner 265 and 265S for a run at the same time. These are the same watch, with the same heart rate sensor, differing only in size. You can read about my results here, but the graph tells the story pretty well: The smaller 265S (42 mm) captured my heart rate more accurately than the 265 (46 mm).

If heart rate accuracy were the only issue, I would tell you to get the smallest watch you can find. It wouldn’t even look weird; before smartwatches became popular, wristwatches were often much smaller than a person’s wrist. (Watch old movies and you’ll see some tiny watches, even on people who don’t have tiny wrists.)

But watches are big and seem to be getting bigger. A bigger screen means numbers, text, and icons are easier to read. This is great news if your vision isn’t the best anymore, or if you use the watch while exercising and want to be able to see what’s on your screen with a quick glance.

Bigger watches can provide bigger screens, but also bigger batteries and more room for other internals. This is probably at least partly why more “premium” watches come in bigger sizes. That said, it’s hard for me to distinguish when a watch is huge because it needs room to pack in its hardware features, versus when the company is making the watch bigger so it will seem fancier and thus justify a higher price.

And this brings me to something I need to rant about. Smartwatch and fitness watch companies know that watch size matters: They often offer two sizes for at least some of their models. The Apple Watch Series 12 comes in 42 or 46 millimeters, while the Garmin Forerunner 570 comes in 42 or 47 millimeters, to give a couple of examples.

But when it comes to premium models, those options often disappear. The Garmin Forerunner 970, far more feature-rich than the 570, only comes in a 47-millimeter size, matching the larger size of the 570 but without a smaller option. The Apple Watch Ultra 4 only comes in a 49-millimeter size, which is even larger than the larger size of the Series 12. I’m about to start testing a Hyrox feature that only comes on Amazfit’s Balance 3 or Balance Ultra watches; those watches are 51 and 52 millimeters, respectively. This is getting ridiculous.

As a woman reporting on tech, I can’t ignore the way gender plays into this. According to ANSUR II, a U.S. Army study, women have an average wrist size of 154 millimeters (about 6.1 inches), and men have an average wrist size of 176 mm (about 6.9 inches). My wrists are right around 6 inches, making me very average for a woman.

When companies make two sizes of watch, the smaller size usually fits the average woman’s wrist fairly well, and the larger size is a better fit for men’s wrists. This isn’t a perfect match, of course, since plenty of women may fit or prefer the larger size, and vice versa. I’m glad the companies market these as “small” and “large” or just by the numerical size.

But as a person with six-inch wrists, I can’t wear a large watch without dealing with bouncing, rubbing, and potentially inaccurate data. I’m no twig; I’m perfectly average for my half of the population. But those larger watches just aren’t going to be as accurate for me as they are for the average man. I noticed a similar phenomenon when I tested the heart rate tracking in Powerbeats Pro 2 earbuds: They barely worked for me, but did fine when I tried them on a man with larger ears.

There's also an uncomfortable intersection between this gender dynamic and the way watch companies use size as a signifier of how fancy a watch is supposed to be. While Garmin only offers the 970 in a large size (47 mm), they only offer the cheaper 70 and 170 in smaller sizes (both 43 mm). Garmin’s Venu 4 comes in both 41 and 45 millimeter sizes, but the cheaper Vivoactive 6 is only available in 42. Having a small wrist can save you money, but it still shuts you off from some of the more premium options out there.

For my work reviewing and testing watches for Lifehacker, I’ve worn smartwatches ranging from around 40 millimeters (an older Apple Watch) up to 48 millimeters (a tie between the Kospet Tank and the Amazfit T-Rex 3 Pro). I love a watch in the 40-42 millimeter range; I can tolerate one around 46 millimeters if it’s lightweight enough that it doesn’t bounce too much; and I really have a hard time with anything larger.

Those bigger watches are hard to snug up sufficiently for a workout; get them tight enough not to bounce, and they’ll feel like you’re nearly cutting off your circulation. After a long run with a big, heavy watch, I’ve sometimes gotten red, irritated spots on my wrist from the inevitable friction. You may not be able to judge how badly a watch will fit when it's still in the box, but I do have a rule of thumb for when you try it on.

To judge visually, I find it helps to put a watch on your wrist, rest your arm on a table, and look directly down at it. If the lugs of the watch extend past your wrist, it’s too big. You want the watch to fit on your wrist, not overhang the sides.

And if you prefer a watch size that isn’t going to be the most accurate for heart rate, remember that you can always pair a chest strap to a watch that supports it, or to your phone if you don’t have a compatible watch. If you prefer a larger watch for more readable text or maps, or if you want a premium feature that doesn’t come in smaller sizes, a chest strap may be the way to get the best of both worlds.
