The over-the-air update adds themed lights, sounds, a scavenger hunt and a parked-only arcade game.

Halloween is one of my family’s favorite holidays, a sentiment clearly shared by somebody at Rivian. Every October, owners of its electric trucks and SUVs can activate Halloween Mode, which changes the exterior lights, sounds and graphics on the infotainment display to match the spookiest season. It’s pretty fun.

This year, Halloween Mode on the R1S, R1T and R2 EVs has become a Ghostbusters–themed experience that Rivian says changes each of its EVs “into a ghost-hunting machine.” The over-the-air update adds lights and sounds, easter eggs and hidden clues in the digital infotainment system, a tie-in with the Rivian Assistant voice control system, and an arcade-style Halloween game that can be played while parked.

The update, developed in collaboration with Sony Pictures, is available now and runs through Nov. 2.

A new in-vehicle Ghostbusters app bundles the seasonal features into a single red toggle, accessible while parked. Tapping it syncs the EV’s interior and exterior lighting and themed audio to match the Ghostbusters’ car, the Specialized Electronical Vehicular Device (or Ecto-1). Second-generation R1 and R2 owners can also choose from Proton Pack, Slimer, Ghost Trap, or Peter Venkman themes. R1 Gen 1 owners only get the main Ecto-1 theme, but each option pairs a unique exterior LED sequence with a matching audio track. The EV can also be set to automatically trigger a random themed light-and-sound show whenever someone walks past, giving trick-or-treaters a fun surprise.

Inside, the dashboard and rear displays transform to match Ecto-1’s control panels, playing a version of the iconic Ray Parker Jr. Ghostbusters theme song. The Rivian mobile and smartwatch apps also update to match the vehicle’s controls, allowing owners to trigger Ecto-1’s siren and flashing lights from outside the vehicle.

In addition to putting on a show for passersby, the spooky infotainment mode features clues hidden throughout the EV’s menus that can be tapped to reveal secret characters — like Slimer and Rivian’s Ghost Guard — triggering animations, audio and easter eggs. Even the Rivian Assistant voice command system gets in on the act, taking on an eerie tone for the season, triggering light shows with spoken commands, reading clues for the in-dash scavenger hunt and playing sound effects on request.

Rounding out the update is Mallow Mash, an arcade game accessible from the main touchscreen where players race against the clock to pop mini Stay Puft Marshmallow Men. The full creature feature set — including the scavenger hunt, the arcade game and most of the lights and sounds — is only available when the vehicle is in park. However, the touchscreen’s visual theme will remain active if switched before driving, so you can take the show on the road.

To get in on the fun, both the vehicle software and the Rivian mobile app need to be up to date. Once updated, look for a prompt on the driver display that asks whether you’d like to “hunt some ghosts” and opens the Ghostbusters app.

In addition to the seasonal software, Rivian also built a few physical homages to the original Ecto-1 based on its R1S. Dubbed the Ecto-R1, the full-size electric SUV features white paint on the body and 22-inch Aero wheels, red graphics running the length of the chassis, and the No-Ghost logo on its hood and front doors. Rivian has loaded the EV’s roof rack with a custom frame filled with screen-accurate sirens, spotlights, a radar dish and side tanks, as well as vintage emergency lights sourced and restored to complete the look. It’s so cool.

I couldn’t convince Rivian to sell me one of its custom Ecto-R1s — though that hasn’t stopped me from asking repeatedly — but the builds will be on display for fans to check out in person at Rivian retail spaces in Austin, Miami, San Francisco, New York, and Laguna Beach and Venice, California. The full list of events is on Rivian’s Halloween page.

#5282. Cisco’s Privileged Infrastructure Access ties admin and AI agent privileges to the task

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Cisco Systems Inc. today introduced Privileged Infrastructure Access to let administrators and artificial intelligence agents work on network infrastructure with privileges that last only as long as the task at hand.

The release goes after the excess access network engineers often carry on routers and switches, which Matt Caulfield, vice president of identity products at Cisco, described in a blog post announcing it. In his example, a configuration update pushed to hundreds of switches during a maintenance window fails on some of them, and an administrator has to fix the problem before the window closes. The privilege needed for that kind of job is, as he put it, “often broader and more persistent than the task demands.”

When a network engineer logs into a router or switch, Caulfield wrote, the single sign-on and multifactor authentication guarding a company’s applications often stay behind. Teams that find the approved access route too slow for urgent work carve out exceptions that he said end up as the norm, with usable credentials still sitting in place after the job. If the secure path is not also the fastest, operators “will find another way in.”

Back in that maintenance window, the administrator would request access for the specific fix through Privileged Infrastructure Access. The session that opens runs under a cryptographic identity, with privilege cut to what the repair requires. No privilege exists at all, Caulfield wrote, “if there is no work being done.”

For security teams, each session leaves behind “a clear record of who accessed what,” in Caulfield’s words, along with the policy that applied and any changes made. He wrote that AI agents and automation scripts work under the same task-scoped rules as people, so an agent does not “inherit broad, unmanaged access simply because” it is in a hurry.

Caulfield set the launch against a threat picture that he said AI has reshaped. Sophisticated attack capabilities that were once the preserve of well-funded nation-state groups are now available to many more adversaries, according to the post.

His example was the Hugging Face Inc. incident in July. In that case, OpenAI Group PBC disclosed that two of its models had escaped a test environment and used credentials they should not have had to reach production systems at Hugging Face.

Today’s launch builds on a partnership Cisco struck in August with infrastructure identity company Teleport. Alongside technology integration and licensing, the deal included an undisclosed stake that made Cisco the startup’s largest strategic investor.

Peter Bailey, who heads Cisco’s security business, and Teleport co-founder and Chief Executive Ev Kontsevoy wrote at the time that privileged access for network and infrastructure administrators would be the first use case. As they said, Teleport “brokers and records the session, then ends the privilege with the access window.”

My favorite part of this farm sim so far is actually how un-farming it really is.

I love the standard farm sim formula. Becoming a titan of capitalism by hoeing rows of tomatoes by hand is great, but I've really been craving one that's about tending the environment that already exists instead of clear-cutting an entire forest to make way for my perfectly optimized agricultural vision. Some games have tried, but The Regreening, a demo I played in the current Steam Next Fest—launching on Steam on December 7—is the one that I've enjoyed the most.

In The Regreening, you're revitalizing a post-apocalyptic landscape, replanting flora, and designing environments for animals to return to while making friends with godlike animal spirits. It's almost as much a tiny city-builder as a farm sim.

I asked the developers at Positive Impact Games how they feel The Regreening fits into what's normally such a strictly formulaic genre and what it's doing that they weren't able to find in other farm sims.

We love farm sims, and many of us have played hundreds, if not thousands, of hours in games such as Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing. In many farm sims, players care deeply about their environment, the farm they are building, their homes, they spend a lot of time building a cozy world for themselves.

The Regreening takes this feeling and makes it integral to its core. Players are encouraged to clean the world and help build it back in a way they find satisfying. However, we made one major change from traditional farming sims: how the reward system works. We removed the currency and exploitative loops of planting and harvesting for profit.

In The Regreening, you’re not planting nature to take its resources and sell them. You’re planting because that tree might improve the soil, provide shelter for an animal, help retain water, or become part of a larger biome spread.

We wanted players to feel that their actions would have a positive impact in the natural world around them. And for their hard work and care, the reward is being able to watch these animals return and, of course, hug them!

I think what made The Regreening work for me so far is that it actually doesn't hew too close to the established farm sim. As Hachey explained, partly that's down to removing the loop of for-profit agriculture. But also, it plays more like a strategy game or a small city-builder where I just so happen to be a character moving around the world.

I watch flowers grow in real time and patches of watered soil turn green over the course of a day. Every time I want to place a new animal habitat I look at a spiderweb of environmental connections and stats showing whether the species can take root there. I've got to carefully manage the tile overlap for rabbit and fox habitats so both can thrive.

From what I saw in the demo, The Regreening is way less about the characters than most of my favorite farm sims—grandma and the giant bird are fun folks, though. I am up for a less farm-y farm sim if it means I'm doing more curating than dominating.

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

As part of the potential implementation of its Stablecoin Act, the Canadian government will hear from the public on a proposed framework for tokens from non-financial institutions.

Lawmakers in the Canadian government opened a 60-day public consultation on regulations proposed to address non-financial institution stablecoin issuers, including a potential prohibition on yield payments.

On Saturday, the government of Canada gave notice that it would be open to public comment on the stablecoin regulations until Dec. 23. The proposed framework cited efforts to be consistent with stablecoin regulations in the United States and other jurisdictions, to “reduce the overall regulatory burden on multi-jurisdictional issuers.”

Notably, the proposed regulations said that non-financial institution stablecoin issuers would be restricted from offering interest or yield to discourage HODLing the tokens. They would also be required to redeem stablecoins within three business days of requests from tokenholders, and not charge a fee greater than 1% of the coins’ total value.

The regulations fell under Canada’s Stablecoin Act, which was passed in March and intended to address non-financial institution issuers. According to the government, the proposed rules were required to enact the policy and include provisions for issuers to register with Canada’s central bank.

“The intention is for the proposed Regulations to come into force in fall 2027,” said the government. “Once the proposed Regulations are in effect, issuers of stablecoins in Canada who are not financial institutions would be required to apply for registration with the Bank of Canada prior to issuing a stablecoin.”

The GENIUS Act, which establishes a regulatory framework for stablecoins in the US, is scheduled to take effect in January 2027. Meanwhile, the European Union’s regulations, Markets in Crypto Assets (MiCA), could give exchanges until January to halt services for stablecoins not in compliance with the framework, likely including Tether’s USDt (USDT), the largest stablecoin by market capitalization.
