Keeping track of all the upcoming releases from favorite artists can sometimes be a bit of a challenge. It’s not unusual for musicians to tease an album, then reveal a release date, only for the LP to be delayed for one reason or another (in some cases, multiple times), often without a new release date attached. Then there are albums that are announced mere days before they drop!

But we’ve got you! Musicians from across genres started teasing new projects and announcing album release dates for the new year as it barely started. And like the last several trips around the sun, we’re keeping a calendar to keep track of all the notable releases of the year.

One of the most highly anticipated releases was Hilary Duff’s Luck…or Something — her first album in 10 years — which dropped in February. The singer-actress first announced her return to music in September, when she revealed a new label deal with Atlantic Records, as well as new music and a docuseries that will follow her musical comeback.

BTS also gave its ARMY news to celebrate just days after the new year kicked off. On Jan. 4, the K-pop supergroup revealed that its highly anticipated next album — which comes after the members took a yearslong break as a group to complete mandatory duties for the South Korean military and focus on solo ventures — would arrive in March.

Not to be outdone, fellow K-pop supergroup BLACKPINK also announced its own new music in early January, with DEADLINE dropping in February. (Then in August, three of the ladies announced solo projects to arrive this year!)

Harry Styles then followed up BLACKPINK’s announcement the next day with his own album reveal. The pop superstar’s fourth studio album made its debut in March.

As winter moved into spring, women superstars not only teased, but announced highly anticipated albums. Among them? Madonna, Olivia Rodrigo and Ariana Grande, with all three projects arriving before the end of July.

As September rolled around, some big names in rock released their projects. Among them are Anthrax and Rage Against the Machine’s Tom Morello, while October will anticipate an album by Nickelback, as well as iconic industrial band Ministry, who returns with its final album. And before the end of the month, Miley Cyrus returned with Bass Persuades.

But the biggest surprise was yet to come. Taylor Swift revealed on Sept. 22 that she had a new song titled “Patient Zero” coming before the end of the month. Less than 24 hours later, she surprised Swifties with the news that not only was there a new track coming, but The Life of a Showgirl: The Encore, featuring not just one new song, but four.

That’s just several of the big releases of 2026. To help you stay on top of it all, we’ve organized this calendar chronologically by month and week, and have also included a section for albums that have been announced, but do not yet have a release date.

#1391. Serie A | Sassuolo 2-6 Lazio: Unstoppable Aquile hit six past 10 men

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Lazio are cruising at the top of the Serie A standings after thrashing 10-man Sassuolo 6-2, despite Stefano Turati saving two penalty kicks, after Kristian Thorstvedt saw red.

The Biancocelesti were joint top of the table with Inter going into this midweek round, but while Albert Gudmundsson, Adam Marusic and Nicolò Rovella were still out of action, Andrea Pinamonti returned to the bench following a throat infection. Diogo Leite allowed Oliver Provstgaard to rest. The hosts scraped just one point from the last four rounds, a 1-1 draw with Milan, missing Jay Idzes, Fedde Leysen, Ismael Koné and Daniel Boloca.

Lazio fans are boycotting the home games in protest at President Claudio Lotito, but this felt like their territory, as they practically outnumbered the home supporters.

It took under 120 seconds to break the deadlock, as Kenneth Taylor sent Gustav Isaksen down the right, who tried to roll across for David Frattesi, but the Luca Lipani interception accidentally laid it off for Taylor to sweep home from 14 yards. Frattesi was offside, but not interfering with Stefano Turati’s attempted save.

Play had barely resumed after the long VAR check when Sassuolo equalised, as Josh Doig rolled across from the left for Kristian Thorstvedt’s first-time finish, with potential deflection off Bosko Sutalo.

Lazio restored their advantage when Mattia Zaccagni shrugged off Lipani and cut inside from the left, finding Isaksen to score relatively undisturbed just inside the area, into the far bottom corner with the inside of the left foot.

They had the ball in the net for a third time just three minutes later, when Taylor’s low slide-rule pass from the left went past both Zaccagni and Tijjani Noslin, eventually tapped in at the back post by Romano Floriani Mussolini. His celebrations were cut short by the offside flag, but the Lazio youth product did manage to celebrate his first Serie A goal after a VAR check.

The last time four goals were scored in the opening 16 minutes of a Serie A match was Atalanta-Bologna in April 2019.

Noslin fired just over, while Christos Mandas almost paid for a hasty rush off his line to clear, as Darryl Bakola couldn’t find the empty net from midfield.

Sassuolo’s day went from bad to worse, as Thorstvedt was shown a straight red card for a leg-breaker tackle on Zaccagni, stamping on his ankle and shin, completely mis-timing the challenge.

Armand Laurienté had the ball in the net, but only after wandering offside on the long ball, and Sassuolo did get one back before the break with Simone Cinquegrano nodding back a cross from the by-line, Sebastiano Esposito controlled with his chest and fired in from 14 yards.

In first half stoppages, Isaksen floated a ball over the top to find Zaccagni unmarked for a free header from six yards.

#346. RAF Fairford plot ‘coordinated by handler working on behalf of Iran’

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Two men charged under National Security Act were directed by handler believed to have been working for Iranian intelligence, court told

A group of men were directed to drive vans loaded with petrol to Fairford RAF base by a handler known as Ali, who police believe was working on behalf of Iran, a court heard on Tuesday.

One of the five men tasked with driving to the base called police in the early hours of 27 September after the handler warned the group about a bomb in the vans, prosecutors told Westminster magistrates court.

Two men, Mohmed Abdulkarim, 24, and Nouman Essghir, 24, both appeared in court on Tuesday charged under the National Security Act 2023.

Abdulkarim, of London, was charged with assisting a foreign intelligence service. He is accused of buying the vans used in the Fairford incident on the instruction of Ali.

Essghir, also of London, was charged with entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the UK, after initially being arrested at the scene near the Gloucestershire base on 27 September and then re-arrested on 20 October. He is accused of visiting RAF Fairford and filming around the facility on a video call with Ali before returning a month later in the convoy of vans.

Abdulkarim has been released on bail under conditions including the surrender of his passport, and Essghir has been remanded in custody. They will appear at the Old Bailey on 20 November.

Prosecutor Louise Gray alleged the defendants were in contact with an individual known as Ali who is now believed to be abroad.

She said that during the events of 27 September at the RAF Fairford base, five men who were arrested travelled in vans, parked and got out “and were told to do so by someone known as Mr Ali”.

One of them, she said, had claimed they were told there was a bomb in the van and called 999.

The court heard that the person calling stated that they were from London and that he was one of a group of five males. The caller told the police that they had been tasked to drive vans to the location for the purpose of a new business venture. When the vans arrived at the location, the organiser was in communication with them over the telephone.

Gray alleged he then told them to park the vans and to walk away from them. After they did that, the organiser told them to run as he had planted a bomb in the van. The caller indicated that he and his colleagues had run away from the vans and were now calling the police for help.

The defendants are believed to have known Ali for “a number of years”, she said.

Essghir is accused of travelling to RAF Fairford on 22 August and contacting Ali.

Abdulkarim is accused of buying mobile phones and collecting cash for Ali, as well as buying five vans including the three that were driven to RAF Fairford.

The court heard he had known Ali for 10 years and it was claimed the latter had been linked to a drone incident at the Israeli embassy.

Counter Terror Policing said they believed the foreign intelligence service involved in the case was Iranian.

The assistant commissioner Laurence Taylor, head of national counter-terrorism policing in the UK, said: “We have seen a range of incidents in recent months that we believe have been directed by the Iranian state, and as the director general of MI5 said just the other week, we are working together to intervene, identify and respond to that threat.

“The public can help us with this, and as always I would urge them to report anything that they’re worried about – those reports can and do make a difference.”

A 25-year-old UK-Iranian dual national who was also arrested on 20 October on suspicion of aiding Iran’s intelligence service has been released without charge but remains on police bail after being arrested on 1 October on suspicion of preparation of terrorist acts.

Five other men arrested during the investigation remain on police bail.

Frank Ferguson, the chief crown prosecutor for the CPS special crime and counter-terrorism division, said: “The Crown Prosecution Service has decided to prosecute two individuals with National Security Act offences following a Counter Terrorism Policing investigation into a high-profile incident involving RAF Fairford. We will continue to work closely with our partners as this complex investigation continues.”
