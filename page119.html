#227. Is an Under-Sink Reverse Osmosis System Worth It? Benefits, Costs, and Drawbacks

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

That funny taste in your tap water may be a harmless nuisance—or it may be cause for serious concern. Either way, you can do something about it by installing a reverse osmosis (RO) water filtration system in your home.

Stand-alone RO systems and pitchers sit on your countertop and generally require manual refilling from the tap. You’ll save space and avoid the hassle of constantly refilling the unit by installing an under-sink or under-counter model that connects directly to your plumbing lines. Either way, RO systems aren’t cheap. But for some homeowners, they may be an invaluable investment.

Osmosis is the movement of molecules—usually water—through a semipermeable membrane that blocks larger particles like salts and sugars from passing through. After osmosis, the concentration of particles on both sides of the membrane ends up in equilibrium. It’s how plants get water from the soil, and it’s how your kidneys filter junk out of your blood.

While osmosis is a spontaneous phenomenon that happens naturally, reverse osmosis requires pressure to force water through a membrane in the other direction, effectively removing all of those compounds from the water to render it pure. It’s how salt is removed from seawater to make it drinkable, and it’s one way tap water can be purified in your home.

RO differs considerably from traditional filtration systems, which rely on physical barriers, typically activated carbon, to manually capture particles that pass through. Eventually, these filters become saturated with sediments and other matter and have to be replaced. While carbon filters are fairly blunt, able to capture larger particles of at least 0.1 microns in size, RO systems have a much finer-grained membrane that captures contaminants on a microscopic level (typically down to 0.0001 microns).

Carbon filters can make your water taste and smell better, but with RO you are getting much, much cleaner water than what that inexpensive Brita filter can muster. (That said, RO can’t capture everything, and carbon filters are still useful for capturing certain volatile organic compounds through adsorption. Most RO systems also have an additional carbon filtration system included.)

Note that two types of RO systems are available: Tanked RO systems filter water slowly (albeit passively, without electricity) and sequester it in a (rather large) tank under your sink. Today, tankless RO systems are more popular, processing water on demand but requiring electricity to keep things flowing expeditiously. Tankless systems are considerably more expensive due to their inherent complexity.

“US tap water is safe for most people most of the time,” says John Voll, an industrial water treatment veteran and the founder of Tap & Air, a website that monitors water and air quality for hundreds of municipalities. “The problems are local, not universal.”

The first step to understanding your personal risk is to gauge whether your own water is up to snuff by simply looking up its most recent data report online. The Safe Drinking Water Act requires community water systems to publish an annual report on the quality of water they’re delivering, due by July 1 each year.

These are heady documents, so to keep things simple, Voll says to keep your eye on three key pollutant metrics: lead, nitrate or arsenic, and PFAS (so-called forever chemicals popular in nonstick cookware and other products). While the first two are serious issues, they’re fairly rare today. PFAS, however, are a particularly significant problem across the country. Voll says that 98 of the 200 largest US cities have at least one PFAS compound detected in their water supply. Reverse osmosis systems can handle them—as well as lead and other compounds. Carbon filters usually can’t.

“Most people don't need RO,” Voll says, “but some households clearly do, and the way to tell is to read your own water report first.”

RO systems come with a host of downsides, but most are related in some fashion to cost.

Based on my analysis of my city’s water report, no I don’t really need an RO system, as there’s nothing hazardous to worry about. However, as the linked report notes, our water supplier uses chloramine as a disinfectant, and drinking that chloramine-treated water straight from the tap has a slight but noticeable, off-putting taste. This is exacerbated by the extreme hardness of the water; this part of Texas is basically built on top of nothing but limestone, and the resulting amount of calcium carbonate in the water can add a slightly bitter flavor, as well.

For me, RO is all about the way the water tastes. Any lingering contaminants that are removed along the way are just a bonus.

I tested the Waterdrop X12 for this story, hiring a plumber to remove a well-aged unit under the sink and replace it with this more capable one. The job was done in less than an hour.

Under-sink RO filters are separate from your existing plumbing, and most use three color-coded lengths of tubing to tie into it. One connects to your incoming water supply through an adapter valve, one clips into your wastewater drainpipe via an included fitting, and one connects to the faucet. I am confident I could have made these connections on my own—especially since I was replacing an existing system—but I’ve seen how even a small water leak can lead to devastating problems and was happy for the peace of mind that professional oversight supplied.

Once installed and powered up, the X12 launches a 35-minute priming process designed to flush the filters before first use. It seems like an awfully long time for the sink to run, and it is. If you have some way to capture and use all this extra water, plan ahead to collect it. After the session completed (it stops automatically), I was ready to start sipping from the tap.

The X12 is exceedingly simple to use. Tap the LED-equipped button on top of the faucet to start or stop the flow of water, or twist the same button to dial in the number of ounces you want to dispense (up to 999) before depressing it. This way, you can drop your 64-ounce Stanley Quencher in the sink and walk away without having to wait around or worry about an overflow, with a digital timer first offering a measurement of your water’s current TDS (total dissolved solids), then counting down the ounces as they’re dispensed. The good news is that you won’t have to wait long: The X12 blasts water out of its spigot faster than my kitchen sink does—a far cry from the sad dribble of H2O my old RO system provided.

The unit can also be switched into one of two alternate operating modes, including an energy-saving mode (which quiets the device) and “flow mode,” which is designed for high-use households. Both require pressing certain button combinations on the filtration unit. I have used the device in all three modes, and (contrary to other reviews I’ve seen) I’ve never found it to be noisy at all, even though it’s located just 20 feet from my bed.

As for the water, it tastes great. The remineralization feature and pH adjustment (to about 7.5, which I confirmed with a pH test strip) give the water a pleasant tang while avoiding any harshness or chemical flavors. Unfortunately it doesn’t come out particularly cold, which means having to add non-RO ice to the glass to chill it down. First-world problem, I know.

I was especially pleased to discover that the X12 has one of the best water efficiency rates in the business, with a 3:1 pure-to-drain ratio (3 gallons of pure water production to 1 gallon lost). Replacement filters (three are needed in total) are sold à la carte for $30 to $160 each.

For an even more powerful version—ideal for larger households—consider the Waterdrop X16, which uses the same cartridges and offers the same features as the X12. WIRED editor Kat Merck, whose municipal water supply does contain PFAS, has had one installed for a year and a half without any maintenance or reliability issues.

#4924. Crypto Midterm Election Spending Reaches $176 Million as Industry Builds for the Next Congress

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The crypto industry has spent about $176 million on more than 100 midterm races to build a bipartisan bloc in Congress that can protect its interests after Trump leaves office.

The crypto industry has spent about $176 million across more than 100 midterm races this cycle, according to a Reuters review of campaign spending. The money is part of a plan to build a bipartisan coalition in Congress that can protect the sector after President Donald Trump leaves office.

Fairshake, the industry’s largest super PAC, leads the effort. The group spent $196 million in the 2024 cycle and is directing strategy again. Through October 20, Fairshake and its affiliate PACs had spent about $150.8 million this year, while other crypto groups spent around $25 million. Fairshake said 53 of the 58 candidates it backed in primaries or special elections won, and those candidates will be on the November 3 ballot, per Reuters.

Super PACs can raise and spend unlimited sums but cannot coordinate with campaigns. Fairshake’s ads run through affiliates with names such as “Defend American Jobs” and “Protect Progress,” and they often focus on taxes and immigration rather than crypto. Voters have little way of knowing who pays for them.

The watchdog group Public Citizen said crypto was the largest corporate spender in federal races in 2026. Crypto firms led corporate political spending at that point, citing the same watchdog’s analysis.

Coinbase, Ripple and Andreessen Horowitz are the primary financiers. Industry strategists and campaign staff said the money is a calling card meant to build relationships with elected officials.

Colin McLaren, head of government relations at the Solana Policy Institute, which has given $4.9 million to the midterms, called it “an investment in the future,” per Reuters.

The approach reflects a sharp change. In 2018, lawmakers could not secure a room in the House or Senate office buildings for the first bipartisan crypto roundtable, so they met at the Library of Congress. Then, under the Biden administration, the Securities and Exchange Commission levied more than $6 billion in penalties across 98 cases, according to a Georgetown University Law study.

Executives concluded they needed political force. Coinbase Chief Policy Officer Faryar Shirzad helped launch Stand With Crypto and Fairshake in 2023. The number of lawmakers rated “A” by Stand With Crypto rose to 332 from 96 when the group launched.

Legislation followed. The GENIUS Act, enacted in 2025 with broad bipartisan support, created the first federal regulatory framework for stablecoins. The industry’s next goal, the Clarity Act, would place oversight under the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. That bill stalled this session. Senate Democrats blocked it from reaching a final vote over an ethics provision that would have forced the Trump family to sell its crypto holdings.

Fairshake helped defeat Sherrod Brown, who chaired the Senate Banking Committee, in 2024, and the industry has since spent more than $68 million on his rematch against Republican Jon Husted, according to AdImpact.

Fairshake’s ads against Brown do not mention crypto, and Brown’s campaign manager, Patrick Eisenhauer, said “the special interests are panicking.” In Illinois, Fairshake spent over $10.1 million attacking Lieutenant Governor Juliana Stratton and $2.5 million against state Representative La Shawn Ford, per Reuters, yet both are expected to win on November 3.

Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss each gave about $13 million this cycle, most of it to MAGA Inc.

Public attitudes are mixed. A September survey seen by Bitcoin Magazine of 1,545 U.S. adults by Ipsos for the DeFi Education Fund found that 22% have owned or used crypto, up from 18% in 2025. Only 19% believe the U.S. financial system is secure.

The survey also found 35% say it is important for candidates to support building the next generation of financial technology in the United States. Only 13% said the same when the question named blockchain, crypto or DeFi. The DeFi Education Fund is a digital asset advocacy group, so its framing reflects that perspective.

Stand With Crypto’s leadership has argued that the crypto vote will decide the midterms.

The Department of Health and Human Services and the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services want hospitals to take a voluntary step toward serving healthier food to their patients.

The “Make Hospital Food Healthier Pledge” unveiled this week is the latest in Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr.’s and other health officials’ emphasis on nutrition as a factor in chronic disease prevention and general long-term wellness.

Though voluntary, it follows concrete commitments the administration has scored from dozens of educational institutions to bring nutrition into the medical curriculum.

“Patients recovering from serious medical conditions deserve better than ultra-processed and deep-fried junk foods,” Kennedy said in HHS’ announcement of the pledge. “President Trump has directed HHS to put real food at the center of American health. Today, we’re challenging hospitals across the country to lead by example by serving nutritious, minimally processed meals that help patients heal, reduce chronic disease, and help Make America Healthy Again.”

HHS began pointing hospitals in this direction back in March, when CMS issued a Quality and Safety Special Alert Memo underscoring Medicare Conditions of Participation (CoP) that require in-house or contracted food delivery “in accordance with recognized dietary practices.” Recommendations included in the memo were in line with HHS and the Department of Agriculture’s overhauled dietary guidelines, which discourage “ultraprocessed” foods and prioritize foods with proteins, healthy fats and whole grains.

The Make Hospital Food Healthier Pledge hits each of those beats with additional commitments to eliminate deep-fried foods, “limit” sugar-sweetened beverages and broadly wind down foods high in added sugars, sodium or other artificial additives.

Outside of a “farm-to-hospital” food sourcing initiative at Nicklaus Children’s Hospital that officials highlighted back in March, HHS has not named any other hospitals that have made similar voluntary healthy food commitments. The webpage for HHS’ new hospital pledge does not display the names of any organizations that have signed on.

#261. Fears grow for Iranian athletes on death row as mountaineer latest to be hanged

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Two sportspeople have been executed in the past month and at least six more face the same fate, says activist group

Fears are growing for more Iranian athletes facing execution after mountain climber Ali-Sina Mostafavi became the latest in a string to be killed by the regime.

Mostafavi – who documented his ascents of some of the world’s highest peaks, including Mount Everest – was executed on 14 October having been arrested in April after the anti-government protests a few months earlier. His death followed the killing of Siavash Jamshidi Kheirabadi, a taekwondo athlete, at the start of October, and the mixed martial arts fighter Gholamreza Khani Shakarab, who was hanged in May.

Now Human Rights for Sport – a group of former Iranian athletes based in Oslo that has been documenting the growing number of cases – has warned that at least six more are facing execution after having their sentences upheld by Iran’s supreme court. They include the swimmer Ali Pishevarzadeh and the footballer Ehsan Hosseinpour, who were both arrested during the January protests, and Mohammad Javad Vafaei Sani – a boxer arrested in 2020 for taking part in protests a year earlier. He saw his request for a retrial rejected last year.

Another MMA fighter, Benyamin Naghdi, equestrian athlete Taraneh Rahimi and the climber Maryam Siri have also been condemned to death this year.

Human Rights for Sport said six athletes have already been executed in cases linked to the January protests or earlier political charges.

Dr Zohreh Abdollahkhani of Human Rights for Sport, said: “The Iranian sporting community is under enormous pressure. For one of those sentenced to death, we managed to raise attention and awareness through other organisations and they halted his execution. But it was only temporary and I’m very concerned about all of them.”

#236. I Tested Every Major AI Agent for a Day. Here’s the Best One

Last year, I remember sitting in WIRED’s office, complaining to my colleagues nonstop about how much AI agents suck. They were just bad at everything.

As a software reporter, I sat through meeting after meeting where tech bros—mostly men—demonstrated how their new agent would revolutionize my everyday life and automate away the tedium. The catch was that none of these so-called “agents” actually worked.

Fast-forward to today, and always-on AI agents are booming, with major players racing to release their own versions to attract early adopters. The key reason these personal agents are hitting the larger zeitgeist, beyond Silicon Valley nerds, is that they work. Well, at least more reliably than last year. AI agents still make mistakes, but I also see significant improvements in following through on tasks and controlling a browser. These little guys got their glow-up.

It can be hard to know where to start with AI agents and which one best fits your automation needs. So I spent a day experimenting with every major AI agent to understand their strengths and weaknesses. Before going to bed on Wednesday last week, I set up six always-on agents on my iPhone: Meta’s Muse, OpenAI’s Dots, Instinct, Anthropic’s Claude Duet, Google’s Gemini Spark, and xAI’s Grok Bot.

Each agent differs slightly in price and function. Muse is free to use and has dominated the smartphone app download charts since its September release. Instinct is also free, but you need an invite code from a user; it’s operated through your texts rather than an app. OpenAI’s Dots are behind a $100-a-month paywall, the most expensive I tested, and built inside of ChatGPT. Google’s Gemini Spark is a beta inside the Gemini app and costs $20 a month. xAI’s Grok Bot costs $30 a month and is in its own app. Lastly, Anthropic’s Claude Duet costs $20 a month and is the newest of the bunch—released as a wait-listed research preview the night before my tests.

OpenClaw isn't on my list. Despite the AI agent sparking much of this innovation earlier this year, it’s too clunky for the average person to set up. Maybe it will be in a future ranking, where I could also include smaller players like Hermes by Nous Research and Poke.

If you’ve never used an AI agent—I have zero judgment for the holdouts—it's like texting a digital assistant to complete tasks: scan your email for important missed messages, track vacation flight deals, and help with daily shopping decisions.

Compared to a chatbot’s turn-based experience, these tools are more proactive and will nudge you first when they detect an opportunity to be helpful. #1600. Clasico: Tchouaméni, Konaté, Mbappé, Camavinga… the Blues had a great time

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Zinédine Zidane has reason to rejoice. During the Clasico won by Real Madrid on the pitch of FC Barcelona (1-2), in La Liga, the four French people sent a strong signal. Aurélien Tchouaméni dominated the midfield, Ibrahima Konaté responded in a very high-level meeting, Eduardo Camavinga turned the game on its head after his entry and Kylian Mbappé once again donned his role as a decisive player with his double. A few weeks before the next international events, these performances give arguments to the four men, all French internationals, even if their situations and their current dynamics differ. And in a match where Real had to resist the return of the Catalans, the French representatives weighed on the result in several ways.

The first to make an impression was Ibrahima Konaté. Arriving this summer from Liverpool, the central defender approached this season with a certain pressure after a very disappointing 2025-2026 financial year, prolonged by a World Cup where he had not found his best level. His arrival at Real Madrid had raised questions, with some doubting his ability to establish himself in an environment where every mistake comes at a high price. But the Frenchman seems to be gradually reversing the trend. Against Barça, he delivered a particularly solid performance, winning numerous duels against Raphinha and limiting the Brazilian’s influence. He was also clean in his first attempts, without getting rid of the ball under pressure. In such an exposed encounter, Konaté displayed a mixture of power, aggressiveness and above all serenity. After months of doubts, Konaté showed that he can still fit into the plans of the France team.

For his part, Aurélien Tchouaméni also delivered a demonstration which should not go unnoticed. Disappointing during the World Cup, particularly against Spain, then hampered by an injury at the start of the season, the midfielder had not yet regained his full influence. His absence from the last meeting of the Blues was explained by his recent return to competition, but his performance at Camp Nou could reshuffle certain cards. Lined up in midfield alongside Bernardo Silva, the Frenchman imposed his physical impact and his reading of the game for a large part of the match. His interventions disrupted the Barcelona circuits, while his aggressiveness in the duels considerably hampered the opposing midfielders. He also shone in the use of the ball, with a precise long game and restarts capable of breaking the first lines of pressure. Indeed, with six tackles and eleven recoveries, Tchouaméni was also valuable in the air and did not hesitate to make contact. A truly complete match from him, rated 7/10 by the editorial staff of French Football Weekly.

Kylian Mbappé did not need an additional Clasico to remind us of his importance to Real Madrid, but he was still present at the decisive moment. The French striker opened the scoring in the 41st minute in fortunate circumstances, after Gerard Martín’s clearance came back at him before finishing in the Barcelona net. Barça then regained the score through Raphinha after the break, and Real had to wait until the end of the match to regain the advantage. Mbappé finally scored a double, with a decisive achievement in the 87th minute after a remarkable entry from Camavinga, and now brings his tally to fourteen goals in thirteen matches in all competitions. It is also his eighth goal in seven Clasicos. But beyond his two achievements, the Frenchman respected his role as number nine without constantly encroaching on the left lane occupied by Yan Diomandé. He made multiple calls, participated in the game and helped with defensive work, while supporting Jude Bellingham. Even Daniel Riolo, often critical of him, praised his influence on RMC : “Mbappé is the decisive man this evening. It’s not brand new, but he’s becoming the scorer and that’s it. Doing that is his football. » A few hours before the Ballon d’Or ceremony, the Frenchman delivered the best possible response on the pitch.

But the sequence that most electrified Madrid supporters perhaps came from Eduardo Camavinga. Not retained by Didier Deschamps this summer after a very complicated previous season, marked among other things by his expulsion against Bayern Munich, the French midfielder seems to be regaining momentum since his return to form and his performances with the Blues under Zidane. His start to the season at Real was already promising, but his entry in the 80th minute against Barcelona gave a new dimension to this dynamic. While the match remained undecided, Camavinga brought energy, percussion and an ability to attack spaces that his team had previously lacked. On the action of Mbappé’s victorious goal, he accelerated, eliminated his opponent and served his compatriot in the best conditions.

José Mourinho also explained the role he asked him to play: “I told Camavinga that Tchouaméni would stay low to protect the defense, and that he would exploit the spaces. It’s difficult for any striker to exploit spaces against Barcelona; the only solution is to do it with the midfielders. When the attackers attempt this, Barca’s defense is prepared and trained, and they find themselves offside, or the pass is intercepted by Joan Garcia. The solution is to use midfielders like Camavinga and Bellingham to break that line, and it worked. » An analysis which highlights the complementarity sought between the Frenchman and Bellingham, but also the importance of Camavinga taking the initiative. The only French international from Real Madrid not to have participated in this meeting is Ferland Mendy, still injured in his thigh. So, at the Camp Nou, the French from Casa Blanca responded. And for Zinédine Zidane, this evening was enough to attract attention.
