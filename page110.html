My biggest concern with Matthias Jaissle isn’t the Newcastle United results.

It is his apparent reluctance to make meaningful changes to a system that, judging by our performances, simply isn’t delivering.

Newcastle United are creating very few chances while allowing opponents large numbers of shots.

Yet Matthias Jaissle continues to favour a Newcastle United midfield set up that is easy to play through.

We all know what happens to Amorim, Postecoglou etc.

That got me wondering whether there’s any historical evidence that this level of tactical rigidity eventually pays off in the Premier League, particularly at clubs without enormous transfer budgets. So I asked AI for some help.

Looking back over the last 25 years, I struggled to find a convincing example of a manager who initially performed poorly, steadfastly persisted with essentially the same formation despite those problems, and eventually achieved at least two successful Premier League seasons without substantial financial backing.

Consider these past and present Premier League managers:

Jurgen Klopp: Stuck to his footballing philosophy but adapted his formations and tactics. Sustained success. Mikel Arteta: Maintained his principles but changed formation and tactics during difficult periods. Sustained success, albeit with considerable investment. Mauricio Pochettino: Consistent philosophy, flexible formations. Sustained success without the spending power of Manchester City or Chelsea. David Moyes: Adapted his tactics and formation to the players available. Sustained success relative to resources. Sean Dyche: Probably the closest example of sticking to a recognisable system on a limited budget, but even he made tactical adjustments. Sustained success relative to resources. Marcelo Bielsa: Relatively rigid tactical approach. Initially successful at Leeds but couldn’t sustain it into a second successful Premier League season. Ange Postecoglou: Remained strongly committed to his attacking philosophy despite deteriorating results. No sustained Premier League success. Ruben Amorim: Persisted largely with his preferred back-three system despite struggling. No sustained Premier League success.

The pattern among these examples is interesting. Managers who achieved sustained success generally retained their footballing principles but were prepared to change formations or tactics when necessary. Those who remained particularly rigid struggled to achieve comparable long-term results.

As another Newcastle United fan mentioned in their article over the weekend, whilst the Matthias Jaissle achievements are genuine, there is little evidence of him achieving sustained success at a club operating under comparable financial constraints to those Newcastle United face relative to their Premier League competitors. We don’t have the funds to buy the players to suit his system this season.

Surely part of the manager’s responsibility is to adapt his approach to the strengths and weaknesses of the players available?

I’m not suggesting Matthias Jaissle should abandon his footballing philosophy after a few poor results. Nor am I suggesting he’s made no tactical adjustments whatsoever. But there’s a fundamental difference between sticking to your principles and stubbornly sticking to a formation.

My concern is that, among the historical examples I’ve examined, there’s very little evidence of managers persisting with a system that is producing consistently poor performances, making no significant structural changes, and eventually enjoying sustained Premier League success without major financial advantages.

Perhaps Matthias Jaissle will prove to be the exception. But if our midfield continues to be overrun and the shot statistics remain so heavily against us, what evidence is there that simply persevering with the same basic structure will eventually make it work?

I accept that a manager needs time to establish his ideas. But surely that patience should be accompanied by evidence of progress, or at least a willingness to address obvious weaknesses.

For me, tactical flexibility isn’t a sign of weakness. It’s one of the qualities that separates successful managers from those who ultimately get the sack.

#141. Netherlands pulls out of Eurovision again over Israel participation

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

Claude Kiambe was the last Netherlands entrant in 2025

The Netherlands has pulled out of next year's Eurovision Song Contest, with public broadcaster NPO citing "sensitivities" over Israel's participation - reversing a previous decision to take part.

The Netherlands and four other countries boycotted this year's event, after Dutch network Avrotros pulled out.

Avrotros said it would also not take part next year, but NPO, which oversees public broadcasting in the country, stepped in and pledged last month that the country would return in 2027.

However, NPO's media director Jurre Bosman said on Tuesday that "Israel's participation elicits various reactions in society and causes discussion and division", and NPO "has decided to forgo participation in 2027".

NPO chair Jet de Ranitz said the organisation had held discussions with broadcasters about participating next year.

"In those discussions, we initially felt there was sufficient support to return. Unfortunately, we were unable to find a suitable format for this.

"As a result, we must conclude that it is currently too early to participate in the Eurovision Song Contest in 2027.

"Due to the sensitivities surrounding the themes currently at play, we have decided to take a step back for the time being."

NPO acknowledged that some Dutch fans may be disappointed, but that the contest would still be aired in the Netherlands.

Eurovision Song Contest director Martin Green said he was "deeply sorry" that the Netherlands would not take part, but added that "we understand their decision, and we respect the strength of feeling behind it".

Green added that organisers recognise that opinions about the contest "differ widely, both within countries and between them", adding: "We do not dismiss any of those views.

"At the same time, we believe the Eurovision Song Contest matters in a divided world. It is one of the few moments each year when people from across Europe and beyond come together around music, creativity and shared experience.

"That spirit of unity is what the contest has always stood for, and we are committed to protecting it."

Green said they looked forward to welcoming a Dutch entry again when they "feel the time is right".

The Netherlands, Iceland, Spain, Ireland and Slovenia all bocotted the 2026 contest over Israel's participation, amid criticism of its military actions in Gaza and the Middle East.

Eurovision organisers have announced a number of changes in an attempt to address the divisions, including to exclude any country involved in an armed conflict from hosting the competition, and changes to the voting.

As well as the Netherlands, Ireland will also not take part in 2027, and the head of the Spanish public broadcaster has come out in favour of a boycott.

The Czech public broadcaster has also pulled out, but cited spending cuts as the reason.

Eurovision 2027 will take place in Burgas in Bulgaria on 15 May, following Dara's win in Vienna this year with the dance track Bangaranga.

Ethereum developers have agreed on a fix for a flaw in the upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade that could have sent staking deposits to the wrong account. The correction was merged into the network’s technical specifications on October 26.

The issue is about a proposed change to Ethereum, not a confirmed theft. Glamsterdam has not launched on the main network, and the teams building Ethereum’s software will still need to incorporate the revised rules.

Ethereum relies on validators to help it secure its network. To become a validator, an operator deposits ETH, and they do this with the help of a staking service.

Glamsterdam introduces a separate role for block builders, who assemble transactions. Builders can also make deposits, but those funds are meant to be tracked separately from validators’ ETH.

George Avsetsin [a researcher] discovered that the proposed rules could lead to confusion between the two during the upgrade. If a builder and a new validator used the same public key, a validator deposit waiting to be processed could be credited to the builder instead, by mistake.

Depending on the circumstances, an operator controlling that builder account can then withdraw the funds that is supposed to be for staking. The researcher said these are potential risks, not as attacks that are confirmed.

The merged change is designed to ensure that validators’ deposits are kept in the proper queue, even when a builder uses the same key.

The correction did not go through on its first attempt. Developers closed the proposal on October 19 because there was not enough support from the teams responsible for Ethereum’s software.

They then reopened it on October 23, and after further review and additional tests, the change was merged three days later.

That decision came while Glamsterdam’s testing schedule was already being revised. The Ethereum Foundation postponed an October 26 activation on the Hoodi test network to investigate a separate issue involving how another proposed change could affect existing applications.

The Foundation said that it has not set a new Hoodi date yet, and no time has been set for the mainnet launch either.

For now, the deposit fix is part of the planned upgrade rules. The next step is for software teams to build and test those rules as Glamsterdam moves toward another public test.

Data from DefiLlama showed that there are currently 17.13 million staked ETH, worth close to $40 billion.

#4110. Low testosterone may make sugar far more harmful to your liver

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Low testosterone can contribute to several health problems, and an unhealthy diet may make some of those risks even worse. One major concern is metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), a condition that affects approximately 40% of adult men worldwide and has become a significant global health issue.

Fatty liver, the earliest stage of MASLD, occurs when excess fat builds up in the liver. Several factors are associated with the condition, including obesity, type 2 diabetes, low testosterone levels, and excessive fructose consumption from sugary drinks and processed foods. However, scientists have not fully understood how these factors interact or whether their combined effects are more harmful than each one individually.

How Low Testosterone and Fructose Affect the Liver

To investigate this connection, a research team from the Graduate School of Agriculture, led by graduate student Hiroki Takahashi and Associate Professor Naoki Harada, examined how reduced testosterone and high fructose intake influence liver health.

The researchers used eight-week-old male mice and divided them into six experimental groups. Some underwent castration to lower testosterone levels, while others received a sham operation. The groups included Sham/Control, Sham/Fructose, Sham/Fructose + Antibiotics, Castration/Control, Castration/Fructose, and Castration/Fructose + Antibiotics.

To determine how these conditions affected the animals, the team analyzed liver cells and tissues, blood plasma, organic acids from the cecum, and samples of gut bacteria.

The results revealed that mice with reduced testosterone levels developed heavier livers when they consumed fructose. However, this increase in liver weight was smaller in mice that also received antibiotics.

When examined separately, low testosterone and fructose intake produced only modest changes in liver triglyceride levels. When combined, however, the two factors had a much stronger effect, significantly increasing fat accumulation and worsening fatty liver.

The researchers also identified changes in the composition of gut bacteria, altered liver gene activity, and elevated levels of pyruvate in the cecum among mice exposed to both reduced testosterone and fructose.

These findings suggested that the gut microbiome could play an important role in explaining why the combination was particularly harmful.

Further experiments helped the researchers identify a possible biological explanation for the increased liver fat.

"Upon examining this mechanism, we found that changes in the gut microbiota led to increased levels of pyruvate within the intestine. Furthermore, experiments using mouse-derived primary hepatocytes revealed that pyruvate acts synergistically with fructose to promote neutral lipid accumulation in hepatocytes," explained Takahashi.

In other words, changes in gut bacteria appeared to increase intestinal pyruvate, a compound involved in energy metabolism. Laboratory experiments with liver cells isolated from mice showed that pyruvate and fructose worked together to increase fat storage in those cells.

The discovery suggests that interactions among testosterone levels, dietary fructose, and gut bacteria may help explain how fatty liver develops and worsens.

Potential New Ways to Prevent Fatty Liver Disease

Understanding this relationship could eventually help researchers identify new approaches to preventing or treating fatty liver disease. In particular, targeting the processes through which pyruvate contributes to fat accumulation may offer promising possibilities.

"Going forward, we hope to clarify the mechanism by which pyruvate promotes triglyceride accumulation to pioneer the development of therapeutic drugs and establishment of preventive methods through dietary interventions," added Professor Harada.

Although the findings come from experiments in mice and isolated mouse liver cells, they provide new insight into how hormonal changes and diet may interact to influence liver health.

The study was published in American Journal of Physiology-Endocrinology and Metabolism.

#3474. Python's experimental JIT to be removed

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Python's experimental just-in-time (JIT) compiler, which was added to CPython in 2024 for the 3.13 release, is on its way out. In June, the Python steering council announced that it was pausing work on the JIT in CPython's main branch until there was an accepted Python Enhancement Proposal (PEP) that addressed concerns about maintenance, compatibility, and set clear success metrics as well as a timeline for completion. After creating PEP 836 ("JIT Go Brrr: The Path to a Supported JIT Compiler for CPython") to try to meet the council's request, and after months of discussion, the JIT maintainers have withdrawn the PEP. For now, it appears that development on the once-promising project has come to an end.

The JIT was added to CPython by Brandt Bucher in early 2024. Generally, such a major feature would be expected to land only after the council had approved a standards-track PEP. Bucher had given a talk (YouTube video, slides) about the JIT at a Python core developer sprint, and announced his intent to merge the feature—but he had not taken the step of authoring or submitting a PEP for its approval. He was prodded into doing so by steering council member Thomas Wouters, who was also serving as the release manager for Python 3.13.

Bucher and Savannah Ostrowski drafted PEP 744 ("JIT Compilation"), which was an informational PEP—meaning it only provides information to the Python community and does not represent formal consensus to proceed on a feature or project. LWN covered the discussion of PEP 744 in April 2024, and later covered Bucher's talk on the JIT at PyCon US 2024, as well as the follow-up talk at PyCon US 2025.

Work on the JIT had been proceeding on the basis of PEP 744 until this June, when the council had put forward its formal request for the JIT maintainers to create a standards-track PEP. One might wonder, as Antoine Pitrou did, why the council had decided that it was time to speak up and ask for a new PEP. Wouters said that there was no "single triggering event for this request", but that the council had been discussing the topic for a while and indicated that it was looking to ensure the JIT work did not interfere with other projects. "There's been work trying to get the JIT to play nice with instrumentation, debugging, and profiling. There's also external JITs (like CinderX), JIT-adjacent things (like Numba and PyTorch's compile), and discussions about a tier 3 JIT." The council had waited until after the Python 3.15 beta release "to be clear that we're OK with the status quo for 3.15".

The council asked for a PEP that would make the case for the JIT "as a supported, non-experimental part of CPython: its guarantees, its maintenance commitments, and its impact on redistributors". The council set a deadline of six months for the PEP to be submitted and accepted; failing that, the council would require the JIT code to be removed from the CPython main branch.

Bucher, Ostrowski, and Ken Jin drafted PEP 836 and published it on the Python forum for community feedback on July 3. It generated quite a bit of discussion, some favorable and some critical. One of the key themes in the comments, such as this one by Wouters, and this comment by Mark Shannon, was that the draft felt like it should be two separate PEPs. Shannon, for example, thought that the PEP should only cover goals for accepting the JIT work and felt that it should leave out implementation details. There were also quite a few calls for additional information.

Bucher pushed back a bit on the suggestion that it should be two PEPs, and Ostrowski defended the structure of the PEP as it was. "Outlining goals like we have in the PEP without being able to share the plan and everything we've thought through alongside it would make me uncomfortable. To me, goals without plans behind them aren't really goals at all."

When we last looked at this topic in September, the discussion about the PEP had slowed down without a commitment to develop new PEPs or a formal statement from the council. The last word from the council at that time, in the form of a summary of its August 6 meeting, was that it had met with the PEP authors and was "discussing the state of the PEP and whether it answered the questions the SC asked in its request for a PEP".

On September 21, Wouters posted a statement from the steering council, minus Ostrowski (who is currently a member of the steering council but did not participate since she is a co-author of the PEP), that summarized its position. The statement said that PEP did answer all of the questions that were highlighted in the council's request, "although perhaps some less convincingly than others".

The council wished to make clear that it was not looking for the criteria that the JIT should meet to be a supported feature, or whether it should be considered such. Instead, the council was evaluating whether the JIT should continue as an experiment in CPython's main branch.

The council said that the PEP was not specific enough about support plans, performance criteria, and too vague or too optimistic about a number of other areas. The statement included a ten-point list of items that the council wanted "to see either spelled out in the PEP, or at least discussed and rejected". The council's list expressed concerns ranging from memory impact to security. For example, on the topic of platform parity, the council wanted to ensure that the JIT was designed to be easily adopted for new platforms. It wanted assurance that the JIT's design would not be tied to specific platform or require platform-specific knowledge to maintain.

Another area that the council called out was user impact. It said that the PEP was "overly optimistic" about user-visible differences with the JIT enabled. There were many cases where users depend on idiosyncrasies in the interpreter design, "as we've seen with other Python implementations or even just between major Python versions", and it wanted clarification on how those differences would be handled. Would they be considered bugs, how would situations be handled if a difference in behavior was unavoidable, and so on.

Security was "an underserved area in the PEP", according to the council; it suggested that a discussion with the Python security response team was in order and that a security review should be part of the criteria for supported status as well.

The council also worried about the JIT's design decisions being made "without critical review by the larger group of developers ultimately responsible for [CPython] development, maintenance and support". The bottom line, it said, was that PEP 836 "mostly answers the questions we asked" with no missing pieces, but it wanted more discussion on those topics so it could "gauge community support and consensus before we're asked to pronounce on this PEP".

Jin replied a few days later to thank the council for its detailed feedback and said that the group would work on its suggestions.

After some consideration, it appears, the PEP's authors have decided not to continue pursing the council's approval. On October 19, Ostrowski (who is also release manager for Python 3.16 and 3.17) announced that the PEP was being withdrawn:

Barry Scott asked if that meant that work on the JIT was stopped. Brett Cannon replied that the work would be stopped in the main repository, but it would be up to the community whether the work would continue elsewhere. Dunc Graham suggested that there might be time for an alternative PEP to be submitted before the council's deadline. Cannon said that was not likely, "as all the people actively driving the JIT were either authors on that PEP or contributors to it".

With the PEP withdrawn, the JIT will likely be removed from CPython main fairly soon to ensure that any problems caused by its removal can be addressed early in the Python 3.16 development cycle. It is unclear whether the original drivers of the JIT work intend to continue working on it at all, or if it will simply be left for others to pick up if there is enough community interest. I have emailed the PEP authors for clarification, but have not yet received a reply.

Hopefully this won't be the end of the work, because Python can always benefit from better performance.

For one thing, pypy only suppports Python 3.12, 3.11 and 2.7. More significantly, pypy isn't CPython - it's an alternative implementation that is mostly compatible but with a few noticeable differences (the pypy website lists these). This would have added a JIT to CPython (the reference implementation) directly, meaning all new Python versions - and all included modules, etc. - get access to JIT compilation. In the PEP 836 authors' own words:

Alternative Python implementations, such as PyPy and GraalPy, demonstrate that larger speedups are possible for some Python programs, and we deeply value those projects. However, many users cannot adopt an alternative runtime even when it may perform better on their code due to factors such as supported Python versions, extension compatibility, embedding requirements, deployment constraints, and tooling support.

#591. Team success, the ‘Bundesliga tax’ and the World Cup: three takeaways from the 2026 Ballon d’Or

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

There was plenty to learn from this year’s ceremony in London as Harry Kane took home the top honor

It was an awards show that, as Michael Owen admitted, could have been a phone call – as it was in 2001 when he won. A quarter-century on, a ballroom full of footballers, power brokers, a gravity-defying dancer and a handful of heckling rabble-rousers plodded through a three-hour slog of a ceremony. Aside from a reminder to viewers at home just how precious our free time is, what could be gleaned from the men’s element of the Ballon d’Or ceremony?

As early voting for the midterms progresses in the United States, the party affiliation of a candidate carries as much weight as their name. This year, as with many this century, the panel of voters were similarly interested in team performance beyond individual accomplishments.

Not to say that Harry Kane didn’t make a strong individual case to win. The presumptive frontrunner this year, Kane also took home the honor as top men’s striker of the year roughly 45 minutes before the top prize went his way. Scoring 64 goals in 56 club matches is staggering. So, too, is his growing reputation as a world-class chance creator with the through balls he threads with increasing ease.

Kane stands out as a stat-accumulator in a waning era of meticulous systems, and his gilded peers playing for teams operating off such intricate models were largely held back. Premier League-winning Arsenal also reached the Champions League final, yet none of their players ranked higher than 15th (Declan Rice). Paris Saint-Germain won club of the year, yet their top finisher (Khvicha Kvaratskhelia) was down in sixth.

Likewise, a (relatively) poor season for a team can sink their stars’ cases. Rodri (fourth) and Erling Haaland (eighth) were immense at the World Cup this summer – more on that later – but Manchester City’s inability to overcome Arsenal seemingly dinged each star’s candidacy. A year after controversially moaning about Ousmane Dembélé pipping Vinícius Júnior to the honor, Real Madrid’s chaotic campaign sank a few otherwise intriguing candidates. The big pre-ceremony shock came when the award’s social media admins posted that Jude Bellingham finished 11th. Clearly, his club form had sideswiped his World Cup brilliance.

In the end, this came down to two of this era’s great frontmen whose individual brilliance commanded attention beyond the rest of their storied clubs. Kylian Mbappé ended his campaign on Spain’s second-best team and with a fourth-place showing at the World Cup. Kane’s England outlasted France in the fever-dream third-place game this summer, but his status at the fore of the Bundesliga champions and Champions League semi-finalists Bayern Munich likely provided that crucial final nudge.

And so, Mbappé was left in sunken posture to watch a competitive rival claim the prize he has long coveted.

Germany’s attacking-friendly ethos has held back many a Ballon d’Or hopeful playing for Bayern Munich and other clubs – the idea being that goals scored in Europe’s highest-scoring top-five league count for less. Kane’s win snapped a 30-year drought for the Bundesliga, with its last honoree coming in 1996 (Matthias Sammer). Historians may append a caveat to that, as Robert Lewandowski seemed a shoo-in for the award in 2020 until the organizers canceled it due to the Covid-19 pandemic. Nevertheless, the stigma endured for another six years.

The question, as ever, was whether voters would account for the sort of “Bundesliga tax” more commonly cited when analyzing a player transfer. Would Kane’s goals for Bayern on all fronts outshine, say, Mbappé’s 43 in 47 games for Madrid?

Bayern seemed to recognize the moment, with their three other shortlisted players (Michael Olise, ninth; Luis Díaz, 24th; Dayot Upamecano, 26th) in attendance despite some certainty that they wouldn’t win. Over the past few weeks, the club’s and league’s social media accounts have been pushing Kane’s worthiness for the award, asking him countless times what such an honor mean. (A lot, it turns out.)

In truth, it also means quite a bit for club and circuit. Since 1996, looking at the club which employed the eventual winner for most of that year, La Liga put forth 17 winners – including seven not named Lionel or Cristiano – while Serie A had five, the Premier League three and Ligue 1 two. Messi technically claimed his eighth and final award as a member of Inter Miami, and although that was awarded for his performances with PSG and Argentina, it technically meant an MLS player won the Ballon d’Or more recently than a Bundesliga player.

Vincent Kompany’s Bayern seem to be challenging those perceptions, with last year’s enthralling run to the Champions League semi-final a reminder of what the club can be at its best.

Kane’s win extends a trend of the prize going to players who aren’t a reigning World Cup winner. Since Fabio Cannavaro’s win in 2006, only Messi in 2023 has won the honor in the year corresponding with a global title, Argentina’s 2022 World Cup triumph having come in December in Qatar, after the 2022 award had been given.

In truth, it’s a sane break from 20th-century custom by the voters. The weeks-long tournament can crystalize legacies, but it won’t overshadow the previous 11 months of work for club and country towards prizes like these.

Spain were a juggernaut en route to winning a second World Cup. But while Luis de la Fuente was a fine pick for coach of the year and Unai Simón’s stonewalling exploits secured him goalkeeper honors, Lamine Yamal (third) and Rodri were just below the true frontrunners.
