#2969. Cowboys at Eagles: The good, the bad, and ugly from Week 7

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

In what was a must-win game, it was good to see the Dallas Cowboys take care of business in prime time on Monday Night Football to secure the 38-21 victory over their division rival, the Philadelphia Eagles. They now have a chance to string some wins together over the next few weeks and hopefully continue to get better.

While it was good to see the Cowboys secure the win in the first of two matchups with the Eagles this year, it was also obvious this team still has a lot to work on. There seemed to be more positives than negatives this week, which is a good sign things could be heading in the right direction. Hopefully that continues.

For the first time this season the Cowboys leaned heavily on the running game and it paid off in a big way. Dallas rushed for over 200 yards, but it was Javonte Williams who led the way, rushing for 155 yards and one touchdown on 25 carries. This is the first time this season No. 33 rushed for 100+ yards, averaging an impressive 6.2 yards per carry. If this continues Dallas' offense can get back on track and become lethal once again.

The Cowboys defense is still a major concern. They faced a shorthanded Eagles offense without their starting QB and their top two receivers and still allowed Philadelphia to put up 21 points on them. And, had Saquon Barkley been a 100%, the outcome of this game may have been different. He alone managed to rush for 62 yards and a TD on just seven carries. Christian Parker still is a lot of work on before his defense isn't considered such a liability.

The Cowboys offensive line has been criticized in the first six weeks of the season. Considering the talent they have across the board, they had been underperforming and it has negatively impacted the offensive production. On Monday night against the Eagles they looked much improved. They provided a clean pocket for Dak Prescott to throw from the and opened up huge holes for Javonte Williams to run through.

The Cowboys gave up zero sacks and zero tackles for loss in the game. Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis were non-factors. The offensive line made Philadelphia's defensive line look ugly.

#3154. AI debt fears ease ahead of Oracle, Alphabet and Amazon updates. Here's what to watch.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Fears over artificial intelligence debt have eased after a bout of volatility. But more tests for AI debt are coming.

Oracle holds its Investor Day on Wednesday. And hyperscalers Alphabet and Amazon.com are scheduled to report quarterly results after the market close on Wednesday and Thursday, respectively.

Credit markets are on edge over a flood of AI debt, though fears have eased up a little lately. Meta Platforms' plans for a $95 billion stock offering, announced on Monday, helped bring down its credit-default swap spreads. Those spreads had risen last week when the company reported negative free cash flow in the third quarter. Other CDS spreads had moved higher too, though they have since backed off.

Investors had expected Meta to bring a large bond deal to market shortly after its earnings announcement, said Richard Cheng, head of Nuveen's investment-grade corporate sector team. The fact that it wasn't announced came as a relief.

"Just the absence of the bond deal itself is a direct catalyst for AI-related spread tightening," he said.

A credit spread is the difference in the yield between the corporate bonds and Treasurys of similar maturities. When spreads tighten, it means investors are becoming more confident about the bond issuer.

The market action over the last few days shows that investors are recognizing that "credit fears may have been overblown," said CNBC contributor Paul Meeks, head of technology research at Freedom Capital Markets.

"I expect that these better feelings, maybe not good but better, may continue as companies announce their quarters over the next few weeks."

Still, spreads on CDS remain wider over the past year due to a surge of debt issuance and free cash flows turning negative across the AI space. Meta's CDS spread, for instance, is still up 177% year to date, while Amazon's spread has gained 129%.

"It's not necessarily fundamentals," said CreditSights analyst Jordan Chalfin. "It's more, I'd say, 80% driven by technicals and sentiment."

Alphabet and Amazon pose the next big hurdle for tech bonds as they update their capex plans and free cash flow forecasts. Microsoft's earnings are also due Wednesday, but the company has yet to issue any bonds to pay for its AI spending.

Both Amazon and Alphabet reported negative free cash flow in the second quarter and analysts expect that trend to have continue.

Analysts expect Alphabet to report free cash flow of -$2.3 billion while spending $55.2 billion on capital expenditures in the third quarter, according to FactSet consensus estimates. Amazon is expected to report -$9.9 billion in free cash flow against capex of $58.7 billion.

S&P Global Ratings estimated in an August report that the hyperscalers will have negative free operating cash flow in 2026 and 2027.

More critical will be their outlooks, Cheng says. "The singular focus on the investors' minds is really what the capex guidance trajectory looks like across the hyperscaler space."

Meeks believes elevated capex is a sign of strong AI demand. The hyperscalers have "decided that with all the evidence they have — and they have more than you and me — that this is worthwhile spending, and not just worthwhile; it's required," he said.

CreditSights' Chalfin doesn't expect the hyperscalers to announce major new bond issuance around their earnings reports. Oracle's updates may be more critical for credit markets. The company has issued $117 billion in long-term debt and is clinging to an investment-grade credit rating of BBB-1, the lowest level of investment grade above junk, according to S&P.

"It's the most important IG tech credit, just given how it's a bit more stressed relative to the A-rated names," he said. "It's trading more like a high-yield credit, even though it's IG, and so there's just a lot of investor concern on that one."

One wrinkle to the AI debt story: balance sheets don't reveal the whole picture. The companies are using complicated off-balance sheet arrangements for data centers and chip financing, and they aren't fully transparent, S&P Global said in a September report.

"There are commitments they are entering into, for example, into data center leases or purchase commitments on chips that aren't necessarily treated as debt," Mark Habib, digital infrastructure sector lead at S&P Global Ratings, said on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street" Monday.

S&P is trying to provide the market with more visibility on those commitments "to give a fuller picture of what kinds of obligations they're actually entering into," he added.

Oracle aside, hyperscaler credit ratings are all comfortably above A levels — reflecting the fact that they have broadly diversified revenue streams, ample coverage for interest payments, and capacity to add leverage if they choose.

Nuveen's Cheng isn't concerned, pointing out that while there has been some credit repricing, there is no credit crisis.

"They continue to have exceptional credit profiles, strong cash generation liquidity," he said of the largest hyperscalers.

"The market is really not concerned about credit quality at this point," Cheng added. "It's really just the trajectory of the capex needs and the supply of debt to fund that. Both of those are pointing in one direction — and that's up."

#5140. Chynna Phillips Reveals Living in Separate Houses Is Helping Save Her Marriage to Billy Baldwin

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Chynna Phillips says living apart from husband Billy Baldwin is helping their marriage—after he moved into a guesthouse just 50 yards away.

The couple, who married in 1995, offered a candid look at their relationship in an Instagram video while promoting their upcoming YouTube series, “Holding On,” which premieres on Nov. 17.

“Married 30+ years … and we live in separate houses. Here’s why. New series, 'Holding On,' drops Nov 17—watch us find our way back,” Phillips wrote in the caption of one post.

The accompanying footage captures tense exchanges between the spouses as they discuss their communication struggles and decision to live under separate roofs.

“You can’t admit when you’re wrong. That’s just it,” Baldwin tells Phillips in one clip.

Another exchange shows Baldwin reflecting on how their conversations can unravel.

“Here’s the problem. Sometimes the way I communicate causes her to have a nervous breakdown,” he says.

Turning to Phillips, he adds, “And you control and manipulate.”

Elsewhere in the footage, Phillips questions the expectation that married couples must share a home.

“Where in the Bible does it say I have to live under the same roof as my husband?” she asks.

Baldwin also recalls Phillips telling him she wanted to “spend my life” with him—but wanted to “spend it adjacent.”

In another clip, Phillips explains how the arrangement took shape: “He basically said, ‘I’m gonna go live in the guesthouse.’”

Despite the difficult conversations shown in the preview, Phillips says she has found value in documenting their efforts to work through their problems.

“Basically, we’re just working out our issues in real time, and it’s super cathartic and I’m really enjoying it. We’re excited about it,” she says.

In a separate Instagram post, Phillips offered a more pointed assessment of their marriage before Baldwin’s move—and said the short distance between their homes appears to be helping.

“We are so excited to take you on this journey. Literally our marriage was going down like the Titanic, and Billy moved 50 yards away from me in the guesthouse, and so far, so good. It’s kind of working. I’m not really sure what’s going to come of it, but hey, I’m not fighting it,” she said.

The couple, who share three adult children, have previously spoken publicly about living apart.

In 2024, Phillips revealed that she was staying in Santa Barbara while Baldwin was living in Beverly Hills.

At the time, she said they “don't ever want to separate,” but described a “sort of allergy to one another.”

By January 2025, Phillips said they were sharing a home again. Their latest updates reveal another change to that arrangement, with Baldwin now living in the nearby guesthouse as they continue working on their marriage.
