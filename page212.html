#4673. How to turn your smartphone into a mobile video editing suite

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Published Oct 24, 2026 8:00 AM EDT

We may earn revenue from the products available on this page and participate in affiliate programs. Learn more ›

With so many smartphone apps in the modern age locked (or partially locked) behind paywalls, it’s important to grab the free ones when they become available—and on rare occasions, it’s the biggest software companies on the planet offering apps free of charge. Adobe Premiere for iOS launched back in September 2025, and a year later, we got Adobe Premiere for Android too. You don’t need an Adobe account or a subscription to use these apps (unlike the desktop equivalents)—the only paid components are credits for AI image and video generation, if you want to use those tools.

With these apps available, there’s really no excuse for sharing sub-standard video. You get a wealth of editing features right on your mobile screen, all optimized to suit the smaller display and the more limited capabilities of a phone versus a full computer.

We’re going to concentrate on the Android app here, as it’s the one most recently launched, but the features are the same on the iPhone too. You can even use Premiere across multiple Android and iOS devices.

It’s easy enough to get started with Premiere: From the main screen, tap the + (plus) button to choose a saved video clip or image to use as the starting point for a new project, or select New blank project. Either will take you to the timeline view, and you can then use the + (plus) button on the timeline to load in more content.

The layout is going to be familiar if you’ve used a video editor before. Photos and videos make up the main timeline, and if you select them, you get handles around them—use these handles to shorten or lengthen the clips. Select a video on the timeline, then tap Split to cut it in two at the current playhead position (that’s wherever the long vertical white line is in the timeline).

Your video project is previewed above the timeline, and you can use the playback controls to go backward and forward through it. The other options you’ll see on the toolbar include Audio (for dropping in a voiceover or some stock music), Text (for titles and overlays), and Generate (for producing generative AI content). You can also select Files to load local files (such as audio snippets) from your phone.

Select anything on the timeline to get more options for it. For a video clip, these will include Retime (for changing the speed of a clip or freezing it), Filters (for adjusting the look of a clip), Effects (tweaks like glows or shakes), and tools to mirror, flip, or duplicate whatever you’ve got selected. To change transitions, tap on the borders between scenes.

Then there are the generative AI tools, which you can get to by tapping on the Generate button underneath the timeline. This part of the app requires an Adobe account—the account itself is free, but you’ll be restricted in terms of your AI usage before you have to start paying for credits.

If you want a quicker, easier way to make content, there are plenty of ready-made templates to use. You’ll see these on the front screen, under headings like Lifestyle and Beauty. Tap on a template to view it, then choose Use this template to start making it your own.

Making it your own means picking images and videos from your phone’s library to add to the template you just viewed. As you make your selections, you’ll see the template slots fill up underneath (you don’t have to use all of them)—when you’re done, choose Next to see the finished product.

This will have the same transitions, timings, titles, and audio as your template, and if you’re happy with that, then tap Export to send the video somewhere else. There are some basic editing tools here as well: Select one of the images or videos you’ve dropped in, and you can trim them or replace them with the buttons at the bottom.

To customize the clip even more, choose Full editor to return to the fully featured interface we looked at above, complete with filters, effects, and everything else. Note that once you’ve converted your template-based video to work with the full timeline interface, you can’t go back into template mode with the same project.

It’s an app that works well for quick video creations and more detailed projects you want to do on your phone, and you’ll also get numerous settings (resolution, frame rate, quality) when it’s time to export your work. If you do sign up for an Adobe account and pay for the Creative Cloud suite, you can easily sync your projects with Premiere on the desktop.

#4550. Getting More Physical Activity May Help With Your Perimenopause Symptoms

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

A new study found that exercising more frequently may help alleviate perimenopause symptoms like hot flashes, anxiety, and urinary issues.

The study results were recently presented at The Menopause Society’s 2026 Annual Conference.

Perimenopause is the transitional period leading up to menopause.

Due to hormonal changes that mark the end of a person’s reproductive years, symptoms like hot flashes, mood swings, brain fog, insomnia, and urinary incontinence are common.

Past studies show that lifestyle modifications, such as eating a healthy diet, managing stress, and getting enough physical activity, can help reduce some perimenopause symptoms.

“Physical activity is low cost, accessible, and already known to benefit sleep, mood, and heart and bone health — all areas that matter a great deal during perimenopause,” said study lead and co-author Liudmila Zhaunova, PhD, director of science at Flo Health — the developer of a women’s health tracking app, which was used to collect health data for this study. “But when we looked at the research, there was surprisingly little evidence specific to perimenopause.”

“Hormone therapy isn’t suitable or desired for everyone, so women deserve a broader set of evidence-based options,” Zhaunova told Healthline. “The more we understand about accessible, lifestyle-based approaches, the better equipped women and their healthcare providers are to build a plan that works for them.”

Study researchers recruited more than 1,800 women from around the globe who were using the Flo app.

Study participants were asked to complete online surveys about their physical activity, from not being physically active to exercising more than 5 days a week.

Participants were also asked to report on their perimenopause symptoms in three categories — somatic (body-based issues like hot flashes), psychological (mental health issues like mood changes), and urogenital (issues regarding the urinary or vaginal areas). Researchers rated the severity of these symptoms using the Menopause Rating Scale (MRS).

The researchers found that higher rates of physical activity were associated with fewer perimenopausal symptoms across all three categories.

“Perimenopause symptoms are wide-ranging, and women often experience several at once,” Zhaunova said. “Seeing a lower symptom burden across all three domains suggests that being more active may be relevant to women’s overall experience, not just one symptom.”

Additionally, the scientists discovered that the greatest exercise benefit was observed in the reduction of perimenopausal psychological symptoms.

“Psychological symptoms, such as low mood, irritability, anxiety, and exhaustion, are among the most disruptive parts of perimenopause, and they’re often overlooked or not recognized as related to this life stage,” Zhaunova explained.

“Our findings suggest that physical activity may matter most for mood and anxiety-related symptoms,” she continued.

“This could help women and clinicians have more informed conversations about how to support mental well-being during perimenopause. That said, women with more symptoms may find it harder to exercise, so we need further research to understand the direction of the relationship.”

Sherry Ross, MD, a board certified OB-GYN and Women’s Health Expert at Providence Saint John’s Health Center in Santa Monica, CA, said that regular exercise could potentially help relieve perimenopause symptoms like hot flashes and mood changes. Ross wasn’t involved in this study, but noted other benefits of exercising during this life stage:

“Exercise releases endorphins, improves blood vessel health, lowers inflammation, helps reset circadian rhythm, and improves the body’s internal cooling system,” Ross told Healthline. “Collectively, these changes ease anxiety, irritability, mood changes, sleep, and fatigue.”

“There were mixed reviews on how exercise benefits hot flashes, but exercise does help with weight control, which may benefit hot flashes,” she said. “Strengthening the pelvic floor and core may help with the urinary symptoms women many experience in perimenopause.”

Prudence Hall, MD, an OB-GYN in private practice in Santa Monica, CA, and author of “Radiant Again & Forever,” said that building a sustainable exercise routine requires consistency, joy, and community. Hall wasn’t involved in the study, but shared some exercise tips for perimenopausal women.

“Too often in midlife, previously stable women suddenly plummet into deep depression and anxiety, only to be prescribed multiple medications with debilitating side effects that never address the underlying biology,” Hall said.

“While hormone optimization remains essential to correct these endocrine deficiencies, exercise serves as a powerful, non-pharmaceutical therapy that directly targets the true root causes by revitalizing our cellular health and restoring overall vitality.”

Arsenal have won 38, outscoring them 109–36. What’s more, their only win in north London this millennium came during the pandemic, so it doesn’t really count. We’ve faced each other 226 times in competitive fixtures. Plenty of history to get through, most of it rather enjoyable from our perspective.

My own fond memories stretch back to the early nineties, listening to Jonathan Pearce’s commentary on Capital Gold Sportstime. That’s how I experienced Anders Limpar’s four assists for Wrighty in 1991, Wrighty’s lob over Neville Southall the following year, and even Tony Adams’ title-clinching goal in 1997/98. We’ve given Everton a few batterings since then, too, including the 7-0 at Highbury in 2005 and a couple of 5-1 wins at our new home in the last decade.

More of the same would be lovely, although the Premier League rarely dishes out winning margins like that these days. A disciplined David Moyes side who’ve conceded just seven league goals won’t be inclined to hand out gifts. The Toffees have also had a whole week to stew on their 2-0 defeat to Chelsea, while we’ve been to Germany and back, picking up a nasty injury to our star player along the way.

As you might expect, Bukayo Saka was a hot topic at yesterday’s press conference. Arteta sounded pretty pessimistic, despite the winger still needing another MRI scan and further specialist opinions. I know we shouldn’t speculate on such matters (unless we have English Literature degrees), but I’d be surprised if we see him again this year.

It does raise uncomfortable questions about how we’ve managed him. Asked whether Saka is less robust than he used to be, Arteta pointed to the demanding schedule.

“He’s certainly been different in the last year or so, but the demands that go into the players are immense with club and international competitions, and the times between games are getting narrower. There are less than 66 or 67 hours between games, and every hour is important. The support would be really appreciated, and it’s the same for every team.”

He has a valid point. But isn’t it also our responsibility to manage him through that schedule? Did he need to start nine of the 12 games we’ve played this season?

Remember, he went all the way to the final weekend of the World Cup, then played the last half-hour of the Community Shield less than a month later. I know schools of thought differ on these matters. Players want to maintain their rhythm, managers want their best players on the pitch, and so on. But he was missing games with an Achilles problem in April and was still managing it Stateside, where he had to sit out England matches.

Saka remains a phenomenal player. It just feels as though the minutes he played between breaking through as a 17-year-old and the hamstring injury that required surgery in December 2024 may have caught up with him. That’s just my feeling. Understandably, Arteta sees it differently.

Asked if Saka is burnt out, the manager said: “I don’t have that feeling at all. But certainly what we have to be very mindful of is to create the right ecosystem around a player to maximise his qualities. We need to understand what he’s capable of sustaining when he comes back, whenever that is.”

Ever the pragmatist, Arteta has already told Saka that, whatever he’s dealing with, the team still needs his energy around the place.

“I’ve already told him, go in that building and be the first one there with a good spirit and energy because that’s what the team needs. If everybody is like, ‘Bukayo, how are you? Are you sad? Are you OK?’ That’s exactly what we don’t need.

“It is what it is. This is part of our job to have this, to have some nice and bad results, a defeat, a problem; we need to deal with that. We cannot change that now. What we certainly can dominate is the narrative from here to the Everton game, together with the best energy and the spirit to go and win the game again.”

When it comes to who can fill in for Saka, options are thin on the ground. It looks as though Noni Madueke’s brief flirtation with the left flank is over as quickly as it started, or else it’s Max Dowman. You’d expect the former to start today, with the latter getting minutes against Fleetwood Town in the League Cup on Tuesday. Given Max’s dramatic contribution against the Toffees earlier this year, though, you can’t rule out an appearance this afternoon. Running the ball from the halfway line into an empty net is a decent audition.

Elsewhere, Arteta confirmed Piero Hincapie is available after his recent ankle problem, giving him the option to hand Riccardo Calafiori a breather. Ben White could cover for Jurrien Timber, while Myles Lewis-Skelly, Mikel Merino and Viktor Gyokeres will be champing at the bit to start after coming off the bench in Munich. There’s a balance to strike between resting the likes of Rice and Bruno and giving their new partnership time to bed in. I expect some, but not lots of rotation.

Having spent much of this season warming the bench, Martin Zubimendi must be wondering how he gets back into the side. He was our second-most-used outfield player last season, so it’s quite a change in circumstances. Arteta touched on his situation yesterday:

“He had a few [opportunities], then he had the [ankle] injury, and it’s true he had one game where he was almost fully available to play some minutes, but not to start the game. And from now, he needs to earn the right to play. I need to find the right games to give him the opportunity, and I know when I do that, what his response is going to be. But he’s been exceptional, whatever the role that he had to assume. And I expect that from every player. And he’s a player that deserves more minutes at the moment.”

From what I can tell, Everton arrive with a completely clean bill of health, which is pretty remarkable given their squad is so small. After joining from Arsenal with an Injury, Christian Norgaard was fit enough to make his long-awaited debut last weekend. I was watching at home, and to say he looked off the pace would be an understatement. He was comically slow, like he was wearing tree trunks for boots.

Speaking yesterday about the cameo, David Moyes said: “When we brought him on, we realised he’s a little bit further away than we hoped…Christian was the first one to come to me and say he was sorry about it.”

Once today is out of the way, let’s hope he gets up to speed, any kind of speed, because he seems like a nice fella. The Dane isn’t the only reunion on the cards, we’ll also welcome back Ainsley ‘the Bird Catcher’ Maitland-Niles. Honestly (and I know it’s wishful thinking), but if we’re a few goals ahead in the dying seconds, I’d be up for conceding a cheeky penalty just to see what he does.

At this stage of the season, it’s about putting points on the board, even if the usual tension is dampened somewhat by Manchester City’s impending relegation to Hackney Marshes. The league ‘leaders’ face Aston Villa in the early kick-off, but whether they win 10-0 or lose by the same scoreline, they currently exist like a turd that can’t be flushed.

To be fair, given the weather and the state of the UK’s godforsaken water companies, flushing them would probably only ruin some poor swimmer’s day. So maybe we can wait a bit longer.

I hate to segue from such grotesque imagery to the recently departed Mr Friar, but I believe the club will be paying special tribute to our life president this afternoon. There have been plenty of warm words and anecdotes about his incredible service to Arsenal, and it’ll be good for those of us heading to N5 to pay our respects together.

That’s about it from me. As you’ll know by now, Andrew M has been out of action for a couple of days, so the rest of the team will do its best to bring you as much of the usual matchday coverage as possible. I don’t yet know whether that will include a Live Blog or Player Ratings, but remember, the new Arseblog app on iOS and Android has plenty of new features, including live match updates. There’s just less swearing…

Right, I’m off to find my wetsuit. Come on you Gunners!
