#543. The Qubitunes Soundstage Toy Is a Great Gift for Kids

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Most parents worry about exposing their kids to too much screen time, but it can be tough to find alternatives that children find entertaining and that might be beneficial. Enter Qubitunes, a screen-free play stage and speaker for kids. Content comes in wooden cartridges that you slot in the top, and kids can place wooden character pieces on the circular soundstage, divided into four sections, to change the audio it plays.

This seemingly simple design allows for interactive stories that change based on the characters and spots your kids choose, music that can be layered up with different instruments, and themed games and quizzes. I’m a huge fan of Qubitunes, and it has a deserving place in my guides to the best STEM toys and speakers for kids. As Black Friday and gifting season approaches, the Qubitunes starter kit is one toy to keep an eye on for discounts.

I played with Qubitunes for hours when it arrived and really enjoyed it. The stereo speakers are decent, there's a headphone port, and it can be recharged via USB-C for up to six hours of play. It has since gone into my wife’s classroom, and it’s usually the first thing picked when kids have finished their work and it’s choosing time. It’s very popular with 7- and 8-year-olds, but kids aged 3 years and up (all the way up to adults) will potentially get a kick out of this.

There is a caveat: the need to purchase content separately. These add-ons cost between $10 and $20 each. Stories, games, and other options come in wooden blocks that slot into the device so you can download the content. You need a Wi-Fi connection for the initial content download and firmware updates, but then you can play offline. They usually come with three or four character blocks and a labelled bag to keep them in.

My top pick is the Little Maestro set ($20) featuring players in an orchestra covering Mozart, Vivaldi, Beethoven, and Bach. There are also stories about quirky characters, such as The Groblies ($20), a fun cooking game called Bernard’s Kitchen ($20) with different ingredients, and various themed soundscapes and quizzes. I prefer activities where there’s some creativity or a learning game involved, but the stories and soundscapes are good for very young kids. The interactive nature of these sets elevates the experience beyond passive listening and provides replay value, as kids experiment to discover what happens when they move and replace pieces on the stage.

Qubs has also recently released a Maestro expansion ($10) with four new composers, a Jurassic Sounds Dinosaur Game ($20) with facts and T-Rex roars, and versions of Little Red Riding Hood and Hansel & Gretel, among many others. If you like the idea of sustainable, screen-free toys, the maker Qubs also made a clever wooden car STEM toy called Cody Block ($70) that introduces kids to basic coding concepts, and it’s on sale right now, down from $100.

#2058. Bayern Munich insiders were worried about 'workaholic' Vincent Kompany weeks before Man City legend's training ground collapse, new report claims - as German giants confirm boss will be out for 'weeks'

Published: 09:55 GMT, 24 October 2026 | Updated: 11:35 GMT, 24 October 2026

Bayern Munich insiders had expressed concern for Vincent Kompany weeks before their manager's collapse at the club's training ground, a new report has revealed.

The German giants confirmed on Saturday that Kompany, 40, will be out for weeks after he was rushed to hospital on Friday following his collapse while watching the second team training.

He had earlier taken training for the first team ahead of their Bundesliga clash against Freiburg on Sunday. The session was then abandoned, BILD said, with those present said to be 'deeply affected' by the incident.

The Man City legend has been suffering from a viral infection which is said to have caused problems with old scar tissue in his hip, leaving him in pain and with difficulties climbing stairs.

Kompany, who won four Premier League titles while captain at the Etihad Stadium, is understood to be in a stable condition.

According to BILD, several figures at the German giants had noted the 'workaholic' Belgian's lack of work-life balance, claiming he was getting too little sleep, eating irregularly, barely exercising, drinking excessive amounts of coffee, and taking on too much work.

Bayern Munich insiders were reportedly worried about Vincent Kompany before his collapse

Kompany is pictured with his wife Carla at Oktoberfest in Munich last month

Bayern Munich chief Max Eberl provided an updated on Saturday, confirming Kompany is stable, but his recovery may not be a swift one.

He said: 'The good news is: He's stable. I spoke with him last night, as well as with his wife.

'That was no pleasant moment for any of us; he collapsed on the pitch yesterday and will have to stay in the hospital for a few more days until everything has been checked and verified.

'This once again shows that health is the most important thing in life—not football.

'Therefore, the best wishes for a speedy recovery from all of us go out to him; we at FC Bayern stand together.

'The most important thing is that the cause is found and that he gets fit again. That he only comes back once he's fit.

'Vinnie will be given all the time he needs. We're not starting to build any timeframes.

'We need to figure out what it is, and then we'll discuss what recovery time means.

'We won't be talking about days, but weeks. We all understand that. And we have such a fantastic coaching staff, with Vinnie at its head.

'But the success is based on an outstanding team behind the scenes. There's absolutely no thought of changing anything.

'All the guys who are here are outstanding – and therefore we can all approach the next few weeks in the spirit of Vinnie, which everyone has within them.'

After Wednesday night's 2-2 draw against Arsenal in the Champions League, Kompany admitted that he hadn't listened to his body while battling his viral infection.

'As a coach, you always want to do everything. I caught a pretty nasty virus last week, and of course, I didn't listen to my body, and then at some point, my body just said: 'That's enough!''

Kompany is deeply embedded in the entire football operation at Bayern Munich, with the report adding that he has been the driving force behind the construction of new XXL privacy screens at the training ground to prevent outsiders from spying on sessions.

Assistant coach Aaron Danks will be standing in for Kompany for Sunday's game, but suspects it will be difficult to force his boss to take an extended break.

'It's really not easy to get Vincent to rest,' he said. 'That's a challenge – you know him as a person! He's a true leader and works hard. But he has a virus, and it's important that he rests.'

Kompany had been spectating the reserve taam with their new coach Dante, 43, before collapsing 20 minutes into the session.

It is understood that most players were back in the changing rooms when the incident occurred but those who witnessed it were shaken, and the reserve team's game against DJK Vilzing on Saturday has been called off.

Kompany's viral infection caused issues with his hip that left him struggling to stand for long periods of time and was thoroughly examined by team medical staff earlier in the week and was given a pill by one of the doctors early on in the game against Arsenal.

BILD claim insiders noted Kompany's lack of work-life balance leading up to the episode

Bayern said in a statement: 'Vincent Kompany had to seek medical treatment on Friday after collapsing during training for FC Bayern's amateurs.

'The first-team coach had been watching the second team's session. Kompany will undergo thorough examination in the hospital and remain in medical care. For the time being, his coaching staff will manage training and match operations.'

Bayern's sporting director, Max Eberl, added: 'The health of Vincent Kompany is our utmost priority. At FC Bayern, we will do everything to ensure he recovers quickly.

'Vinnie is receiving all necessary medical care and support, as well as all the time he needs. His coaching team, which we fully trust, will take over his duties. All of us at FC Bayern wish Vinnie a speedy recovery.'

Kompany's former club Manchester City were among those to send a message of support to the Belgian following the incident. City wrote on X: 'Everyone at Manchester City is wishing a speedy recovery to our former captain and Club legend, Vincent Kompany. Get well soon, Vinny.'

The Belgian was captain of Man City during some of their most successful years under Pep Guardiola but was regularly ruled out through injuries.

Kompany moved to Munich in 2024 and has won two Bundesliga titles with England captain Harry Kane as his star player in attack. He has three children with wife Carla, who he met in Manchester.

His viral infection was the talk of the build-up before Wednesday's Champions League match.

Kompany's assistant coach Aaron Danks said on Tuesday: 'It has affected his legs, yes. He has had pain in his legs.

'The most important thing for him now is to rest and I don't know if he will be ready tomorrow or in two or three days' time.

'Getting him to rest is hard. He's a leader. But it's a virus and it's really important that he rests.'

Bayern are top of Germany's Bundesliga after four wins and two draws in their opening six matches. They lie 14th in the Champions League's league phase after a win and two draws.

Juventus will travel to Lecce without Zeki Celik and Nico Gonzalez after both players picked up muscle injuries in Thursday’s 2-2 Europa League draw with Rennes, adding two more names to an already long absence list for Luciano Spalletti.

The club confirmed the diagnoses in an official medical update on Friday, October 23. Both players had felt muscle discomfort after the Rennes match and went for tests at J|medical that morning.

According to the club, Celik has a low-grade injury to the biceps femoris in his left leg, while Nico Gonzalez has a low-grade injury to the soleus muscle in his right calf. Juventus said both players have already started their rehabilitation programmes, but the statement did not give a return date for either of them.

Supporters will also have to wait until matchday to hear from the coach. Tuttosport reports that Spalletti, in agreement with the club, will not hold his usual press conference on the eve of the Lecce game. The paper links the decision to the packed schedule after the Europa League fixture, and Virgilio Sport notes that Spalletti has taken the same approach before during congested spells, with no controversy behind the choice.

The squeeze on the squad is now the bigger story. With two more first-team players ruled out and the injury list still growing, Spalletti will need his remaining options to carry the load through a busy stretch of Serie A and European fixtures before the international break.

Tyrell Malacia has revealed he was bombed out of Ruben Amorim's first team squad with no explanation, as the Man United flop opened up on his nightmare spell at Old Trafford.

Malacia, 27, enjoyed a strong debut campaign under Erik ten Hag following his £14.6m arrival in 2022, before a complicated knee injury left the full-back sidelined for 18 months.

By the time he returned to full fitness, ten Hag had made way for Amorim and Malacia had been shipped out on loan to PSV Eindhoven for six months, returning for pre-season in 2025 hoping for a fresh start.

However, before a ball had been kicked at Carrington, United announced that Malacia, along with Marcus Rashford, Antony, Alejandro Garnacho and Jadon Sancho, would train separately from Amorim's squad and had no future at the club under the Portuguese.

Malacia, who now plays for MLS side FC Cincinnati, is still none the wiser as to the reasoning behind his exile.

Tyrell Malacia has revealed how Ruben Amorim brutally exiled him from his Man United squad

Malacia, who now plays in the MLS, was shunned by the former United boss last summer

'I don't know where that came from, I don't know if it was Ruben or higher up or anything, but I never said anything about it, I just kept working and doing my thing even though it was difficult sometimes training in a group of five,' he told BBC Sport.

'At some point Marcus, Garnacho and even Antony left. Then I stayed on my own, training with the second team, even with the under-18s and below. It's tough. It's tough mentally.

'I could have said 'OK, I'm not coming into training'. But I thought 'that's not going to help me'. It's a reason for them to say 'this is the reason you are with them or training on your own'. I just kept my head down and did the work.

Despite this, Malacia had his heart set on a January exit and held frank discussions with incoming interim boss Michael Carrick over his future.

'At the end of the day everybody is there for themselves. I'm there for myself obviously in the first place and then obviously the team but also the manager is there for himself, he needs to save his job or do his job well.'

Malacia featured off the bench away to Newcastle on March 5, and was given the opportunity to say farewell to United fans against Brighton on May 24.

'Still, I don't regret anything,' he said. 'I had a great time in my first season and the people who worked there and the players I met there were amazing, so, nah, I really enjoyed my time.'
