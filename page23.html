From the start of his second term, the president has taken aim at the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (Noaa), slashing staff levels, canceling contracts and grants, and shutting down databases and research laboratories. The agency that has long been considered a global crown jewel in climate science and forecasting, has lost roughly 20% of its staff since January 2025.

The National Weather Service, which is housed within Noaa, has also taken a hit. Weather stations have had to delay data collection and cut operating hours, with hundreds of meteorological and other technical positions still vacant across its 122 forecast offices.

The effects have already been felt at Noaa, where a chaotic and volatile environment has sent morale plummeting, increased burnout and hampered vital work. While capacity has quietly unravelled, a large-scale disaster will quickly expose the most dangerous gaps.

It’s already climbing off the charts and is expected to strengthen through the northern hemisphere’s autumn and winter.

Along with dangerous spikes in temperature, El Niño will supercharge storms in parts of the American west, raise sea levels in California by up to a foot, and set the stage for debris flows, landslides and flash floods.

“Whether we as a country can handle the kind of coast-to-coast extremes that could potentially happen this winter is a big question,” Di Liberto said.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Volkswagen’s latest recall now extends to Audi, affecting more than 9,000 Q4 E-Tron and Q4 Sportback E-Tron vehicles. And just like with the ID.4, owners are being told to limit charging to 80% of the battery’s capacity.

Earlier this month, Volkswagen issued a recall impacting 22,524 ID.4 electric SUVs, model years 2023 to 2026.

The recall is due to the risk of thermal propagation, or a battery fire, under certain conditions when charged to over 80% of its limit.

Volkswagen said in a safety recall report with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) that “certain charging profiles combined with a state of charge over 80% may trigger thermal propagation.”

While the root cause is unknown, Volkswagen urged owners to limit charging to 80%. Owners of affected vehicles received an email saying VW “intends to apply a temporary vehicle setting that limits charging to 80%,” unless they opted out by October 21.

In the letter sent to the NHTSA on October 23, Volkswagen Group of America said it was issuing a recall affecting 9,048 Audi Q4 E-Tron and Q4 Sportback E-Tron vehicles model years 2024 to 2025.

The safety recall follows Audi’s Product Safety Committee being informed of battery thermal events involving other Volkswagen Group EVs that use the same batteries as the Audi Q4 E-Tron.

Following an internal investigation, Audi found the same battery and production characteristics as the impacted VW Group vehicles. Audi said the majority of these were produced for the North American market.

Audi is not aware of any claims, crashes, fires, injuries, or deaths associated with the Q4 E-Tron’s battery issue.

The root cause has yet to be determined, and until a remedy is available, Audi is advising owners to limit charging to 80%.

Owners can take their vehicles to an authorized Audi dealer, where the dealer will run a diagnostic check on the battery. If needed, the dealer will impose a temporary state-of-charge limit to 80%.

Audi expects to mail out interim letters notifying owners of the safety risks on December 18, 2026. Additional letters will send additional letters once a remedy is available.

Owners can contact Audi’s customer service at 1-800-253-2834 with questions or concerns. Audi’s recall number is 93SK. You can also contact the NHTSA Hotline at 1-888-327-4236 or visit nhtsa.gov for more information. The NHTSA campaign number is 26V698.

Volkswagen’s battery recall doesn’t just affect electric vehicles in the US. Last week, Volkswagen Group issued three separate recalls with the German Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA), affecting 72,668 EVs worldwide, including 9,881 in Germany.

The recall affects certain batches of the ID. Buzz, ID.3, ID.4, and ID.7 models produced between 2022 and 2025, as well as Cupra Born models from 2022 to 2024.

#3410. Where Will Jazz Pharmaceuticals Be in 5 Years? Oncology Is Becoming Its Growth Engine

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Could Jazz Pharmaceuticals (JAZZ +0.53%) turn over a new leaf? Over the past five years, the company has faced various challenges that have contributed to unimpressive top-line growth. But the drugmaker is increasingly pushing into oncology, which has helped improve its financial results. Does that make Jazz Pharmaceuticals an attractive stock to own through the next five years? Let's find out how the company might perform through 2031.

In the second quarter, Jazz Pharmaceuticals' revenue grew by 16% year over year to $1.2 billion. That was a fairly strong performance for the company, and several products contributed to that. For instance, Jazz's Xywav, a medicine approved to treat cataplexy in narcolepsy patients, posted sales of $471.2 million, up 13.5% year over year. Xywav has done a good job of replacing Jazz Pharmaceuticals' Xyrem, a medicine approved across Xywav's indications and once the company's best-selling medicine.

Xyrem started facing generic competition in the U.S. in 2023, and it no longer contributes much to Jazz's results. Other key growth drivers for the biotech company include Epidiolex, a medicine approved to treat seizures associated with certain rare forms of epilepsy. Epidiolex's second-quarter revenue grew 16% year over year to $292.1 million. Epidiolex appears to have a solid medium-term outlook. It likely won't face generic competition within the next five years, and Jazz Pharmaceuticals is seeking to launch it in other countries, including Japan.

But Xywav's prospects are a bit more uncertain, as the company faces competition from Lumryz, a medicine approved for cataplexy in narcolepsy patients with a more convenient once-nightly dosing schedule (Xywav is taken up to twice nightly). Xywav also continues to compete with generics for Xyrem. The drug has performed fairly well despite that, largely because of its lower sodium content (high sodium levels can cause serious health complications).

Still, given that this medicine accounted for about 39% of Jazz Pharmaceuticals' top line in the second quarter, competitive threats could weigh on the stock until the company successfully diversifies its portfolio. That's what it is doing thanks to its oncology lineup. In the second quarter, Jazz Pharmaceuticals' oncology revenue increased by almost 32% year over year to $362.3 million.

The company's Zepzelca, a lung cancer medicine, was the best-selling product in the oncology business. It posted sales of $105.8 million, up 42% year over year. The question is whether Jazz Pharmaceuticals' oncology segment can continue performing well over the medium term.

Jazz Pharmaceuticals is running more oncology clinical trials than in any other area, and one of its more promising products is called Ziihera. Jazz Pharmaceuticals first earned approval for this medicine in the U.S. in 2024. In August, this therapy earned a label expansion to treat certain patients with gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. Ziihera is also being tested across at least half a dozen other potential indications.

Ziihera did not contribute much to sales in the second quarter, which isn't that surprising, given it hasn't been on the market for very long. But with its recent label expansion and potential additional indications, it could become a meaningful growth driver for the company. So could some of Jazz Pharmaceuticals' other candidates. The company earned U.S. approval for Modeyso, a medicine for diffuse midline glioma, last year, and it is also undergoing other clinical trials that could support label expansions.

Jazz's recent oncology launches look promising, and the company's deep pipeline offers significant opportunities to expand its market presence and boost sales growth, potentially allowing it to diversify away from Xywav. By 2031, the company could have a much stronger approved lineup that generates consistent sales and earnings growth. Does that make Jazz Pharmaceuticals a buy right now? My view is that the stock is attractive at current levels for investors willing to hold onto its shares for at least five years. In addition to Epidiolex, which could help drive sales growth for a while, and its improving oncology lineup, Jazz is trading at just 10.9x forward earnings, which compares very favorably with the 19.2x average for healthcare stocks.
