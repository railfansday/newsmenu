Nvidia's RTX 5090 has been a subject of much speculation recently, as it was rumoured to be discontinued last month when it vanished from the company's official marketplace, only for Nvidia to confirm it wasn't discontinued. The plot has thickened, though, as AIB partners—the companies actually designing and manufacturing RTX 50 series graphics cards—suggest even they can't get their hands on the parts required to make the card.

This is according to an Nvidia AIB partner who spoke to YouTuber Der8auer. They say, "No, Nvidia did not inform us that RTX 5090 is EOL. But currently we cannot purchase GB202 [the GPU] together with 32 GB GDDR7 as [a] complete set, and we also don't know if ordering will be possible again in the future."

The unnamed board partner clarifies that in previous shortages, they could place backorders with Nvidia, but this is "currently not possible anymore."

This is all to say that this AIB partner is unable to get the GPU and memory required to make the RTX 5090, and doesn't know when that supply issue will be fixed. Though this isn't the card being discontinued, for many partners, not getting the GPU and VRAM is as good as doing so.

Whether it's the GPU or the memory that's causing the biggest headache isn't specified, though in the current context, one can assume it's likely both to some degree.

As of right now, companies are reliant on GPU and VRAM bundles from Nvidia before they can make their own cards. There have previously been rumours that Nvidia would stop bundling its GPUs and VRAM together, but even if it did that, this would only partly solve the issue of the memory crisis for Nvidia. Those who might have been able to buy just the GPU would still be subject to a price squeeze on the VRAM when trying to source it direct from one of the major memory manufacturers.

Der8auer also spoke to an RTX 5090 cooler manufacturer who said, "Two of our partners already reduced quite much the order forecast for RTX 5090 based heatsinks in Q1 2027, when compare[d] with Q3 2026."

Although World of Warcraft: Forever is still in Beta, racial abilities and traits have been a hot topic in the WoW community since, well, forever – and Blizzard's recent update to them is no different, adding fuel to the seemingly eternal fire.

Every race has its own unique abilities, known as "racials" for short. For instance, modifiers to damage and the like. Folks are never quite happy with how they play out, though, it seems, with players on both sides expressing discontent in some way or another – but Blizzard's well aware. That's why, as per the developer's official forums, the studio is opting to shuffle them around a bit while prioritizing "racial identity."

"Some players have expressed that some of the new racial traits feel too generic, while others have raised concerns that some are objectively stronger than others," the devs write. "In taking a final pass on these, we've prioritized racial identity, which means that some races will naturally be slightly more effective than others at certain combat roles." And, uh, it's safe to say that this "natural" effectiveness isn't going over well with fans.

The changes, as Blizzard lists them out, affect Humans, Dwarves, Tauren, Orcs, and the Skyborne. Some of them see buffs moving from one to the other, like the Tauren losing its 1% hit chance modifier but Humans gaining it – and that's where the drama starts. Players see this as a slight against the Horde faction. Over on Reddit, you can find them saying as much, with one comment reading, "Why even play Horde at this point?"

Another fan adds, "LMAO the bias by these devs is unbelievable. They take 1% hit away from Tauren and give it to Dwarves. What a joke." Right underneath, somebody else says, "Blizzard has no idea what they are doing with these racials" – and I see a good few replies echoing this sentiment. It's not just there, either. A different thread sums up the entire issue as a "favoritism" problem from the World of Warcraft studio. "Racials are NOT fine," it reads. "The favoritism towards alliance is getting ridiculous…" It's important to note that not everyone agrees here, however. I see plenty of people arguing that this isn't a matter of "favoritism" but rather what Blizzard itself explains – that some races are more well-equipped for certain forms of combat and the like. As one player puts it, "Alliance has always been the better PvE side," while the Horde is superior in PvP.

Others simply state that they feel it's fair as this is "how Alliance felt for 20 years," clapping back that Blizzard previously favored the Horde. Honestly, my head hurts just reading back and forth between the discussions, but I understand both sides. It's tough to strike a proper balance that'll please everyone, though. I'm happy with what Final Fantasy 14 did… you know, making the racial buffs and whatnot essentially obsolete.

I doubt that'll ever happen in World of Warcraft, of course, but it'll be interesting to see how this conversation develops as WoW: Forever releases into the wild early next month – and how Blizzard chooses to react in regards to updates, too.

Harry Kane says he has no plans to return to the Premier League to topple Alan Shearer’s goalscoring record, and has instead set his sights on winning the Champions League with Bayern Munich and a major trophy with England.

Kane left Tottenham three years ago after scoring 213 Premier League goals, and he remains second on the list of all-time scorers behind Shearer, who scored 260.

The move to Bayern has been vindicated by two Bundesliga titles and a German Cup in his three seasons at the club, having scored 156 goals in 159 games, and his talents have finally been recognised after winning the Ballon d’Or in London on Monday evening.

Asked whether he might want to return to England to overtake Shearer’s mark, Kane said: “Not at the moment, not right now. I’m loving life in Munich. Bayern are such a great club. We have an amazing team, a great coach [Vincent Kompany]. So yeah, right now that’s where my focus is, and I’m extremely happy, and to be winning awards like this just shows why I made the move in the first place.”

Kane is 33 but is determined to keep playing at the highest level into his late 30s, and says his biggest goal is finally winning a trophy with England after a series of near misses over the past eight years.

“There’s always ambition, there’s always dreams for new achievements, new things. I still have a lot to achieve in this game – the Champions League, from club level, and to win a major trophy with England, it’s my number one priority for sure.

“There’s so many great things I want to achieve, and I’ve learned in a career, in life, you don’t really have much of a say. You just have to go with the flow, see where it takes you, believe in what you’re going to do, like I think making the move, and then just go full gas.

“Right now it’s with Munich, and we’ll see where that takes me.”

Kane spent the evening celebrating with family and friends in London before returning to Munich on an early flight to train with his teammates ahead of a German Cup game on Wednesday.

Kane said he spoke with his manager, Kompany, on the morning of the Ballon d’Or ceremony. Kompany is being treated in hospital for a viral infection after collapsing during a training session.

“He actually phoned me this morning, bright and early at 9am,” Kane said. “I was just out of bed, thankfully, so I was able to answer it. He just wanted to wish me all the best, good luck.

“I asked him how he was doing, he said he feels good, he’s on the mend. I know he’s the type of character who just wants to be back with the team already, he wants to be out on the training field with us, on the bench with us. But it’s important that he rests. He’s been going through a bit of a tough time with his health recently, and we can hopefully cover it, and the staff can cover it whilst he’s not there.

“It will be massive for me to go back to Munich, to see him, all the staff, all my teammates. I’ve spoken about how much it is theirs as well as it is mine, so just to celebrate with them a little bit will be special.”

#3743. 9-1-1: Nashville's Hunter McVey Recalls Significant Weight Loss Amid Filming

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

9-1-1: Nashville‘s Hunter McVey is getting honest about how his “ever-changing” relationship with his body has influenced his career.

“It’s been a very interesting and changing relationship with health and how I view my body over the years,” McVey, 27, exclusively told Us while promoting his business, Bring Your Nutrition.

McVey recalled how he was “always insecure” as a child.

“Being heavier and bigger than everybody else, I always hated what I saw in the mirror. I was always self-confident and never liked the way I looked. Even when I would get skinnier or in shape or lose weight, I would be critiquing myself on, ‘Oh, I need to be skinnier around the waist,’ or whatnot,” he explained. “It’s not something that a lot of men talk about.”

The actor has heard similar stories from other men, saying, “I’ve talked to a lot of men over the years as well that are in the gym that look good and the funny thing is, the more and more you’re in the gym or in tune with your physique or your fitness, a lot of times the body dysmorphia continues to increase. Because then you’re being more picky and more picky.”

After booking his role as Blue on 9-1-1: Nashville, McVey’s relationship with his health evolved once more.

“Before I started filming 9-1-1: Nashville, I was about 225 pounds and that was mostly solid muscle. Because of how busy the 9-1-1 season was, I wasn’t able to eat as much, and instead of training six days a week, I could maybe train two. I went down to about 200 pounds,” he shared with Us. “I lost about 25 pounds of muscle all over. If you were to tell me that two years ago, I probably would have freaked out, because I tied a lot more of my value and my self worth to how I looked on the outside.”

He continued: “Now, I’ve just only — through experience and many, many years — been able to let myself know that I’m valuable even though I don’t have that extra 25 pounds of muscle on me. I still need to and like to stay in shape — obviously, not only for the camera, but for my peace of mind. The gym and fitness does a lot for my mental health, but I don’t tie my self worth, my identity and my confidence to how big or muscular or ripped I am, because if you compare yourself to others — especially in the age of social media — it’s a losing battle.”

McVey used his own experience with health and wellness as inspiration in his professional life.

“[It is] what I attribute some of my successes, discipline and good skills that have transferred to other areas of my life since I was 12. It was the first time I lost 40 pounds and started counting calories, macronutrients and going to the gym and working out,” he continued. “I had bounced up and down multiple times throughout my youth and through college. Then after school when I started doing social media and showing people how I’d lost weight, how I put on muscle, the transparency between between hormones, peptides and coaching people on how to improve their lives and some of the things that improved my lives, I wanted to create a business that provided value for people and helped people. That was one of my biggest goals. Because you can only post shirtless for so long on Instagram.”

In addition to starring on TV, McVey has been working in Bring Your Nutrition, which is set to launch a non-toxic food storage container line made from platinum silicone.

“The vulcanization process of that silicon allows these containers to heat up food in the microwave or the oven without leaching any toxins or microplastics into your food. Most Americans are consuming about a credit card worth of microplastics every single week and from many different sources,” he explained to Us. “Now, we’re finding that those microplastics are endocrine disruptors and we’re seeing in younger and younger people all of these issues — diseases, cancers, neurological disorders and problems — stemming from a lot of these microplastics.”

He added: “They’re meal containers that you can travel with and store your food in. The lid seal is perfect and keeps everything in there. You can bring them to the gym and they don’t break or they’re not heavy like glass. Then you’re not getting any toxins or any microplastics in your food when you heat them up in the microwave or in the oven up to 425 degrees Fahrenheit.”

McVey is proud of the career milestone, which comes as he has balanced acting with other responsibilities. “I’m focusing on doing the best job that I can with 9-1-1: Nashville, with being the best husband I can and with running the businesses the best I can,” he said.

The model also acknowledged, however, that finding balance isn’t always easy.

“I’ve always thrived in the chaos a little bit. I’m lucky that at this point in my life and in my 20s I have the energy and the push and the drive and the ambition to [start my own business]. The most important things that I prioritize in my life are time and family, so I figured that if I can spend the majority of my 20s building what I want to build then I can set myself, my wife and my family up,” McVey told Us. “I attribute the balance in being able to do what I do all to my wife Julia because I don’t know and haven’t met anybody else that could handle what my schedule is like on a daily basis. The support that I have out of my partner is how I’m able to keep going and how I’m able to do this and I wouldn’t be able to do it alone. She recognizes the goals that we have in mind of time and time with family and she knows that I enjoy these things and what makes me happy is creating. I’m very passionate about it and I love what I do, so that makes her happy.”

#4737. Nasdaq-100 comes onchain as Janus Henderson and Centrifuge launch first licensed fund

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Janus Henderson, a London-based asset manager overseeing roughly $500 billion, is bringing one of Wall Street’s best-known stock indexes onto blockchain rails through an issuer-sponsored fund. The launch comes amid an escalating debate over who gets to tokenize traditional securities.

The firm teamed up with tokenization platform Centrifuge to launch JQQQ, a fund tracking the Nasdaq-100 Index, home to tech giants including Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft. The fund is issued on the Stellar XLM$0.1853 blockchain, the companies said Tuesday.

JQQQ follows the introduction of a similar onchain index fund product last year, the Janus Henderson Anemoy S&P 500 Fund (SPXA), which was the first tokenized index fund licensed by the S&P Dow Jones Indices.

Janus Henderson said its onchain investment platform now includes more than $1 billion across Treasuries, structured credit and equities.

The launch marks another step in Wall Street’s push to bring traditional investments onchain, expanding beyond U.S. Treasuries, money market funds and private credit into stock-market products. It also highlights a growing divide between funds issued onchain by their managers and third-party tokens created without the underlying companies’ involvement.

JQQQ follows the issuer-sponsored model. Janus Henderson issues and manages the fund, Centrifuge provides the blockchain infrastructure and Nasdaq licenses the index to track.

That differs from third-party stock tokens, which may be backed by shares held in custody or structured as separate instruments tracking stock prices. Investors in those products don't necessarily own the underlying shares or receive shareholder rights.

The distinction has become central in the debate around tokenization efforts. Most recently, 22 companies objected to plans for blockchain-based trading of their shares on the OKX-ICE venue, adding to a debate over whether issuers should have a say when their stocks are tokenized.

“As investors explore new ways to access markets through digital infrastructure, demand continues to grow for exposure to established, transparent benchmarks,” said Sean Wasserman, head of index product management at Nasdaq.

The fund also shows how tokenization is moving beyond digital versions of existing investments toward products built to operate on blockchains from the start.

Such funds could eventually allow investors to use their holdings as collateral for loans or move them between blockchain-based financial applications, subject to regulatory requirements and platform support.

"Tokenization becomes much more powerful when the assets investors want to own can also become building blocks for an onchain financial system," said Anil Sood, chief strategy and growth officer at Centrifuge.

Hyperscalers’ interest in on-site and nearby energy storage has converted into firm orders this year, the global energy consultancy said last week in its latest industry outlook.

Energy storage deployments have far outpaced solar capacity additions in recent years, with the global cumulative installed solar-to-storage ratio falling from 94:1 in 2016 to 7:1 in 2026, BNEF said Thursday.

BNEF sees the capacity ratio stabilizing at 4:1 in 2030, and annual deployments remaining steady at 3:1 thereafter, as the energy storage industry matures.

Much of the new solar and storage capacity is colocated. The United States had installed 20.7 GW/66.4 GWh of energy storage projects colocated with 41.3 GW of solar by the end of 2025, about 30% of total colocated solar-plus-storage capacity, according to BNEF. Some 4 GW of storage was colocated with solar in the U.S. in the first nine months of 2026, the group said.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, which extended federal tax credit eligibility for energy storage through 2035 while sunsetting solar eligibility later this decade, will support a pipeline of 83.7 GW/166.1 GWh of energy storage to be paired with 149.2 GW of solar, BNEF said.

The robust U.S. pipeline comes despite the Trump administration imposing trade barriers BNEF characterized as world-leading, including import restrictions on foreign-made power conversion systems and recent broad limits on imports of bulk power system equipment. The combined duty on stationary storage batteries imported from mainland China is 40.9%, according to BNEF.

“The US has been making the most aggressive [protectionist] efforts by favoring the domestic battery storage industry while restricting the use of supply chains involving mainland China through the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and other policy measures,” the report said.

Though still a relatively small share of overall U.S. battery deployment, on-site data center energy storage is growing quickly, with 4.9 GW/7.4 GWh expected this year — up from 500 MW/1.4 GWh in 2025, BNEF said.

The group sees on-site data center energy storage deployments reaching 6.3 GW/8.4GWh in 2027 and continuing through the decade thanks in part to large-scale deployments like the 2.6-GW battery array NextEra Energy plans to build alongside a 2-GW gas-fired power plant at a $100 billion proposed Brookfield Asset Management computing campus in Kentucky.

Multiple battery suppliers have announced firm commitments from data center customers or utilities serving them this year, BNEF said. Fluence Energy inked a $300 million deal with a data center operator for behind-the-meter batteries earlier this year, while Canadian Solar subsidiary e-Storage said in March it would deliver 500 MW/2.5 GWh of energy storage to “a major U.S. utility” in 2027 to support data center grid infrastructure.

Cumulative data center energy storage could reach 36 GW/66 GWh in 2036, much of it on grids overseen by the Electric Reliability Council of Texas, PJM and the Midcontinent Independent System Operator. Data centers in PJM are more likely to use energy storage for load shifting than behind-the-meter gas due to tighter restrictions on power plant emissions in the region, BNEF said.

Though lithium-iron-phosphate will remain the dominant chemistry for new energy storage deployments over the next 10 years, sodium-ion alternatives will begin to eat into its market share, reaching 9% penetration by 2036, BNEF said. Sodium-ion batteries can be passively cooled, reducing parasitic energy load, and may be more durable than lithium batteries over a 20-year service life, proponents say.

Led by Google, data center operators are experimenting with alternative chemistries that can discharge power for longer than lithium- or sodium-based batteries, including a proposed 10 MW/100 MWh zinc battery in West Virginia and a 300 MW/30 GWh iron-air battery in Minnesota. But those efforts are too early to include in deployment forecasts, BNEF said.
