#3823. Taylor Swift Celebrates 20 Years Of 'Taylor Swift'

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Forget for a moment the Taylor Swift we know now: The sold-out stadiums, the Hollywood friendships, and the Easter eggs scattered with extreme precision. Before she became a pop star capable of turning every album into a global event and every ex-boyfriend into a potential hit song, there was simply Taylor Swift the sixteen-year-old, with a cascade of blonde curls, an acoustic guitar, and a remarkable ability to recount her teenage dramas in a compelling, best-selling way.

Taylor Swift sings the National Anthem as the Detroit Lions host the Miami Dolphins on Thanksgiving Day, November 23, 2006.

On October 24, 2006, Swift dropped her first, eponymous album, Taylor Swift. Eleven country-pop songs behind a cover with a distinctly 2000s aesthetic, from a Pennsylvania girl with her sights set on conquering Nashville. Little did she know that, twenty years later, she would have conquered practically everything else.

People has retraced the album’s origins, unearthing anecdotes that seem straight out of a teen movie script. It all began in Wyomissing, Pennsylvania, where the future star grew up on a Christmas tree farm. Swift's childhood was tailor-made for a Hallmark movie—but the future pop star already had other plans. In 2004, with Swift was 14, the family moved to Tennessee to support her musical ambitions. Soon thereafter, she became the youngest songwriter to sign a contract with Sony/ATV Music Publishing.

Taylor Swift sings the National Anthem on opening day at Dodger Stadium on April 9, 2007.

There’s one detail that, looking back on it today, reveals a lot about the future queen of pop. Before releasing her album, the singer had secured a development deal with RCA Records, but when she realized that the path proposed by the label might mean more waiting and less creative freedom, she decided not to proceed. A bold choice for a young girl who hadn’t yet released an album. But even then, Swift knew exactly what she wanted: to sing her own songs, tell her own stories, and make her own decisions on what her voice should sound like.

A meeting with Scott Borchetta changed the course of her career. The music executive was preparing to launch Big Machine Records, and he immediately recognized Swift's potential. Swift was his new label's first signed artist, and the rest, as they say, is music history. But it’s also a great lesson in stubbornness applied to business.

Scott Borchetta and Taylor Swift on September 19, 2011.

Among the tracks on her debut album is “The Outside,” which Swift wrote when she was just 12 years old. The theme? Loneliness, feeling different, the sensation of watching the world from the outside while everyone else seems to know exactly where they’re going. This is where we catch a glimpse of one of the secrets to her success: Taylor Swift has never tried to recount extraordinary experiences, but rather ordinary ones—often painful and embarrassing—which she transforms into songs that millions of people find relatable. A formula that, twenty years later, continues to work. Loves, cities, and musical genres may change, but that girl who felt left out has never really stopped writing.

At first, the sales figures pointed to a promising artist, not a global phenomenon. Taylor Swift sold about 40,000 copies in the first week, but sales grew steadily thanks to country radio and word of mouth. The album remained at number one on Billboard’s Top Country Albums chart for 24 weeks. Five singles were certified platinum in the United States.

Taylor Swift accepts the award for Breakthrough Video of the Year for the song "Tim McGraw" at the 2007 CMT Music Awards on April 16, 2007.

Twenty years have passed since October 24, 2006. Since then, she's garnered fourteen Grammys, shifted her musical genre, gone on record-breaking tours, and fought a long battle for control of her own recordings. In 2026, Swift also became the youngest woman to be inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame. An honor that seems to bring things full circle for that stubborn little girl who dreamed of performing on stages around the world from her farm.

A tribute to the 20th anniversary has appeared on the singer’s official website, featuring confetti and photographs from her various eras. So while fans the world over celebrate her first album, they're also scrutinizing every detail for clues on the release of her next album. The singer has already revealed that she rerecorded her debut, and the date would certainly be auspicious for a big reveal.

As of publication time, however, no new release has been announced, leaving fans to quote Swift's 2010 song, “Sparks Fly.” “And I could wait patiently,” Swift sang on that track from Speak Now, “but I really wish you would drop everything now.”

For a man recently described as a ‘dead man strutting’, Alvaro Arbeloa now has an extra spring in his step. Few fortunes in life lurch quite so violently as a football manager’s but, even for the Fulham boss, this resuscitation is quite something.

Fulham started the day second from bottom in the Premier League table, even after their first victory of the season against Hull, but now have back-to-back wins thanks to Oscar Bobb’s late winner, sit 12th in the table ahead of Tottenham’s game tonight, and just three points off the top six.

That strutting dead man, as one dressing room source called Arbeloa to Daily Mail Sport recently, is very much alive.

For a game so sorely lacking in quality, you always got the sense that it would only need one sliver of it to decide this wet, cold, turgid contest in the West Midlands. For all the mountains of possession Fulham had, for all the little openings they created, they rarely looked like scoring after Josh King hit the woodwork early on and Shea Charles forced a save in the 13th minute.

There was one passage of play when Fulham kept the ball for what seemed like an eternity, passed it around, only for Timothy Castagne to lump it out of play.

Oscar Bobb created the most chances all game and, for his late winner, did it all himself

Bobb dribbled into the box, pulled out all the tricks and stepovers before waltzing past Jack Rudoni and curling it into the far corner

But if there was anyone ever likely to provide it, it was going to be Bobb. The former Manchester City winger created the most chances all game and, for his late winner, did it all himself. He dribbled into the box, pulled out all the tricks and stepovers before waltzing past Jack Rudoni and curling it into the far corner for his first league goal for Fulham since joining in January.

'When you are losing it's difficult to say you are doing so many things well but always we have believed we are on the right track,’ said Arbeloa.

'Sometimes you need to win games because of the quality of your players, it was a good game as a team but the quality of Oscar in one moment decides the game and it's important for us because we have players who can make the difference.'

Boos rang out from the home supporters as the final whistle blew at the CBS Arena. They knew how big this game was, the first of two back-to-back home games against Fulham and Sunderland, the only two sides below them before a ball was kicked.

Once again, though, they discovered the gap between the Championship and the Premier League. They simply cannot dominate matches as they did on their way to promotion last season.

And when they did have their moments, they just couldn’t make it count. Of their 13 shots, only two were on target.

‘We didn’t have the zip at the top end of the pitch like we would want,’ said Coventry boss Frank Lampard.

‘If you want to find a bit more, it’s not always there for you.’

The lack of cutting edge in the final third made it even more strange to see Lampard leave striker Ellis Simms, who scored in the win against Newcastle, out of the squad.

Of all the players who perhaps looked most likely to bridge that gap to the top flight for Coventry, it was captain Matt Grimes. The skipper was so frequently the talisman and tempo-setter in the Championship but is struggling to stop games passing him by.

Bobb breezed past him early on to set up Shea Charles for a shot. He overhit a routine pass to Jay Dasilva that went straight out of play. Coventry don’t have enough of the ball to allow him to dictate play and, when they do get hold of it, he often finds himself with it in between the centre-backs, and without the ball struggles to have the physicality to wrench the game back into his side’s favour.

One man who has proven that he can make that step up is central defender Bobby Thomas. The man mountain boasts some of the best defensive stats in the Premier League and has been a rock for Lampard’s men so far this season. He even had a chance in the first half when he put that massive head on to a Jack Rudoni cross only to head wide.

Thomas, though, picked up a problem early in the first half, tried to continue, but soon sat down on the pitch and limped off before the hour was up. Lampard said he was waiting to find out how bad it was. His replacement Stephen Mfuni went close with a header from a corner but, with a massive home match against Sunderland coming up, his absence will be a hammer blow for Lampard.

If Fulham’s form is to continue, they need more from striker Gonzalo Garcia, who must have wondered what on earth his career has come to as he trudged around the sodden grass, wet to the bone. He used to play for Real Madrid, for heaven’s sake.

Not that you’d know it from his recent performances. He didn’t muster a single shot on target, had the fewest touches of any of Fulham’s starters, including his goalkeeper before being dragged off with 15 minutes to go with the game still level.

Arguably one of their best defenders, he was always going to be difficult to replace and Udinese opted for a veteran here, getting David Alaba on a free transfer after his Real Madrid contract expired.

The Austrian veteran is still being eased into things, but there’s no doubt that his experience could be crucial moving forward. Other notable signings included Mergim Vojvoda from Como (a really tidy player), Lazar Jovanovic from Stuttgart and Idrissa Gueye from Metz.

But okay, even if the summer business perhaps left a little to be desired overall, few expected Udinese to start the season like this. They have won just one game so far and even worse, they have lost all of their last five league games (!). That’s shocking form.

They did manage a draw against the hyped Como at the start of the season, but their win came against another struggling side, namely Monza. In short, something clearly isn’t clicking and Udinese have confirmed that Runjaic now has three games to prove his worth. Talk about pressure.

Shifting focus to the squad status, it’s not fantastic for Runjaic as the aforementioned Alaba suffered a muscle injury in the 3-0 defeat to Lecce. He won’t be available, as such, and the same goes for the new midfield signing Gueye, the right-back Alessandro Zanoli, and the forward Bayo.

Two changes are expected in defence, with James Abankwah and Matteo Palma coming in, while Jakub Piotrowski looks set to replace Oier Zarraga in midfield. Other than that, it will be the expected Udinese with key players like  Jesper Karlström, Jurgen Ekkelenkamp and Kenan Davies.

Oumar Solet is in the predicted eleven right now, but Runjaic did warn that he might not make it due to a muscle problem.

Predicted Udinese XI (3-4-2-1): Okoye; Abankwah, Palma, Solet; Vojvoda, Piotrowski, Karlström, Kamara; Chakvetadze, Ekkelenkamp; Davis.

There have been 109 encounters between Milan and Udinese so far, which will make Sunday’s clash the 110th. The Rossoneri have a pretty clear advantage so far with 48 wins, compared to Udinese’s 24, but there have also been a high 37 draws.

That said, if we look at just the last four seasons, there have been zero draws in this fixture: eight games with five wins for the Rossoneri and three for Udinese. There have been many goals as well, with 27 scored in those eight games (average of 3.38 per game!).

The most recent clash broke a bit of a trend as Udinese ended a four-game winning run for Milan in the fixture, beating them 3-0 at San Siro. If we focus on the latest Friuli clash, however, the Rossoneri prevailed with the same scoreline after goals from Christian Pulisic and Youssouf Fofana.

➤ Udinese and Milan have not drawn any of their last eight Serie A matches. In that period, Milan have won five times and Udinese three times. In the most recent game, the Friuli side prevailed 3-0 on April 11.

➤ Although Milan have scored 24 goals in their last 13 Serie A matches against Udinese (an average of 1.8 per game), they failed to find the net in their most recent one. The last time the Bianconeri kept two consecutive clean sheets against the Rossoneri was between 1993 and 1994 (two 0-0 draws).

➤ Milan have won their last three Serie A away games against Udinese, with an aggregate score of 10-2; only once have they managed four consecutive away wins against this opponent in the competition, between 1999 and 2001.

➤ Udinese have lost each of their last five Serie A matches, with an aggregate score of 5-13. The last time they recorded more consecutive defeats in the tournament dates back to the period between February and April 2018 (11, under Massimo Oddo).

➤ Milan have drawn each of their last three away games in Serie A; the last time they recorded four consecutive draws on the road in the competition was in April-May 2017, under Vincenzo Montella.

➤ Udinese have lost five of their seven Serie A games this season (W1 D1); only three times in their history in the competition have they recorded more defeats in the first eight games of a season: in 1960/61 (six), 1961/62 (seven) and 2017/18 (six).

➤ Ruben Amorim is the third manager to remain undefeated in his first seven Serie A games with Milan, following Hector Puricelli in 1955 (undefeated in his first eight) and Luis Carniglia in 1963 (undefeated in his first 13).

➤ Only Troyes (-9) and Racing Santander (-9) have a worse goal difference than Udinese considering only the second halves of the five major European leagues currently underway: -8 for the Friulians (like Borussia M’gladbach) – two goals scored and 10 conceded after half-time.

➤ The only time Jurgen Ekkelenkamp has both scored and provided an assist in a Serie A match was on April 11 against AC Milan. The Udinese player, who has yet to find the net at home in the current campaign, is the midfielder with the second most shots attempted in this season (20, behind only Nico Paz’s 35).

➤ Adrien Rabiot has scored or provided an assist in each of his last four Serie A appearances (three goals and two assists). The only Milan midfielders to have been involved in at least one goal in five consecutive Serie A games since this data was available (since 2004/05) are Hakan Çalhanoglu (in July 2020) and Kaká (in October 2004 and May 2008).

Starting with Milan, Pulisic ran riot in this fixture last season and his quality will certainly be needed amid the heavy absences. We all know how much of a difference he can make for the Rossoneri and against a struggling Udinese at the back, he will surely aim for goal.

Another player I would keep an eye on is Maignan. It hasn’t been the easiest past few days for him after the red card against Bournemouth and the subsequent (shameful) racial abuse. Udinese away hasn’t always been kind to him on that front either…

For the home side, I’m a huge fan of Mergim Vojvoda and he has caused problems for Milan before, especially when he played for Torino. He has a very good shot and will need to be kept away from opportunities on the edge of the box.

Ekkelenkamp also deserves a mention after his stellar performance at San Siro last season. Of course, he’s yet to score this season as pointed out by the stats, but he has the quality to help Udinese make life a bit more difficult for Milan.

We can talk about Udinese’s poor start as much as we like, but the reality is that this won’t be an easy one for Milan, or at least I don’t think so. The absences have reached the point of being overwhelming and although there are still some pillars to rely on, it could be a closer affair than initially expected.
