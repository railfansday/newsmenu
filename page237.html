#4824. The Emerging Design Trends From High Point Market Fall 2026

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

When it comes to surviving High Point Market’s packed agenda of previews, panels, and cocktail parties, sometimes you need a boost. I was standing in the Century showroom, checking out designer Windsor Smith’s new Greco-Roman Anthelion collection, when I pulled out a ginger juice shot I had carried on the plane from New York. Suddenly, Smith herself was beside me, raising her own bottle of ginger juice. “This is my High Point secret weapon,” she said, introducing me to local juice shop Organic AF (a 5-minute drive from IHFC). “It’s my go-to, pre-Market stop.” We clinked bottles and downed our energizing elixirs.

That boost came in handy. Fall Market’s cold-weather curse, layered with gusty winds and rain, was in full effect over the weekend. Luckily, there was plenty of excitement indoors. Oohing and ahhing could be heard at Hudson Valley Lighting, as the company relaunched Schoolhouse (acquired in Food52’s bankruptcy auction earlier this year), returning much-needed color, pattern, and coziness back to the Portland, Oregon-bred brand. And new-ish exhibition halls 313 Space and Broad Hall corralled a smart mix of fresh vendors and non-furniture players like Monogram, Artistic Tile, Cosentino, and Miele—generating some of the most compelling displays at Market.

Read on for the emerging design trends, industry chatter, and fresh perspectives coming out of the furniture fair this fall.

The Rainey sectional by Four Hands is wrapped in a walnut burlwood.

The Stephen Church collection for Theodore Alexander

Richly grained, high-contrast burlwood has emerged across aesthetics. Whether crafted into a traditional Biedermeier silhouette or a low-slung Modernist coffee table, this distinctive material is one designers of all stripes seem to agree on. As Adam Dunn, senior vice president of design and creative director at Four Hands, told me, “Anything with high-figure, deep piles, and an energy that makes you want to walk up and touch it—that’s in.” On the way out, he added, are “really flat, dull veneers—anything that’s a plain, straight-grain material that doesn’t have any movement.”

Four Hands is doubling-down with the Rainey sectional, an elegant ellipse-shaped seat wrapped in walnut burl. Meanwhile, Theodore Alexander satisfies traditional tastes with its new Stephen Church collection, in which the English cabinetmaker employs jaunty okoume, khaya, and satinwood veneers.

The metallic moire High Society wallcovering, part of Donghia’s new Skyline collection, was a High Point Market standout.

Many of this season’s collections took a note from the S/S27 Paris runways, where Anthony Vaccarello’s Saint Laurent farewell glittered in gold and a high-shine mesh halter dress won raves at Schiaparelli. Hudson Valley Lighting brought the glamour with its Hollis Hills pendant, featuring scalloped capiz shells radiating a golden iridescence, while Bernhardt introduced a burnished gold finish for the desk and coffee table in its new Chatham Park collection. In an edgier take, Four Hands dreamt up an acid-wash antiqued brass finish for several SKUs in its 30th Anniversary capsule. One of the biggest Market standouts was Donghia’s Skyline wallcovering collection, articulating artisan techniques in alluring, cosmopolitan style. From the metallic moire High Society to the tonal jacquard weave of Rolling Hills, digital lookbooks simply don’t do these designs justice.

At 313 Space at High Point Market, Little Green celebrated Marle (355), its 2027 Color of the Year.

There was a lot of playing-it-safe when it came to palettes—a prevalence of muted blues and greens (known bestsellers) with doses of beet, butterscotch, and chocolate. Regina Andrew launched its antique brass Veiller lighting collection with fan-pleated shades in Olive, Mist Blue, and Oyster colorways. Little Greene celebrated its 2027 Color of the Year, Marle (355), a quiet, reflective brown. And while he wasn’t launching any new SKUs this season, Thomas O’Brien refreshed his always-inspiring Century vignettes in the shades, revealing Silken Pine and Fresh Brew as Benjamin Moore varieties that he returns to time and again.

The Veiller sconce by Regina Andrew, shown in Olive.

Brown Jordan’s outdoor palette of Butter (a yellow), Canyon (terracotta), Harbor (gray-blue), Bamboo (a crisp green) felt especially spot-on. The hues—a capsule of the outdoor furniture maker’s 25 frame finishes—shined on the latest clean-lined Vero collection (Brown Jordan’s more affordable line). But the real treasure was seeing it on the neoclassical Venetian Collection, designed by Richard Frinier in 1987 and still a brand bestseller today. Exciting news for outdoor living: The company just announced that it’s expanding its furniture color palette to the cabinetry range for Brown Jordan Outdoor Kitchens.

A first look at the Pooky x Samuel & Sons collaboration, a 13-piece lighting collection that launches of November 3.

Across many of the new collections, the sentiment from creative directors was clear: We’re seeing a shift away from precision and perfection toward textures and techniques that reveal the artisan’s touch. “I think we’re all craving a sense of humanness,” said designer Anne-Marie Barton, who launched several richly tactile new fixtures for Visual Comfort & Co, of emerging design trends. Sean Lavin, chief design officer for Visual Comfort, echoed the sentiment. “Our factory team are saints given the amount of times I had them readjust panels and re-weld marks,” he said, pointing out how the company’s new faceted brass and alabaster pendants reveal their joinery welds. “That’s the maker’s hand.”

The Margaux flush mount, part of the Pooky and Samuel & Sons collaboration.

This return to craft resonated at Morris & Co., which previewed its very first embroidered grasscloth (available to the trade in January), and at lighting maker Pooky, whose dashing new collaboration with passementerie house Samuel & Sons officially launches on November 3. Artisan tassels and trimmings elevate pleated silk shades in punchy hues of coral, light blue, and emerald green. The Margaux flush mount, bordered in Samuel & Sons’s handwoven Crete trim, was a High Point crowd-favorite, said Chief Creative Director Jo Plant. Hinting at collections to come, she emphasized the lighting maker’s focus on all things artisan. “We’ve also been bringing out a lot more ceramics, reactive glazes, and textural forms. It feels a bit handmade, and people understand that not every piece will look the same,” she said. “They appreciate that it’s an artisan process.”

Ronaldo subsequently left Portugal’s training camp in Copenhagen on September 30, triggering disciplinary proceedings. He later apologized to his teammates and supporters, acknowledged responsibility for his actions and stated that he would accept the consequences imposed by the federation.

The disagreement became particularly sensitive because Jesus had previously coached the veteran superstar at Al-Nassr. Their professional relationship had appeared successful, making the breakdown in communication an unexpected complication for the Selecao.

#3395. Cristiano Ronaldo’s 2008 season leads Lionel Messi by a huge margin in Ballon d’Or of All Time vote

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Cristiano Ronaldo’s 2008 season leads Lionel Messi by a huge margin in the Ballon d’Or of All Time fan vote.

Cristiano Ronaldo is far ahead of Lionel Messi in the Ballon d’Or of All Time fan vote. France Football opened a public poll to crown the best Ballon d’Or season in history, and Ronaldo’s 2008 award is dominating the standings with just days left to vote.

According to the official Ballon d’Or website, Ronaldo’s 2008 season leads with 75% of the votes, a huge margin over the rest of the field. Messi’s 2012 season sits second with 9%, while Ronaldo’s 2017 award is third with 6%. The vote is a fan initiative to celebrate the passion of supporters, not an official trophy, and the percentages could still change while the poll remains open.

Ronaldo’s 2008 campaign is the one fans have chosen as the standout. He was named the world’s best player after winning the Premier League and the Champions League with Manchester United, claiming the European Golden Shoe and scoring 42 goals in all competitions.

With the 2008 recognition, Cristiano became the third Portuguese player to win the award, after Eusebio in 1965 and Luis Figo in 2000. Despite his huge lead over Messi, fans can keep voting until Oct. 27, and the final results will be published on Oct. 28 on the official Ballon d’Or website.

How the Ballon d’Or of All Time vote works

Fans are not choosing the best player in history, but the best season that earned a Ballon d’Or, and each vote costs one euro. That means a player’s career is divided into separate options, which hurts those with more awards. Messi appears eight times, for his awards in 2009, 2010, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2019, 2021 and 2023, so the votes for his career are split among several seasons.

Only players who won the award at least twice are on the ballot: Ronaldo, Messi, Ronaldo Nazario, Marco van Basten, Michel Platini, Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, Kevin Keegan, Franz Beckenbauer, Johan Cruyff and Alfredo Di Stefano.

The result adds another chapter to the long-running debate between Ronaldo and Messi, two players who shaped an era and combined for 13 Ballon d’Or awards. Whether the gap holds or narrows, the poll shows that for many fans, Ronaldo’s 2008 season is the one that stands above the rest.

#389. Cruise lines chase luxury travelers with suites for up to $25,000 a night

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The latest push into high-end travel is taking place on the high seas, as cruise lines increasingly lean on luxury offerings.

On Regent's Seven Seas Splendor, an all-luxury ship, cabins cost anywhere from $1,800 to $15,000 per night for its top-of-the-line booking, the Regent Suite.

The difference is not just the roughly 5,100 square feet of space. The top-tier suite has a Steinway piano, a private dining room and a wraparound deck with a hot tub and a view comparable to the one from the ship's bridge.

Multiple passengers wanted the suite for the Splendor’s 151-night global cruise that embarks in January, Regent told CNBC, and it is almost always occupied.

Regent is setting its sights even higher, as the cruise line plans to debut the Seven Seas Prestige in December, its first new ship class in a decade. Its top suite will cost $25,000 per night.

While megaships with surfing and waterslides can carry more than 7,500 passengers, the smaller, elite ships — some carrying fewer than a thousand passengers — are powering profits for companies like Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings. The company owns luxury lines Regent and Oceania.

Norwegian CEO John Chidsey has said he's focused on sharpening the luxury brands' positioning, improving the products and guest experience, and strengthening their commercial performance.

Regent and Oceania, he said, are in good shape for 2027, though Chidsey did not break out metrics between the companies.

Cruise competitors are also betting big on luxury offerings, investing in new ships and more specialized experiences.

Regent competes with established luxury cruise lines including Royal Caribbean's Silversea and Carnival's Seabourn, as well as newer entrants carrying hotel names into the market. Four Seasons, Aman and Orient Express are among the brands bringing luxury hospitality to yacht-style vessels.

The cruise industry projects the luxury cruise fleet will grow from 104 ships in 2026 to at least 130 by 2036.

And while global cruise passengers are forecast to increase about 11% between 2025 and 2028, according to the Cruise Lines International Association, or CLIA, the industry's investment dollars are increasingly flowing to luxury and expedition brands, with orders rising for smaller ships and fewer berths through the next decade.

From 2020 through 2025, there has been a 71% increase in the number of luxury ships and an 84% increase in berths, according to CLIA. The orders indicate another 26% jump in the number of ships through the end of 2029 and a 32% increase in capacity.

Other hospitality companies are investing and expanding their uber-upscale offerings, too. Hotels, casinos, airlines, restaurants, spas and other travel and leisure companies see the trend: Affluent customers remain willing to pay for more space, personal service and distinctive experiences.

United Airlines this year launched its larger Polaris Studio suites with privacy doors and caviar service. American Airlines increased its lie-flat capacity and reported on Thursday that its premium passenger unit revenue rose 18.5% in the third quarter, compared with 14.4% in its main cabin.

Marriott’s second-quarter results showed luxury revenue per available room growing faster than the broader hotel business in the U.S. and Canada, rising 9.1% year over year, compared with 5% for all Marriott hotels in the region. In June, CEO Anthony Capuano told CNBC there's an “almost insatiable appetite” for luxury travel.

Wynn Resorts, which caters almost exclusively to high-end customers, has opted to maintain its high room rates in Las Vegas even as visitation has slowed over the past year, rather than filling every room with travelers looking for a bargain.

Asked onstage at the Global Gaming Expo in September whether Wynn is extracting more profit from fewer customers, CEO Craig Billings said, "We will always focus on what's the most comfortable experience for the customer and where can we drive the highest possible EBITDA."

But offering luxury vacations doesn't guarantee a company's fortunes.

The Ritz-Carlton Yacht Collection, for instance, has struggled with occupancy despite major marketing campaigns and has accumulated nearly $700 million in losses since 2017, according to the Financial Times.

More competition in the space could undercut the perception of scarcity. As more companies add luxury products, they have to convince travelers that the experience is worth the price.

Las Vegas Sands reported earnings Wednesday that missed expectations for revenue and profit, hurt by the high-end segments in both Macao and Singapore.

"When the customers don't show up, it's a little problematic," CEO Patrick Dumont said on the company's earnings call.

Dumont said he couldn't point to any single cause for the softness, adding, "perhaps people are not feeling as good about the market," but he said he believes this quarter is an aberration.

CNBC asked Dumont during the NBA China Games in Macao on Oct. 9 whether luxury travel could become overcrowded.

In response, he pointed to the long-term wealth creation in Asia and said the challenge was making experiences customers want to return to.

“It’s not about, are there enough customers,” he said. “It’s, can we create enough of an interesting set of experiences where people find us indispensable in their lives.”

#1244. Bruno Fernandes' rebellion in Como underlines Manchester United's never-ending problem

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

"Sire, it is said that the Manchester United players are revolting."

And they're not much better on turf either.

The rabble inside the Old Trafford dressing room has been roused. Their rebellion will rage on until it ends the same way every other one does. The great ruler will dismiss himself of some unwise counsellor or another. Though it has been suggested he is safe for the time being, it is hard to see a future that does not end with sheriff Michael Carrick being run out of town. Chancellor Jason Wilcox will find himself in a hairy situation. The banishments will come with promises that the realm's most loyal subjects will never be so mistreated again. Their cries have been heard.

After all, the only shared trait of the eight men to have taken on a meaningful run of Manchester United games since Sir Alex Ferguson's retirement is that the players have turned on them. Bruno Fernandes' open disgruntlement of Michael Carrick is not even that unique, bringing to mind Cristiano Ronaldo's withering, petulant dismissal of Erik ten Hag two years ago.

The club that employed football management's last great authoritarian have spent more than a decade dripping the same message to the first team. The guy who picks the team, he is accountable to you. You will outlast him. He will bear the consequences of your actions. That is not to argue that Michael Carrick is remotely blameless for United's faltering start to the season, let alone the game plan at Como.

Fernandes was not wrong in his assessment that allowing Nico Paz, Martin Baturina and Jacobo Ramon the space and time to pick a pass was probably going to imbue a very capable opponent with confidence. He was of course wrong to air those concerns in front of television cameras. Far further beyond the pale, however, was what appeared to be Fernandes' refusal to play along to the plan that Carrick had assigned to his side.

With less than 26 minutes played, Fernandes failed to control a pass from Mason Mount as Ramon closed in, the sort of error that was at least partially on the receiver. Como now had possession on the edge of the attacking third. In response, the United captain threw his hands up in frustration. The camera drifted away from him for a moment, following the ball in a way that Fernandes wasn't.

When he returned to the shot, with Mount tangling with Baturina as he and Paz attempted to break into the area, Fernandes was still walking. Como's possession went backwards and yet United's most advanced player was looking the other way. Maximo Perrone is scanning the pitch as he receives possession, a little check over the shoulder presumably showing to the Argentine that the No. 8 in white was no threat. A second later, Fernandes is picking at his boots.

A third of the game was gone, and yes, United probably were playing to the wrong game plan. Then again, teams set themselves up in the wrong way all across the world every week and find a way to win football matches. Most of those do not have players of the quality of Fernandes, Bryan Mbeumo and Matheus Cunha. The collective tends to trump the individual in this sport but Fernandes is one of the best individuals. If he sets his mind to it, he can at least give his opponents an almighty challenge no matter if the circumstances are aligned against him.

"Whatever system you play, whatever tactics you play, if you don't run and you don't run forward and you don't run back, you aren't going to win," said Carrick afterwards, in what in that moment seemed to be his final press conference.

For the time being, he remains, due to face the media again ahead of Sunday's visit of Bournemouth to Old Trafford. Should United choose to make a managerial change, CBS Sports sources indicate that Antonio Conte would be open to talks over the vacancy.

Would anything really change with a new man in the dugout? Perhaps in the short term. There are quick tactical fixes that a Carrick successor could make, among them sorting out a shot diet that indulges the likes of Fernandes swinging from bad positions outside the penalty area.

Really though, bigger change is required than that which can be engendered by any head coach. If a leader as mild-mannered and measured as Carrick is unable to keep his players from venting their spleen on a weekly basis, then the dressing room is unsalvageable. That Fernandes had a point last week does not really matter.

Nor does it that there was barely a word misspoken by Lisandro Martinez last week when he called out director of football Jason Wilcox and those who employ him. Far be it for this column to argue against players speaking their mind to the media but a confident organisation should feel able to hold its employees to account. It has been suggested that United will activate the option in Martinez's contract to preserve his value. What message does that send to the next player who might have something to say about Carrick, Sir Jim Ratcliffe, the Old Trafford support or whatever else they take issue with? #5622. New Jersey may finally reverse its draconian e-bike law after massive backlash

📅 Oct 22, 2026  |  Category: Automotive  |  Read Source →

New Jersey lawmakers are already looking to walk back one of the country’s most controversial e-bike laws, just months after the sweeping new regulations took effect and sparked widespread backlash from riders, bicycle shops, and advocacy groups.

A newly introduced bill, S4830, sponsored by State Senator Raj Mukherji, would remove licensing, registration, and insurance requirements for low-speed electric bicycles with motors rated at 750W or less and a maximum motor-powered speed of 20 mph (32 km/h). This includes Class 1 and Class 2 electric bicycles.

The proposal would essentially restore bicycle-like treatment for these two classes of e-bikes, importantly including throttle-equipped models that make up the majority of e-bikes sold in the state and more broadly in the US, while maintaining stricter requirements for faster and more powerful electric bicycles.

The move comes after New Jersey’s previous governor, Phil Murphy, signed a sweeping e-bike law in January following several tragic crashes involving young riders.

That legislation, which took full effect in July, eliminated the state’s previous three-class e-bike framework and introduced licensing and registration requirements for all electric bicycles, including ordinary low-speed commuter models. Throttle-equipped and faster e-bikes also faced insurance requirements.

As we previously reported, New Jersey bicycle shops experienced devastating sales declines after the law took effect, with some reporting drops of 20% to 90%. Several businesses were forced to lay off employees, downsize, or even close entirely as e-bike sales and usage plummeted.

Meanwhile, riders faced confusing new requirements that made e-bikes considerably less accessible, which was particularly impactful for lower-income residents, people with disabilities, and those who rely on electric bicycles for daily transportation.

I own a Class 3 e-bike, but I also acknowledge that with its potential assisted speed of 28mph it's of a different breed than Class 1/2 bikes -- that's just too fast in crowded bike lanes or shared use paths.

The first priority should be to target reckless riding by anyone, and illegal bikes should be confiscated if they're involved.

The original law was intended to improve safety, but critics argued that it largely punished responsible riders instead of addressing the growing problem of high-powered electric motorcycles masquerading as bicycles. Only responsible riders actually registered their bikes as required by law, while others were deterred from cycling, and the hooligan riders who often gave e-bikes a bad name continued to ride illegally.

After all, requiring someone riding a 20 mph (32 km/h) commuter e-bike to obtain a driver’s license and register their bicycle does little to address reckless riding on machines capable of highway speeds.

The newly proposed legislation appears to acknowledge that distinction, offering a more targeted approach that could preserve reasonable safety regulations without making ordinary e-bike ownership unnecessarily complicated.

S4830 still needs to work its way through the legislature before becoming law, but for New Jersey’s struggling e-bike industry, it’s a promising sign that lawmakers may finally be listening. It is still widely argued that requiring electric bicycles from 21-28 mph (32-45 km/h) to require a driver’s license and registration is overly burdensome, but at least the state appears to be moving back toward the right direction.
