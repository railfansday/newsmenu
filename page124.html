#4848. An AI label alone no longer wins lenders, JPMorgan says

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

An AI label alone doesn't secure funding as lenders now weigh a firm’s business and financial viability, according to JPMorgan.

“The AI label alone is no longer enough to reassure lenders. Companies must show that they can finance investment, service their debt and eventually turn spending into cash flow,” Justin Biemann, global investment strategist at J.P. Morgan Private Bank, said in a report published Oct. 23.

Elon Musk issued a similar warning on Sunday, saying debt funding is drying up for AI neoclouds, firms whose revenue depends almost entirely on data centers.

That matters for crypto. AI has been one of the most potent narratives driving risk-taking across financial markets, bitcoin included. Bitcoin and Nvidia both bottomed within weeks of ChatGPT’s debut in late 2022 and have rallied significantly since. Instability in AI could therefore spill over into crypto.

These warnings come as markets grow uneasy over whether AI’s promise will show up on balance sheets. The tech-heavy Nasdaq has slid 6.7% to 29,263 from 31,360 since Oct. 6, with AI-linked firms such as Nvidia and Oracle falling more than the index.

The stress is showing up in credit markets too. The cost of insuring against defaults on the debt of several AI-linked firms has hit record levels. This insurance is called a credit default swap (CDS). The buyer pays a yearly premium, and if the borrower goes bankrupt or fails to pay, the buyer gets a large payout. The premium, also known as the spread, is quoted in basis points. At 100 basis points, insuring $10 million of debt costs $100,000 a year. A wider spread means investors want more compensation to take on the credit risk.

The scramble for CDS shows in volumes too. According to Morgan Stanley, weekly trading in CDS linked to bonds issued by Oracle (ORCL), SpaceX SPCX$148.60·Market Closed, Alphabet GOOG$316.85·Market Closed, Amazon AMZN$253.15·Market Closed, Meta META$620.09·Market Closed, Broadcom (AVGO) and Nvidia NVDA$217.91·Market Closed averaged more than $9 billion in September, up significantly from under $2 billion a week in July.

The picture is particularly worrying for the most debt-dependent firms. Without naming it, Biemann said one financing-dependent AI infrastructure company has taken on tens of billions of dollars in debt and other obligations. Its five-year CDS jumped to 1,100 basis points, so insuring $10 million of its debt would cost more than $1 million a year.

“Pricing at these levels implies near or above a 50% probability of default over five years, up from below 30% a year ago,” Biemann wrote.

Biemann pointed to Lehman Brothers as an example of CDS working as an early warning. Lehman’s CDS averaged less than 100 basis points in the third quarter of 2007 and topped 1,400 by mid-September 2008, when it filed for bankruptcy and rocked global markets.

Biemann said investors can view CDS today “as warning lights rather than countdown clocks.”

In other words, AI firms aren’t necessarily headed the Lehman way. Besides, activity in CDS tied to the large AI leaders looks more like hedging than positioning for a crisis.

Large AI leaders with strong cash flow pay 50 to 120 basis points for five-year protection, Biemann said. He said these levels “still look more like the price of portfolio insurance than a prediction of financial distress.”

Some established infrastructure borrowers have climbed toward 300 basis points. Protection on the broad investment grade market remains comparatively contained. “The concern is confined to business models that require debt markets to remain open while spending stays high and cash flow remains limited,” he said.

#610. Instagram is testing 90-minute videos

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Instagram is introducing longform video. Called Features, the new format will allow users to upload videos up to 90 minutes long.

"So many creators are producing longer videos, and they're using clips of those videos to drive discovery on Reels and help build their audience," said Instagram VP of Product Tessa Lyons, announcing Features in a video alongside Head of Instagram Adam Mosseri on Monday.

"Reels is still going to be a core part of Instagram. But when you want to go deeper with creators you love and the content they're creating, you should be able to do that on Instagram."

Longform video creators have typically favoured YouTube, which allows verified accounts to upload videos up to 12 hours long. In contrast, Instagram Reels are restricted to a maximum length of 20 minutes. Features aims to allow these creators to share their longer videos on Instagram as well, keeping their audience on the platform.

Features appears to focus on content shot in landscape, and will be available on desktop, the mobile app, and Instagram for TV. Lyons stated that part of the new format's purpose is to help populate Instagram's television app, which allows users to watch Reels and video content on their TV.

"We've been working on a TV version of Reels, and we know that to do that well, we need more than just short-form video," said Lyons.

"People might discover a long form video that they love by seeing a Reel that's a clip of the video when they're watching Reels on mobile, and then when they go at the end of the day and open their TV and open Instagram on TV, we'll know that they watched that Reel and we'll be able to say, 'Hey, do you want to finish watching…?'"

Though Instagram is introducing longform functionality, it doesn't yet offer creators the same financial incentives as YouTube. While Instagram creators are primarily paid through third-party brand partnerships and collaborations, the YouTube Partner Program gives YouTubers a cut of revenue from ads placed on their videos (55 percent for longform and 45 percent for Shorts).

At time of writing, the most liked comment on Mosseri and Lyons' post was from mental health content creator Divija Bhasin (@awkwardgoat3), who asked, "Why would anyone post an unpaid long video on Instagram if we can just post it on YouTube and actually get paid for the effort?"

In a statement to TechCrunch, an Instagram spokesperson said that it is exploring potential monetisation models, "including performance-based incentives." However, it isn't ready to announce any such plans, the spokesperson instead focusing on users potentially being able to find more brand partnerships by expanding their audience through Features.

"Over time, we think there will be compounding benefits for creators to tell new stories, reach new audiences, and find new partners, which should lead to many monetization opportunities," the Instagram spokesperson told TechCrunch.

Judging from Lyons and Mosseri's announcement video, Instagram Features is currently missing a lot of the functionality available on YouTube. While viewers can adjust the playback speed, features such as captions and chapters appear absent during this early testing stage. Of course, such functionality may be added later, with Instagram currently conducting small tests of Features in the U.S. and Brazil.

#3455. I tried Windows 11’s customizable right-click menu – and it’s a much-needed change

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The latest Windows Insider build lets you add, remove, and manage commands in the right-click menu so it's less crowded and confusing.

The right-click menu in Windows seems like a great idea. Quickly access different commands, settings, and apps by right-clicking a folder, file, or other item in File Explorer or on your desktop. But as you install more applications, the menu gets more disorganized and difficult to navigate.

Also: 9 free Windows apps I can’t live or work without – and 8 work on Mac and Linux, too

With Windows 11, Microsoft revamped the menu with a smaller and simpler layout. But this approach doesn’t work well either, as it often excludes commands and apps you frequently use. With another mouse click, you’re able to view the older-style menu, but that brings us back to the original problem.

Now Microsoft has introduced a customizable right-click menu that finally gives you control over the items that appear.

With the latest Windows 11 Insider Beta preview, you can try out the new customizable menu for yourself. You’re able to add or remove specific commands. You can move certain items to different spots. And you can include entries for certain actions, such as editing a text file with Notepad or opening an image in the Photos app. The goal is to reduce the clutter and confusion of the current menu.

To try this yourself, you’ll need to join Microsoft’s Windows Insider Program if you haven’t already done so. Choose the Beta channel and then just download the latest updates. Since these Insider builds can be unstable, don’t set this up on your primary PC. Instead, use a spare computer, a dual-boot setup, or a virtual machine. I run the Insider builds in a VM through VMware Workstation Pro.

With the latest update installed, open File Explorer and right-click on a folder or file. You’ll now see a new entry to customize the menu. Select that to tweak the context menu. Otherwise, go to Settings, select Personalization, and then click Context menu.

Also: Microsoft reveals customizable context menu for Windows users – how to try it out

First on the list, you can turn certain file actions on or off, such as Send to, Print, Create shortcut, Copy as path, and Rotate image. As always, the actions that appear are context-sensitive. So, the command for Rotate image appears only if you right-click an image file.

Next, you can choose which apps pop up all the time, either in the main menu or in a submenu. These include Copilot, Clipchamp, Notepad, OneDrive, Paint, and Photos. But the list will also incorporate any third-party apps you install, so you can easily decide whether or not they should appear.

Finally, under additional options, you can choose to display the standard file commands (Cut, Copy, Paste, Rename, Delete) either at the top of the menu or within the menu. You can elect to put cloud-storage actions in their own section, show the Customize menu command in the menu itself, and move the Properties command to the bottom of the menu. You’re also able to display a command to show more options, which displays the old-style menu.

Also: How to join Microsoft’s Windows Insider Program (and why you should)

“We’re rolling out an updated context menu experience in File Explorer, designed to feel faster, cleaner, and easier to tailor to the way you work,” Microsoft said in an article about the latest preview build. “The new design reduces top-level clutter, keeps commonly used actions easy to access, and adds a new Settings experience that gives you more control over what appears in the menu. As we continue refining this experience with Insider feedback, our goal is to make right-click feel more responsive, predictable, and personal.”

I’ve been trying the new menu, and it certainly offers a much-needed change to the otherwise cluttered and complicated right-click menu. However, I’d like to see Microsoft eventually get rid of the kludgy old-style menu completely. Otherwise, you’re still stuck with the dual-personality approach that gets in the way of using the current menu more smoothly.

Also: Latest Windows update disables key security setting – but there’s a workaround

Since the new customizable menu is currently in the Beta channel, it’s one step closer to its official debut. That time frame will depend in part on the feedback from Windows Insiders. To share your own thoughts about the feature, open the Feedback Hub in Windows and select the My Feedback option. If the overall feedback is positive, we could certainly see the new menu reach all Windows 11 users before the end of the year.

#1973. World Cup double and hat-trick heroics: The three Harry Kane performances which won the 2026 Ballon d'Or

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Harry Kane has been crowned the 2026 Ballon d’Or winner, becoming the first Englishman in 25 years to lift the prestigious award.

The England and Bayern Munich forward enjoyed a superb campaign for club and country, netting 73 goals in all competitions.

Kane is the first Englishman to win the Ballon d’Or since Michael Owen in 2001 and just the fifth in history after leading England to the World Cup semi-finals and winning a domestic double with Bayern.

The 33-year-old beat Kylian Mbappe, Lamine Yamal and Lionel Messi to the Ballon d’or in front of a star-studded list of attendees at the London Palladium.

Here, Standard Sport looks back at the three performances that propelled Kane on to Ballon d’Or glory...

England were on the brink of an embarrassing early World Cup exit when they found themselves 1-0 down to DR Congo in Atalanta.

That was before Kane took the game by the scruff of the neck, scoring twice in the space of 11 minutes at the end of the second half to secure England’s place in the last 16.

The England captain headed the Three Lions level, connecting with Anthony Gordon’s inswinging cross in the 75th minute to give Thomas Tuchel’s side a lifeline.

The game appeared to be going to extra time until Kane pulled out a thumping right-footed effort from the edge of the box in the 86th minute.

The man for the big occasion, Kane then scored in the dramatic last-16 victory over Mexico to draw level with Gary Lineker as the England player with the most World Cup knockout goals.

Kane was at the centre of arguably the most entertaining game in Europe last season as PSG beat Bayern Munich 5-4 in the first leg of the Champions League semi-final in Paris.

Bayern took the lead when Kane scored from the penalty spot in the 17th minute before showcasing his playmaking capabilities by dropping deep to assist Luis Diaz with an inch-perfect pass in behind.

On a night when Bayern went toe-to-toe with the eventual winners, Kane led the line with aplomb as the Bundesliga giants came close to completing a remarkable comeback.

Kane’s strike in the French capital also made him the first English player to score in six consecutive Champions League outings, surpassing Steven Gerrard’s run of five.

Kane scored 10 goals in six DFB Pokal games last season, netting his first goals in a major final for club or country, with a hat-trick in the final to secure a 3-0 win over Stuttgart.

Cup competitions are often an opportunity to rest key players. But such is Kane’s importance to Bayern, he started and scored in every round, including crucial opening goals in the quarter-finals and semi-finals against RB Leipzig and Bayer Leverkusen.

His second-half treble against Stuttgart settled an even contest and marked an historic night for Kane.

He opened the scoring in the 55th minute with a stooped header from Michael Olise’s right-wing cross before doubling Bayern’s lead with a superb swivel and shot inside the 18-yard-box with 10 minutes left.

Kane completed his hat-trick, the fifth of a goal-laden campaign, from the penalty spot in added time to seal Bayern’s domestic double in style.

#3738. A Rod Serling War Story Led To One Of Paul Newman's Most Underrated Movies

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Arnold Laven's 1956 military courtroom drama "The Rack" isn't terribly well-remembered these days. If it's remarked upon at all, it may be for its performance from Paul Newman in one of his earliest film roles. It's also notable because its screenplay, by Stewart Stern, was adapted from a 1955 episode of the theatrical anthology series "The United States Steel Hour," which was penned by Rod Serling. Said episode, also called "The Rack," originally starred Marshall Thompson in the central role of Edward H. Hall Jr., a U.S. military Captain who has been accused of collaborating with the enemy during the Korean War.

In the movie version of "The Rack," Newman plays Captain Hall, while Walter Pidgeon plays Hall's father, a retired colonel. Hall returns from the war, having survived in a POW camp for a spell, and is mourning the loss of his brother, Peter, in combat. It's soon revealed that Captain Hall is about to be court-martialed for collaborating with the enemy. When Hall's father confronts him on the matter, he instantly admits that yes, he did collaborate with the enemy.

During the subsequent trial, it's revealed that Hall followed enemy instructions, making speeches and signing documents for them. Other American soldiers, meanwhile, withstood torture rather than giving aid to the opposing side. Hall is accused of being a coward. He wishes to plead guilty. Naturally, there is more to this story than initially implicated. The climax of the movie then reveals the horrors that Hall suffered at the hands of the enemy. However bad you think it is, dear readers, it's probably worse. Newman's performance is frank, tormented, and very good, and "The Rack" itself is one of the many underrated Paul Newman movies you should check out.

The "The Rack" was clearly designed to explore the horrors of war. When one goes back to the original Rod Serling teleplay, one can clearly see that "The Rack" is meant to be a condemnation of war violence, detailing just how horrible combat and wartime torture are. One can further read an essay on the Rod Serling website detailing Serling's PTSD and how he feared becoming a monster after fighting in World War II. Men his age had a unique set of phobias and concerns about combat. "The Rack" was clearly a means to explore Serling's fears.

The hour-long 1955 TV version of "The Rack" has been handily archived by the UCLA Film & Television Archive and can be accessed on YouTube. One will see immediately that the TV version is far more melodramatic than the movie iteration and features bigger, showier speeches. "I sold my soul for a filthy blanket that stunk of fish and urine!" Marshall Thompson's Captain Hall yells. "And three hours of uninterrupted sleep! And you know what else, Colonel? At the time, I felt it was one hell of a bargain!"

Thompson is a more demonstrative actor than Paul Newman, so one cannot even really compare their performances. It's like night and day. Thompson plays every emotion enormously, allowing his Captain Hall to emerge as wounded and pathetic. Newman, however, underplays everything, which makes the character richer and more intriguing. He's someone who knows how deeply his sins run.

As for the film version of "The Rack," one can find a vintage New York Times review, written by the inimitable Bosley Crowther, that praised Newman's performance. At the same time, though, Crowther disliked the movie's screenplay, describing it as "thin."

Having watched some of both versions of "The Rack," I can say that the TV version is way preachier. Rod Serling, as we know from his most famous creation, "The Twilight Zone," wasn't always a subtle author, preferring to get to the point.

Both iterations of "The Rack" were notable career turning points for Serling and Paul Newman, in turn. It was only after writing the TV version of "The Rack" that Sterling began contributing more heartily to the TV series "Playhouse 90," and, later on, writing screenplays for movies like "Patterns," "Saddle the Wind," and 1968's "Planet of the Apes." Then, "The Twilight Zone" kicked off in 1959, putting Serling on the American cultural map in perpetuity.

Newman, meanwhile, was a rising star when the big screen version of "The Rack" was released. He had previously been in the films "Somebody Up There Likes Me" and "The Silver Chalice," a disastrous flop that Newman once called his worst movie ever. (Seriously, it's bad.) Similarly, he had only made his TV debut a few years before on the underrated sci-fi anthology show "Tales of Tomorrow" (namely, in the 1952 episode "Ice from Space").

From there, of course, Newman continued to act for decades. No matter when you grew up in the time that followed, you were most likely introduced to him as a child, whether it was through his performance in "Cool Hand Luke" in the 1960s, "The Sting" in the 1970s, "The Color of Money" in the 1980s, "Nobody's Fool" in the 1990s, or even "Cars" in the 2000s. The man lived quite the life.
