Lionel Messi and Harry Kane have produced some of the most remarkable individual seasons in modern soccer, but their latest statistical comparison places them in a category of their own. Kane’s historic 2025-26 campaign helped him secure the Ballon d’Or, while Messi’s extraordinary achievements continue to provide the benchmark against which the game’s greatest goalscorers are measured.

The England captain enjoyed a phenomenal season with Bayern Munich and his national team, finishing ahead of Kylian Mbappe and Lamine Yamal in the 2026 Ballon d’Or rankings. His goalscoring numbers, however, tell an even more remarkable story, connecting him with one of Messi’s most iconic campaigns.

Kane was crowned the 2026 Ballon d’Or winner, becoming the first Englishman to receive soccer’s most prestigious individual award since Michael Owen in 2001. The Bayern Munich striker finished ahead of Mbappe in second place and Barcelona youngster Yamal in third, while Messi placed fifth. The award recognized the striker’s outstanding performances throughout the 2025-26 season, during which he scored 73 goals for club and country.

His prolific form helped Bayern secure domestic silverware and reinforced his reputation as one of the most consistent and clinical forwards in world soccer. Speaking after receiving the award in London, Kane struggled to put the achievement into words. “I am almost speechless. Being here in London has made it extra special – that made it a good omen for me,” he said. “Thank you to everyone who voted for me. I can imagine it was tough this year. There are some world-class players in the room tonight.”

The victory represented the culmination of a long journey for the Englishman, who worked his way through several loan spells early in his career before establishing himself among the world’s elite. His perseverance and sustained improvement ultimately brought him the individual recognition that had previously eluded him.

The most striking detail of Kane’s season is the rarity of his goalscoring achievement. According to Opta, only two players from Europe’s top-five leagues have scored at least 70 goals across club and international soccer in a single season during the 21st century: Lionel Messi and Harry Kane.

Messi established the benchmark in 2011-12, scoring 82 goals, while Kane followed with 73 during the 2025-26 campaign. The statistic underlines the extraordinary level of consistency required to maintain such production across an entire season.

Although Kane did not match Messi’s total, reaching 70 goals remains an exceptional accomplishment in an era of demanding schedules, organized defences and intense competition.

Messi’s 2011-12 campaign remains one of the most extraordinary individual seasons in soccer history. The Argentine scored 82 goals, setting a standard that few players have come close to matching, even among the sport’s most celebrated attackers.

Kane’s achievement now gives that historic campaign a new point of comparison. While the England captain fell nine goals short of Messi’s total, his 73-goal haul places him in an exceptionally exclusive statistical category.

#3317. Santoli: We are nearing a now-or-never moment for the AI trade to rebound

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The difference between a restorative rest and unhealthy stagnation is time. Distinguishing between resilience and fatigue isn't always easy.

The S&P 500 has been sideways for months, last week dipping to levels first reached on June 1. It has spent nearly all of the last three months within the range the index traveled during a peppy two-day rally Aug. 3-4.

Along the way, the former leaders of the AI trade, captured in the iShares AI Innovation ETF, have collapsed by 35 percentage points relative to the equal-weight S&P 500, then regained half the loss, then gave up most of that bounce.

Aside from giving all of us plenty of time and room for overthinking it, this process of constant churn and testing and positioning shocks has done some good, in a tactical sense.

It has created short-term washout conditions in semiconductors and other former momentum AI-hardware plays. Meantime, the cyclical pockets of the market – from consumer discretionary to industrials to banks - have undergone double-digit setbacks as higher bond yields and elevated oil prices restrain growth expectations.

Equity investors have shown a clear preference for quality and defense, as the debt markets have begun rationing growth capital more stringently, raising hurdle rates for the more marginal AI-data-center borrowers.

Along the way, some familiar intermarket relationships have been scrambled: Treasury yields have stopped moving closely with oil, yields retreating well off their highs as crude stays above $100 a barrel. And the S&P 500's former inverse correlation with Treasury yields has loosened, as the bond market bid seems more tied to hiccups in the AI buildout, which has stymied the Big Tech players.

The Goldman Sachs equity desk over the weekend noted "trader exhaustion," in part from the momentum-factor stock basket having moved at least 5% in a day 30 times in 2026 – more often than in any other full year, including ones when there were crashes such as 2000, 2009 and 2020: "Not sure we can handle much more flipping of the thematic script(s)."

Of course, confusion and stress ultimately can reset positioning and attitudes for the good. The sentiment and positioning composite kept by Macro Risk Advisors' John Kolovos that runs here weekly is approaching a "fat pitch" for a tactical rebound, he says.  By most measures, hedge funds have radically cut their leverage and exposure to tech. Broad institutional positioning in tech is not far above the Iran-war lows, according to Deutsche Bank, which adds that "the last two reversals higher in tech coincided with strong earnings releases."

This setup takes shape as the mega-cap cloud platforms Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft report results this week. Further, AI-funding anxiety should arguably ease a bit with the Anthropic IPO filing showing good operating metrics and $100 billion in cash before the IPO proceeds are collected, alongside Blackstone's financing for SpaceX capex and Broadcom lining up $63 billion to fund chips for Anthropic.

It's not quite a now-or-never moment for the AI trade to find some relief, but it's not far from one.

If there's a nagging outlier to the evidence of investor retrenchment and waning risk appetites, it's the ceaseless buying by retail traders of even the worst-looking tech charts.

Scott Rubner, head of equity and equity derivatives strategy at Citadel Securities, wrote on Friday after Thursday's nasty 3% tech selloff, "Despite commentary from several sell-side banks suggesting retail capitulation, our platform data showed the opposite." Namely, the retail order flow Citadel handles as a top market maker revealed extreme dip buying in the leveraged semi ETFs, Micron, Meta and other hard-hit names. This matches data from Robinhood, which according to the firm's Sherwood Media showed its traders stampeded into Bloom Energy on Friday as it fell 11% with the second-highest day ever of net buying in the stock.

While impressive on some level, and even admirable to some, this aggressive buying of severe weakness is not something that has typically coincided with important market lows. Citadel's data tell us that the late-March and late-July bottoms in the market occurred when retail traders panicked out and were net sellers for a week or two.

Not to say that retail's stubborn dip-buying precludes some relief in hard-hit parts of the market, but it likely limits how strong or durable a rally might become.

John Kolovos of Macro Risk Advisors on the latest reading: "Sentiment and breadth are finally giving us something to like, and a fat pitch may be approaching, but the macro needs to cooperate. Our studies show that improving momentum amid tight financial conditions has historically produced disappointing returns and left the market dangerously vulnerable to left-tail outcomes or market corrections."

-Sometimes assessing the weight of the evidence requires a long wait for all the evidence. I grant that the anxious churn and valuation compression of recent months has generated conditions for upside surprise and a relief trade higher.

But bigger picture, I want to stay alert to changing atmospheric conditions.

As I've noted in the weeks since this newsletter launched, I think we're feeling late-cycle tremors – narrower market leadership, investors' unwillingness to pay up for fast earnings growth, a flattening yield curve, stingier credit markets.

I've suggested the market might be telling us companies are "over-earning" through front-loaded capex recycling. It's probable that the Iran war and its macro disturbances prevented the AI theme from going full bubble mode, restricting the craziness to semis and related groups. But that doesn't mean we're sure to rebuild toward a broad bubble this cycle.

And I've argued that even if stocks do fine, bonds now offer a useful yield cushion against concentrated equity risk.

Last year around this time, cyclical-sector outperformance, approaching fiscal tailwinds and benign credit conditions were telling us not to worry about economic growth into 2026. So then what are the recent defensive-stock leadership, fading fiscal impulse and unsettled corporate credit whispering in investors' ears now?

I also believe in the laws of mean-reversion, even though they are enforced only sporadically. A post by Ben Carlson of Ritholtz Wealth Management on his "A Wealth of Common Sense" blog details how great trailing 15-year S&P 500 returns have been, and what this could mean looking ahead.

-This writeup by Dario Perkins of TS Lombard entered heavy rotation in the past couple of days, for good reason. It's a nicely articulated and sobering thought experiment on how the AI boom might now be incubating its own nasty bust.

The migration of money toward pure, traditional defensive stocks has been a bit too mechanical to infer that it's entirely a macro "tell," given the clockwork factor rotations that look almost too scripted to be trusted.

Still, the results are a convergence of valuations between consumer staples and technology. This might seem perverse for current-generation investors but there was a time when staples commanded a premium for their stability and enduring brands, while tech was viewed as cyclical and fickle.

Procter & Gamble has long been the class of the consumer-products industry, standing in for quality management in the way Pampers are synonymous with diapers. And somewhat the way Google is interchangeable with looking something up on the Internet.

Whether this crossover in P/E strikes you as the world's proper order being restored or as a screaming buy signal for Alphabet shares is the measure of what sort of investor you are.

There are few movie franchises with the cultural impact of the Star Wars movies. Generations grew up with the galaxy far, far away, both on the big screen and streaming with a Disney+ subscription. Actress Natalie Portman famously played Padmé in the prequel trilogy, and recalled a big lesson she learned when The Phantom Menace got panned by critics.

The pressure was on for Star Wars' prequel trilogy when it hit theaters, given it was the franchise's first time back on the big screen since George Lucas' originals. In an interview with Harper's Bazaar, the Oscar-winning actress spoke about why she was "lucky" for the The Phantom Menace's poor reviews. In her words:

#551. Alexia Putellas a worthy Ballon d’Or winner but the limited shortlist illustrates flaws

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Neglect of African football and a third win for the Spanish great does little to challenge narrative that the prize is reserved only for top nations’ players

After a four-year wait, Alexia Putellas is the queen of women’s football once again. Receiving the Ballon d’Or for a record‑equalling third time to continue Spain’s domination of the award, the 32-year-old reinforces her status as one of the greatest players of all time.

It was the sixth consecutive year in which the honour went to either Putellas or her international teammate Aitana Bonmatí, and it was symbolic of Putellas’s resurgence after her comeback from the anterior cruciate ligament injury she sustained in 2022, her refusal to surrender her career to that setback, and her majestic rise back to the top.

The midfielder’s competition‑leading 14 goal contributions – eight goals and six assists – in 11 Women’s Champions League appearances last season, helping Barcelona to lift the trophy, went a long way to her securing the most votes, as well as her starring role in Spain’s victory in the Nations League finals last December. Her new club, London City Lionesses, will rejoice in the prestige of having the Ballon d’Or winner in their squad but Putellas, who spent 14 years at Barcelona, made it clear whom she wanted to thank. As she said in her acceptance speech: “This prize is for Barcelona.”

Barcelona collected a clean sweep of the night’s bigger awards, with Cata Coll taking the goalkeeper prize, Pere Romeu the coach award and Vicky López the young player award, as well as Ewa Pajor finishing second behind Putellas for the main honour and Clara Serrajordi finishing second in the young player vote. It was almost a perfect night for the European champions, until their president, Joan Laporta, spoke bizarrely about his belief that their men’s team could win the Champions League this season, appearing to have eyes only for the men’s game despite specifically accepting the award for the women’s club of the year.

That slip aside, the glamorous ceremony at the London Palladium on Monday night passed without the sexist controversy that tarnished the early years after France Football belatedly added the women’s award for the first time in 2018. Progress has been made – the women’s game does now at least receive a prize for the same categories as the men, but the event still does not quite offer full equality. Specifically, there is still not quite parity in the lengths of shortlists. Why, for example, were 10 players nominated for the men’s young talent award, but only five for the women’s? It was the same for the goalkeeper awards.

The live show also demonstrated a disappointing lack of women’s players presenting prizes, aside from Lucy Bronze who was on stage to hand López her prize. Putellas receiving the Ballon d’Or Féminin from Michael Owen – rather than, perhaps Mia Hamm, Birgit Prinz or Sun Wen – perhaps served as a reminder that there are very few former winners of the women’s prize for the organisers to choose from – only Ada Hegerberg, Megan Rapinoe, Putellas and Bonmatí – because it was introduced so late.

Many fans will also have felt it was a shame that the dates covered by the relevant voting period – 3 August 2025 to 19 July 2026, effectively for last season – were based clearly on the conclusion of the men’s World Cup this summer, and therefore did not include this year’s rearranged Wafcon, which was held from 26 July to 16 August. Performances at that tournament will instead be taken into account for next year’s prizes. It meant, for example, that Cameroon’s Wafcon‑winning head coach, Valentine Nguele, was not nominated for the coach of the year award, and there was an overall dearth of African players recognised, aside from Temwa Chawinga becoming the first African player to finish in the women’s top 10. Arguably she deserved to be even higher.

Putellas was a very worthy winner of the main prize, although some will feel it is a missed opportunity for a player from one of the countries less likely to succeed in major international competitions to be recognised, given that the Women’s World Cup next year will inevitably ensure that the annual awards nights focus on whatever happens in Brazil. Poland’s Pajor, for example, finished second after a tremendous campaign with Barcelona but she feels unlikely to go one better next year, simply because of her nationality.

The same goes for Jamaica’s Khadija Shaw, who came third after firing Manchester City to their first domestic title in a decade – although the fact that she made the top three in spite of not playing Champions League football said a lot about how revered she is. Haiti’s Melchie Dumornay – the best player Moving the Goalposts had the pleasure of seeing play live in the 2025-26 season – finished fourth. Aged 23, her time may well come in the future, but it is most likely to be in an even-numbered year.

If Putellas continues her form, it would take a brave person to bet against her retaining her crown next year, as Spain target a successful defence of the world title. A humble role model for the sport, it is a privilege watching her grace the pitch with her deft touches, leadership and creativity and being a three-time Ballon d’Or winner is no less than her storied career deserves.

Published: 12:30 GMT, 27 October 2026 | Updated: 15:28 GMT, 27 October 2026

On an evening that rewards the best in class, London's hosting of the 2026 Ballon d'Or fell horribly short.

Clashes between fans and security that put a risk alert on the red carpet outside. A 45-minute delay to the ceremony even getting underway. Frantic phone calls to delete three articles that claimed to reveal the 'winner' on the award's website hours before it became a reality. A failed YouTube stunt by Strictly Come Dancing's John Nellis. Filming violations in the auditorium. Players criticising officials for poor planning and logistics.

Glitzy events at the scale of the Ballon d'Or are often laced with problems, and sometimes those problems can be out of the organisers' control.

It was Daily Mail Sport who first alerted those on the ground at the London Palladium to the presence of three articles on the official Ballon d'Or website which claimed that each of Harry Kane, Kylian Mbappe and Lamine Yamal had won the main prize.

A series of frantic phone calls and WhatsApp messages were sent off, with our accreditation photographed for some unknown reason, and within 32 minutes, all three articles were removed.

There was chaos at the glitzy Ballon d'Or - on a night laced with problems for organisers

Outside on the red carpet, more drama was brewing with conspiracy theories rife

'Portraits of some Ballon d'Or nominees prepared by Ballondor.com were inadvertently published online,' a statement read.

'These articles had been prepared in advance for all nominees in the event of a win. They do not, in any way, reveal the podium, which remains unknown to everyone at this time.'

By that stage, the damage was done and conspiracies swirled. As it ended up – and as everyone realised immediately – they were the top three in the voting. That was obvious the minute the mistake was made.

Outside on the red carpet, more drama was brewing, not least when one photographer was grabbed by a security guard, who appeared to thrust his head towards him, as he walked through DAZN's live broadcast.

Much like a Formula One gridwalk, red carpets are notoriously chaotic but this moment went viral and was a poor look for the organiser, and of course their security detail.

Squeezing between presenters Lianne Sanderson and Fabrizio Romano, the unaware photographer brushed past them before he was physically halted.

Footage shared on social media showed crowds being forced back by security as they attempted to gain access to the Ballon d'Or red carpet at the London Palladium (Source: TikTok: @winstonso7)

A photographer was seen clashing with a security guard on the event's official broadcast after he was pulled back on the red carpet (Source: DAZN)

'Stop grabbing me!' the photographer was picked up shouting by the red carpet microphones.

It was a short flare-up that paled into insignificance compared to the serious pressures put on those standing in front of barriers, desperately trying to stop hundreds of rabid fans from breaching the exterior security wall.

Organisers were alerted to the problems outside but provided no statement of any kind to Daily Mail Sport when asked what had failed in their logistical operation outside.

Various videos that surfaced on X and TikTok showed metal barriers being pushed and thrown, with one French journalist capturing a moment near the entrance to the London Palladium in which police and stewards desperately tried to push back a growing group trying to break through to the red carpet.

When Kylian Mbappe came into view near the Oxford Street Entrance, security found themselves overwhelmed, but did manage to prevent any football fan from getting onto the red carpet, despite clashes.

Amid all the chaos in London, the Ballon d'Or made a series of preventable gaffes on its own social media pages.

Strictly Come Dancing contestant John Nellis conducted a botched stunt at the ceremony. (Source: Instagram/John Nellis)

Nellis has become a star on Strictly but he had his tail between his legs at the Palladium

On the biggest night of the year for the Ballon d'Or, its social media accounts spoke of Jude Bellingham finishing in '11st' place and Pau Cubarsi finishing '12nd'.

There was also a video of Kane arriving with wife Katie Goodland. The caption? 'Harry Kane & Michael Olise!' It looked amateur.

So, by the time people filtered into the auditorium, eyebrows had long been raised at how so many things appeared to be going wrong. Then came the announcement over the theatre's PA system: the ceremony was delayed. No explanation as to why. It transpired traffic jams meant organisers were 45 minutes behind schedule.

At the players' hotel, clubs and star players were extremely irritable. It didn't take long for those in the French media to report on the discontent, not least from former Ballon d'Or winner Ousmane Dembele.

RMC Sport disclosed that Dembele had to wait for around an hour at his hotel before heading to the Palladium, with claims of there being no refreshments in place to welcome players. Many top executives are said to have taken a dim view.

In a bizarre scene, minutes before the ceremony started, YouTuber and Strictly Come Dancing contestant John Nellis was seen guiding another man in a blindfold towards the front of the stage and approaching the compere.

'He's been blindfolded with headphones on in isolation for four hours,' Nellis was heard explaining, as he made a request to have a star player surprise his friend, who was totally unaware (apparently) of his surroundings.

Kylian Mbappe and Ousmane Dembele both failed to stand to shake Harry Kane's hand at the Ballon d'Or ceremony in London on Monday night. (Source: X/Kyks98)

Harry Kane and his wife Kate Goodland shared a kiss after he won the striker award (left) and Ballon d'Or (right) on Monday night

Nellis, who has amassed more than 20 million followers on his social media pages, was told his stunt would not be possible. He had to go back to his seat with his tail between his legs. We now await the inevitable YouTube video.

And so the ceremony finally got underway, albeit with a teleprompter that wasn't ready, according to co-host Thierry Henry.

Awards were handed out, many against the backdrop of loud heckles from the upper tier, with very vocal Barcelona, Paris Saint-Germain, Tottenham Hotspur and Real Madrid fans in attendance.

Dembele's acceptance speech for PSG winning Men's Club of the Year was barely audible. Stewards in the upper tier showed no ability to quell the heckles and shouts.

Instead the focus was on monitoring media members like a hawk to make sure they weren't filming or taking photos, something numerous journalists in attendance ignored.

By the time Kane landed the top gong – there were few hiccups between the start and the end aside from Rafa Benitez being handed the wrong card for Coaches of the Year – there will have been a relief to wind the evening down.

Stars were ushered back down the red carpet, only to find a backlog of them trying to get into private cars. By 11:36pm the carpet was packed with many vehicles unable to reach pick-up points due to the size of crowds still outside clamouring for photos.

It was a night to reward excellence, put on against a backdrop of mistakes more worthy of slapstick comedy.

#5396. Lectric launches XP Lite3 e-bike, the new best low-cost model in the US

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Lectric eBikes, the largest electric bike company in the US by just about any metric you can measure, has just unveiled its latest model: the Lectric XP Lite3. And with a starting price of just $799, the new lightweight folding electric bike is continuing the company’s tradition of packing surprisingly impressive features into remarkably affordable e-bikes.

The XP Lite2 had been widely considered the best bang-for-your-buck option in the ultra-budget category, offering an affordable yet punchy Class 2 e-bike for a song, despite still coming from a reputable US-based company with strong reputation for warranty and support.

Now, the third-generation model builds on what has become one of the most successful budget e-bike platforms in the country, bringing a long list of upgrades designed to make the bike easier to ride, fold, transport, and live with.

And perhaps most importantly, Lectric has managed to keep that familiar $799 starting price that debuted on the original XP Lite1 all the way back in 2022.

The new Lectric XP Lite3 weighs just 49 lb (22.2 kg), making it one of the lighter options in Lectric’s extensive lineup of folding electric bikes. That’s still no featherweight, partly due to Lectric’s insistence on not neutering the bike’s performance, but it’s still a solid 10-20 lb lighter than the average direct-to-consumer e-bike these days.

The bike retains the compact folding design that made its predecessors popular with everyone from college students to RV owners, but introduces a redesigned frame with improved ergonomics and a reinforced folding latch borrowed from the company’s flagship XP4 model.

New quick-release pedals further reduce the bike’s storage footprint, making it easier to squeeze into a car trunk, apartment closet, or RV storage compartment.

Despite its compact dimensions, the XP Lite3 can support a maximum payload of 275 lb (125 kg) and accommodates riders ranging from 4’8” to 6’2”.

The bike reaches speeds of up to 20 mph (32 km/h) and offers up to 40 miles (64 km) or 80 miles (128 km) of range in Eco mode, though expect significantly less range when using higher-power pedal assist or throttle-only riding. The range discrepancy comes from two different battery offerings. The base model features a 374 Wh battery, while the long-range version has nearly twice the capacity at 672 Wh.

Power comes from Lectric’s Stealth M24 motor, which is rated at 300W continuous but over 800 peak watts. The motor combines with Lectric’s pedal assist selector to offer five levels of pedal assist that are personalizable, letting riders dial in exactly how much boost they’d like on their ride. Riders also get a twist throttle, a more comfortable style of throttle enabled by the lack of shifters crowding the bars on this single-speed e-bike.

Several other upgrades help the XP Lite3 punch above its weight class, including integrated rear turn signals, a bright 3.5-inch full-color display brought over from other recently launched models in the Lectric lineup, and a redesigned LevelUp Lite rear rack with new mounting possibilities.

Stopping power comes from Star Union 602 hydraulic disc brakes with thicker 2.3 mm rotors, another welcome feature on a bike at this price point.

Lectric is also offering a Gates Carbon Belt Drive version of the XP Lite3, giving riders a cleaner, quieter, and virtually maintenance-free alternative to a traditional chain drivetrain. That Gates belt drive model fetches a $100 premium, but is likely worth it for those who value the cleaner, maintenance-free alternative that is likely to outlast the bike itself.

The chain-drive version comes in Arctic White, Blue Mist, Matte Grey, and Matte Lavender, while the belt-drive model is offered in JW Black and Ballerina Pink.

It’s difficult to overstate just how much Lectric has changed the American electric bicycle industry.

The Phoenix, Arizona-based company has grown into the country’s largest e-bike brand by both sales volume and the total number of e-bikes on the road. In doing so, it has become one of the most recognizable names in affordable electric transportation.

But Lectric didn’t get there by simply building cheap electric bikes.

Instead, the company helped revolutionize what consumers should expect from a budget-friendly e-bike, combining capable hardware, practical designs, and aggressive pricing with something that has historically been missing from much of the low-cost e-bike market: a real American company standing behind the product.

While countless inexpensive e-bikes sold online come from little-known overseas brands with questionable long-term support, Lectric has built its reputation around US-based customer service, accessible replacement parts, and a company that riders can actually reach when something goes wrong.

That’s a big part of what has made the XP Lite series so important.

Since its introduction, the XP Lite series has arguably set the standard for what an entry-level electric bike should be. It isn’t necessarily the most powerful, fastest, or fanciest e-bike on the road, but it delivers the essentials at a price that opens electric cycling to an entirely new audience.

And importantly, it does so without requiring riders to gamble on an unknown brand.

The XP Lite3 continues that formula, adding features like hydraulic disc brakes, integrated turn signals, and a color display that would have been difficult to imagine on a $799 e-bike just a few years ago.

I’ve been following Lectric since the company’s earliest days, and it’s been fascinating to watch how the brand has consistently raised the bar for affordable electric bikes. I rode one of Lectric’s first 10 bikes off their nascent production line all the way back in 2019, and I think they’d agree that few people outside the company know the brand better than I do. So I can say that, without a doubt, this is one of the company’s most important launches to date because of how it revolutionizes how much bike someone can get without stretching their budget farther than they have to. And at a time when inflation is still rising unchecked and fuel prices are through the roof, this is what the industry needs more than ever.

Keep in mind that the $799 price tag for the XP Lite3 announced today is the same price that the original XP Lite carried back in 2022, despite that value ballooning to $939 in 2026 dollars due to rising inflation.

What impresses me most isn’t necessarily any single feature on the new XP Lite3. It’s the combination of all those features at a price that remains accessible to so many riders.

Hydraulic brakes, a color display, integrated turn signals, a refined folding mechanism, and even the option for a Gates belt drive? That’s quite a lot of bike for the money. And remember, the performance is still there. This is still a 48V e-bike with a legit 20 mph (32 km/h) top speed on throttle and pedal assist.

Sure, there are lighter e-bikes out there, and there are certainly more premium models with fancier components. But that’s not really the point of the XP Lite.

The point is to make electric biking accessible without making it feel like a compromise at every turn.

And when you consider that you’re getting all of this from the biggest e-bike company in the country, complete with US-based support and a proven track record, it’s hard to argue with the value proposition.

The original XP Lite helped establish what a good affordable electric bike could be. The XP Lite3 looks ready to move that benchmark forward once again.

And on a personal note, not only do I want my own Lectric XP Lite3 now, but I think my wife’s new e-bike will soon be an XP Lite3 in Ballerina Pink with a Gates belt drive. Remember, “Happy wife, happy life!”

As Android Authority reports, Google typically doesn't personalize ads based on content from Drive, Gmail, or Google Photos by default. However, the policy now makes these data available for that purpose if you've connected these apps to other Google services—like Personal Intelligence—that do support personalized ads. Personal Intelligence is the opt-in Gemini feature launched earlier this year that links with other Google apps to give you more tailored, contextual responses in Gemini and Google Search's AI Mode.

Personal Intelligence is opt-in by default, but as Ars Technica points out, users are presented with opportunities to enable this feature and connect apps to it without realizing it or understanding the potential consequences of doing so.

You can check whether any of your Google content apps are connected to Personal Intelligence and disable them if you'd prefer. In your Google Account, tap Data & Privacy. Under Personalization & activity, select Personalization settings, then click Manage Personalized Recommendations in Search Services. Select Connected Content Apps under Additional Personal Intelligence and disconnect any apps you don't want granted access.

With the Carabao Cup fourth round taking place this week, fans will be hoping that their side will advance to the next round and get on step closer to Wembley.

The quarter-finals of the Carabao Cup, sometimes called the fifth round, will take place in December, but who is still in the competition?

There are six all-Premier League ties, but the first two games of round four of the EFL Cup feature sides from outside of England’s top tiers.

League One duo Bradford City and Peterborough United are in action on Tuesday, October 27, guaranteeing that there will be one third-tier outfit in the draw for the quarter finals.

On the same day, Premier League champions Arsenal travel to Highbury Stadium to face League Two Fleetwood Town - the lowest ranked team still in the competition. Both games kick off at 7.45pm.

Wednesday night sees Manchester City face a stern test in Brighton & Hove Albion - the Seagulls knocking out City’s arch-rivals, Manchester United, in the previous round.

There are five fixtures in total on Wednesday night, with the City-Brighton fixture kicking off first at 7.30pm. The latest kick-off sees Liverpool host Chelsea at 8pm in what is arguably the pick of the bunch.

Elsewhere on Wednesday south Londoners Fulham and Crystal Palace meet at Craven Cottage, while their neighbours Brentford travel north to face Sunderland. Those fixtures, and the clash between Europa League Bournemouth take on Champions League Aston Villa at Vitality Stadium, all kick-off at 7.45pm.

Finally, the fourth round action ends with Everton welcoming Newcastle United to Merseyside on Thursday, with kick-off at 7.45pm.
