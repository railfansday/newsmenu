#4989. Strategy skips Bitcoin purchases, spends $205M on STRC buybacks

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Strategy made no Bitcoin purchases or sales last week, as its STRC preferred stock buybacks reduced its USD Cash balance by 46% to $242.4 million.

Michael Saylor’s Strategy refrained from buying or selling Bitcoin last week, choosing instead to spend $204.9 million on STRC preferred stock buybacks.

Strategy repurchased 2.07 million shares between Monday and Friday, reporting no Bitcoin (BTC) transactions or stock sales through its at-the-market offering program, according to its Monday filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission.

The company said its Bitcoin holdings remained at 848,517 BTC, acquired for $64.02 billion at an average price of $75,446 per coin. At Monday’s US equities open, the biggest cryptocurrency was trading hands at roughly $79,992 apiece.

Strategy’s US dollar reserve remained unchanged from the previous week, at $4.83 billion, Its USD Cash balance fell around 46% to $242.4 million from $447.3 million after management used $204.9 million to repurchase STRC shares.

Since October began, Strategy has spent about $664 million repurchasing STRC preferred shares, compared with $72.3 million buying 851 BTC.

The pause came as Bitcoin fell toward $80,000 last week after reaching $84,777 on Wednesday, according to CoinGecko.

Strategy’s common stock (MSTR) fell 2.2% to $142.80 in Monday’s premarket activity, while its STRC preferred shares edged down 0.01% to $99.35, according to Yahoo Finance.

#693. Davide Ancelotti wants to make a name for himself – will he do it at Lille?

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Lille’s results under Carlo’s son in Ligue 1 are encouraging but they have been exposed in the Champions League

In a restaurant in Rio de Janeiro, two aspiring young managers sit around a table. Their respective clubs, Flamengo and Botafogo, are fierce rivals on the pitch. The relationship between the managers of the two clubs, Filipe Luís and Davide Ancelotti, however, is significantly more cordial.

Speaking before his first meeting with Ancelotti in Ligue 1 on Sunday, the Monaco manager, Filipe Luís, described his counterpart at Lille as a “really formidable person”. The Brazilian added: “It is something special for us. It will be a pleasure to face my friend.”

There were 12 managerial appointments in Ligue 1 over the summer; Filipe Luís and Ancelotti were two of the most interesting. Filipe Luís won the Premier League with Chelsea but is probably best known in Europe for his eight years at Atlético Madrid, where he won La Liga, the Copa del Rey and two Europa League titles across two spells. He carried on that success in his first job as a manager, winning the Copa Libertadores with Flamengo. “He knows what he’s talking about and how to motivate us,” says the Monaco forward Matthis Abline. “It motivates us even more to have a manager like him behind us.”

Davide Ancelotti has had a different route into management. When your surname is Ancelotti, you can’t exactly make a name for yourself; his father, Carlo, has already seen to that. “I hope to be able to write my story, with my first name, too,” said Davide before Lille played in the Champions League earlier this season. No manager has won more titles (five) or managed as many games (218) in the competition as his father.

Bar a brief 33-game stint as manager at Botafogo, Davide has spent the last 14 years following his father from club to club and then ultimately to the Brazil national team. From the banks of the river Mersey, to Bavaria, the fervour of the Stadio Diego Armando Maradona, the grandeur of the Bernabéu and the vibrancy of the Maracanã. His trip around the world was enriching but he was always in the shadows. Until now.

Ancelotti’s appointment over the summer marked a radical departure at Lille. There was a common thread linking the club’s appointments of Christophe Galtier, Jocelyn Gourvennec, Paulo Fonseca and Bruno Génésio: they were all “a safe pair of hands” and most had considerable experience in Ligue 1. Génésio left Lille in the summer after his contract expired even though he had met the club’s key objective of qualifying for this season’s Champions League. The club’s president, Olivier Létang, simply said it “was the right moment” for a parting of the ways.

Unlike the wider public, Létang was at least familiar with Davide Ancelotti thanks to their time together at Paris Saint-Germain in 2012. Carlo Ancelotti was manager at the time, Létang was the director of football and Davide was the fitness coach. Létang did, nonetheless, use Carlo as a middleman when making his approach for Davide this summer. Carlo has even observed Lille’s start to the season from the stands, attending a draw with PSG in August.

Lille have lost all three of their matches in the Champions League this season and sit second from bottom in the 36-team table but, after their 1-1 draw at Monaco on Sunday, they were just one point behind the Ligue 1 leaders, Rennes. The point earned at the Stade Louis II took Lille’s tally to 15 points from eight games; they have not started a domestic campaign this well since they won the title in 2020-21.

Comparisons to his father are unfair but they are inevitable; the expectations on Davide have been moulded by two decades of his father’s unprecedented success. And it will always be extremely hard, borderline impossible, to live up to them. Every game is seen as proof of hereditary genius or – as had sometimes been the case in the opening weeks – its absence.

The game on Sunday did not offer much evidence of genius. Ethan Mbappé gave Lille the lead from the only clear chance of the first half. Thereafter, they retreated inside their shell. Even before Tanguy Nianzou was sent off for a reckless challenge on Paris Brunner, Monaco had them under the cosh. Ancelotti’s decision to take off a striker in the form of Soriba Diaoune for a defender in Isaac Cossier reflected the manager’s infectious fear.

Lille could not hold on for the win and, in the end, they nearly lost the game. Monaco equalised through Edan Diop with 15 minutes to play and had numerous efforts during a late siege. One side dominated, but the points were shared. In the absence of any clear identity, Ancelotti praised his side’s “solidarity”. For signs of who Davide is beyond the son of the most prolific manager in Europe, the wait goes on. He is out of his father’s shadow but not yet truly in the light.

There was no “headline” fixture in France on Sunday night. The game between Paris Saint-Germain and Lyon was postponed until Monday. As was the case last year, it means that many of PSG’s nine Ballon d’Or nominees will miss the ceremony in London. Their absence last year was the result of a storm, which led to their game in Marseille being delayed by 24 hours.

Unlike in the provincial cities, the planned protest in Paris on Saturday was not banned. With a significant police presence in the capital in an attempt to prevent a recurrence of last week’s riots and looting, and an NFL game to host on Sunday afternoon, the prefecture issued the directive, later adopted by the French league, on Friday.

There was further disruption. Elsewhere in Ligue 1, Toulouse’s home game against Troyes was moved from Saturday to Sunday. In Ligue 2, Montpellier’s game against Red Star was moved from Saturday to Monday. There wasn’t the same degree of disruption in Saint-Étienne, who nonetheless delayed the kick-off of their game against Nantes by 45 minutes following a police directive.

Another year, another call for bids for Ligue 1’s broadcasting rights, the value of which has plummeted in recent seasons. Dazn pulled out after just one season in 2024, giving rise to the Ligue 1+ experiment. The league-owned broadcaster, now in its second season, has been an editorial success but not a financial one. As a result, the president of the French league, Vincent Labrune, has announced a fresh auction for Ligue 1’s broadcasting rights, which will begin next month and end in December.

The prospective deal would begin at the start of next season. Labrune spoke of a “new context” and one more conducive to bigger offers. Recent changes in legislation mean that the rights can be sold in a single “bundle”, offering the option of exclusivity to any prospective buyer. Without citing any broadcasters, Labrune also revealed “registered concrete interests from different actors”. Ligue 1 cannot afford another botched deal.

#3974. Call Of Duty Movie Will Not Be About, “Hey, Let’s Shoot Them All Up,” Director Says

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The upcoming Call of Duty movie aims to show the "cost of war," according to director Peter Berg. He told ComicBook that the film, set in the Modern Warfare universe, will strive to capture the spirit of those games, which means lots of guns and violence, but also heart and soul.

"So Modern Warfare, if you really look at the missions, and the storylines, and the characters--like Captain Price, and Soap, and Ghost, and Alex--these are complicated humans,” Berg said. “These are not--and no disrespect--cartoon characters or comic books. These are very nuanced storylines; like, there’s a real soul to the Modern Warfare universe," he said.

"And, yes, there’s violence, and there’s hardware, and equipment, and there’s killing--but underneath it I think the game, and the craters of the game, are really looking at the cost of war, the cost of having to be a warrior who’s, for whatever reason, chosen to put himself between real danger and the rest of us, and the burden that these guys carry."

Berg said this is "real stuff," and it demands a movie that respects that. "This is not just ‘Hey, let’s shoot them all up and shoot popcorn!’ Like, there’s a soul underneath it," he said.

Berg went on to say that the Call of Duty movie's writer, Taylor Sheridan, understands this as well. "There will be a very strong soul, and consequence of war within the film, called 'duty,'" he said.

Sheridan is also known for his work in the military/war space. One of his most highly acclaimed works is 2015's Sicario, a film that leaves the viewer thinking about moral grey areas, which sounds like what Berg is going for. Sheridan wrote that film, along with other acclaimed movies like Hell or High Water and Wind River. He also created the very Call of Duty-like TV show Lioness.

One of Berg's best-known and most highly acclaimed movies was 2013's Lone Survivor. This film told the story of real-life Navy SEAL Marcus Luttrell, who was the lone survivor of an ambush in Afghanistan. Berg also directed movies based on other real-world tragedies, including Deepwater Horizon and Patriots Day.

The Call of Duty movie is set for release in June 2028. It's in the works at David Ellison's Skydance, which just acquired Warner Bros. Discovery, and it will be released theatrically. The newest Call of Duty game, meanwhile, was Modern Warfare 4, which just launched on October 23.

In addition to a Call of Duty movie, a Battlefield movie is in the works as well, with major talent attached.

Buying a used car inherently involves financial risk, but a thorough pre-purchase inspection is widely considered the ultimate safety net. Buyers gladly pay independent mechanics to check fluids, run diagnostics, and examine the undercarriage to avoid bringing home a lemon. However, a recent incident involving a freshly purchased Mazda reminds consumers that even comprehensive inspections are not foolproof guarantees against immediate failures.

According to Motor1, a couple purchased a used Mazda6 after specifically paying for an independent inspection. The mechanic gave the vehicle a clean bill of health, clearing the way for the sale. Yet, just 30 minutes after leaving the lot, the engine began emitting loud noises before failing entirely. This rapid deterioration highlights a grim reality of the used market. An inspection only reveals what is visibly broken at that exact moment. It cannot accurately predict internal component failures lingering on the verge of snapping, especially if previous owners hid severe maintenance neglect.

Nightmare scenarios often lead frustrated buyers to question a manufacturer’s entire lineup. However, looking at broader data, the automaker’s overall brand reliability remains largely intact. When evaluating long-term ownership, buyer satisfaction surveys consistently demonstrate that Mazda drivers are fiercely loyal, frequently praising the brand’s sharp driving dynamics and premium interiors compared to economy competitors.

Yet, when comparing Japanese brands for long-term durability, the actual winner depends entirely on the specific maintenance history of the individual vehicle. A neglected Mazda will always be a worse purchase than a meticulously serviced Nissan, regardless of corporate reputation. For example, compact commuter reliability might look fantastic on paper. However, if that specific car was abused as a rental or ignored by previous owners, it can still suffer catastrophic failure mere minutes after passing a seemingly clean mechanic’s inspection.

It is easy to read a nightmare scenario like this and conclude that paying for an inspection is a waste of money. Skipping this step, however, remains the worst mistake a shopper can make. With new data exposing a severe auto affordability crisis, buying second-hand is often the most realistic way to combat skyrocketing prices. A mechanic does not have a crystal ball to predict a thrown rod bearing, but they will absolutely catch a leaking water pump, failing brakes, or hidden rust damage.

The ultimate takeaway is that an inspection is about risk mitigation, not absolute risk elimination. Used cars are complex machines subjected to unpredictable wear and tear. While an inspection cannot offer an ironclad guarantee against sudden engine failure, it remains the most effective tool for filtering out obvious financial disasters before you ever sign the final paperwork.

Bernstein analysts expect prediction markets to become one of the incremental earnings drivers for trading platform Robinhood Markets (HOOD), potentially surpassing its crypto-derived revenue.

Prediction markets could drive up to $1.7 billion in business for Robinhood by 2028, surpassing the projected $1.6 billion in crypto-based revenues for that year, according to a Bernstein analysts’ forecast shared with Cointelegraph on Monday.

The analysts expects Robinhood’s prediction market revenue to increase to $1.7 billion in 2028 from about $650 million this year, accounting for roughly 18% of Robinhood’s projected $9.4 billion in total 2028 revenue.

The forecast comes a day ahead of Robinhood’s third-quarter 2026 report and shows that prediction markets are becoming a growing revenue source for the retail-focused trading platform.

Bernstein expects Robinhood’s Q3 revenue to increase 13% quarter-over-quarter to around $1.5 billion, driven by growing equity notional volumes, options volumes, event contract volumes, along with a recovery in crypto trading volume.

Bernstein raised Robinhood’s revenue estimates by 3% for 2026, 4% for 2027 and 3% for 2028, while keeping its $160 price target unchanged, citing higher equities and crypto revenue assumptions.

The Nasdaq-traded shares were poised to open down on Monday, with premarket activity paring the stock price down to $93.60, according to Yahoo Finance. HOOD stock is down more than 29% over the past 12 months.

#4465. Tested: 2027 BMW X5 40 xDrive Looks Radical, Drives Familiar

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Despite an all-new platform and a Neue Klasse visual makeover, the new X5 still carries the torch of BMW’s most popular SUV.

BMW holds the X5 in a special light. The luxury SUV defined Bavaria’s take on the sport utility vehicle, and the first four generations have enjoyed considerable success in the North American market. But the X5’s real charm has always been behind the wheel: This mid-size SUV is historically one of the best-driving cars in its segment. With an all-new platform and radical new design direction, does the 2027 BMW X5 live up to its pedigree?

The fifth-generation X5 rides on a new shared platform with the all-electric iX5. BMW’s B58 inline-six engine is currently the sole powertrain for the X5, though a plug-in hybrid powertrain and even a V-8 will likely arrive soon. The engine is a familiar B58 turbocharged 3.0-liter inline-six making peaks of 394 hp and 428 lb-ft of torque. Buyers will be able to choose between rear-wheel drive and an xDrive-branded all-wheel-drive system, but the Neue Klasse exterior design and BMW’s new panoramic infotainment system are standard.

Engine: turbocharged and mild-hybridized 2998-cc inline-four

Transmission: 8-speed automatic, all-wheel drive

As the relative humility of its 40 branding implies, there will doubtless be several grander gasoline-powered versions of this generation of X5. But the X5 40 xDrive definitely didn’t feel like a base model from behind the wheel. Combining a retuned, electrified version of the long-running B58 straight-six with an eight-speed automatic transmission and standard adaptive dampers, the new X5 hasn’t lost its grasp on either performance or luxury.

Performance was brisk but delivered without much excitement. The mild-hybrid six-cylinder engine is characteristically smooth, and peak torque arrives at a low 1980 rpm. But it feels like it is working against plenty of mass, with the X5 set to tip the scales at over 5000 pounds. Finding real shove required winding up the engine to (and beyond) the 5500 rpm at which peak power is made. Unlike some luxury SUVs, the powertrain didn’t feel like the star of the show. The ZF transmission dutifully shifted up and down automatically but was slower than I liked when commanded through the paddle shifters behind the steering wheel.

My test car was equipped with the Adaptive Chassis Control Professional system, which adds two-axle air suspension with ride height adjustment and an active anti-roll system. The result was a properly plush ride that still felt sturdy enough for deficient pavement and dynamic duties. Both X5 and iX5 models share a wishbone front and a multilink rear suspension setup, but a 1000-pound weight differential means the combustion X5 handled undulations and imperfect pavement more gracefully. Across concrete sections of I-5 and through twisty canyon roads, the X5 exhibited near perfect damping behavior.

The X5’s steering system is also praiseworthy. Not only was the rack quick to change direction, but I found the assistance weighting felt more natural than on the iX5. Climbing into Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, the X5 felt impressively nimble for something of its size and weight, the active roll stabilization reducing lean. The X5 40 definitely felt lighter on its feet than its all-electric sibling. The only place the X5’s true mass showed was under braking—slowing it down required a heavier foot than I was expecting.

Running day-to-day errands only adds to the X5’s appeal. Rear-wheel-steering made this heavy SUV feel particularly maneuverable, and an array of cameras helped me keep the shiny new bodywork safe. The new crystal toggle-switch shifter was easy enough to use, if a bit gaudy. And BMW’s Hands-Free Highway Assistant semi-autonomous driving system accomplished its mission well.

Neue Klasse translates directly to new class, and the interior of the 2027 BMW X5 certainly felt like a step up from the old car. The heart of the experience was BMW’s Panoramic iDrive system, which removed physical iDrive controls in place of a fully touchscreen system. The 17.9-inch central infotainment display with a slant-sided screen works together with a recessed 14.6-inch passenger’s screen. Above both is BMW’s Panoramic iDrive dashboard, which trades a traditional instrument cluster for a projected span of information almost the full width of the windshield’s base.

The infotainment screen is where nearly every function lives. Save for a scrolling volume knob and physical seat-position controls on the door, I operated everything from the dynamic vehicle settings to the radio and climate controls through the central touchscreen. BMW has done an excellent job with the screen response speed and adding a permanent menu with icons for climate control, vehicle settings, navigation, and media. Overall, navigating through the screen menus was fairly easy but often required multiple steps. For example, adjusting suspension stiffness on the X5 was a three-step process on the screen. And tuning to a new SiriusXM channel took too many inputs for ease of use.

BMW’s new projected dashboard system was my favorite piece of newfangled tech. Projected across a span of masked glass where the dashboard meets the windshield I found information such as speed and driving range right where I wanted it. Music information and directions were projected toward the center of the screen, while the least essential information was beamed toward the passenger’s end. Paired with an improved seating position, the panoramic dashboard made driving the X5 feel more carlike.

As in the fully electric iX5, I dislike BMW’s fussy new steering-wheel design and its all haptic-touch buttons, although I found the paddle shifters to be ergonomic. Important to note, BMW has nailed the physical touch points of the new X5. Merino leather seats welcomed me gently into the cabin, and Alcantara trim was everywhere. Noise intrusion inside the cabin is minimal; the gasoline version is nearly as refined as the freakishly quiet iX5. My test car featured ventilated and massaging front seats, which weren’t strictly necessary on my 150-mile drive but performed well. I even enjoyed briefly browsing YouTube on the passenger’s-side screen while riding shotgun, without worrying about distracting the driver.

Plus, with 27 cubic feet of space available behind the second row and a 7700-pound tow rating, the X5 is practical as well as luxurious.

The BMW X5 has enjoyed a long career at the top of the luxury-SUV range, and it should plan to stay there for a while longer. Up against models such as the Porsche Cayenne, the Genesis GV80, and the Audi Q8, the X5 strikes a strong balance between luxury amenities and performance prowess. And its price sits right in the middle of the segment too.

At a base price of $73,550, the BMW X5 is about $20,000 less than the corresponding Porsche Cayenne, although only a little less expensive than the significantly improved 2027 Mercedes GLE 450. Alternatively, the Genesis GV80 is similarly well apportioned for $10,000 less, but the Korean SUV isn’t as much fun to drive. If you like the Neue Klasse styling, the BMW X5 is as good as it ever was.

BMW’s Panoramic Vision infotainment dashboard represents genuine progress for instrument clusters, and my favorite feature was a built-in altimeter. Descending into Borrego Springs, California, the altimeter dutifully read out my altitude deviation at a staggeringly quick rate. Beyond the simple fun of watching the car climb and descend, an altimeter adds situational awareness related to temperature or performance restrictions at high elevations.

#4592. The 2026 Mama Bear Award goes to a black bear with a mullet

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Published Oct 26, 2026 1:08 PM EDT

While Fat Bear Week has come and gone, there is another group of Alaskan bears currently getting their due. The United States Forest Service team at Tongass National Forest are doling out the Anan Bear Awards to the 39 bears spotted at the Anan Wildlife Observatory in southeastern Alaska.

Tongass is the nation’s largest national forest and covers most of Southeast Alaska, including the capital city of Juneau. It is home to Mendanhall Glacier (a popular destination for Alaskan cruises), numerous hiking trails, eagles, and plenty of bears. One of the hotspots is Anan Creek, which is home to one of the largest runs of pink salmon in Southeast Alaska.

According to Friends of Anan, there are 39 known bears that feast on the salmon at Anan Creek. Thirty-one are black bears (Ursus americanus) and eight are brown bears (Ursus arctos). Like their counterparts further to the north in Katmai National Park, the bears return here every summer to eat salmon and teach their young how to fish and survive. And also like the bears of Katmai, every bear here has their own habits.

The U.S. Forest Service tracks and documents every bear and explore.org’s cameras broadcast live streams of their antics.

Osso! The adult female black bear was first spotted in 2022 and has a white chest patch shaped like the letter I. She usually has a mullet of hair on the top of her head at the end of the summer season. Her name is spelled either Osso (Italian for bone), because her chest patch also resembles a bone or Oso (Spanish for bear). She had one cub in 2019, two in 2024, and three this year.

Mother bears show fearless aggression when sensing any danger to their young, and the Forest Service says Osso is a prime example of this tenacity.

“Feeding and watching out for three cubs was more than a full-time job, especially with so many other bears about,” the team writes. “Scuba Sue, a female brown bear, got too close for comfort on this occasion and Osso didn’t hesitate before sending her cubs up a tree and taking a swipe at Sue. Not all heroes wear capes, some pop their jaws and raise their claws.”

Unfortunately, Osso was only seen with two cubs in August. While it is not clear what happened to her third cub, the Forest Service assumes she did her best to protect the cubs.

#4474. Audi Announces Plans for 1st U.S. Production Facility in Georgia

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

President Donald Trump announced the new factory, which will entail a $4 billion investment by the carmaker, during a rally on Saturday, October 24.

Audi is set to build its first-ever United States production facility in Macon, Georgia, with work starting in 2027. President Donald Trump announced the factory during a rally for Republican U.S. Senate candidate Mike Collins in Perry, Georgia, on Saturday.

The Volkswagen Group-owned luxury automaker will invest around $4 billion to build what it described in a press release as a “mega U.S. production site,” a facility capable of an annual capacity of up to 200,000 vehicles. Production will begin in the early 2030s, building high-end SUV models that are particularly successful in the U.S. market. Audi said the investment will establish North America as a major pillar of its global production network.

“We have much greater ambitions for Audi in America. The United States is one of the world's most important premium markets, and we have not yet realized our full potential,” Gernot Döllner, chairman of the board of management of Audi AG, said. “We are determined to change that — with premium products, a stronger brand and a competitive local production base. This is about building a stronger Audi business in America — not simply about building a factory.”

Once the new plant is finished, Audi said it will be one of the brand’s largest production footprints globally. The facility will create around 4500 jobs and reflects the German automaker's ambition to grow further into the U.S. market. The plant will exclusively produce Audi vehicles, 50 percent of which will be sold in the U.S. market, Audi of America president Vito Paladino said.

Trump celebrated the decision at the rally on October 24, and Georgia governor Brian Kemp echoed his sentiments. “Think of that. 200,000 cars a year. That’s incredible,” Trump said at the rally.

“Georgia has built a reputation as a place where global companies come to shape the future of their industries. Audi’s decision to establish its first American manufacturing plant here is another defining moment in that journey. We are proud to welcome one of the world’s most iconic automotive brands to Georgia,” Governor Kemp said in a statement.

Döllner explained that Audi’s quest for a U.S. production facility covered over 70 locations during the three-year search. The automaker investigated building a facility near Volkswagen’s Chattanooga factory, and even explored using the Scout Motors facility in Blythewood, South Carolina, according to Automotive News. Key considerations included clear access to energy supplies, proximity to Volkswagen’s North American supplier network, and a level site. However, Audi’s decision to build in Georgia was reportedly sped up by growing tariff pains: Currently, Audi imports all of its models to the U.S. market from Mexico or Europe, which accounted for $1.4 billion in duties last year. Similarly, the VW Group paid $4 billion in U.S. tariffs in 2025.

Volkswagen’s supervisory board has not officially approved the U.S. production plan yet. The board will convene to finalize the details over the next few weeks, and a decision will likely be made by the end of the year, Döllner said. Either way, the Audi Q5 will continue to be produced in Mexico, while the Q3 production will remain in Hungary, according to AN. Additionally, an annual 25,000 Audi units will be built in neighboring South Carolina, according to Reuters.

Either way, Audi is hoping to turn around its North American sales, which fell 16 percent in 2025. It is not immediately clear whether this deal includes tariff relief for the German automaker or Volkswagen Group at large.
