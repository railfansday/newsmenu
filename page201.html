Michael Carrick is in regular contact with Sir Alex Ferguson and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer as he fights to survive Manchester United’s disastrous start to the season.

Carrick is under huge pressure going into Sunday’s game against Bournemouth at Old Trafford after a run of seven games without a win.

United have made their worst start to a league season in 40 years since Ferguson was brought in to replace Ron Atkinson, and Carrick apologised to fans in Como in midweek after the club’s heaviest defeat in Europe since 1994.

The 45-year-old gave an impassioned speech before his press conference at Carrington on Friday, in which he summoned the spirit of Ferguson by calling on everyone at United to put the club first, and later revealed that he has been speaking regularly to the legendary Old Trafford boss.

#1917. Baur admits there's no hiding from Celtic's European form but is confident club will find their way again

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By JOHN MCGARRY, SCOTTISH CHIEF SPORTS FEATURE WRITER

Published: 22:30 GMT, 24 October 2026 | Updated: 22:30 GMT, 24 October 2026

Mika Baur's hopes for the future in a Celtic jersey don’t prevent him from accepting the harsh reality of the present.

When a side loses four consecutive European matches but just one nine in the league, the charge which is inevitably levelled is that it’s simply incapable of coping at a higher level.

The German would dearly love to shoot the accusation down in flames and, in the fullness of time, he intends to do just that.

For now, though, the midfielder accepts that the mounting statistics are simply too damning and too convincing to contradict.

‘We lost three games,’ Baur said after the 2-1 home defeat to Celta Vigo on Thursday.

‘What should I tell you? Maybe it’s like that at the moment. But I believe in us that we can turn it around absolutely and do better against Besiktas in two weeks.

‘I don’t believe that we are not capable but, at the moment, maybe it looks like that.’

Baur tussles with Celta captain Iago Aspas in the defeat at Parkhead on Thursday night

Any team can lose a match in Europe, but when the losses are being chalked up as routinely as Celtic right now then it becomes easy to believe that the side simply don’t have the wherewithal to make an appreciable impact any more – even in UEFA’s secondary competition.

The 2-1 loss to the Spaniards followed an increasingly familiar script. Martin O’Neill’s side were out-played in the first half yet managed to strike first in the contest just after the interval.

Once Celta deservedly levelled, though, there was only ever going to be one winner.

For Celtic supporters watching their side in Europe in these days, it felt like a rerun of a dreary old movie.

Coming after he’d netted against Ferencvaros, Baur’s opening goal proved to be scant consolation.

‘Very disappointing for this to happen for the second time,’ he reflected.

‘I think it was an open first half, it was fine. Maybe they controlled it a bit more, but everything was okay.

‘Then we had a great 15 minutes in the second half and we scored.

‘We got the control, we played really, really well with the ball.

‘But they scored and the momentum switched and we were not capable to switch it again.’

The German midfielder put the Bhoys in front with his fourth goal for the club

The position Celtic now find themselves in is nothing short of desperate. With zero point and a goal difference of minus six, only Ararat-Armenia sit beneath them in the 36-team group.

With Besiktas next up in Glasgow and further challenges to come featuring the likes of Marseille and Union SG, identifying how this side somehow conjures up 10 points in five games to yet progress is inordinately difficult.

Until the point comes when rudimentary arithmetic says it cannot be done though, hope will spring eternal.

‘What else should I think? Yeah, sure,’ insisted Baur.

‘We have five games ahead, so it’s not getting any easier because we lost again. But, yes, five games with 15 points to play for, so what else?’

More immediately, the task for O’Neill’s men is to bounce back at lunchtime.

As was the case following their loss to Benfica, another opponent from the capital lies in wait with Hibernian praying they aren’t made to suffer the way Hearts were last weekend.

The Celtic manager can only hope that his players repeat the trick of parking another tough European night to pick up another three league points.

Asked what the psychological secret was as a switch was flicked in the short turnaround after Lisbon, Baur said: ‘There is no time. Maybe that’s the good point — here’s no time. The next game is on Sunday. We can be disappointed on Thursday night, the next day we do regeneration.

‘But then, by Saturday, the focus is all on Hibs.

‘So, maybe that’s the good thing, to play every three days.’

Javi Rodriguez's double condemned Celtic to a third straight defeat in the Europa League

Under Marink Reedijk, Hibs have started poorly with two losses extending the form slump which cost David Gray his job.

But Baur sees an inherent danger in assuming anything about a trip to face the division’s bottom club.

Asked if Celtic had to treat the fixture as if they were facing a top European side, the German replied: ‘Not treat them like a European game, but if you’re not doing it, it’s not possible to win.

‘It’s like that. It’s easy. If you’re not treating them like a real good opponent, it’s not possible to win that game.

‘European football is much more difficult, for sure.

‘But we’ve had really, really tough games in the league as well.

‘It’s not getting easier to go away and play St Mirren, St Johnstone or whoever. We should move on in the league, be confident, like we did last Sunday.

‘That was not an easy game. It maybe it looked like that, but it was well deserved.

‘But it’s very disappointing that we are not capable at the moment to perform like that in the Europa League.’

While the overall standard of Celtic’s recruitment is a contentious issue, there are no question marks over the initial £4.5m spent to bring Baur in from Paderborn in the summer.

Already with four goals to his name, half of which have come in Europe, the 22-year-old believes there’s more to come.

‘I try to do my best all the time,’ he said. ‘I try to help the team by scoring and hopefully deliver a few assists as well in the next games. But firstly, it’s Callum (McGregor’s) and my job in the midfield to control it.

‘We did a few times really well, a few times not. So, it’s on us to do that. But personally, I feel more confident for sure in the last weeks.’

#5468. Canada And EU Are Slip Sliding Away On Vehicle Emissions

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

J. Wellington Wimpy, of Popeye comics fame, had a favorite expression. It went like this: “I will gladly pay you Tuesday for a cheeseburger today.” As the Earth gets hotter and hotter, most countries have made commitments to reduce their climate warming emissions by 2030, or 2050, or 2077, or 2100. Their position papers and high level speeches are filled with passionate promises and flowery language, but their actions seldom match their pious protestations.

This week, Canada and the EU both backed away from their existing commitments to reduce motor vehicle emissions. Oh, they still promise to get around to it, just not now, while political turmoil is roiling their economies. The promises will be kept — make no mistake about that — just not now. They need breathing room to attend to the high prices resulting from America’s failed effort to wipe Iran off the map and to address extreme right wing movements who threaten to bring down their governments. There are always plenty of reasons to delay, delay, and delay some more.

According to Canadian website Electric Autonomy, the government of Canada this week announced a freeze on its vehicle emissions mandates for three years, while promising  to still reach its goal of 75 percent of new car sales to be electric by 2035. That and three loonies will get you a doughnut at Tim Horton’s.

The current standard is 172 grams of greenhouse gas emissions per mile (107 grams/km)  and it will remain at the level now through the end of 2029. Thereafter, God willing and the creek don’t rise, the standard will begin ratcheting down every year until it reaches 74 grams per mile (46 grams/km).

A joint release by prime minister Mark Carney and Ontario premier Doug Ford said, “Maintaining the fleet average performance standard will provide manufacturers with near-term flexibility at a time when the industry is facing trade-related pressures. It will also provide the ability to generate greenhouse gas credits for use in later years, while continuing to improve emissions performance and fuel economy over time.” In addition to the revised emissions timeline, the federal and Ontario governments are investing $2.5 billion over two years to support capital projects by Canadian auto manufacturers.

Reaction to the new policy has been mixed. Climate activists are disappointed to see any retreat from the emissions standards that were in place, since the new rules will undermine the transition to electric cars. Industries hoped the announcement would bring a sense of direction, clarity, and certainty to the future. In any event, Chinese manufacturers are waiting in the wings, as Canada has opened a door for up to 49,000 Chinese EVs to be imported with only a 6.1 percent tariff.

“[T]he decision to pause any increase in the regulation until model year 2030 puts significantly more pressure on the policy’s later years and could undermine success down the road,” Clean Energy Canada said in a statement. “The unexpected delay will impact the potential benefits of stronger EV uptake, including thousands in fuel cost savings for households and up to $21 billion in charging industry investments. At the same time, Canadian communities will face the increased health costs and impacts from worse air quality.”

Pembina Institute, a British Columbia based clean energy advocacy group, took issue with the new policy, claiming it will make it virtually impossible for Canada to achieve its EV goals. “Earlier this year, the federal government promised stronger vehicle pollution standards starting in 2027, but they are now delaying that to 2029. This makes its goal of 75 per cent EVs by 2035 next to impossible, delaying progress toward giving Canadians more affordable vehicle choices.”

Canadian automakers are quite pleased with the longer timeline the federal government is providing for scaling up EV sales, believing it will help protect Canadian jobs and investments. “We welcome the measures to support Canada’s auto industry through this challenging period,” Brian Kingston, CEO of the Canadian Vehicle Manufacturers Association, said in a statement.  “Today’s announcement is an important step toward a more predictable, competitive, and achievable regulatory environment needed to protect Canadian auto jobs, production, and investment.

Doug Ford acknowledged the midterm elections in the US, coupled with the approaching halfway point of the worst presidency in history, could be inflection points. Yet they are unlikely to undo the changes already reshaping cross-border trade. “As we stand here today, it’s clear that Canada’s relationship with the United States has changed permanently. No matter what happens in the midterms, no matter who sits in the White House, we’re not going back to the way things were, and we need to plan accordingly,” he said.

In Europe, things are infinitely more complex, due to the need to achieve consensus among representatives from multiple political parties and 27 countries. Automakers there are also pleading for more time to transition to electric vehicles. A few weeks ago, France and Germany were able to secure an agreement that backed away somewhat from the EU’s zero emissions by 2035 policy, but that was just part of the process.

Now Germany’s Handelsblatt reports that a consensus achieved by a number of centrist parties would allow the 100 percent reduction target to be lowered to as little as 87.5 percent, provided the manufacturers use at least 7.5 percent green steel to build their vehicles. Sharp eyed readers will note that means the EV sales target will be 95 percent by 2035 rather than 100 percent.

The next step is for the Environment Committee to vote in early November, according to information from the Der Spiegel, with the plenary session likely to take place in December. To push the package through, the EU Parliament must first approve it, followed by the member states in the EU Council. A simple majority is sufficient in the EU Parliament, while a so-called “qualifietd majority” is required in the EU Council. This means the approval of at least 15 countries representing at least 65 percent of the EU population.

Phew! With all that rigmarole needed to set a final policy, it’s a wonder the EU ever gets anything done. the takeaway is that short term concerns so often interfere with long term commitments. No one would suggest that major industries should be forced to go out of business. There are too many people dependent on them for employment for that to happen. But the climate emergency won’t wait.

In the words of Paul Simon, “The nearer your destination, the more you’re slip sliding away.” That’s exactly what’s happening. Regardless of the justifications offered, the Earth can’t wait. There won’t be any industries, or jobs, or customers once the Sixth Extinction event gets going in earnest, which may happen much sooner than anyone expects.

I don't think Abelina is a puzzle game, but it does look like you have to put some thought into your platforming at points. The roadshow of condemned men is demanding more creativity from those who feel wronged by Manchester City than just chucking verbal tomatoes at players who have nothing to do with any guilty verdicts. For a couple of locals around Aston Villa, that meant an afternoon at Hobbycraft.

Armed with a pack of multi-coloured chalk down Trinity Road, they set about scrawling inventive messages for their visitors. Among the top hits were ‘UAE = Fake Sheikhs’ and ‘Boycott Dubai’. Given the sentiments, they might not have been Villa supporters. Or geography fans, either.

The bleak barrage of weather washed the chalk away by the time City’s bus rolled into town, a route it would ordinarily have taken were it not for significant redevelopment going on at Villa Park.

And it is that building work, eventually raising capacity to over 50,000, that will serve as a driver towards swelling Villa’s matchday revenue, pushing up their financial ceiling in a way that could help Unai Emery build a squad that can consistently qualify for the Champions League and bridge the gap between themselves and those who want them kept out of the elite.

For that reason, the reaction to City arriving in the Midlands - at a stadium they almost always struggle and where they have now lost four consecutive matches - provided real intrigue. Those up in the boardroom, who want to progress Villa further than PSR rules allow and build them back into the powerhouse of yore, are likely observing City’s appeal with hope of a favourable result for the defendants. If not exoneration, then certainly a bloody nose to the Premier League and general chaos in creating a scenario that would nudge conversation among those in positions of power to talking about how the current set of rules serve to constrain.

Because it cannot be right that Villa have had to sell so many of their top talent despite bobbing in and out of the Champions League and winning the Europa League. Unai Emery’s relative silence on City’s guilty verdicts, a refusal to add to widespread censure, speaks volumes and he has instead focused on shining a light on how his club are relying on selling high and buying potential to balance the books.

So the matchday discussions over an early lunch between executives will have been cordial and friendly on Saturday morning, before Emery’s glorious counter-attacking undid the Premier League leaders. Like a few other clubs, Villa might well sound more sympathetic to City’s legal scrapes behind the scenes and are watching the next few months with interest.

There is some supporter sentiment in that direction as well, believing they are being hamstrung – with the consequences of missing out on Champions League football in 2025 on goal difference, thus unable to keep hold of Marcus Rashford and Marco Asensio, an easily identifiable case of injustice.

But then there are the sporting moments in time that fans remember, which can make them diametrically opposed to what City are and what they represent. It’s always boisterous at Villa for City, always hostile, and they do not forget Fabian Delph, Gareth Barry and Jack Grealish being taken from them. Callously, in their view.

There was no world in which City’s latest trip to this part of Birmingham, the first of Enzo Maresca’s reign, would be any different. Yet City teams have, in the past, suffered worse – notably when Delph returned to the sight of the toy snakes in the stands. The building site behind one goal will have contributed to a degree.

But some of that felt like pantomime, as it did at Anfield a fortnight ago, and there felt like more humour to their indignation – suggesting in unison that an errant pass for Jeremy Doku flying straight out for a throw was evidence of why City are going down.

‘Premier League, corrupt as f***’ rang out when Johan Manzambi’s appeals for a penalty were waved away by Peter Bankes. Villa were actually louder as a collective when Bankes became more involved and once Nicolas Jackson netted his second, responding to footballing factors than any deep-seated hate for those who stood opposite their side.

City’s travelling party next heads to Nottingham Forest in a fortnight. A club with similarly ambitious owners, with aspirations of far more than their current lot.
