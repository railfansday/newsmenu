#4820. Intel, Oracle, IBM Join Objections to OKX-ICE Tokenized Stock Venue, Bringing Total to 26

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Intel, Oracle, IBM Join Objections to OKX-ICE Tokenized Stock Venue, Bringing Total to 26

The tech sector’s resistance to tokenized stocks keeps building.

Intel, Oracle, IBM, and quantum computing firm IonQ are the latest companies to object to having their shares traded on OKXICE TSV, according to an updated public notice from the venue dated Monday.

The planned platform, a 50-50 joint venture between NYSE owner Intercontinental Exchange and OKX’s U.S. holding company, now lists 26 objecting issuers.

That’s up from 22 in its Oct. 23 notice, which added Wells Fargo, Cisco, and Marvell. The tally stood at 16 on Oct. 19 and nine on Oct. 15, when the first batch included Verizon, Broadcom and UnitedHealth Group. In less than two weeks, the list has nearly tripled.

Technology companies dominate the roster. Microsoft, Alphabet, Broadcom, Qualcomm, Arm, Cisco, and now Intel and Oracle have all objected, along with banks Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo.

IBM and IonQ add two quantum computing players. As before, the notices don’t explain the companies’ reasons or what an objection means for whether a stock can trade on the platform.

OKXICE TSV plans to let approved users trade tokenized U.S. stocks 24/7 through permissioned Uniswap v4 liquidity pools on OKX’s XLayer blockchain, paired with stablecoins such as USDC.

Users must pass identity and sanctions checks and hold a nontransferable “soulbound” token to trade. The venue is set to operate under the SEC’s innovation exemption, which the agency unveiled in September after the Clarity Act stalled in the Senate.

The latest notice highlights a key risk for investors. Each token represents a claim on a real share held by a third-party “tokenizer” through a registered broker-dealer. If that broker-dealer failed, the notice says, it’s uncertain whether tokenized shares in a self-custodied wallet would count as customer property or be covered by the Securities Investor Protection Corporation, which protects brokerage customers when firms fail.

The notice also says trades must be fully funded, with no margin or borrowing. Liquidity providers don’t have to be registered market makers and can pull their liquidity at any time.

OKX and ICE first teamed up on tokenized securities earlier this year before unveiling plans for the venue this month.

#5374. The KTM X-Bow Is Finally Coming To American Roads

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The KTM X-Bow has been around since 2008, but there has never been a road-legal production version available in the United States — until now. KTM X-Bow has just announced the X-Bow GT-XA, a U.S. road version of its nimble sports car. The spun-off Austrian manufacturer plans to build 30 cars for the model's first year of production, but there is just one hiccup. The GT-XA has yet to be approved for U.S. road use.

KTM X-Bow stated that its exclusive U.S. partner, Carbon Yard, is working to secure U.S. road legalization for the GT-XA. Regardless of how long that takes, the factory in Graz, Austria, will be assembling sports cars with a delivery target of 2027 in mind. Prospective buyers can place reservations for $50,000, which will remain fully refundable until the GT-XA becomes road legal. KTM X-Bow has yet to announce technical specifications or a final MSRP for the U.S.-spec two-seater, but is anticipating a $490,000 price tag.

The X-Bow was forever changed when it went racing in 2015. KTM produced a factory racing version for GT4 competition, which necessitated a closed cockpit to fit the regulations. Engineers fitted a canopy over the cockpit because the existing monocoque made doors impractical. The canopy would make its street-legal X-Bow debut in 2022 with the GT-XR, the likely basis for the upcoming GT-XA. According to X-Bow, the U.S.-spec version will feature a front-axle lift system to make it easier to pull into driveways.

The canopy undoubtedly expanded the X-Bow's customer base, but it strayed from the car's initial ethos. The 2,760-pound GT-XR is over 1,000 pounds heavier than the original open-cockpit model. It might not be a true fighter jet canopy, as KTM X-Bow advertises, but I wouldn't want to be inside a canopied X-Bow if it flipped on a public road. I'm not sure I could squeeze my way out of one of those tiny windows. At a race track, at least the marshals are there to respond and help you escape if you end up trapped in an overturned X-Bow.

I don't want to sound too critical of creature comforts, but the original X-Bow was a breath of fresh air for the realm of enthusiast sports cars from a very unusual source. An Austrian motorcycle manufacturer produced a lightweight, open-cockpit sports car constructed around a carbon fiber monocoque. The partnership with dominant single-seat builder Dallara led many to think of the X-Bow as a street-legal, formula-style race car, but its raw agility could easily be described as a four-wheeled motorcycle. The GT-XR just isn't that. I guess I should also mention that, after KTM's massive restructuring, the X-Bow was spun off into a separate company with different owners than the bike maker.

#3629. Oct 28 Could Be A Huge Day For The Stock Market

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Wednesday, Oct. 28, 2026 could bring some big swings for the stock market. That's because the Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee (FOMC) announces whether interest rates will move higher, lower, or be held steady.

Rate announcements tend to spark a burst of trading activity across the market regardless of the circumstances, but with bond yields on the rise and a significant portion of the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.60%) concentrated in artificial intelligence (AI) -- an industry that is now, more than ever, leaning on debt markets, the Fed's decision carries even more weight than usual.

Here's what investors should be watching.

The Fed has two main jobs: keep prices stable (ie, fight inflation) and drive maximum employment. This is its "dual mandate." The two goals generally pull in different directions: higher interest rates slow inflation by making borrowing and spending more expensive, but that tends to curb growth and keep businesses from hiring. Lower rates tend to make borrowing cheaper, leading to expansion, more employment, and higher wages. But all that money flowing through the system leads to inflation.

Here's what the Fed has to go on at this point.

While unemployment is low (4.2%), the latest jobs report gives officials a reason to be somewhat cautious about hiking rates. The economy added just 29,000 jobs in September. Hiring has generally been sluggish for some time, averaging 45,000 jobs a month over the last year.

At the same time, the other side of the equation is moving in the wrong direction: the Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose 3.7% in September from a year earlier. That was up from 3.4% in August.

The Personal Consumption Expenditures price index (PCE) -- generally the inflation metric the Fed leans on most -- showed prices rising 3.4% annually in August. The September reading actually comes out on Thursday, a day after the rate decision. Keep in mind, the Fed's "target" for inflation is 2%.

Combined, it presents a somewhat mixed picture, with rising inflation and a less-than-stellar job market. That puts the Fed in a tough spot.

The biggest single factor driving inflation is energy. Gasoline prices jumped more than 9% in September and were responsible for about half of the monthly PCI increase.

The big question for the Fed is whether to "look through" the current inflation. If you remove the spike in gas prices, the recent readings look much tamer, and because the current spike in gas prices is largely being driven by the War in Iran, it's possible that the inflation could be "transitory" -- that is, it could cool down without the Fed's help if the war is brought to a close.

At the same time, the Fed may believe hiking rates would have little effect on gas prices given the reason they are elevated.

But if the current dynamic holds, those elevated gas prices could bleed into the rest of the economy and turn transitory inflation into something more systemic. That's basically the argument New York Fed President John Williams made in September.

The Fed is expected to leave rates unchanged tomorrow, choosing another month of wait-and-see. I don't expect a surprise here. But what investors should pay attention to is any signaling as to where rates might move in the future. If it signals more hikes are coming -- I think they will -- it could have a real impact on the AI trade.

Building data centers and filling them with chips is expensive, and companies -- even the massive hyperscalers like Meta and Amazon -- are increasingly borrowing to pay for it. Higher financing costs make those projects harder to justify and could lead to a pullback in spending if rates move high enough.

The rate announcement is set for Oct. 28 at 2 p.m. ET and will be followed by a press conference at 2:30 p.m.

Nvidia continues to dominate the market for AI chips and has much of the cutting-edge technology that hyperscalers need, so it's important for companies like Meta to establish a close relationship with Nvidia.

For Nvidia, it's the latest example of the company shoring up its AI empire by bringing customers, suppliers, and other stakeholders into its fold through a series of investments, partnerships, and contractual commitments.

In fact, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk recently declared that his Nvidia's Vera Rubin architecture was superior and said that SpaceX would exclusively build on Nvidia hardware.

Expect Nvidia to continue to forge more deals like the one it just did with Meta, especially as the AI infrastructure spenders look for new sources of capital. Nvidia is becoming a kingmaker, and that will only widen its economic moat as the AI boom continues.

From November 16, 2026, it may also start showing what your community is talking about. Including this helps someone with a question find their way to your community if it already has the answer.

Channel Summaries. These show a small slice of what's being talked about in your server, directly on your Server Web Page. These automated summaries focus on active conversations from November 16, 2026, onwards. Admins can change which channels they draw from, curate the summaries that are shown, or turn them off entirely. Usernames and avatars will not be shown in summaries.

Channel Messages. From mid-December 2026, answers to questions your members sent in public channels can be found in a web search, in their own words. Server admins decide which channels take part, and server members can choose whether their own messages appear. Usernames and avatars will not be shown alongside these messages.

You can already see an early version of this on a few Server Web Pages. We’ve been piloting Channel Messages with Discoverable game communities, including NBA 2K, Rust, and Dead by Daylight. (During this pilot, all posts were from admins and moderators, not members.)

If you're running a community, useful answers to common community questions can show up in web search: how to unlock an easter egg, new builds or strategies, or a boss guide. Someone looking for help will be able to find a real answer from a real person, and can step straight into the conversation that it came from by clicking through and opening Discord.

Starting mid-December 2026, if a server’s admins allow it, messages from public channels can appear on Server Web Pages and in search results, without usernames and avatars. Messages you sent before then won’t be shown. Channels where content may appear on the web will be labeled, so you know before you send a message.

If you'd rather your messages weren't shown, you can turn that off today in User Settings > Data and Privacy. One switch covers every Discoverable Server you're a part of.

We're rolling this out gradually, so it may be a few weeks before the first messages from those channels go live. Learn more.

Your server controls start rolling out today, in Server Settings > Web Presence. You can choose which channels take part, or turn them all off.

If you don’t change anything, public channels stay on, your Server Web Page goes live on November 16, 2026 (if it's not already published), and Channel Summaries can start the same day. Channel Messages start rolling out in mid-December 2026. You can adjust your Channel Summaries and Channel Messages settings before they roll out. And if you'd rather publish your Server Web Page yourself, you can do that today.

However you set things up, we hope this is a step toward bringing back what made forums great: answers anyone can find, and an open door to the community that wrote them.
