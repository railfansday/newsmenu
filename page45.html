#3679. Will King Charles Be the Last Windsor to Lead the Commonwealth?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

According to a report by The Times, King Charles is reportedly aware that he may be the last member of the British royal family to serve as head of the Commonwealth. The reason? His son William, the future monarch of England, reportedly has no intention of succeeding him in this role as well. A prospect that, if confirmed, would mark a significant change in relations between the British monarchy and the organization that brings together 56 countries. For while it is true that William is destined to inherit the throne, it is by no means certain that he wishes to retain every responsibility and tradition associated with the Crown. (Vanity Fair has reached out to Buckingham Palace for comment on the Times report.)

The issue, in reality, is more complex than it might seem. Unlike the British throne, in fact, the leadership of the Commonwealth is not automatically passed from a sovereign to their heir. It is a symbolic role, and succession is decided by the leaders of the member states. Charles assumed the role after the death of his mother, Elizabeth, but his appointment had been agreed upon by the Commonwealth heads of government in 2018. William, therefore, would not be obligated to accept it.

According to The Times’ report, the Prince of Wales has indicated that he does not wish to become head of the organization when he ascends to the throne. However, this is neither an official announcement from Kensington Palace nor a decision that has already been formalized. To understand the significance of this potential decision, we must look back to Elizabeth. During her 70-year reign, the monarch made the Commonwealth one of the pillars of her institutional work, guiding the transformation of an organization tied to the history of the British Empire into a voluntary association of independent states.

Charles has embraced that legacy with conviction. His commitment to the Commonwealth is an important part of his diplomatic vision and the relationship he intends to maintain with countries united by a shared—and often complex—history. And it is precisely this complexity that makes the current moment particularly delicate. The king is, in fact, preparing for a trip to the Caribbean, scheduled for October 27 through November 4, that will include stops in the Bahamas, Guyana, and Antigua and Barbuda.

This visit comes as the debate over Britain’s colonial past, slavery, and calls for economic reparations continues to take center stage in relations between London and several Caribbean nations.

For some time now, however, Prince William has been associated with the idea of a more streamlined monarchy, with a smaller number of members engaged in official duties and a greater focus on the causes he considers priorities, from the environment to mental health. In this context, a potential relinquishment of the leadership of the Commonwealth could be interpreted as a further sign of change. This does not necessarily mean distancing himself from member countries, but rather a different interpretation of the role the British monarch should play in relation to them.

After all, the Commonwealth does not consist solely of territories that recognize Charles as head of state. It also includes numerous republics, and his leadership does not entail governing powers over member countries. William’s potential selection would therefore raise a primarily symbolic question: Who could represent the organization after Charles, and what relationship would they wish to maintain with the monarchy?

For Charles, who is deeply attached to the Crown’s international role and to continuity with his mother’s reign, the prospect of being the last Windsor to lead the Commonwealth would hold historical significance. For William, on the other hand, it could represent a way to distinguish the constitutional responsibilities of the future sovereign from those belonging to a particular phase of British history.

For now, this is merely a scenario, not a succession that has already been decided. But the question raised by The Times goes beyond an institutional role: It concerns how the British monarchy intends to redefine its presence in the world. The future King William will certainly inherit a crown. But it may be precisely what he chooses not to inherit that best reflects the monarchy to come.

BloombergNEF estimates that grid-connected data centers alone could add an additional 15 billion cubic feet per day of natural gas demand by 2035, a 15 percent increase in overall demand. Anthropic has traditionally relied on renting compute from major cloud service providers. Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and SpaceX are specifically named in the IPO. The Matthias Jaissle Everton press conference has taken place on Tuesday.

This is ahead of the Newcastle United match on Thursday, a 7.45pm kick-off at the Hill Dickinson Stadium.

Newcastle United looking to follow up Sunday’s Premier League victory at Crystal Palace.

As well as of course hoping to progress to the Carabao Cup quarter-finals.

Matthias Jaissle on team selection, with United facing Everton in the Premier League four days after this cup encounter:

“Due to the history for the club in that competition, I have the same approach, to play with the best possible line-up. We are getting huge support again in an away match. It is really special, during the week, that we will have 8,000 fans with us, taking the journey to support us. It shows the importance about that competition and we will also be aligned with that and go all in. The fans have huge expectations and they have that taste now with winning the competition.”

Matthias Jaissle on the appointment of Tomás Amaral as the club’s new Head of First Team Recruitment:

“It is always good to improve things and Ross [Wilson] is the responsible guy and leading that department. It’s always good for experts to have fresh energy and we’re looking forward to working together. Like it was in the last couple of days in the [summer] transfer window, it is a pure and clear alignment between everyone involved. As a coach, you thrive for high expectations when it comes to signings.”

Matthias Jaissle on Sunday’s win against Crystal Palace:

“We were doing the simple things a bit better in the second half. We emphasised, like always at half-time, the potential and what we can improve in our game. We had a more offensive mindset after ball wins and improved in possession with our structure. Now, it’s time to build on that. We have shown a solid performance and deserved that win.”

Matthias Jaissle on last night’s NUFC Under-21s win over Sunderland at St. James’ Park, winning 3-2 after having trailed 0-1 and 1-2:

“It was nice. First of all, I’m happy for the young lads getting the opportunity to feel and taste a little bit of that atmosphere. It should give them that vision of where they want to be in the future, playing in a full stadium. They did a great job.”

Matthias Jaissle asked about the availability of Lewis Miley and Matias Fernandez-Pardo for the Everton Cup match after missing the win at Crystal Palace:

“These two players are still doubtful…Question marks on if they can make it for Thursday night. We need to see step-by-step over the next two days to decide if they are able to be in the squad or not. With both of them we don’t want to take any additional risk and hopefully, they are getting back soon.”

Matthias Jaissle also asked for an update on Tino Livramento:

“I don’t want to put too much pressure on him…also the medical department. That is usually my approach when it comes to injuries. I can just assure you that I see him every day with a great professionalism in his rehab, hard working, so hopefully we have him back soon and that he is stable in his approach when he is joining us.”
