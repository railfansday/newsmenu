#4773. Here’s what happened in crypto today

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Need to know what happened in crypto today? Here is the latest news on daily trends and events impacting Bitcoin price, blockchain, DeFi, Web3 and crypto regulation.

Today in crypto, Circle’s USDC overtook Tether’s USDT in euro stablecoin trading as EU regulations reshaped local markets, Binance applied for a crypto license in Brazil ahead of an Oct. 30 deadline and Ripple secured final US regulatory approval to establish a national trust bank.

Circle’s USDC has overtaken Tether’s USDt in stablecoin trading against the euro, capturing 56% of the market as European exchanges adjust to the EU’s Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), according to Kaiko.

In a new report, Kaiko said USDC’s share has surged from just 10% in January 2024, with the shift accelerating after major exchanges restricted USDT trading for customers in the European Economic Area.

USDC has also gained a liquidity advantage, with euro trading order books now roughly 40% deeper than USDT’s. However, Tether remains the dominant stablecoin globally, with a circulating supply approximately 2.5 times larger than USDC’s.

The findings follow the European Banking Authority’s decision to classify USDC as the EU’s first significant e-money token under MiCA, placing it under direct regulatory supervision.

Kaiko suggested a similar shift could emerge in the United States as regulators implement the GENIUS Act. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency recently finalized reserve and redemption requirements for supervised stablecoin issuers, effective January 2027.

Further restrictions on noncompliant stablecoins are scheduled for July 2028, potentially benefiting Circle while Tether pursues the US market through its separately regulated USAT stablecoin.

Binance has applied for a cryptocurrency service provider license in Brazil, seeking to maintain its operations in one of Latin America’s largest crypto markets ahead of a regulatory deadline on Friday, Oct. 30.

The exchange submitted its application to the Central Bank of Brazil on Friday, Oct. 23, through its local entity, BBrasil, according to a statement shared with Cointelegraph.

The company said the filing would allow Brazilian users to continue accessing crypto assets, stablecoin trading, payments and investment services.

Binance has also announced changes for Brazilian users effective Tuesday, including restrictions on crypto lending, margin trading and other services. The exchange is also delisting 22 crypto assets in the country.

Brazil requires existing crypto service providers to submit authorization applications by Oct. 30 to continue operating under transitional arrangements. Filing an application does not constitute regulatory approval.

Ripple announced Monday that its national trust bank has secured final approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC).

The approval completes the chartering process for Ripple National Trust Bank (RNTB), which received conditional approval from the OCC in December 2025.

The federally supervised trust bank is authorized to manage reserves backing Ripple USD (RLUSD), act as collateral trustee for the benefit of RLUSD holders and provide digital asset custody services, according to Ripple.

RLUSD will continue to be issued by Standard Custody & Trust Company, Ripple’s limited purpose trust company regulated by the New York Department of Financial Services.

“We’ve always said crypto and banks work better together — this is what that looks like. Real innovation, real accountability, federal oversight,” Ripple CEO Brad Garlinghouse said on X on Monday.

#2888. Hornets to retire the jersey of Muggsy Bogues, the shortest player in NBA history

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The Charlotte Hornets will retire the No. 1 jersey of Muggsy Bogues on Feb. 11, the team announced Tuesday. Bogues, at 5-foot-3, is the shortest player in NBA history, and now that the Hornets will hang his jersey in the rafters of the Spectrum Center, he will become, by a wide margin, the shortest player in NBA history to have his jersey retired. That distinction was previously held by Calvin Murphy, the 5-foot-9 point guard who played his entire career for the San Diego and later Houston Rockets.

Bogues, a college star at Wake Forest, was drafted No. 12 overall by the Washington Bullets in 1987. That allowed him to start his career playing next to the 7-foot-7 Manute Bol, who, at the time, was the tallest player in NBA history (a title he now shares with Gheorghe Mureșan). However, the Bullets left Bogues unprotected in the 1988 expansion draft, which allowed the incoming Charlotte Hornets to swipe him before their inaugural season.

on february 11th, we will honor the one & only muggsy bogues. 🐝press release: https://t.co/gq6cYbwKM9 | @MuggsyBogues pic.twitter.com/jJ8zkefsWq

Washington's loss was Charlotte's gain. Bogues grew into the full-time starting point guard for the Hornets during the 1989-90 season and held his post through 1997. He never made an All-Star Team or averaged more than 11.1 points per game, but he averaged double-digit assists on multiple occasions and is Charlotte's all-time leader in steals. The 90s represent the most successful era in Hornets history, with Bogues, Larry Johnson and Alonzo Mourning leading Charlotte to several postseason trips.

Now, aside from his distinction as the NBA's shortest player ever, Bogues is remembered fondly for his role in Space Jam. He was one of the five then-active NBA players whose talent was stolen by the MonStars to face Michael Jordan and the Looney Tunes.

Bogues will become the third member of the Hornets to have his jersey retired, joining former teammates Bobby Phills, who died in a car accident in 2000, and Dell Curry, who remains with the team as a broadcaster.

#3471. Why Herc Holdings Rocketed Over 20% Today

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Shares of construction equipment rental company Herc Holdings (HRI +22.76%) rocketed 23.4% today as of 2:21 p.m. EDT.

Herc rallied on an exceptionally strong earnings report published this morning, with a beat on both the top and bottom lines, and an increase to full-year guidance.

Herc had been trading at a fairly low valuation relative to equipment rental peers, amid concerns about its high debt stemming from last year's acquisition of H&E Equipment Services. However, those fears were allayed by the company's resilient growth and fleet utilization last quarter.

In the third quarter, Herc saw total revenue rise by 4.6% to $1.36 billion; however, the company's bottom line showed much stronger growth, with adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share up a whopping 76.1% to $3.91. Both figures handily beat expectations.

The massive increase in profitability was due to a 12% increase in equipment rental revenue and a 3.6 percentage-point increase in overall utilization. The improved efficiency was due to the company's successful integration of H&E, which closed in June of 2025.

While local residential and non-residential construction has been sluggish, Herc has apparently landed enough "mega-projects" in areas like data centers and oil and gas to continue growing.

While higher interest rates can be a headwind for construction, higher borrowing costs may also lead more construction entities to lease equipment from companies like Herc rather than buy it outright. That may be playing a role here as well.

Today's solid and improving results led to a big move in the stock because Herc came into the day trading at a steep discount to other large equipment rental companies, such as United Rentals (URI +3.57%) and Sunbelt Rentals (SUNB +3.07%). This was due to concerns over Herc's debt load resulting from the H&E acquisition. However, today's results appeared to indicate the realization of both cost and revenue synergies, resulting in higher-than-expected profitability.

After today's leap, Herc stock trades at roughly 21.5 times 2026 adjusted earnings estimates and about 14.4 times 2027 earnings estimates. That's a very reasonable price to pay for the stock, as long as the company can continue to find projects to utilize its expanded fleet. Data center growth appears set to continue through next year, and if the housing slump ever recovers, that would be another tailwind for this well-executing industrial stock.

An experienced keeper at a Dutch zoo has died days after being mauled in a leopard enclosure, the safari park has confirmed.

The woman, who had worked at Beekse Bergen park for 40 years, was caring for two Persian leopards in an indoor facility when she was attacked on Saturday.

She sustained injuries to her head and neck but despite undergoing surgery, the park confirmed her death on Tuesday.

An independent investigation will be launched into the incident, it added in a statement.

Announcing her death, Beekse Bergen - in the south of the Netherlands - said the keeper began her career there when she was 17 and had worked there on-and-off for four decades.

"Her passing has deeply affected us all, and our hearts go out to her family, friends, and loved ones. At this moment, our primary focus is on supporting her family and her colleagues," the statement added.

The two leopards, which are usually kept away from staff, had been brought inside due to bad weather conditions.

It is not clear exactly what happened in the enclosure because there are no CCTV cameras inside.

Local news website NL Times reported that despite her injures she managed to alert colleagues via her walkie-talkie. She was then taken to hospital in a helicopter in a critical condition before she died days later.

A park spokesperson told Omroep Brabant, a local media outlet, that the leopards were behaving naturally and they would not be euthanised.

Beekse Bergen said it would create a memorial location for guests and staff members to share their condolences.
