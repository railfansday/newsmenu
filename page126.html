US spot Bitcoin ETFs remained in the red for October, with about $202 million in net outflows for the month despite Monday’s rebound.

US-listed spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) returned to net inflows on Monday despite weakening market sentiment and continued volatility in BTC price.

The funds attracted $110 million in net inflows on Monday, bouncing back from $774 million in withdrawals over the previous two trading sessions, according to SoSoValue.

Despite the rebound, October remained negative, with approximately $202 million in net outflows through Monday.

Flows in US spot Bitcoin ETFs since Oct. 19. Source: SoSoValue

Bitcoin traded at $79,595 at the time of publication and was down 2.6% over the past seven days, according to CoinGecko.

Market sentiment also weakened, with the Crypto Fear & Greed Index falling to 48 from 50 the previous day, while remaining in “Neutral” territory.

CryptoQuant data also showed retail investors sending more Bitcoin to Binance while large holders reduced their deposits.

Analyst Darkfost wrote on CryptoQuant that retail Bitcoin deposits to Binance nearly doubled to $10.8 billion over 30 days, while deposits from large holders fell to $3.8 billion. Darkfost suggested that smaller investors might be using the recent price rebound as an opportunity to sell.

By Lewis Blain | 27 Oct 2026 07:33

Manchester United are reportedly considering selling captain Bruno Fernandes next summer following a dramatic deterioration in his relationship with Michael Carrick.

The Portugal international was left on the bench for Sunday's defeat to AFC Bournemouth after publicly questioning the manager's tactics.

United could now look to extend his contract to protect his transfer value before deciding whether it is time to move on from their influential skipper.

The Red Devils hierarchy are assessing the possibility of selling Fernandes next summer, according to emerging reports, with sources close to the situation indicating that his departure is now a genuine option.

The 31-year-old is approaching the end of his current contract, although United hold an option to extend his deal by a further year. The club are considering activating that clause to preserve his value before potentially sanctioning a sale.

Fernandes' situation has changed considerably in a short space of time. Despite recently being named Premier League Player of the Year and breaking the competition's record for assists in a single season, he is now facing increased scrutiny following his criticism of Carrick's tactics.

The midfielder was subsequently left as an unused substitute during their 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth, having started every previous Premier League game this season.

Fernandes has registered three goals and one assist in seven league appearances, but his performances and body language have attracted criticism in recent weeks.

Interest from Saudi Arabia could also resurface in January or in the summer, potentially giving United a lucrative opportunity to cash in. A departure is far from guaranteed, but the club appear increasingly willing to consider their options.

Fernandes has been one of United's most important players for years, but there is a growing argument that a separation could now benefit both parties.

His quality is not in question. The problem is that United's squad remains so imbalanced that successive managers have struggled to play him in his best position, as a no.10.

Carrick has used Fernandes on the right wing and up front, while Ruben Amorim deployed him in a deeper midfield role. Those adjustments have often appeared designed to accommodate other players rather than maximise the captain's creative influence.

Fernandes is at his best when he can operate between the lines, dictate attacking transitions and take risks in the final third. United need a more balanced midfield behind him to support that, and upgrades to their attack if they are to get the most out of a player of his calibre.

There is also the question of his relationship with Carrick. Publicly challenging the manager and subsequently being dropped has added another complication to an already difficult season.

Selling Fernandes would be a significant decision, particularly given his importance to the team, but extending his contract before considering a transfer could make financial sense.

If United can secure a substantial fee and reinvest it in players better suited to a coherent long-term system, moving on may be the right decision for everyone.

Fernandes deserves a team built around his strengths, and United need a squad that works collectively rather than one that relies so heavily on a single creative outlet.

Is liquidity what’s missing from Solana’s recent weakness?

From a technical perspective, SOL has performed extremely poorly in October, falling over 15%. The asset has seen its market cap fall by more than $10 billion to $58 billion and its price has fallen below the key $100 level. This may cause more panic selling to occur as macro uncertainty grows.

However, is this weakness being caused by the overall altcoin market falling against Bitcoin, or “Solana-specific” outflows? The chart suggests that capital is flowing out of the altcoins overall with SOL/BTC down over 11% so far this month. That puts it on track for the worst monthly performance since November 2025.

In essence, Solana’s recent weakness looks more macro-driven than network-driven.

This is backed up by data from Token Terminal which shows that Solana has already executed over 9 billion transactions this month alone, with activity holding near the 10 billion mark. This suggests on-chain activity remains strong despite the price correction, potentially setting up an “undervaluation” opportunity for SOL if market sentiment flips back to risk-on.

So, the key question is: Could a shift back to risk-on sentiment trigger Solana’s [SOL] next recovery?

Strong on-chain liquidity is usually a sign of healthy fundamentals for any L1.

However, Solana itself paints a different picture. SOL’s Total Value Locked (TVL) has fallen by 10.28% from $6.42 billion to $5.76 billion. This comes compared to ETH which has seen its TVL fall by 8.42% from $95 billion to $87 billion. This indicates that liquidity is flowing out of Solana more quickly than Ethereum.

To add to the pressure, Solana stablecoin market cap has been declining since its peak of $16 billion in early October. Despite high on-chain activity, users are not pushing more capital into the chain. It is evident in the falling TVL and stablecoin liquidity. Even though there is high user activity on Solana, less capital is being locked up and used within the DeFi ecosystem which may be causing downward pressure on SOL.

In essence, Solana’s weakness goes beyond broader market pressure.

This indicates that there are more headwinds specific to Solana, as there is less capital being put into DeFi and therefore the SOL underperformance could be due to this lack of liquidity. Until the capital flows stabilize and new liquidity enters the market, SOL could continue to face downward pressure even if on-chain transaction activity remains strong.

#616. What’s happening with Ethan and Hila Klein? The H3 Podcast fallout, explained

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ethan Klein has spent years dissecting other people's internet scandals on the H3 Podcast, the comedy and commentary show he built with his wife, Hila. Now, their own relationship is at the center of the conversation.

Hila has accused Ethan of having an affair lasting more than six months, leaving the couple's marriage and the future of their show in question. Fans are trying to make sense of the news, while people across social media are digging through old episodes and swapping what they believe are clues.

If you've seen the posts but don't know the people behind them, here's what happened — and how a marriage, a podcast, and years of internet history became tangled together.

Before the podcast, there was h3h3Productions. Ethan and Hila launched the YouTube channel in 2011, posting sketches and reaction videos that poked fun at viral clips, internet trends, and other creators. Ethan was often the louder, more confrontational presence, with Hila appearing alongside him.

One of their breakout videos was 2016's "Vape Nation," (30 million views) which followed Ethan around New York City playing an exaggerated vaping enthusiast. The outfits, enormous vape clouds, and interactions with strangers helped make him a recognizable figure in YouTube comedy.

The couple met in Israel in 2007, while Ethan was visiting on a Birthright trip, and married in 2012. As their audience grew, viewers got to know them as both creative partners and a couple.

Their H3 Podcast expanded that world into longer conversations, mixing internet commentary with interviews featuring guests such as Post Malone and Joji. The production crew became part of the show, too, joining discussions and developing their own followings.

That dynamic also powered Frenemies, the show Ethan began co-hosting with YouTuber Trisha Paytas in 2020. Sitting in front of a pink-and-black backdrop, the two traded personal stories, discussed other creators, dressed in costumes, and, most commonly, argued.

The partnership ended in June 2021 amid disputes over money, production, and creative input. But Paytas remained connected to the family: She married Hila's brother, Moses Hacmon, later that year.

Through these different versions of H3, the Kleins' relationship remained a familiar presence. Viewers followed them as they built businesses and raised their three children. Hila also founded the clothing brand Teddy Fresh in 2017 and later launched the makeup brand Teddy Glow.

Their lives and work became closely intertwined. But now, their separation means figuring out how to untangle them.

On Oct. 22, Ethan announced on Instagram Stories that the H3 Podcast was going on "indefinite hiatus." Hila’s subsequent Instagram statement revealed the personal crisis unfolding behind the scenes.

"I am living in a nightmare I can’t wake up from," she wrote. "My husband has been having an affair. I have been fed lie after lie after lie for months. I am tired of lies. Here is the truth."

Hila said she had sensed something was wrong and tried to discuss her concerns with Ethan. According to her account, he dismissed her questions, but the behavior that worried her continued.

"So I trusted him. Because I loved him," she wrote. "I stopped questioning him and began to question myself, my own instincts. My sanity."

When she eventually caught him in a lie, she said, his explanation kept changing. "'It was just a kiss’ became ‘it was emotional cheating,'" she wrote. "After I dragged these lies from him, he finally admitted he’d been in an affair for over 6 months, the extent of which I’m sure I still don’t know."

"He was my safe place, my partner in marriage, parenting, and business," she continued. "My only family on this side of the world. How do I even begin to make sense of this?" Hila said she was navigating separation and co-parenting while focusing on herself and their children. She did not identify the other person allegedly involved.

The crew's response quickly made clear that the fallout would extend into the workplace.

"We are all shocked, angry, and heartbroken to learn about Ethan’s ongoing affair," the team wrote in its own Instagram statement. "We stand with Hila and will support her every step of the way."

"As soon as we found out, we all agreed: we would not continue working with Ethan involved or profiting in any way,” they added. "We don’t yet know what that means for the future. There’s a lot to work through, and we wish we had more answers for you."

Ethan subsequently said he was working to remove his financial interests from the businesses he shares with Hila.

"I am working with Hila to urgently remove my ownership and financial stakes in any podcasts, Teddy Fresh, Teddy Glow or any joint venture that I would profit from," he wrote on Instagram Stories.

"All of your support will go directly to Hila and the crew. I do this in the hope that people can still support Hila and the crew without the concern of enriching me." He also praised the team and said its members could make an "awesome show."

In a separate statement, Ethan asked people not to contact his family seeking him or their comments. "They have nothing to do with this and share in your outrage and disappointment,”"he wrote.

#4867. Ethereum fixes Glamsterdam bug that could have sent staking deposits to builders

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Ethereum developers have patched a flaw in the upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade that could have let someone walk off with ETH that staking services meant for new validators.

The fix, which changed the rules for handling validator deposits, was merged Monday, a week after developers turned it down.

Glamsterdam splits up two jobs that are bundled together today. Builders bundle transactions into blocks, and validators, who stake ETH to help secure the network, pick and confirm them. Both can deposit ETH, and their money is supposed to be tracked separately.

The problem appeared during the upgrade itself, when Ethereum would process deposits that were still waiting to be credited.

Researcher Avsetsin identified a scenario in which an operator first registers as a builder using a particular public key, the identifier tied to a validator or builder. If a staking service then deposits ETH to start a validator under that key, the draft rules would credit both deposits to the builder.

“The operator can withdraw the protocol’s funds,” Avsetsin wrote. No validator gets created, so the staking service can’t produce the proof it would need to recover its money.

Avsetsin, listed as a member of Lido’s GitHub organization, examined how Lido, Origin, and Rocket Pool could be affected in what the researcher called “quick AI research.” The findings depended on how each protocol handles deposits.

“These are conditional code findings, not confirmed live exploits,” avsetsin wrote, adding that existing checks rejecting an earlier builder deposit could stop the attack. No losses have been reported.

Developers closed the proposed fix on Oct. 19. “There was not enough support from clients to make this change at this time,” wrote Justin Traglia, who maintains Ethereum’s consensus specifications. Clients are the separate software programs validators run to take part in the network.

The fix was reopened after further review, avsetsin added tests on Oct. 23, and Traglia merged it Monday.

Under the new rules, pending deposits are sorted by their withdrawal credentials, the setting that says where a deposit’s money can eventually go. Validator deposits stay with validators even if a builder is using the same key.

The change is now part of Ethereum’s consensus specifications, and client teams still have to build it into their software. Glamsterdam has already run on the Sepolia test network, but its Oct. 26 launch on Hoodi, a second test network, was postponed.

Glamsterdam needs a new Hoodi date before a mainnet launch can be scheduled.
