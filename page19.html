#3364. Royal Caribbean: 3 Key Takeaways for Long-Term Investors

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

On its third quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 27, 2026, Royal Caribbean (RCL +5.46%) announced a $3 billion investment for a 50% stake in Sandals and Beaches Resorts as it expands beyond cruise operations. Extending into all-inclusive land resorts targets a broader share of consumer travel spending and creates new cross-promotional opportunities across its vacation brands. Three things management said on the call stand out.

Expanding beyond cruises into land-based all-inclusive resorts allows the business to acquire customer spending across multiple vacation types. The cruise line currently capturing 20% to 25% of a guest's annual vacation spending. With nearly one-third of those guests already visiting Caribbean all-inclusive properties, the joint venture capitalizes on existing consumer demand to increase that percentage.

Customer demand for cruise experiences continues to outpace prior years, filling future capacity further in advance and at higher prices. High booking volume builds a strong financial baseline for revenue stability into 2027.

Connecting brand experiences through loyalty programs keeps customers within the company's ecosystem and encourages more frequent travel. Half of all passengers are returning guests, which reduces customer acquisition expenses and increases lifetime spending.

Leisure travel demand is accelerating as consumers prioritize experiences, and the business is capturing that momentum across its brands.

High guest satisfaction scores, proprietary land destinations, and connected loyalty programs provide durable competitive moats to drive repeat visits while leadership focuses on pricing discipline and capacity expansion. Expanding into river cruises and all-inclusive land resorts secures a larger footprint across the global travel ecosystem.

#3372. I'd Own a Small Position in AMD Stock Through 2030, but I Wouldn't Expect It to Triple Again

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Shares of Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +5.46%) have dropped since hitting a record closing high of $649.42 on Oct. 6. As I write, the company's stock is above $550, or roughly 15% below its peak. Third-quarter results are due after the close on Tuesday, Nov. 3.

Where the stock ends 2030, though, depends less on the report than on how much AMD earns a share by then and what investors will pay for those earnings.

Going by AMD's own long-term earnings target, I think the stock ends 2030 somewhere between $600 and $750 a share.

That's a return of around 2% to 8% a year from here. It's decent. But it's a far cry from the past 52 weeks, when the stock more than tripled from a low of around $188 to its October record.

At its November 2025 financial analyst day, AMD set targets for the next three to five years, including yearly revenue growth over 35% and earnings per share above $20 on a non-GAAP (adjusted) basis. Five years after the event falls in late 2030.

Showing how big a leap that is, AMD's adjusted earnings per share came to $4.17 in 2025, a 26% gain over 2024. Quarterly earnings have grown since, but not in a straight line. Adjusted earnings per share went from $1.20 in Q3 2025 to $1.53 in Q4, slipped to $1.37 in Q1 2026, and then hit a record $1.66 in Q2.

Even that record quarter annualizes to under $7 a share. In other words, the target asks AMD to about triple its current earnings pace.

Management, though, thinks AMD can do better than that.

"[W]e expect to significantly exceed our $20 annual EPS target within our strategic time frame," CEO Lisa Su said on the company's Aug. 4 earnings call.

Helios, AMD's rack-scale artificial intelligence (AI) system, is a big part of that confidence, and management said in August that first shipments would start in the second half of 2026. And besides its gigawatt-scale deals with OpenAI and Meta Platforms (META -1.65%), AMD has also signed up Anthropic, which plans to deploy up to 2 gigawatts of AMD's MI450 series graphics processing units (GPUs) in Helios racks.

Under its 6-gigawatt deals with OpenAI and Meta, AMD gave each a warrant to buy up to 160 million AMD shares at a cent apiece. Tranches vest as each customer's GPU purchases meet milestones and as AMD's stock price tops set levels. As of June 27, none had vested.

AMD expects around 1.66 billion diluted shares for the third quarter. At $20 a share, that number means around $33 billion in yearly adjusted profit. Spread over about 1.98 billion shares, with all 320 million warrant shares added, the same profit is just around $17 a share.

Sure, every warrant share vests only if both customers buy their full 6 gigawatts. That's also the case where AMD's profit will probably be largest.

So I assume AMD earns $20 to $25 a share in 2030 with the larger share count. The low end takes the target at face value with every warrant share counted, and the high end gives Su's "significantly exceed" some room. Getting there takes yearly adjusted profit of about $40 billion to $50 billion -- roughly four times the second quarter's pace, when adjusted net income climbed 22% sequentially to about $2.8 billion.

Today's price is around 35 times expected 2027 earnings, based on analysts' consensus estimate. By the end of 2030, AMD should be a far bigger company growing at a slower rate. So I'd pick a price-to-earnings ratio of around 30 for 2030, a small drop from today's level.

At 30 times earnings, $20 to $25 per share means a stock price of $600 to $750 by the close of 2030.

Sure, the range could be too low. Su also said AMD now expects revenue to rise much faster than its previous target of over 35% a year. But chip demand has been cyclical for decades, and a slowdown in AI spending before 2030 could hurt both AMD's earnings and the price-to-earnings ratio investors are willing to pay. At 25 times earnings, the low end of my range drops to $500, under today's price.

Overall, I think AMD's plan is credible. It has more big Helios customers lined up than it had a year ago, and its earnings are rising. But the stock's price today arguably already reflects much of what AMD expects to earn by 2030.

I think the stock is still worth owning as a small position. I just wouldn't count on much more than single-digit yearly returns from here.

#3092. Three Surprises That Have Kept the Giants Atop the NFC East

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The New York Giants have blown away all expectations and are now heading into their bye week.

After the season-ending injury to Jaxson Dart, followed by even more year-long injuries to Brian Burns and Tremaine Edmunds, all hope felt lost.

Yet the Giants continue to persevere — they are firmly in first place in the NFC East with a 4-2-1 record and are in shape to win the division for the first time since 2011.

With the laundry list of roadblocks faced so far, there have been major surprises that have allowed them to keep their stride.

Before the 2026 season, New York's defensive back talent was one of the biggest worries, but it's become a strength. To headline the success is former first-rounder Deonte Banks.

Banks, who seemed to be on his way out after disastrous second and third seasons, has been a true lockdown corner. He's on pace for his lowest yards allowed, completion percentage and the most pass deflections in his career. Most recently, he completely shut down Pro Bowler Nico Collins, holding him to just one catch for four yards on 14 snaps.

He's always been able to keep up with anyone, but now Banks is finally turning his head around on time, attacking the ball ferociously. His physical gifts and newfound poise have made him one of the NFL's top breakout players.

Beyond Banks, the group has shown strong improvement under the new coaching regime. According to NFLPro, in nickel formation (five defensive backs), the Giants allow the 10th-fewest pass yards per play (5.5).

Safety Jevon Holland is also creating a strong Pro Bowl campaign. He has three interceptions (tied for first), two forced fumbles, and a fumble recovery. Holland's electric closing on the ball has led to his lowest completion percentage by far: 36.8%.

This group's success will remain pivotal with the injured front seven.

Though many of the Giants' new coaches have improved the team, defensive coordinator Dennard Wilson may be at the top of the thank-you list.

The unit is still far from perfect, but the leaps made this season are very noteworthy. The Giants allow 5.3 yards per play and have already forced nine turnovers, ranking in the top half of the league.

Wilson's blitz-heavy philosophy has meshed excellently with the G-Men. NFLPro lists them blitzing on 40.3% of dropbacks, the sixth most in the league. When blitzing, they have an EPA/pass (expected points added per pass) of -0.10, also ranking in the top 10.

These heavy blitz packages leave the Giants running a heavy dose of man coverage. Their man coverage on 55% of passes ranks second. Both their blitz and man percentages are 10%+ increases from last season.

The defensive backs' previously stated success is greatly benefited from these schemes. It allows them to rely on their athletic gifts and stay physical with their matchups.

Up front, the extra rushers have also clearly helped, especially without Dexter Lawrence. There's now a lack of extra blockers to account for star edge rusher Abdul Carter; it's helped him maintain his monstrous quick pressure rate from last season even without the presence of Brian Burns. Carter's 16 quick pressures (pressures <2.5 seconds) are the fifth best in the NFL.

Dennard Wilson's scheme has let the defensive stars shine and fully unlocked their suppressed gifts.

Whether it's their midseason quarterback acquisition or the Day 1 and 2 2026 draft picks, offensive newcomers on the New York team are making their presence felt.

In a quite shocking development, J.J. McCarthy has been a big-play machine in his five quarters as the Giants' quarterback.

Against the Houston Texans, McCarthy was as good as any New York fan could ask. His 252 yards on 20/28 passing came from multiple accurate passes downfield, keeping the ball out of harm's way and focusing on getting the ball to the offense's best player: Malik Nabers.

There's still room for improvement all-around, but his first start in Big Blue leaves an exciting outlook for the rest of the season.

As for the rookies Francis Mauigoa and Malachi Fields, there are substantial positives.

The No. 10 pick, Mauigoa, has had growing pains moving to right guard, but the talent is clear. He's had multiple zero-pressure games so far and has reps of pure manhandling. He was the only rookie to post a 100% pass-block win rate in Week 7, already showing growth.

Fields had his best game as a pro against Houston. His four catches for 83 yards were momentous, headlined by his 47-yard catch-and-run. Fields' gifted YAC ability and smooth hands leave him as a viable safety valve at all times. The team has sorely missed a body type like his, and his 6-foot-4 frame has unlocked exciting play types for the Giants on both the run and through the air.

Each of these players has added a compelling new layer to the G-Men's offense and offers an exhilarating outlook for the team when it's fully healthy in 2027.
