#4203. Scientists overcome a hidden obstacle to more powerful quantum computers

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Silicon spin qubits could play a major role in the future of quantum computing because they can be produced using the same semiconductor manufacturing methods that make today's computer chips. However, researchers have faced a significant challenge. When multiple silicon qubits are controlled at the same time, their operations tend to become less accurate. Building practical, large quantum computers will require scientists to control many qubits simultaneously while keeping errors to a minimum.

Researchers at the RIKEN Center for Emergent Matter Science and their collaborators have now made important progress toward solving this problem. In a study published in Nature Physics, the team demonstrated highly accurate control of several silicon spin qubits operating simultaneously. Their experiments used an array of five qubits connected to a shared microwave control line. By identifying a major cause of errors during simultaneous operation, the scientists developed a technique to reduce those errors and maintain precision.

Leon Camenzind, a RIKEN research scientist and co-first author who supervised the experiments, explains the significance of the results: "This shows that high-fidelity control can be maintained even when several qubits are operated at the same time and represents an important step toward scaling up silicon-based quantum processors."

Scientists Identify a Hidden Source of Quantum Computing Errors

Unlike conventional computers, which store information as bits representing either 0 or 1, quantum computers use quantum bits, or qubits, that can exist in combinations of both states. Silicon spin qubits store this information using the spin of individual electrons confined within silicon. Performing reliable quantum calculations requires extremely precise control over these electron spins.

Microwave signals provide that control, but operating multiple qubits simultaneously can create unwanted effects that interfere with their accuracy. The researchers traced much of this problem to a phenomenon called the AC Stark shift.

This effect occurs when microwave control signals alter a qubit's effective energy levels, producing changes in the phase of its quantum state. Those phase shifts can disrupt the intended operation of individual qubits, particularly when several are being controlled at once.

To counteract the problem, the team created a calibration method that compensates for these unwanted shifts. Rather than separately calibrating every possible combination of qubits, the technique relies on calibrations performed between pairs of qubits. This makes the approach more practical for systems containing larger numbers of quantum bits.

"Rather than requiring an entirely separate calibration procedure for every possible combination of qubits, our method uses calibrations between pairs of qubits to compensate for the unwanted phase shifts," explains Yi-Hsien Wu, the first author who carried out the experiments as part of his PhD research.

Quantum Qubits Achieve Accuracy Above 99.99%

The researchers combined their calibration technique with an improved device design and carefully adjusted microwave control pulses. Their results showed that exceptionally high accuracy could be maintained even when multiple qubits were operating simultaneously.

When the five qubits were tested individually, each achieved single-qubit gate fidelity well above 99.99%, with the highest approaching 99.999%. Fidelity describes how closely a quantum operation matches its intended result.

The team then tested how well the system performed when several qubits were active together. With as many as three qubits operating simultaneously, fidelity remained above 99.99%. Even when all five qubits were controlled at once, accuracy stayed at approximately 99.9%, approaching the levels needed for practical fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Although the calibration method greatly reduced errors, it did not eliminate them entirely. The researchers found that the remaining loss of accuracy was linked to drive-induced decoherence, an effect caused by the greater microwave power required to operate multiple qubits simultaneously.

Unlike the unwanted phase shifts corrected by the new technique, decoherence involves the loss of the delicate quantum coherence needed for reliable operations.

According to Camenzind, "This is a limitation, but further improvements to device design could help mitigate this source of error."

A New Path Toward Larger Quantum Processors

The findings show that controlling several silicon spin qubits at the same time does not necessarily require a major sacrifice in accuracy. By correcting a key source of errors, the researchers have demonstrated a promising approach for operating more qubits through shared microwave control lines.

This is especially important for the development of large quantum processors. Giving every qubit its own independent control system would make increasingly complex devices difficult to manage. Shared control lines could offer a more practical way to expand these systems while maintaining the precision needed for quantum calculations.

"By addressing a key source of control errors through pairwise calibration," says Camenzind. "Our study supports the use of shared control lines as a practical approach to scaling up quantum processors. Combined with silicon's compatibility with established semiconductor manufacturing, we believe that this advance could help pave the way toward larger quantum processors capable of performing increasingly complex computations."

The broader objective is to develop quantum computers that can reliably perform calculations despite the errors and disturbances that affect quantum systems. Such machines, known as fault-tolerant quantum computers, will require precise, dependable control over large numbers of qubits.

"Our long-term goal is to build a fault-tolerant quantum computer based on silicon spin qubits, and reliable parallel control is an essential step toward that goal," adds Seigo Tarucha, project manager of a silicon quantum computing project under JST's Moonshot Goal 6.

#139. Xi Jinping’s corruption crackdown in the spotlight as China prepares for meeting of top leaders

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

The fifth plenum comes against a backdrop of years of purges that have had some analysts wondering if the issue is corruption or a lack of loyalty

When Xi Jinping came to power in 2012, he pledged to stamp out corruption all through the ranks of the Communist party. More than a decade later, the pace of investigations has not slowed. Last year, China’s disciplinary authorities opened investigations into more than 1 million people, including more than 100 officials at or above ministerial or provincial level.

As Chinese Communist party (CCP) officials kick off an important meeting this week, discipline – and party loyalty – is high on the agenda. For those who try to read the tea leaves of elite Chinese politics, concern about the apparent lack of discipline is dominating CCP discussions.

The “fifth plenum” began on Monday and is set to focus on “major issues on advancing the party’s full and rigorous self-governance”, according to a Chinese state media. In party-speak, “governance” is a byword for discipline, loyalty and anti-corruption.

As Xi continues to crack down on corruption, the fact that investigations nearly always result in a conviction begs the question over whether targets are truly guilty of corruption, or are they just political rivals to the people in charge.

Kerry Brown, director of the Lau China Institute at King’s College London, explained that in Xi’s worldview, which is obsessed with discipline and loyalty, there may not be much difference between the two. “If you’re corrupt, you’re not politically loyal. If you’re politically loyal, you’re not corrupt,” Brown said.

He added that in the CCP system, “corruption is not just material things”, but also “spiritual corruption”, which includes political disloyalty.

Some analysts argue that the reason that the anti-graft drive is so enduring is because corruption is a feature rather than a bug of an authoritarian, top-down political system with a sprawling bureaucracy that governs 1.4 billion people.

Neil Thomas, a senior fellow at the Asia Society thinktank, said: “Xi has made the party less corrupt and strengthened his control, but he knows that tougher punishment cannot fully resolve the problem of officials putting their own interests ahead of the party’s.

“Xi’s answer is to keep tightening discipline, making anti-corruption a permanent method of governing with no obvious point at which he can declare the job done,” Thomas said.

The job may be never ending, but the plenum is an opportunity to refocus the party’s energy on discipline. The fifth plenum is the fifth of seven gatherings that normally happen in every five-year cycle between CCP congresses. The plenums bring together more than 300 members of the CCP’s Central Committee, the most senior officials in the party hierarchy.

The meeting is particularly significant because it is likely to be the last substantive meeting before next year’s 21st Party Congress, where a new Central Committee will be chosen, and where Xi is expected to be anointed for a fourth term as the head of the CCP.

Eliminating anyone who poses a risk to his authority or to the party’s credibility is a key priority for Xi. And potential threats are everywhere. Nearly 15 years after Xi took office, according to CCP proclamations and a host of recent investigations, the problem is still not stamped out. “It is a deeply ingrained part of [Xi’s] worldview,” said Brown. “Everyone else is corrupt”.

In the past, Xi has warned that the party could “lose its ruling status and inevitably be eliminated by history” if it could not get its house in order. “This is by no means an exaggeration,” he said.

The current focus on party governance comes amid a host of high-profile investigations against senior party and military leaders.

In January, Zhang Youxia, China’s top general and a man second only to Xi in the military hierarchy, was placed under investigation; he was expelled from party last month, having already been removed from the military’s top ruling body, the Central Military Commission (CMC)

A host of other purges means that the CMC, which normally has seven members, has been reduced to just two: Xi and Zhang Shengmin, the CMC’s head of discipline.

Other recent scalps include Ma Xingrui, the former party secretary for Xinjiang, and Huang Qifan, the former mayor of Chongqing.

Huang had long been known as the ultimate survivor in Chinese politics because of his connections to the scandals in the early 2010s in which Xi’s political rival, Bo Xilai, the former Party boss for Chongqing, was embroiled in the death of the British businessman Neil Heywood. Bo was ultimately convicted of bribery and embezzlement, for which he is serving a life sentence, but Huang survived. No longer.

Some China analysts have asked whether the recent purges, which have reached the highest echelons of the Party and military ranks, indicate instability in the regime. But Xi likely sees it differently. Corruption investigations demonstrate who is in charge. Plus, Brown added, they are “the most easy and natural tool by which to enforce unity and discipline”.

#4136. Phantom Blade Zero Review In Progress – Shaping Up To Be One Of The Best Action Games I've Ever Played

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Phantom Blade Zero is an astounding achievement for the action genre. So astounding that I feel pulled to experience everything it has to offer… and that means instead of rolling credits, I’ve been busy completing story-critical side quests and more to get the most complete ending. That’s another way of saying I am enjoying this game very, very much but still need more time to render a final verdict, which is why you’re reading a review-in-progress of Phantom Blade Zero rather than a scored piece.

Though I’m not interested in guesstimating a score right now, I know it will be high because Phantom Blade Zero impresses and then some. It is one of the best action games I’ve ever played, and I’m shocked to be writing that. This is a debut of sorts for developer S-Game, which has dabbled in mobile games and smaller-scale titles; it’s the team’s first triple-A release, and its first game of this kind. This studio is so clearly a dedicated student of the genre, analyzing what works here and what works there in other action greats to create something of its own. It looks forward at what action games can do, can play like, can look like, while looking back at the games before it and understanding why those games – games like Devil May Cry, Elden Ring, Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice, Bayonetta, and just about every other quality action game you can think of – continue to be ones we hold so highly. Should it stick the landing, Phantom Blade Zero will join that echelon of action pedigree.

Protagonist Soul is a quiet, mostly stoic martial arts master who prefers to let his blades (of which there are so, so many) do the talking. The Order he’s sworn his life to betrays him, and, in an effort to right wrongs, he discovers a villainous conspiracy to seize full power of the blood-soaked Chinese martial underground known as Wulin. As a result, puppets, secret sects, powerful god-like creatures, monstrous beasts, and much more prowl Wulin’s various biomes. I haven’t kept count, but there are a massive number of enemies to take down, both standard mob variants and bosses, and the game stays fresh throughout. It's even fresher when you consider how many weapons you can acquire on Soul’s journey.

Every weapon is wholly unique, like picking up a new character in a fighting game, each with its own moveset and abilities. Upgrading these weapons through various tiers unlocks additional abilities, and you can even Soul Temper weapons to add one final, game-changing ability or perk. A fully upgraded weapon can have a moveset that uses the majority of buttons on your controller. You can do the same with Phantom Edges, special tide-turning weapons with limited uses (before you build up additional charges). Combined with different accessories and medicine types, Phantom Blade Zero offers hundreds, perhaps thousands, of combat kit permutations. You can play as a drunken fighter who switches between sober, tipsy, and drunken states, with his range and damage output changing as a result; you can focus on a bleed build, ripping enemies to shreds with charged attacks that utilize Soul's own HP to deal extra damage (that can then be earned back with bleed); you can use lightning, wind, fire, and ice to create elemental storms against enemies, and you can use a sword that switches between different elemental states on command as well. You can also just pound enemies to pulp with Phantom Edge fists, too. I’ve struggled to stop using these fists since acquiring them because they’re so satisfying.

Forty-plus hours into Phantom Blade Zero, it feels like I’ve played as dozens of different Souls because I've discovered so many build types. S-Game smartly pushes you to try new builds and weapons as you get them, doling out generous upgrade materials and currency; it encourages you to try everything, and the next weapon might just become your new favorite.

Finding these materials (and additional fights) is great fun, too, because Phantom Blade Zero’s various open-area biomes are a genuine joy to explore. You are always rewarded for taking the optional path, and those rewards are always worthwhile. You might find a healthy pile of upgrade materials, or a boss with a weapon you’ll fall in love with. The way buildings, alleys, caves, cliffsides, and other environmental features weave in and out of each other evokes FromSoftware’s architectural design and the subsequent shortcut “aha!” moments.

That said, Phantom Blade Zero is definitively not a Soulslike, though you’d be forgiven for assuming as much given its challenging suite of bosses, interconnected world, and Phantom Bell checkpoints, which are this game’s equivalent of bonfires. However, its challenge is much more surmountable (with various difficulties and accessories to ease parry and dodge timing), and its world progression is materially different. Kill an enemy and it’s dead for good, which to my surprise, adds a sense of worldbuilding I didn’t know I wanted. Progression through this journey feels tangible because I’m actually removing traces of evil with my weapons, traces that don’t respawn when I interact with checkpoints. It’s a nice change of pace that narratively speaks to Soul’s efforts.

Along the way, countless reading materials shed light on Phantom Blade Zero’s intertwining stories, and tracking down the next memory, scroll, or book entry rewards you if you’re interested in the full picture of this Wuxia story. I’d argue the game’s story is its weakest element, but only when compared to the rest of the package. It doesn't break any new ground narratively, but it provides enough mystery and thrill to keep things moving. And while performances are great, talking to characters is still mostly just two people standing across from each other talking through a new quest. Fortunately, cutscenes pick up the slack with dramatic villains, heartfelt revelations, raw tension, and more.

Production value is high across the board, thanks to incredibly impressive animation and a beautiful rendering of this dark fantasy China. Faces are lifelike, nudging up just shy of the uncanny valley as close-ups reveal porous details, lip quivers, and more, bringing an excellent cast of Chinese performances to life. The English voice acting has its standouts, but Soul’s voice is boring, so I highly recommend the Chinese vocals.

Boss fights draw clear inspiration from Chinese cinema and boast a wondrous sense of awe and scale that only the best in the genre typically offer. There are a ton of bosses, too, with very few repeats (repeats do happen in segmented challenge trials), and it’s impressive to think back on just how many I defeated, and just how many of them delivered a unique fight. I appreciate the checkpoints between boss phases as well. There are so many bosses – nearing 100 or more if I had to guess – and having my progression saved through phases removes some of the repetition I often feel when, say, phase two of a boss continues to kill me and I have to blitz through phase one again and again before taking another stab at it.

That scale and on-screen excitement carry through almost every fight in the game, down to even the simplest mob fights, though these are starting to feel a touch repetitive as I’m barreling toward the game’s end. Soul moves like the world’s greatest martial artist, but your controller inputs make it happen. If you mash the attack button, it looks nice… but if you use each weapon’s prescribed inputs and abilities, Phantom Blade Zero delivers a level of detailed action I’ve seen in few other games. You direct the game’s action, and this lets you turn what might otherwise be just another group of fodder enemies into an electric bout of spectacle.

To say I greatly enjoy this game greatly undersells my feelings – writing this review-in-progress is frustrating because I want to get back to Phantom Blade Zero. I expect to reach credits in the coming days, so please be on the lookout for my final verdict this week.

Barcelona felt like the best-case scenario before El Clasico after both Lamine Yamal and Raphinha got off to strong starts to their seasons, Tuesday's 4-2 loss at Paris Saint-Germain doing little to dampen their spirits. If anything, it was Real Madrid who have become overly reliant on Kylian Mbappe over the years, Sunday's game slated to be no exception.

Over the course of Real Madrid's 2-1 win at the Camp Nou, though, the roles had reversed.

Mbappe and Raphina were each on the scoresheet, neither feat much of a surprise considering each of their exploits this season. Early in his third season with the club, Mbappe notched his 99th and 100th Real Madrid goals on Sunday while Raphina registered his 13th goal in nine LaLiga games this campaign. Both showcased the inherent ability of a dependable superstar, each of them providing a unique snapshot of the teams they represent.

The France World Cup winner was the finishing piece of a team whose defensive efforts won them the game, neutralizing a Barcelona team that had won nine games out of nine to start the domestic campaign. The visitors opted to cede play to the possession-oriented hosts, holding the ball just 37.6% of the time in the process. Los Blancos also let the attack-minded Barcelona roll out Plan A, outshooting the visitors 18 to 10 along the way. Jose Mourinho's side were fully prepared for the challenge, their positional discipline designed to ensure their opponents would be unable to do much with all of their efforts. Hansi Flick's side registered just 1.19 expected goals from all their attempts, well below their average tally of 3.5 expected goals per game in LaLiga this campaign.

Yamal and Raphinha, as well as winger Anthony Gordon, had the ball at their feet on multiple occasions and looked certain to go for goal. They were often restricted to taking shots from sundial positions, Real Madrid's defensive shape holding nicely for much of the game. In the moments the Barcelona players' individual brilliance came through, a Real Madrid defender would find a way to shut things down before it was too late. Aurelien Tchouameni led the charge at the base of Los Blancos' midfield, excellent as the visitors resisted Barcelona's press and prevented them from racking up opportunities in moments of transition. He was hardly alone – Federico Valverde excelled at right back, while Marc Cucurella came in clutch on the flip side. Even Arda Guler and Yan Diomande maintained their standing in their wide attacking positions, limiting Barcelona's ability to create much despite racking up touches and passes.

It allowed Mbappe to pounce when the ball finally landed, even if he was less visible over the course of the 90 minutes than usual. He took just 36 touches and three shots, down from his average in LaLiga this season of 52.7 touches and 5.2 shots per game, but he is the exact type of player to survive a game state like this one. His first was a gift from Barcelona's back line while his second was a team effort, one that showcased his own brilliance as much as it did Eduardo Camavinga's abilities off the bench in maintaining Los Blancos' structure before notching the game-winning assist.

Edward James Olmos is without a doubt one of our most respected actors, and for good reason. The man’s body of work is extensive and, for his efforts, he’s received various honors, including an Emmy award and an Academy Award nomination. One of his most beloved roles is that of Commander William Adama in the reimagined version of Battlestar Galactica. It’s an iconic role, and it alone would solidify Olmos’ position within the pop culture zeitgeist. However, he also attributes that TV show, in particular, to Blade Runner.

Back in the early ‘80s, Olmos famously shared the screen with Harrison Ford in Ridley Scott’s aforementioned 1982 sci-fi film. The actor took on the role of Gaff, a detective who shadows Ford’s Rick Deckard amid a deadly investigation into Replicant activity Olmos reprised the role in 2017’s Blade Runner 2049. Around the time that Scott’s Final Cut of the film was to be released to mark its 25th anniversary, Olmos spoke to SFX Magazine on CinemaBlend. It was during that chat that Olmos weighed in on BR’s relationship to BSG:

They’re re-releasing Blade Runner because of Battlestar. I talked to Ridley about two. weeks ago… Well, I don’t know if it’s because of Battlestar directly but my instincts are it’s because of the fact that there’s such a demand to see this kind of work. That’s the mother, who gave birth to this. Where Blade Runner ends is where this thing begins.

Olmos seemed to suggest that the theatrical re-release of Scott’s film could be chalked up to the popularity of sci-fi content at the time, which was in great part due to BSG. The Stand and Deliver star, however, also made it clear that the ‘80s flick helped paved the way for his version of BSG, which was a fresh take on the short-lived ABC show of the same name. Olmos does make a fair point, especially considering that Scott’s initially misunderstood masterpiece did spark quite a bit of interest in sci-fi. He also said this about the film:

You look at the movie today, 25 years later, and go, ‘Whoa, this movie is intense.’ And today, that will ring truer because of Battlestar more than it even did when it came out. Now you’re going to be able to sit there and go, ‘Wow, this is really, really good. Now I understand this a lot better.’

Olmos’ stint as Adama began with the 2003 Battlestar Galactica miniseries, which spawned the 2004 series. After five seasons and a TV movie in 2009, Olmos’ tenure ended, though his work endures. Not only is his performance as Bill still lauded by fans, but he also still has the respect of his co-stars. Kara “Starbuck” Thrace actress Katee Sackhoff has been candid about getting advice from Olmos, and she and Tahmoh Penikett (who played Karl "Helo" Agathon) initially rejected his wisdom.

All in all, both BSG and Blade Runner have firm footprints in sci-fi culture’s consciousness. Just a few years ago, Peacock was even set to reboot Battlestar Galactica, though the show was scrapped. Now, Scott’s franchise is set to return amid the 2026 TV schedule in the form of a miniseries, Blade Runner 2099, which stars Michelle Yeoh and Hunter Schafer. While it’s very likely that Edward James Olmos will not be appearing on the show, I love how passionate he’s remained about the film’s impact on pop culture and his own career.
