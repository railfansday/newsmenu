#5005. Y Combinator-backed Mochatrade raises $5.5 million to offer US stock perpetuals to Indian traders

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

CoinFund led the latest funding for Mochatrade, which lets Indian traders fund accounts in rupees and trade perpetual futures tied to U.S. stock prices.

Mochatrade, a trading platform focused on Indian users, has raised $5.56 million in total funding to offer perpetual futures tied to U.S. stock prices.

CoinFund led the seed funding, with participation from Coinbase Ventures and Nicky Montana, general partner at Zero Knowledge Ventures, Mochatrade said Monday. Y Combinator and Pioneer Fund backed the pre-seed round worth $690,000, with Y Combinator's $500,000 investment arriving earlier this month entirely in USDC stablecoin on Base, Mochatrade co-founder and chief product officer Chetan Manda told The Block.

Mochatrade said in May it raised the pre-seed round without mentioning the dollar amount at the time. As for the seed round, it closed in July, said Manda.

The funding was raised through post-money simple agreements for future equity, or SAFEs, Manda said, noting that there is no valuation to share as the funding was not a priced equity round.

Mochatrade lets Indian users fund accounts through the Unified Payments Interface, or UPI, and trade contracts tied to companies such as Nvidia, Tesla and Apple. Users can bet on U.S. stock prices rising or falling with leverage, without owning the shares or needing a U.S. brokerage account, Manda said.

He noted that similar products are already available on Hyperliquid and Indian crypto exchanges including CoinDCX, Delta and CoinSwitch.

However, "neither is built for an Indian equity trader who wants to trade U.S. stocks: the offshore venues run on dollar stablecoin balances, and the Indian exchanges do not focus their marketing and positioning on U.S. equities; they focus on crypto. Existing U.S.-stock apps for Indian users are built for buying and holding shares. (Eg: INDmoney, VestedFinance)," he claimed.

"Mochatrade is built for U.S. stock token trading: users fund in rupees through UPI, go long or short on U.S. stock prices with leverage, and withdraw in rupees," Manda added.

Mochatrade operates in India through ALSD Technologies Private Limited, which holds its FIU-IND registration under reference number VA00049752, according to Manda.

The platform is in beta, with early access rolling out and more than 25,000 people on its waitlist, Manda said, adding that the public launch is expected in the coming weeks.

Mochatrade earns fees on trades made through the platform. It has no plans to launch a token "as of now," Manda said.

The funding will go toward trading technology and customer growth. The team also plans to add U.S. stock options, more assets, and social trading features, subject to applicable laws and regulatory approvals, Manda said.

Manda and his co-founders, CEO Utkarsh Sinha and CTO Parth Maheshwari, started building Mochatrade in April 2026 and joined Y Combinator's Spring 2026 batch. The three met at IIT and have been friends for 10 years, according to the startup.

Sinha was previously co-founder and CEO of Martian Wallet, which reached more than 2 million installs across over 50 countries before being acquired last year by Pontem Network, a product studio for Move-based blockchains, Mochatrade said. Manda previously served as director of product management at Sprinklr, a customer experience software company, while Maheshwari worked at Adobe and Navi, according to their LinkedIn profiles.

Mochatrade has 11 full-time employees, with its core team split between New York and Bangalore. It plans to expand its operations, engineering, and growth teams over time, Manda said. The board consists of the three founders, he added.

#4947. NBA names Kalshi, Polymarket preferred exchanges, signs CFTC deal

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The US professional basketball league is expanding into prediction markets with official data agreements and safeguards against insider trading.

The National Basketball Association has named Kalshi and Polymarket its preferred prediction market exchanges and signed an information-sharing agreement with the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) aimed at protecting the integrity of basketball-related markets.

According to Monday’s announcement, the agreements will give Kalshi and Polymarket access to official NBA data supplied by Sportradar, the league’s designated data distribution partner, for settling authorized markets. Both platforms will have rights to use NBA intellectual property and conduct promotional activities in the United States.

The partnerships include integrity safeguards, prohibiting NBA and team personnel from trading NBA event contracts and requiring cooperation on suspicious trading investigations.

Beyond the two exchanges, the NBA expanded its existing agreement with DraftKings to cover prediction markets and said it expects to announce similar arrangements with other industry operators in the coming weeks.

In a separate announcement, CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig said the agreement with the NBA would strengthen information sharing to address insider trading and fraud in prediction markets. The NBA joins Major League Baseball and the National Hockey League in establishing similar arrangements with the regulator.

Related: Kalshi strikes deal with S&P Dow Jones Indices to expand financial event contracts

The NBA’s agreements come amid an ongoing legal battle over whether federal commodities rules supersede state gambling laws governing sports prediction markets.

The dispute has produced conflicting court decisions. In September, the 6th US Circuit Court of Appeals ruled against Kalshi in a case involving Ohio and Tennessee, following a similar decision by the 9th Circuit. An earlier ruling by the 3rd Circuit, however, allowed Kalshi to continue operating in New Jersey while its appeal proceeds.

State enforcement actions have continued. On Oct. 20, Utah Attorney General Derek Brown sued Kalshi, alleging that the platform had facilitated illegal betting in the state since 2024. Utah officials are seeking to recover an estimated $30 million to $60 million in profits allegedly earned by Kalshi and other defendants.

The US Supreme Court has been asked to review the New Jersey dispute, with 39 states and Washington, DC, supporting the petition. The National Football League, which has not entered into commercial partnerships with prediction market exchanges, also backed the petition, arguing that certain sports event contracts threaten game integrity because they are susceptible to manipulation. The justices have not yet agreed to hear the case.

#4390. Audi Recalls 9,048 Q4 E-Trons Because The Battery Could Catch On Fire

Volkswagen Group's EV batteries are going through a rough patch at the moment. Just a couple of weeks ago, VW issued a recall for ID.4 battery packs that could potentially catch fire. Now, the same is happening for the Audi Q4 e-tron, with 9,048 cars being recalled. And just like with VW and the ID.4, Audi is recommending that owners keep their batteries under 80%.

This shouldn't come as too much of a surprise, as the Q4 e-tron and ID.4 are mechanical siblings, and likely share the same battery supplier. It seems like VW's problem is what triggered Audi to investigate the Q4 e-tron, leading it to this recall.

According to the recall notice, the Audi Produce Safety Committee was informed of thermal runaway events in some of VW's high-voltage batteries, the same type used in the Q4. After that, Audi "observed that in certain high-voltage batteries, certain charging profiles combined with a state of charge over 80% may result in thermal propagation within the high-voltage battery."

Most of the batteries that are said to potentially have this issue were sold in North America, although there haven't been any recorded cases of battery fires. While there isn't a fix just yet, Audi ran diagnostics on Q4 e-tron batteries, and any vehicle that needs further assessment will need to be brought in to a dealer for extra testing. Charging be limited to 80% state-of-charge, with a temporary charge limiter put in place. Once the battery is fixed, or it's determined that the battery is OK, the limiter will be removed.

According to the recall, the supplier who provided the batteries is SK Battery Manufacturing in Hungary, part of SK On. There's currently no known reason for why these batteries are facing this issue, in either the VW or Audi. Hopefully, the VW Group can come up with a remedy quickly, as it can be scary for customers to hear of potential battery fires. Admittedly, if you keep the battery under 80%, it shouldn't be an issue, and there are going to be software limiters that prevent overcharging it. However, that also means that customers now lose 20% of the battery capacity they paid for, which can have frustrating effects on peoples' daily lives. We'll see what VW's eventual fix for these cars is, whenever it comes out.

#5011. Bitcoin Wallet Fixes Critical Flaw That Redirected Funds to Attackers

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin Wallet Fixes Critical Flaw That Redirected Funds to Attackers

Bitcoin wallet Electrum patched a high-severity vulnerability this weekend that allowed rogue servers to manipulate pending Bitcoin transactions and redirect funds to attacker-controlled addresses. The flaw affects all versions before 4.8.3, which is now available.

At least one user has confirmed losing money to the exploit.

Electrum is one of the oldest Bitcoin software wallets around. The attack exploits the wallet’s trust model for unconfirmed transactions. When a user broadcasts a payment through a compromised server, the server can suppress the real transaction and inject a fake conflicting one—showing an attacker’s output address and zero fee, making the payment look stuck.

Because Electrum doesn’t verify signatures on unconfirmed transactions from the server, the spoofed transaction appears in the wallet’s history as legitimate.

When the user fee-bumps what looks like a stalled payment, they sign and broadcast a completely new valid transaction—one whose output address belongs to the attacker. The real payment was never sent; the fee-bump is the first transaction that actually hits the network, and it pays the attacker.

Electrum developer SomberNight, who published the advisory, noted that attackers can grind the fake transaction’s output address to match the last four characters of the user’s intended destination. Anyone spot-checking only the tail of a long Bitcoin address would see an apparent match.

The advisory states that “some servers are performing this attack in the wild,” backed by two user reports. A user identified as dannydeezy first flagged the suspicious server behavior on October 11; the manipulated transaction never propagated past the local server, so no funds were lost. A second user, Raju-K12, reported a confirmed loss on October 18 after fee-bumping a manipulated transaction.

The patch in 4.8.3 closes the main attack surface by verifying cryptographic signatures on wallet-owned inputs before adding any unconfirmed transaction to local history. A server can no longer inject a fake transaction touching the user’s own addresses without providing valid signatures—which the attacker cannot forge. The fix was tagged in the repository on October 23 and binaries were published the following day.

Incoming transactions from external senders—where none of the inputs belong to the user’s wallet—are still accepted without signature verification. SomberNight acknowledged this gap in the advisory, noting it would require additional work to close.

This isn’t Electrum’s first rodeo and it has dealt with malicious attacks in the past. A 2018 campaign used rogue servers to push fake update notifications, and by 2020 it had drained an estimated $25 million from Electrum users. Electrum’s open server model—anyone can run a node—is what makes this class of attack possible.

Users who connect exclusively to a server they run themselves are not affected. Anyone who cannot update immediately should manually verify output addresses and amounts before signing any transaction; Electrum 4.8.3 is available at electrum.org alongside the project’s Github.

The PlayStation 2 has just turned 26, and it has me feeling sentimental. As the custodian of my older brother's console since 2018, it's been sitting on my TV stand for years now. But I rarely actually play it, despite having an AVI to HDMI adapter – one of the cheapest and best ways to play PS2 games in 2026. Sure, I'll fire it up a few times a year to relive a childhood favorite, but the retro console still requires a lot of effort to set it up on modern hardware. Even then, the results are varied at best, but there are some stand-outs that I don't see talked about as often as others.

While our list of the best PS2 games ranks the most legendary titles of the console's generation, I want to dig into why some feel better to play than others. A lot of this is down to the DualShock controller's obvious lack of dexterity – especially where ports to modern tech are available. My main sticking point with Vice City is that you really do need a decent adapter to amp up the visuals; I needed to tinker with my TV settings a lot to get the contrast and white balance just right, but once that was sorted, I barely noticed the PS2's odd quirks while replaying Tommy Vercetti's 2001 adventure.

By that, I mean you should play kitschy spin-off Resident Evil: Dead Aim over the iconic Resident Evil 4 any day. Best for: Longer JRPG sessionsRelease date: July 10, 2008 It's finally getting a remake, but Persona 4 still feels good to play on PS2. The catch is that, of course, it's not quite as sharp and clean as its newer-gen brethren – which is a bit of a downer, if you're all about modern flourish.

Best for: Play with friendsRelease date: November 9, 2004Can I be honest with you? It's not kid's stuff, either; from a timed platforming challenge in a library to fighting off hordes of bitey rats, the game is as dark as the source material and much more mature than you might think. Pushing people off ledges, masking environmental traps and kills as accidents, and a new notoriety system all came into play for the first time in Blood Money. It's still one of the defining moments in the Hitman canon, a formative point in time that paved the way for all to come later down the line, and I don't think any PS2 game collection is complete without it.

Simple controls and customization tools mean that Hit & Run sits comfortably between a pared-back racing game and the carjacking mayhem of Rockstar's finest. I was never the biggest Simpsons fan, but it's bright and silly enough to make even the most stoic gamers out there crack a smile and relax a bit. #5450. XPENG’s Human Approach To Technology Awarded Most Popular Employer

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

XPENG has recently been named 2026 Forbes China Most Popular Employer amongst employees. This isn’t the first time XPENG has won an employer award. Having visited their campuses and seen how the company treats its employees, I can understand why. From the approachability and openness of the executives to the employee moms serving meals in the cafeteria, the technology company just feels more human.

The 2026 Forbes employee questionnaire consists of six sections: “Employer Image”, “Organizational Management”, “Training and Development”, “Work Environment”, “Compensation and Benefits” and, new for this year,  “AI Value Creation”.

That last section on AI value creation is particularly important. As Forbes points out, AI being useful does not necessarily mean a better experience. Companies can either use AI to simply try to replace people or to expand the capabilities of their people. If companies take the wrong approach, AI can be stifling. However, if companies the technology correctly and embrace the capabilities, AI can expand creativity, liberate people from mundane tasks and increase employee satisfaction.

Having had the opportunity to meet many people across XPENG on my recent trips, I came away impressed not just with how well the company seemed to treat its people. A dramatic departure from many of the media portrayals of China in the West. But I was also impressed with how passionate people were about the technology and bringing it to customers. Whether it is someone in finance, HR, PR or a technical field, all of them have the technology as their focus. A technology focus that is very human. Providing solutions for human needs with technology.

So, when I had a chance to briefly interview He Xiaopeng directly, I asked him how he inspires that passion in his people (auto translated with Google):

I believe that for a company in a major manufacturing nation, its foundational DNA is essentially a reflection of its founder’s own character. Personally, I have a deep passion for technology, and I value a straightforward approach to life combined with an agile, fast-paced way of getting things done. Consequently, the people I’ve recruited—those who align with this DNA—have stayed, while those who didn’t have naturally moved on. At XPENG, we have a very high concentration of technical talent—over 40% of our team hold master’s or doctoral degrees—all of whom are driven by the desire to develop the best possible technology. This is a core characteristic that has taken shape over the course of our 12-year entrepreneurial journey. Now, as we look toward global expansion, I expect this same DNA to help us succeed internationally; for our team, this represents a new challenge—a fresh evolution to embrace.

When you believe in the mission of a company, it becomes easier to put in the work needed to add value. When that same mission permeates from the top to the bottom of a company, believing in the mission becomes much easier.

Overall, XPENG is focused on using technology to improve people’s lives. And make lives cleaner and safer for their employees, customers and world at large. That overall focus can appeal to many people around the globe, far beyond China, although it will take respect and adaptation to local needs to be fully embraced everywhere.

While we can get caught up in metrics like kWh of battery, coefficient of drag or km of range, human beings are ultimately the reason why every great technology succeeds. They are the source of every innovation. And people are the reason why innovations matter.

While their approaches may be, different XPENG isn’t alone in focusing on the intersection of human beings and technology (Xiaomi and Seres also won employer awards from Forbes). However, having had more exposure to XPENG, I could see the emphasis throughout their organization. Even though these awards are not perfect, we can only hope more companies decide to compete for who treats their employees the best. And we can only hope more companies start focusing on the people who are the reason why the technology matters. That focus also shows up for customers in the products they buy, but that is for another article.
