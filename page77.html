#4384. Seized McLaren Senna, Ferrari SF90, Other High-End Cars Head to Government Auction for 5th Time

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Philippine customs officials are cutting prices on a collection of seized supercars and other luxury vehicles after repeated failed attempts to find buyers.

It doesn’t sound unusual at first: the Philippine Bureau of Customs (BOC) seized a fleet of nearly a dozen exotic and luxury cars, including a McLaren Senna and Ferrari SF90 Stradale, and now it wants to sell the collection at auction. There's just one problem: Nobody seems particularly interested in buying any of them. According to a report by Philippine news outlet Bilyonaryo, this is the fifth attempt by the government to auction the vehicles. And this time, officials have slashed the minimum bid prices in hopes of finally attracting buyers.

The BOC originally seized the 11 luxury cars in question back in February 2025. An investigation into a Pasay City car dealer and another business in Parañaque found that vehicles worth a combined ₱1.4 billion, or roughly $22 million, were illegally imported. The BOC decided to auction the collection, but selling the cars has proven considerably more difficult than seizing them.

The previous auction, held on March 27, was a complete bust. Not a single bidder registered, and none of the cars sold. Hoping to avoid a similar result, the BOC is slashing prices. The minimum bid for the Senna was previously ₱102.04 million, or roughly $1.6 million; now it’s ₱86.74 million, which is approximately $1.35 million. That's still a considerable amount of money, but it's also about a $250,000 discount. (Your local McLaren dealership is unlikely to give you such a deal.) The minimum bid for the Ferrari was also cut by about 15 percent.

According to the BOC documentation, other vehicles up for auction include a 2022 Rolls-Royce Phantom, a 2021 Rolls-Royce Cullinan, a 2022 Bentley Continental, 2023 and 2021 Range Rovers, a 2021 Mercedes-AMG GT, a 2023 BMW X6, a 2019 Toyota Alphard, and a 2019 Lexus ES. All vehicles are available for prospective buyers to inspect between October 27 and October 29, and the auction will take place on October 30.

Will the cars finally be sold this time? It's hard to imagine a McLaren Senna struggling to find a new home. Maybe if this doesn’t work, the BOC should consider just posting the cars on Facebook Marketplace.

BNY and Ripple are expanding custody support to XRP and RLUSD while exploring tokenized trade finance with C2FO.

BNY expanded its partnership with Ripple, adding XRP and the dollar-backed stablecoin RLUSD to its institutional custody platform, the companies said on Tuesday.

The bank already serves as the primary reserve custodian for RLUSD and has a custody relationship with Ripple Prime. Under the new arrangement, the companies intend to let BNY clients mint and redeem RLUSD directly through the bank and explore tokenized trade finance on the XRP Ledger, according to a statement.

"We’re moving past the initial issuance of assets onchain," Monica Long, Ripple's president, said in the statement, adding that the next step involves making tokenized assets "usable for payments and collateral across financial markets."

As part of the expansion, BNY and Ripple also partnered with working capital provider C2FO to test tokenized trade finance. The project targets supply chain receivables, attempting to move corporate working capital onto public ledger infrastructure, the companies said.

Suppliers can receive funds in seconds against approved invoices, rather than waiting several days for bank transfers to clear, according to a separate statement. Participating capital providers will fund the payments, while existing invoice terms and due dates remain unchanged.

Ripple also announced a partnership with Robinhood covering stablecoins, treasury operations and institutional markets. RLUSD is already available on Robinhood Chain and Bitstamp by Robinhood, with availability in the Robinhood app expected later this year. Ripple said Robinhood will use the stablecoin for institutional settlement on the XRP Ledger and integrate Ripple Treasury for cash management, payments and liquidity operations.

RLUSD has grown to about $2.5 billion in market capitalization since launching in December 2024, more than $1 billion of it issued on the XRPL. The ledger has processed over 4.6 billion transactions since launch, settling in three to five seconds, the company said.

Meanwhile, the BNY agreement and C2FO initiative remain subject to further development, with the companies yet to detail when the proposed custody-related services and broader trade finance applications will be available.

That means cooking well, for everyone, and especially on Thanksgiving, when he turns to this way-more-delicious holiday feast.

“This is my go-to method for a juicy, flavorful whole turkey—perfect for holidays or any gathering,” says Carter. “It’s all about the prep, the brine, and that smoke.”

Google Messages is Google's official texting app, and while it hasn't always been first to roll out useful features, it has caught up to what other messaging services offer and has a handful of hidden hacks to make communicating and coordinating with contacts easier and safer. Here are the best tips and tricks that every Google Messages user should know.

Google Messages recently got a Google Keep integration, which allows group chat participants to collaborate on notes and checklists directly in the message thread without having to click over to a separate app. This also centralizes ideas in one place rather than spread across a thread with dozens of separate text messages. Changes to Keep Notes sync automatically for all users. Tap the plus icon in the message compose bar in Google Messages, select Keep Notes, and either choose an existing note or tap to create a new one to be shared in the chat.

Over the last few years, Google has rolled out a number of security features for identifying and defending against scams and fraud spread via calls and text messages. These work by detecting suspected spam and either preventing it from being delivered or hiding it in a separate folder, as well as flagging suspicious links. A newer opt-in enhanced protection mode for Google Messages uses AI to catch more complex threats and alert you if a conversation or unfamiliar link that looks legit might put you at risk. Standard protection is on by default, but you can turn on enhanced protection under Messages settings > Protection & Safety > Spam and scam protection.

If you have an idea you want to write down but not send immediately—because it's outside of work hours or late at night—you can draft it in Google Messages and schedule it for later. "Send later" features in messaging and chat apps are useful for maintaining good boundaries without having to remember to type and send at a different time. Once you've entered your message in the text field, touch and hold Send, choose from the suggested actions, then tap Send again.

Swiping left or right on your conversations in Google Messages will either archive or delete them. You can customize which swipe maps onto which action for quick inbox triaging or disable one or both to prevent accidentally triggering an unwanted action. Tap your profile photo > Message settings > Swipe actions > Customize to select your preferences.

Google Messages has a pinning feature that will anchor conversations at the top of your message history, but you can also star important messages you want to save and return to later. Starring makes it easy to keep track of data like flight information, contacts, or dates that might otherwise get lost in a thread or be difficult to pull up in a basic search—starred messages have their own search filter. Simply tap and hold the message and tap Star at the top, then go to Search conversations > Starred > More options > Starred to see your list of saved messages.

We've all been in the overactive group chat that we don't want to see notifications from in real time but also don't want to leave. You can snooze threads semi-permanently, but you can also turn on a temporary snooze that will lift so notifications restart after a short period of time. This can be useful for threads discussing live events or games you will be catching up on later or if you need silence during a focused work period. Touch and hold the conversation, tap Snooze, then select your duration and tap Snooze to confirm. You can choose from 1 hour, 8 hours, 24 hours, or always.

Google Messages has two group chat modes. Group MMS is your typical group text—it sends a single message to all recipients, and everyone receives replies in the thread. Mass Text, by contrast, is like BCC. This sends your message to each user individually, and replies come only to the original sender. Using this mode allows you to share information with a bunch of people at once without creating the dreaded reply-all thread. You can change your default group messaging settings via profile > Message settings > Advanced > Group messaging.

Google Messages finally launched integrated real-time location sharing earlier this year, which allows you to send your live whereabouts to trusted contacts directly in a message thread. Location sharing makes it easier to coordinate meeting up or let someone know where you are if there are any safety concerns. You can set a limited duration or leave location sharing on indefinitely for specific contacts. Open a conversation and tap Attachment > Real-time location

You don't have to live with the embarrassment of sending a text in error with Google Messages—at least not if you catch the mistake quickly. Google Messages has a "Delete for everyone" function that can remove sent messages from the devices of users who are running the latest version of the app. Conversations must be using RCS for this to work, and you have only a limited window—anywhere from 15 to 40 minutes, according to support documentation—to use the feature before your messages are permanent. Long-press a message to pull up available delete options.

If you want to stay on top of your text threads but get distracted by other things every time you pick up your phone, you can log into the Google Messages web interface on your browser. This also makes it easier to send photos and files from your computer via Google Messages without having to transfer them to your phone first.

The latest development in Ripple’s corner of the digital-asset market is worth examining beyond the headline. A 2026-10-26 announcement describes a concrete change, but the investment consequences will depend on how it works in practice. The original record is available in the original company statement; here is what it establishes, why the distinction matters and what remains unproven.

Ripple said on October 26 that the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency had granted final approval to charter Ripple National Trust Bank. The decision follows conditional approval granted in December 2025 and gives Ripple a federally supervised trust-bank structure for defined activities. This is not simply another exchange license or a general promise of future compliance: the company describes a specific banking charter with named functions, although the scope is not the same as that of a conventional deposit-taking retail bank.

According to Ripple, the approved activities include managing segregated reserves backing Ripple USD, its dollar stablecoin known as RLUSD, acting as collateral trustee for the benefit of RLUSD holders, and fiduciary custody of digital assets. The distinction between reserve management, custody and payment services matters. A reserve custodian safeguards assets and associated legal interests; it does not automatically guarantee the token’s market price, nor does it mean every Ripple product is directly supervised as a national bank.

A federal charter can provide counterparties with a clearer supervisory point of reference. Corporate treasurers and institutional customers have generally had to evaluate a mixture of state licensing, trust arrangements, bank relationships and private attestations before using dollar tokens operationally. Ripple is seeking to simplify part of that assessment through a supervised entity. Its announcement should nonetheless be read as the company’s account of approval, not as evidence that every commercial integration or future reserve-handling workflow is already live.

For cryptocurrency investors, the central point is to distinguish an announced milestone from an immediate change in asset value. The underlying development can strengthen a company’s product, legal position or operating capacity without determining what a token will trade for tomorrow. The linked source establishes what was disclosed; the valuation question requires a separate assessment of customers, costs, execution and market conditions. That gap between a confirmed fact and a forecast is where much of the investment risk sits.

The timing is notable because US stablecoin oversight is changing. The OCC has also finalised rules under the GENIUS Act, with a January 18, 2027 effective date. Trust-bank authority and the payment-stablecoin rulebook are related pieces of a larger framework, but they are separate regulatory questions. An institutional buyer should still examine token redemption terms, the quality and accessibility of backing assets, governance, conflicts and the precise entity against which it holds a claim.

The next useful evidence will be operational rather than ceremonial: confirmation of the trust bank’s commencement, the reporting and controls associated with reserve administration, and disclosures showing how RLUSD users are protected when things go wrong. Rival issuers may seek similar structures, but a charter by itself is not a verdict on market share. For now the verifiable development is the OCC approval announced by Ripple, and the commercial consequences remain to be demonstrated.

Market watchers should also avoid treating a company’s or regulator’s headline as a substitute for terms and conditions. Tokens, shares and regulated service agreements create different rights, even when they coexist in one wallet or platform. A reliable follow-up should ask which entity is responsible, what a holder can actually redeem or transfer, and whether the claimed advantages appear in live activity. Those questions are more informative than an unsupported price target or an assumption that adoption is inevitable.

#31. Meta AI director Rohit Patel leaves to take on Microsoft Office and Google Workspace

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

After eight years developing AI for Meta, Rohit Patel is departing the company, TechCrunch has exclusively learned. The former director at Meta Superintelligence Labs has launched Ndoc, a startup he’s pitching as an AI-native replacement for Microsoft Office suite or Google Workspace.

Patel is the latest in a handful of high-profile departures from Meta following the exits of Chief Global Affairs Officer Joel Kaplan, AI researcher Andrew Tulloch, and Head of Product for AI-for-Work Emily Dalton Smith.

Patel designed Ndoc to be more than just another AI tool layered onto an existing workflow. The goal is to replace the fragmented way people work across tools like Excel, Word, PowerPoint, Tableau, Replit, and “ChatGPT copy-paste” with one unified workspace that all colleagues can access, even if they’re not signed up for Ndoc.

Ndoc brings three components together in one layout. The Stage tab is where presentations of key findings live, like slide decks, dashboards, simulations, or mini apps. The Narrative tab features analyses, numbered citations, generated figures, and generally the “why” behind the numbers. The Model tab is where underlying datasets and Python scripts are housed.

The design reflects Patel’s background. During his tenure at Meta, Patel focused on reinforcement learning — a type of machine learning in which AI agents learn to make decisions through trial and error and maximize rewards — and evaluating whether AI output is trustworthy. Patel believes that if AI is going to build the spreadsheets and slide decks that companies use to make decisions, someone should be able to check its work. For instance, users can find the Python scripts used to create the documents.

“Even if you don’t know any Python, you can ask [the AI chat] to explain, ‘Hey, what does this script do? Which line has my formula?’ And you can verify that formula,” Patel said. “There’s nothing hidden behind the curtain here.”

In practice, users can upload a dataset and ask Ndoc to perform an analysis and write a narrative about the findings. They can also add document-specific context, including audience, tone, and memory. “It maintains a memory across your person, that particular document, your team, and your company,” Patel said.

Patel also says Ndoc is built and fine-tuned using a mixture of open source models. Because the platform was developed from scratch, he says it costs considerably less to run than alternatives built on frontier AI models.

“I thought that’s what people would be excited about, but people don’t care about that,” he said, referring to his early trial users. “What people are excited about is that they love seeing all their end-to-end in one place.”

Patel’s larger ambition is to reinvent the file itself. He says documents can be exported as a single “.ndoc.html” file that can be opened in any browser, a format he plans to open-source so other developers can build apps that work with it without being locked into Ndoc the way so many have been locked into Microsoft or Google for years.

His desire to open-source this project was partly why he left Meta and decided to go it alone. At a big company like Meta, getting everyone to agree on an open source format would undoubtedly require the input of “hundreds of people and opinions on how the format should look.”

Patel wanted to move quickly. “Humans can only move at human pace, whereas…I can move at the pace of what AI can do.”

Trying to become the platform where enterprise work happens is a huge bet for a founder with a team of three and no funding, especially when other AI labs are trying to own the same space.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, SpaceXAI, and Google have all come out with enterprise AI tools that allow users to connect to Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and other workplace apps so their AI models can work directly in those tools. And last month, OpenAI launched Space, a shared workspace inside ChatGPT that’s a more direct competitor to those established tools.

Patel acknowledges that Space could be a threat, but says the labs largely have the wrong idea.

“Companies are basically attaching AI to spreadsheets and it fills your cells for you,” he said. “It just sort of kills my soul. This is like if you just figured out electricity, and then you decided the best use case is to put lights on my bulls that till my soil. No. Get a tractor.”

There’s a reason the AI labs have tried to bring their models to where people are already working, though. It’s hard to change habits and replace entire workflows. Patel says he understands that, which is why he built connectors so customers can import existing data from the tools they already use.

“My bar was that I’m not going to release anything I don’t love using,” he said. “I have very optimized AI workflows for everything I do. If I start using this tool, and I keep going back to my workflows, I haven’t built a good enough tool…I have a graveyard of tools that did not meet that bar.”

Patel has a similar bar for gaining customer traction before he tries to raise funds. “I didn’t want to take money until I had some proof that people love the product,” he said.

As of Tuesday, Ndoc is conducting an enterprise pilot and opening an alpha version to a limited number of customers who join the wait list. It’s free to sign up, but customers will pay for usage and tokens. He hopes to release a beta version in the coming weeks, with a full launch planned for next year.

For Patel, moving as fast as possible is more important than having the perfect product from the get-go.

“Execution is extremely cheap…so go execute on a suboptimal plan and worry about it later,” he said. “By the time you make the best plan, the execution will be done by somebody else.”
