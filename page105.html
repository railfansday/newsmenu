#978. Michael Carrick is STAYING: Man United decide against wielding axe after day of crisis talks - but under-pressure boss must still convince chiefs he is the right man for the job

Michael Carrick is staying as Manchester United head coach for now after being given the chance to turn the club’s season around.

Carrick will remain in charge for Saturday’s trip to Chelsea after Old Trafford bosses decided not to sack him with United in 16th place in the Premier League table, one point clear of the relegation zone.

The 45-year-old still faces a battle to improve results and performances and convince his employers that he remains the right man for the job.

Sources confirmed that Carrick has been given a second chance, and said that everyone is focused on showing a response on the pitch at Stamford Bridge following United’s worst start to a league season in 40 years.

It followed a period of intense speculation over Carrick’s future since Sunday’s 3-0 defeat to Bournemouth at Old Trafford left United without a win in eight games.

The United boss was left in limbo after chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox held crisis talks on Monday with minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and co-chairmen Joel and Avram Glazer.

The first indications that Carrick had earned a reprieve came shortly before 8am on Tuesday when he arrived at Carrington dressed in a club tracksuit and oversaw training as the players returned after a day off.

He had also turned up at the training ground on Monday but was there to watch United’s Under-21s play in a behind-closed-doors game against Hull City alongside his No2 Steve Holland and Under-18s coach Darren Fletcher. He stayed for three hours before leaving with his son Jacey, a member of United’s Under-18s.

Carrick’s job had been hanging by a thread after losing half of his 12 games this season. He was visibly emotional after the defeat to Bournemouth when the fans gave him a rousing show of support while directing their fury at the Glazers, Ratcliffe, Wilcox and the players.

The former United midfielder appeared to say farewell to supporters afterwards, although he later insisted that was not the case.

It followed a heartfelt five-minute monologue to reporters at the start of his pre-match press conference on Friday, two days after he approached the travelling fans in Como to apologise for a 4-1 defeat.

United’s decision to stick with Carrick amid speculation they were lining up Eddie Howe or Julian Nagelsmann to replace him is reminiscent of the situation with Erik ten Hag before the 2024 FA Cup final. Ten Hag kept his job by winning at Wembley but was gone five months later.

Club legend Wayne Rooney earlier launched a furious tirade at the United players who he believes have let his down his former team-mate.

'At least I ran and worked hard and put myself about for the team,' he said on The United Stand. 'I don't think the players have got a leg to stand on. What's not fair is the performances they're giving.

'I was a player who could score a goal, had a bit of ability but at least I f***ing ran and worked hard, put myself about for the team even when it's going tough. These players are not doing that.

#3307. A Team In Need Of The Real Kyle Filipowski

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Monday, October 26th was a disastrous day for Utah Jazz big men. Before gametime, Jaxson Hayes was ruled out due to right shoulder soreness. In the first quarter, Lauri Markkanen suffered a nasty wrist injury on his shooting hand, and he staggered back towards the locker room. Not too long after that, Jaren Jackson Jr. sustained a right knee injury after Ty Jerome stumbled into his leg – if the tweet found below correctly reads coach Hardy's lips, this could keep Trip out for a long time.

So, with 4 separate 7 footers now riding the bench with injury (Nurkic was looking classy as usual), where does the coaching staff turn for big man production? Surely, surely this would be the time for Kyle Filipowski, 3 year Jazzman with thousands of prime developmental minutes under his belt, to step up and show that all of the priority placed on his improvement since the 2024 draft was wisely spent. This would be that magical moment where Flip proves to all that his stats were not empty and his skills not gimmicky. In the face of necessity, Filipowski stands ready.

Instead, this quickly became the Mo Bamba game. And it was fun! He blocked shots, ran the court hard, and climbed Mount Edey in a way I've never before seen. Our defense was struggling, so I get it – Hardy wanted an anchor down low, and Bamba fits that archetype better than Filipowski does. However, Bamba was not the only bench big to get the call-up in the second half; Tony Bradley also reacquainted himself with Jazz fans, to the tune of one of the worst 5 minute stretches of basketball I can remember.

Ultimately, in a game where the Jazz were lacking some serious size in the frontcourt, Kyle Filipowski only played 8 minutes. Last season, he started half of the season's games, and played nearly half of the 48 minutes of each game. And he showed us something! He was a really solid defensive rebounder, and his passing from the top-of-the-key was the perfect fit for Hardy's offense. Of the bench youngsters at the end of last season (Collier, Flip, Cody, and Brice), Filipowski was the one that I was most confident would be a winning NBA player in our first non-tanking season (even if I preferred Brice in the long run). He needed to be able to eat minutes for our bench lineups to make sense.

Throughout the first 4 games, however, we have not been so lucky. Filipowski has flashed nothing so far. The rim protection has not taken the leap that many were (perhaps irrationally) hoping, and it seems that some added weight over the offseason has prevented Flip from playing any meaningful minutes at the power forward spot. On offense, he has been completely invisible. He is shooting 1 for 6 from the field across 3 games (DNP against the Lakers), with zero assists and zero offensive rebounds. On both ends of the court, he has been floating, and it seems that Hardy has noticed and cut his playing time accordingly.

Now, I want to give Filipowski the benefit of the doubt and offer an alternative theory. While training during the Summer, Flip suffered a herniated disk in his back which kept him out of most of the Jazz's preseason contests. Could this disappointment be attributed to rust or justifiably poor conditioning? Has his flexibility been hampered by the remaining effects of his injury? Will Flip be playing more minutes, more effectively in a few weeks when he is completely recovered? I do not know the answer to any of these questions, but the uncertain nature of his injury leaves room for Filipowski to be an unfortunate victim of circumstance.

The insult to this injury is just how well an actualized Filipowski would fit with this team. The Jazz are currently lacking playmaking, especially in the frontcourt with Jusuf Nurkic out. If we could go back to a dribble hand off, top of the key offense, we'd take some of the pressure off of Isaiah Collier which has led to him being one of the worst watches in the league so far. Flip has been able to function this way his entire basketball career up until this season, and he chose the wrong time to lose this superpower. Additionally, one of the lineups I was most looking forward to were the JJJ+Flip combos – their strengths and weaknesses complement each other in a way that I thought would unlock the funkiness of Kyle's game. However, if Flip has meaningfully lost aggression/flexibility/touch, then even that synergy with Jaren isn't enough to get him consistently onto the court. In an ideal world, that doesn't seem to me to be too unrealistic, this very well could've been the best year of Filipowski's career.

So, what should we be looking for as we track Flip's journey back up to his old production? First, he needs to understand his role in Hardy's offense. Currently, it seems that no one really understands their role in Hardy's offense, so this may come in time. But, even if confusion persists and Flip doesn't become a top-of-the-key playmaking hub, he still must find ways to make his presence felt on offense. Crashing the glass, setting hard screens, quick decision making: every scheme requires these things, and even if Hardy's isn't quite getting implemented right now, Flip would certainly be a much more beneficial offensive player if he solidified the little things. Additionally, picking up his defensive rebounding would be a huge sign of life for the young big man. He's snagged 2 rebounds in 3 games, and for a team lacking consistent boardmen, this is unacceptable. Flip is not only a good rebounder, but he's a good passer too – some of our offensive struggles in the first few games could be mitigated with more semi-transition attempts, which may be borne with some solid outlet passing. These are not big changes that Flip needs to make, but they provide a base level of value, even if the shot isn't falling.

Jaren may be out for a long time, and who knows how long Lauri will be sitting on the bench next to him. Hayes will come back soon, and Nurkic won't be too far behind, but as long as 7 footers are healing up, there is a hole that Filipowski can fill. More than ever, this team needs Kyle to prove that the last two years weren't flukes, or the product of tanking teams, because if he doesn't, Tony Bradley might just end up bear-hugging opposing defenders again, for no discernible reason. The Utah Jazz need Kyle Filipowski to play well, and as Kyle nears second contract negotiation time, he may very well need that too, if his goal is to stick around the league for more than a cup of coffee.

The results are not good for the president, especially before an already tricky midterm election. Many of Trump’s foreign-policy views are unpopular with U.S. foreign-policy leaders and the public. Trump has failed to sell fellow Republican foreign-policy leaders or voters on key parts of his vision. Rather than mark a turning point in America’s approach to the world, Trump’s foreign policy may not outlast Trump himself.

On international economic policy, large majorities of every leadership group believe that tariffs worsen the cost of living in the United States, including 86 percent of business leaders, 100 percent of labor leaders, and approximately 80 percent of officials in Congress and federal executive branch agencies. While Republican leaders are more supportive of his trade agenda, 65 percent of them acknowledge that tariffs are worsening the cost of living in the U.S., and 81 percent of Republican leaders said tariffs are bad for U.S. relationships with other countries.

The public has also rejected the Trump trade agenda. Eighty-one percent of the overall public believe that Trump’s tariffs do not improve the cost of living in the U.S., while 63 percent believe that they are bad for creating jobs. Even for Republicans, Trump’s tariff agenda is divisive. While approximately 56 percent of Republicans agree that tariffs help the economy, 62 percent also believe that they raise the cost of living.

At the same time, when asked to identify the most important issue in the upcoming midterm election, more Republican voters point to inflation and the cost of living than any other single issue. Although Trump has repeatedly turned to the message that the U.S. has been “ripped off on TRADE … by friend and foe, alike, for DECADES,” a bipartisan majority of the public believe that international trade benefits both the U.S. and other countries, including 62 percent of Republicans.

Trump has similarly failed to persuade leaders and the public about the wisdom of his approach to America’s traditional allies. Trump’s belief that the United States has been exploited by its security alliances has been a centerpiece of his foreign policy since entering politics in 2015. In May 2025, for example, Trump told a graduating class of West Point military officers, “We’ve been ripped off at the NATO level. We’ve been ripped off like no country has ever been ripped off.” At a campaign rally in February 2024, Trump stated that for those NATO members that did not meet defense spending targets, he would encourage Russia “to do whatever the hell they want.”

This view of U.S. security alliances is decisively opposed by leaders in every professional group surveyed and the U.S. public, including by bipartisan majorities of voters in both parties. While Republican leaders support greater burden-sharing, over 60 percent of Republican leaders and over 90 percent of Democratic leaders support either maintaining or increasing the level of U.S. commitment to NATO, while 85 percent of respondents across all leadership groups support using force if Russia invaded a NATO ally in Eastern Europe. Only 15 percent of the public supports decreasing the U.S. commitment to NATO. If Russia invaded a NATO ally like Poland, 60 percent of the public would support using military force, including majorities of Republican, Democratic, and independent voters.

The conflict in Ukraine further illustrates the gap between Trump and both foreign-policy leaders and the public. Since returning to office in 2025, Trump has ended U.S. military aid to Ukraine, told Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky that he didn’t “have the cards” to reach a favorable settlement with Russia, and called for Ukrainians to replace Zelensky with a new leader.

Yet 84 percent of foreign-policy leaders, including 79 percent of Republicans, favor sending additional military aid to Ukraine. More than 92 percent of leaders, including 94 percent of Republicans, favor increasing economic and diplomatic sanctions on Russia. The public also is supportive of Ukraine. Almost 60 percent of the public supports sending additional military aid to Ukraine, 70 percent support increasing economic and diplomatic sanctions on Russia, and almost 70 percent support admitting Ukraine into NATO, including 60 percent of Republican voters.

And, in the crisis triggered by Trump’s claims over Greenland, 90 percent of the public opposes forcibly annexing Greenland, including 82 percent of Republican voters and almost 90 percent of Republican elites.

In the conflict in Iran, the public is not persuaded that the war is justified, though leaders are more divided. Fifty-eight percent of Republican leaders still believe that the Iran war was the right decision, while 98 percent of Democratic and 73 percent of independent leaders say it was the wrong decision.

After almost eight months of war, however, only a third of the public believes Trump made the right decision. Conversely, 68 percent of the U.S. public believes the war was not worth fighting, and the same percentage opposes using force to overthrow the Iranian government. A majority of Republican voters still approve of the decision to attack Iran but also believe that costs of the war have been high, including by raising the cost of living in the United States.

Perhaps most damning of all for Trump, only 20 percent of the public thinks the United States is more respected on the world stage compared to 10 years ago, including only 40 percent of Republicans.

There are genuine partisan gaps on some issues, including areas where Trump’s foreign-policy agenda retains the support of both the Republican Party leadership and its base, such as on strengthening immigration enforcement, hostility to action on climate change, and withdrawing from numerous multilateral organizations. Yet these tend to be areas where Trump has pursued more traditional Republican foreign-policy priorities, rather than his own idiosyncratic beliefs.

That foreign-policy leaders reject key aspects of the America First agenda may not be surprising. Trump himself has framed his agenda as a rejection of classic elite attitudes. But much of the public is equally hostile to his approach, suggesting that project may not be durable. These attitudes provide an opening for members of Congress and the next U.S. president to pursue a new foreign-policy vision that will resonate more with leaders and the American public, while strengthening America’s standing in the world.

#5138. Holiday Season Is the Best Time For Homebuyers to Score a Deal in These 7 U.S. Metros

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Early November is the best time for homebuyers to score a bargain in three U.S. metro areas: Milwaukee, Riverside, CA and Tampa, FL.

In Detroit and three Florida metros–Fort Lauderdale, Miami and West Palm Beach–house hunters may benefit from holding out a little longer. Mid-December is typically the best time of year to get a deal in those places.

This is according to a Home Economics and Redfin analysis of housing market data; the analysis was originally completed in April. The analysis identifies the time of year when discounts off a home’s original asking price–through price drops and/or negotiations between buyer and seller–typically approach their seasonal peak.

Nationwide, discounts tend to peak in early fall before plateauing or shrinking as winter approaches. But housing is local, and in these seven metros, peak discounts typically arrive later. Homebuying slows as the holidays approach, leaving sellers with fewer potential buyers–giving the buyers who remain more room to negotiate. And some late-year listings have been sitting for months, making sellers more motivated to strike a deal before the end of the year.

“There’s a tradeoff to shopping for a home late in the year: Buyers may have fewer fresh listings to choose from, but they also face less competition, and many sellers are ready to make a deal,” said Redfin Senior Economist Asad Khan. “A seller who has been waiting months for an offer may be more willing to negotiate on price or other costs than they were in the spring. Buyers shouldn’t purchase a home just because the calendar says it’s a good time for deals, but those who find the right home may have room to negotiate.”

In Florida, the best deals for buyers typically arrive in November or December, partly due to weather. South Florida’s biggest wave of buyers–retirees and seasonal residents–often show up after the new year, when the weather is sunny and comfortable in Florida but cold in the Northeast.

“Local buyers have a good shot at getting a deal in November, mainly because it’s just before the snowbirds arrive,” said Todd Edwards, a Redfin Premier agent in Tampa. “House hunters have a window of opportunity to negotiate on listings that have been lingering on the market since summer: Sellers want to close a deal before the new year, and there’s often not much competition from out-of-towners yet.”

Hurricane season, which runs through early fall, also keeps some house hunters away and leaves lingering listings ripe for discounts once it passes. Additionally, Florida’s high concentration of luxury homes tend to sell below asking price, especially late in the year when sellers are eager to get those properties off their books.

The story is a bit different in Milwaukee and Detroit. Those northern metros have a fairly short selling season: Cold-weather markets compress the window for touring and closing on homes; sellers keep at it later into fall, trying to close a deal before the depth of winter hits and buyers hibernate.

That delays the usual seasonal slowdown until November in Milwaukee and December in Detroit, later than in most of the country.

The November and December discount windows in Milwaukee, Riverside, Detroit and much of Florida aren’t a one-time event; they show up as a recurring pattern each year, regardless of whether the broader market favors buyers or sellers.

But the seasonal advantage could be particularly useful to buyers this year. The U.S. housing market is already firmly tilted toward buyers, with 63% more sellers than buyers—the biggest imbalance in Redfin records dating back to 2013. A jump in listings coupled with sluggish homebuying demand has given house hunters in many parts of the country more options and more negotiating power. Miami is one of the strongest buyer’s markets in the nation, with more than twice as many sellers as buyers. Tampa, West Palm Beach, Fort Lauderdale, Riverside and Detroit are also buyer’s markets.

Milwaukee is one of just five seller’s markets in the country. With more buyers than sellers in Milwaukee, deals aren’t commonplace–so buyers there may want to pay extra attention to seasonal patterns.

The best time to buy a home is more complex than the week when buyers are most likely to score a discount.

There’s typically a tradeoff between selection and savings. New listings generally peak months before the best deals arrive. For instance, Milwaukee typically has the most new listings in mid-May and the most fresh inventory in mid-July,then its best deals arrive in early November. Detroit’s new listings typically peak in early August and fresh inventory in early September, months before its mid-December dealmaking sweet spot.

That means buyers who wait until November or December may have fewer homes to choose from. But those who prioritize price—and are flexible about their timing—may find sellers more receptive to negotiations. Ultimately, buyers should balance price, selection and their own circumstances.

Home remodelers have always served two different markets, but the current state of housing is now pulling them opposite directions. First, people tend to remodel when they move, but high interest rates, high home prices and weak consumer sentiment have put the housing market in a deep freeze. Homeowners also tend to renovate when they are unable or don't want to sell. So which way will the remodeling market shift?

Annual spending on home improvements and repairs is expected to remain relatively flat through late 2027, according to the latest Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity (LIRA) from the Remodeling Futures Program at the Joint Center for Housing Studies of Harvard University. It puts spending growth on renovation and repairs at just 1.5% year-over-year by the third quarter."Retail spending on building products has been flat to negative, and remodeling permits have stagnated, which points to continued sluggish renovation activity," said Rachel Bogardus Drew, director of the remodeling futures program at the Center, in a release. The U.S. Census Bureau's monthly read on single-family housing starts for September came in well below expectations, as did building permits, an indicator of future construction. Pending home sales, measured by signed contracts, also plunged beyond expectations, according to the National Association of Realtors.

"Fuel costs remain a pressure point for most businesses, with some pros passing them on to clients through higher prices and surcharges and others absorbing them in their margins."
