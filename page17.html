#5041. Bitcoin Could Follow 2018 and 2022 Playbook as U.S. Midterms Near Before Rally, Analyst Warns

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Bitcoin (BTC) could face further losses ahead of the November 3 U.S. midterm elections before recovering in 2027, as analysts compare it to previous election cycles.

In a Tuesday post on X, CryptoQuant analyst Satoureireal cited data from blockchain analysis firm Alphractal showing that Bitcoin suffered significant corrections around the 2018 and 2022 midterms before recovering the following year. BTC bottomed in late 2018 before rebounding in 2019, while the 2022 bear market gave way to a recovery in 2023.

With the next midterms approaching, Satoureireal questioned whether the same pattern could emerge in 2026, potentially setting the stage for a recovery in 2027.

In a September 13 post, the analyst also highlighted recurring volatility around U.S. midterms, noting that Bitcoin has sometimes rallied before an election and sold off afterward, while declines have emerged later in other cycles.

“During the previous two midterm election cycles in 2018 and 2022, Bitcoin experienced significant corrections before forming a bottom and entering a recovery phase in the following year,” Satoureireal stated.

Notably, this distinction matters because the historical relationship does not prove that elections cause Bitcoin sell-offs. Market liquidity, monetary policy, investor positioning, and broader economic conditions can also influence price movements.

Elsewhere, Technical analyst Frank Cappelleri has also identified similarities between Bitcoin’s current chart and the price structure that preceded its previous major recovery.

Speaking to CNBC recently, the CappThesis founder compared Bitcoin’s roughly 54% decline during the current cycle with the nearly 70% drawdown recorded between 2022 and 2023.

“This also looks like a bullish pattern to me,” Cappelleri said, pointing to how the earlier decline eventually gave way to a strong recovery as market momentum returned.

However, he stopped short of predicting another rally as large as Bitcoin’s previous advance, which approached 400%.

“I’m not calling for that, but I am calling for a very similar foundation to be leveraged and potentially get back to new highs again,” he said.

His analysis suggests Bitcoin could establish a base for another advance, potentially reaching new highs in early 2027.

That said, not all historical comparisons support an immediate bullish outlook.

Recently, popular analyst Ali Charts highlighted Bitcoin’s declines following the previous four U.S. midterm elections.

According to his analysis, Bitcoin fell 72% after the 2010 midterms, 65% after the 2014 elections, 52% after the 2018 vote and 27% following the 2022 midterms.

In a thread on X, the analyst identified $73,000 as a level to watch if the historical pattern repeats.

Meanwhile, on-chain data point to changes in investor behavior that could influence Bitcoin’s next move.

Glassnode reported that profit-taking by short-term Bitcoin holders has eased after contributing to selling pressure in recent weeks.

The analytics firm said the share of exchange inflows from short-term holders sitting on profits reached a one-year high before returning to its average level over the past year.

Santiment also reported that wallets holding between 10 and 10,000 BTC accumulated approximately 88,987 BTC in one month from September 19. Their combined holdings exceeded 13.69 million BTC, the highest level since April, according to the analytics firm.

The firm noted that continued accumulation could support market confidence despite price volatility.

“Bitcoin still drives much of crypto’s direction, so persistent accumulation from its largest influential holders can support not only BTC, but confidence across the market.” The firm noted.

At press time, Bitcoin was trading at $80,341, up 1.02% in the past 24 hours.

In 2004, Bleyer pivoted to Dennis Miller, the two-season (2004-2005) late-night talk and interview show on CNBC. Bleyer wrote on 66 episodes of Miller’s show, and served as a producer.

#3560. L.A. City Council Passes New Motions Aimed at Film-Friendly Overhaul

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

As shoot days fall, the city is making civic properties more accessible to crews and offering free microshoot permits.

As filming levels in the city continue their downward momentum, the Los Angeles City Council passed two measures in a meeting on Wednesday to attempt to stem the bleeding.

The measures, both introduced by City Councilmember Adrin Nazarian, create a free microshoot permit for ultra-small productions and open up city-owned parking lots, yards, civic properties and staging areas to productions for just $1 a day.

The microshoot motion will provide small projects with 10 or fewer production personnel with permits to film on-location in L.A. without a fee, making filmmaking less onerous and expensive for student filmmakers, documentary crews and ultra-low-budget projects in the city. As for the second motion, the idea is to make city-owned spaces that films and television shows generally use for base camp, crew parking and other low-impact activities more accessible and affordable.

“Today marks a major milestone for the Keep Hollywood Home campaign,” Nazarian said in a statement. “With these latest actions, all Phase 1 motions have now been approved by the City. This progress demonstrates the impact of the Ad Hoc Committee and our commitment to keep up the pressure for real reforms.”

The development arrives less than a week after city film office FilmLA found that shoot days for film and television in the city continued to fall from June to September in spite of local and state efforts to encourage more production to return to the city. L.A.’s shoot days dropped nearly five percent compared to the same period in 2025 and 11 percent from the previous quarter.

In 2025, California more than doubled the funding available for its film and television tax credit program, from a previous $330 million a year to $750 million. On a more local level, Mayor Karen Bass issued an executive order in 2025 to streamline the city’s permitting process. Meanwhile, seven initial motions from Nazarian to improve filming practices in L.A. passed the full Council earlier this year.

#3355. A $20 AGNC Share Bought at Its 2008 IPO Has Paid $51 in Dividends. The Stock Trades Near $9.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

At first glance, AGNC (AGNC +1.02%) might seem like a terrible long-term investment. The mortgage real estate investment trust (mREIT) went public at $20 per share on May 15, 2008, but now trades at less than $9 per share.

But since AGNC's market debut, it's paid out $51.16 per share in dividends. If you had reinvested those dividends, you'd be sitting on a total return of roughly 520%. Let's see why there's such a gap between its stock price performance and dividend-driven gains -- and if it's still a good long-term play for income-oriented investors.

As an mREIT, AGNC invests in mortgages and mortgage-backed securities to collect interest. To insulate it from another credit crunch, it mainly invests in agency-backed MBS. It must distribute at least 90% of its taxable income as dividends to maintain a lower tax rate.

AGNC borrows at short-term rates (through "repo" transactions that enable it to sell its MBS to counterparties and buy them back at a set price plus interest at a future date) to fund its purchases of longer-term MBS. For AGNC to keep growing, short-term rates need to be lower than long-term rates. The mortgage market also needs to remain healthy.

Since AGNC distributes most of its profits to its investors, it must continually issue new shares to fund its MBS purchases. That's why its total number of shares skyrocketed from 15 million at the time of its IPO to approximately 1.26 billion shares today. That constant dilution practically guarantees it will underperform the S&P 500 (^GSPC +0.60%) over the long term.

AGNC pays a massive forward dividend yield of 16.4%. That might seem like a bright red flag, but it's sustainable as long as its net interest spread (the gap between the average yield it earns on its MBS and the costs of funding those purchases) stays positive.

That figure has hovered around 2% over the past year, and its projected 2026 EPS of $1.56 will easily cover its forward dividend rate of $1.44 per share. At $9, it trades at less than six times this year's earnings -- so it certainly looks like an undervalued income stock.

However, AGNC isn't necessarily the best dividend play for long-term investors. If you had simply invested in an S&P 500 index fund when AGNC went public and reinvested your dividends, you'd be sitting on a bigger total return of 669%. So while AGNC's dividend payments offset its declining stock price, it still couldn't outperform the market over the long term.

IWGB Game Workers shared Aftermath's story online. The agreement, if passed, will "grant workers a say over issues including pay and bonuses, overtime, redundancies, flexible working, and generative AI policies," the union reiterates.

"The terms of the recognition deal, agreed upon by workers and management, would give workers a say over topics including pay and bonuses, overtime, redundancies, flexible working arrangements, and generative AI policies," a press release reads (thanks, Rock Paper Shotgun).

Shanti Easton-Steel, a committee member for the Rockstar Game Workers Union, discussed what's being voted on: "When management choices are kept hidden, we can’t properly address issues like the gender pay gap, the uneven and ever-changing policies on overtime pay, or the concerns many co-workers hold around job security post-release."

"This is our moment to secure a window onto the decisions that shape our lives, and a seat at the table to improve them. I’m immensely proud of the games Rockstar gives its fans – I want to be equally proud of the voice Rockstar gives its staff," Easton-Steel adds.

This vote follows a messy and ongoing legal dispute over Rockstar staff, including many unionizing staff, who were fired for allegedly leaking "confidential information." Rockstar has repeatedly denied claims of union busting.

Rockstar also has a documented history of crunch, a term often used in game development to describe extended periods of heavy, often under-compensated, and quietly (or loudly) mandated overtime during production – the exact sort of thing that game unions have worked to prevent or control.
