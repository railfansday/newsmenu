#3009. Jerry Jones wants Cowboys to stick with the run

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

On Monday night, the Cowboys ran the ball for 204 yards, while passing it for 212.

“Our whole thing here is to establish the run,” owner and G.M. Jerry Jones said Tuesday on 105.3 The Fan in Dallas, quoting his in-game comments to Stephen A. Smith of ESPN. “We’ve got to stick with this run.”

Later in the segment, Jones confirmed his belief that the Cowboys need to be a run-first team.

That approach doesn’t mesh with the whole “GOTI” vibe, but it’s a formula for winning. It keeps the defense off the field, and the defense continues to be a weakness.

On Monday night, for instance, the Cowboys gave up 321 yards to an offense led by a quarterback who entered the season as No. 3 on the depth chart.

The other question, as raised by Smith on First Take, is whether the Cowboys have lost some confidence in quarterback Dak Prescott. Smith, who spent part of the game sitting next to Jones in his suite, explained that it’s his own interpretation of the situation and nothing Jones said to him.

Still, unless the vast majority of those who witnessed last night’s game, Smith had the perspective of hearing directly from Jones while the game was being played. Which makes it very interesting to see how the offense performs for the rest of the season.

That doesn’t mean they’re down on Dak. He played very well last night. But it’s easier to play well when the running game is softening up the defense, and drawing a safety or two closer to the line of scrimmage.

It hasn’t been working with the Cowboys operating as a pass-first offense. As a run-first offense, it worked on Monday night.

#3139. Position grades from the Patriots’ loss to the Bears

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

With the eyes of the nation on them, the New England Patriots have not played their best football so far. After losing the season opener in Seattle, they went 0-for-2 in prime time games last Thursday night versus the Chicago Bears.

It didn't have to be this way. The Patriots had their opportunities to come away victoriously, but ultimately were undone by some untimely errors and an ability to make the big play when it needed to be made down the stretch.

Let's take a look at the film and hand out some grades.

Quarterback: Drake Maye briefly reverted back to his Week 1-3 self, when he saw a bad throw into the end zone intended for tight end Eli Raridon get intercepted in the second quarter — a pivotal moment in a one-possession game. However, other than that one miscue, the third-year QB played some impressive football. Case in point: even with the turnover, the second most-impactful play of the night in terms of expected points added or lost, he finished with the same EPA per play as Chicago's Caleb Williams (0.34). In a way, it was Maye playing his usual brand of football between making difficult throws look routine, extending plays with his legs, and maneuvering around the pocket even when faced with pressure and a variety of blitz looks. | Grade: A-

Running back: Together with his quarterback, Rhamondre Stevenson seemingly single-handedly willed the Patriots to competitiveness on Thursday. The veteran running back finished with the best receiving output of his career — 93 yards including an improbable 48-yard touchdown to close out the first half — and did what he could behind some underwhelming run blocking. He also looked good as a pass blocker, something that cannot be said for TreVeyon Henderson. The second-year back continues to be uneven in that area, while his feel for run-lane development is also still a major work in progress. Corey Kiner did not fare significantly better. | Grade: C+

Wide receiver: He didn't have the same drop issues he struggled with against the Jets in Week 6, but A.J. Brown's impact was still limited on Thursday. He did move the chains on all three of his receptions but also didn't manage to find the same space he did against the Jets; he looked a bit slow at times while getting up to 101 snaps in a four-day span fresh off a four-week stint on injured reserve. Catching three passes, Brown was still the most productive Patriots receiver against the Bears in terms of volume. The others — DeMario Douglas, Efton Chism, Kyle Williams — were relatively quiet in their rotational roles. Some might have been by design and the running backs and tight ends seeing increased targets, but they also didn't provide consistently-reliable outlets for Drake Maye to target. | Grade: D

Tight end: Hunter Henry continues to be Mr. Reliable in the Patriots' offense, and his chemistry with Drake Maye has been evident for multiple weeks now. On Thursday, he hauled in a team-high seven passes both from in-structure situations and while improvising to assist his quarterback on play extensions. He also continued to perform at a high level as a blocker, something that was not the case earlier in the year. Rookie Eli Raridon, on the other hand, is not there yet and continues to struggle with his run-block assignments. The Patriots are trusting him with a high-pressure role, but he hasn't always been up to task. As a receiver, Raridon has looked a lot better and continues to find positive matchups against second-level defenders including on his 30-yard touchdown catch up the seam: the rookie simply ran past his man to get open for the score. | Grade: B-

Offensive line: They did what they could, but at the end of the day the blocking was the mixed bag that had to be expected given the circumstances. Down starting guards Alijah Vera-Tucker and Ben Brown, the Patriots used Walter Rouse at left guard and Matt Pryor on the right side. Both showed flashes, but their run blocking in particular left some to be desired — unsurprisingly, given that they had only four days to prepare for the starting roles they were asked to fill. And considering how important cohesiveness is to a team's run game, it looked the part on Thursday. Pass protection was a different story, with the O-line holding up surprisingly well and being responsible for just nine total pressures all game. | Grade: C

Defensive line: The Patriots defensive line some positive moments, such as Milton Williams registering a tackle for loss and Christian Barmore taking down Caleb Williams for a 10-yard sack that could have been one of the biggest plays of the game had New England won. However, the highly-paid interior pairing was unable to consistently collapse the pocket from the middle and needs to make impact plays at a higher rate. The same is true on the edge, where Elijah Ponder found himself misplaced on multiple occasions, including a 4-yard D'Andre Swift touchdown run. Gabe Jacas, on the other hand, flashed his impressive skills and high-octane motor once again. He still has room for growth but the arrow is pointing up steeply. | Grade: C

Linebacker: The Bears made it a point of emphasis of challenging the Patriots' off-ball linebackers, and found some success by putting Robert Spillane and Christian Elliss into tough positions. The most notable of those moments came on back-to-back plays in the late first quarter. First, Elliss seemingly did not get the memo on a coverage adjustment which left wide receiver Luther Burden wide open on a 58-yard catch-and-run. The very next play, Spillane missed a tackle while Swift found the end zone on the aforementioned touchdown. Make no mistake, there was a lot of positive as well, but on the whole the pair was still involved in too many missed or big plays. | Grade: D+

Cornerback: By his own lofty standards, Christian Gonzalez did not play his best game on Thursday. The highest-paid cornerback in football, who was added to the injury report with an illness just two hours before kickoff, gave up two separate 21-yard completions and was flagged for illegal use of hands. On the whole, he still ranked fairly low on the list of New England's issues against the Bears, but with high salary comes high expectation. As for the issues, Marcus Jones and to a lesser degree Charles Woods had plenty of them. The pair missed nine combined tackles, with Jones' six a career-worst. The team captain also was involved in two touchdowns, including as the assigned coverage defender on a 12-yard touchdown to Colston Loveland in the third quarter. His lack of size and modest physicality was successfully tested by Chicago all night long. | Grade: D+

Safety: Colston Loveland also made plays when matched up with New England's safeties, including when he beat Jaylen Reed for a 22-yard gain — one of just five reps on the night for the late-August trade pickup. As for the starters, they mixed some good with some bad. Craig Woodson and Kevin Byard both missed one tackle each, and failed to make impact plays even when in decent position — including when Byard was inches from breaking up a 3rd-and-9 completion to Rome Odunze in the fourth quarter. It wasn't a terrible night for the position, but it didn't help lift the defense either. | Grade: C-

Special teams: New England has had better special teams days, with a 47-yard kickoff return (plus 5-yard penalty added on) as one of the worst plays of the season for the unit. In addition, it didn't recover an onside kick that seemed to at least have a chance of being reeled in. The coverage groups also missed a pair of tackles on the day. From the positive department: new dad Andy Borregales made a 47-yard field goal and hit all three of his point-after attempts. | Grade: C-

#54. Flávio Bolsonaro flees Brazil after presidential election loss, reports say

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Far-right senator on plane to US after missing deadline to hand over his passport since loss to Lula

The far-right senator Flávio Bolsonaro has reportedly fled Brazil and is on his way to the United States after losing Sunday’s presidential election to the leftwing incumbent, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.

A report by the news website Metrópoles, citing people close to the politician’s family, said he was en route to the US. “The plane is in flight,” Metrópoles reported, without making clear where it had departed from.

Bolsonaro allies told Metrópoles that he would land in Florida on Tuesday afternoon and planned to request political asylum. “The Donald Trump administration has been informed,” the website added.

Allies also confirmed to the Folha de S Paulo newspaper that he was on his way to the US, and said the escape plan had been “organized and enabled by a lawyer close to the Bolsonaro clan”.

“They say the flight should land in a few hours,” the newspaper added.

The news network CNN Brasil reported that Bolsonaro had fled to the US with his wife and two daughters in the early hours of this morning.

Lawmakers from Lula’s Worker’s party (PT) asked the supreme court to order Bolsonaro’s arrest and requested that his name be put on Interpol’s Red Notice list.

Bolsonaro had not been seen since Monday and missed a Tuesday-morning deadline to hand his passport to the federal police, fuelling rumours that he may have fled Brazil after losing on Sunday.

A report in the Brazilian newspaper O Globo said that during a meeting with friends and lawyers on Monday, the defeated candidate had appeared “possessed and made it clear he was afraid of being arrested”.

A supreme court justice cancelled Bolsonaro’s passport minutes after the result in a ruling related to a criminal case against the senator. He had until 11am to surrender the document to the federal police, but did not appear.

Bolsonaro has had Italian citizenship since 2023 and reportedly possesses a passport from the European country.

The Argentinian news website Infobae reported that on Monday night he reportedly fled to Argentina, where he was expected to board a private jet in Puerto Iguazú bound for the US, but the plane did not take off.

Bolsonaro, who has yet to concede defeat to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, posted a video on Monday in which, commenting on the cancellation of his passport, he said: “I never thought about leaving the country.”

One of the senator’s brothers, the former congressman Eduardo Bolsonaro, who is in self-imposed exile in the US, had said in an interview last Friday that his brother should leave Brazil if he lost.

The Brazilian newspaper Folha de S Paulo reported that allies in the Bolsonaro family’s inner circle had been told that he had indeed left for Argentina and intended to travel from there to the US.

As there is no record of him passing through any regular Brazilian immigration checkpoint, the federal police are trying to establish from Argentinian authorities whether there is any record of him entering the country.

The Argentinian channel Todo Noticias reported that the senator had reportedly crossed the border into Argentina on foot and abandoned plans to board the plane once news of his movements leaked.

Bolsonaro was told to surrender his passport over a criminal case in which the federal police are investigating him for possible involvement in a money-laundering scheme worth about £680m ($905m) involving betting companies.

The senator’s possible flight makes the future of the far right after the presidential election even more uncertain. The family patriarch, Jair Bolsonaro, is under house arrest for attempting a coup, and one of his sons is in the US and the whereabouts of another unclear.

Eduardo Bolsonaro has an outstanding arrest warrant against him in Brazil, after being sentenced to four years in prison for trying to interfere in his father’s trial by lobbying Donald Trump to impose sanctions on the justices hearing the case.

Political scientist Magna Inácio, a professor at the Federal University of Minas Gerais, believes a power vacuum may now emerge within the right.

“The resistance of Brazilian voters to handing victory to this more extremist camp sends a signal that the right leadership is now up for grabs,” she said.

Since Jair Bolsonaro, then a career congressman with few laws to his name, surprisingly won the presidency in 2018, there has effectively been no right wing in Brazil, only the far-right Bolsonarismo movement.

Camila Rocha, a political scientist who studies Brazil’s new right, believes the fact that three Bolsonaros are now potentially out of the picture could trigger a “shift in power” away from blood ties.

“I think there is a tendency for power to move away from the sons,” said Rocha, noting that before the election, polls showed Jair Bolsonaro supporters preferred other potential candidates over his eldest son, including Jair Bolsonaro’s wife, Michelle Bolsonaro, who was recently elected senator, and the São Paulo governor, Tarcísio de Freitas.

Flávio Bolsonaro reportedly did not want to run because he thought a presidential campaign would turn his life into “hell”, and believed his re-election to the senate was assured. But with Eduardo, his father’s “plan A”, now living in the US, the senator was chosen to run.

Most observers agree that Flávio lacks his father’s charisma. He had a 24-year political career in which he passed very few laws as a state legislator and senator, and the only previous time he ran for executive office was for mayor of Rio de Janeiro in 2016, when he fainted during a televised debate and finished fourth.

Even in a family frequently embroiled in legal trouble he stood out, facing longstanding criminal allegations, including suspicions that he embezzled part of his staff’s salaries, as well as more recent accusations.

Even so, 48.21% of Brazilians voted for Flávio Bolsonaro on Sunday, which many analysts see as both an anti-Lula vote and a vote for whomever Jair Bolsonaro had chosen.

Inácio believes the struggle over the Bolsonaro legacy will also play out among family members who remain in Brazil, including Michelle and Bolsonaro’s other political sons, the senator-elect Carlos Bolsonaro and congressman-elect Jair Renan Bolsonaro, who publicly do not get along with their stepmother.

“Michelle clearly has a political project of her own, but she is likely to face strong resistance from the two stepsons who are in congress. It is only a matter of time before tensions between them grow,” said the political scientist.

#3148. Grading the trade: Colts deal Quenton Nelson, who could be major upgrade for Seahawks

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The NFL trade deadline is still two weeks away, but the deals are starting to roll.

The Colts sent offensive guard Quenton Nelson, an eight-time Pro Bowler, to the Seattle Seahawks in exchange for a third-round draft pick in 2027 and a fifth-rounder in 2028.

The trade has major implications for both teams and could signal more future activity from each club. It also takes one of the best blocking pieces off the market for now.

Who got the better end of the trade? Let's dive in.

The firing of general manager Chris Ballard was the first indicator that the Colts' mentality had shifted this season. At 2-2, the team was still very much in the thick of things. After three straight losses, the Colts can't be viewed as contenders, especially as head coach Shane Steichen is having to answer questions about QB Daniel Jones' job security as the team's starter.

So trading away assets feels like a reasonable approach, especially for an expiring-contract player who's on the back half of his career such as Nelson. But on the other hand, Nelson was also a lifelong Colt who was a massive favorite of late team owner Jim Irsay and still one of the team's most dependable, rock-steady players and leaders.

Trading Nelson is an indication that no Colt should feel untouchable with Ed Dodds replacing Ballard (for now) and Carlie Irsay-Gordon taking over the team's operations from her father. Irsay-Gordon has routinely been on broadcasts doing an audit (or a triage?) of the team's game-day operations and clearly is taking her role seriously.

The Colts have other players who are far less tethered to the organization who also soon could be moved. Lacking a first-round draft pick next year from the Sauce Gardner trade, the Colts are trying to recoup as much draft-pick value as possible before the spring. With Jones' struggles, along with the Colts' apparent lack of trust in Anthony Richardson Sr., the team almost certainly will be going QB hunting in the offseason.

The return in value might feel low for a player of Nelson's talent and reputation. But given his age (turning 31 in March), contract status and positional value, the price feels adequate. For comparison, the Chiefs traded a 32-year-old Joe Thuney — in the final year of an expensive deal — to the Bears for a 2026 fourth-round pick in March 2025. Apples to apples, the Colts got plenty more in return.

The Colts were slated to have more than $60 million in salary cap space prior to the Nelson deal, so this opens them up to be even bigger players in free agency. Trading off more assets and dealing with Jones' contract in the offseason figures to open up even more space and flexibility.

It also indicates the depth of the rebuild facing Irsay-Gordon and whoever lands the full-time GM job. You could argue that after the Nelson trade, Jonathan Taylor is the only elite offensive player remaining — and Taylor's name has come up in trade rumors. (Just ask Taylor's wife.) Indy's defense might also lose a few pieces in the coming weeks.

Dealing Nelson makes financial sense. It's probably the right thing to do, considering the circumstances. But it's also a painful loss that opens up old wounds from the team's recent missteps, and those don't always heal quickly.

Guard Quenton Nelson should upgrade the Seahawks' offensive line.

The Seahawks' dominant victory over the Chiefs Sunday night was a good reminder of just how tough Seattle can still be. The Nelson trade figures to only improve their chances of winning a second straight Super Bowl.

That's, of course, far from a given. But with a dominant defense and an offense that has been incomplete to this point, adding known quantities at needed positions feels like a savvy stroke by GM John Schneider.

Nelson also embodies the toughness that head coach Mike Macdonald wants coursing through his roster's veins. There's also an argument to be made that Nelson joining an established contender could light a fire under him. The Colts have gone 31-43-1 over the past five seasons, including this one, and haven't made the postseason since the 2020 campaign.

Generally speaking, Nelson is probably a more efficient run blocker than pass protector, although it's hard to say that he's anything less than above average in the latter department. He hasn't been credited with allowing a sack since Week 14 of last season, per PFF, and has allowed a mere six pressures all season.

Nelson has also continued being the road grader in the run game he had been for years.

The Seahawks have trotted out multiple right guards this season, and it's been a clear sore spot on offense. Neither Christian Haynes nor Anthony Bradford claimed the job, necessitating the Nelson trade.

Nelson has played every snap of his NFL career at left guard. An easy fix would be to move Grey Zabel to the right side, which is where he played in college. That maneuver could actually upgrade both guard spots and have a cumulative effect for what's been an inconsistent run game to date this season.

The Seahawks have now traded away their original third-, fourth-, sixth- and seventh-round picks in 2027, as well as their fifth- and seventh-rounders in 2028. On the surface, that might look sparse. But the Seahawks are slated to receive four mid-round compensatory picks next spring, and they'll receive a third-round pick for executive Nolan Teasley being named Vikings general manager.

That makes the cost of the Nelson trade more palatable. Plus, when you consider that there reportedly were multiple suitors for Nelson, chalk it up as a win the Colts didn't hold out for a second-round pick in return.

Given the stage of his career and his contract status, this could end up being a fairly pricy rental — and that potential cost must be baked into the team's grade. But if Nelson plays to his expected level, or even above the level he's played at this season, and the Seahawks remain a title contender, it would be hard to label this as anything but a win.

It's hard to knock a team for a go-for-it move that appears to be a great fit, both on a football and a cultural level.

#3694. CrowdStrike vs. Fastly: Which Technology Stock Is a Better Buy in 2026?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The cybersecurity and edge computing sectors remain vital as digital transformation accelerates across global enterprises. Choosing between CrowdStrike (CRWD +2.36%) and Fastly (FSLY +6.87%) requires balancing high-octane growth against valuation and operational risks.

CrowdStrike focuses on cloud-native security, protecting endpoints and identities through its unified platform. Fastly operates in the edge cloud space, helping developers build and secure fast digital experiences closer to the end user. Both companies are prominent technology names that cater to the modern enterprise's need for speed and safety.

CrowdStrike sells its AI-native Falcon platform to secure endpoints, cloud workloads, and data for a diverse group of global organizations. It remains a prominent name among tech stocks and maintains a strategic partnership with Nvidia (NVDA +0.90%). Following a content configuration incident in mid-2024, the company implemented customer commitment packages and subscription extensions to mitigate contract terminations.

In the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $4.8 billion, representing growth of approximately 21.7% compared with the prior fiscal year. Despite this top-line expansion, the company reported a net loss of roughly $162.5 million. This reflects a wider net margin of negative 3.4% compared to the negative 0.5% reported previously, as the business invested in customer remediation.

As of its January 2026 balance sheet, the current ratio was close to 1.8x, meaning the company's short-term assets cover its short-term liabilities. The debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x, indicating that total debt, which includes short-term and long-term obligations, is low relative to shareholder equity. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 68% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Fastly provides an edge cloud platform that enables businesses to deliver fast and secure digital experiences by processing data closer to users. Its customer base is concentrated, with the ten largest customers accounting for a significant portion of revenue, including TikTok and Jio Platforms. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business since it increases dependency on a small number of high-volume users.

In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue reached approximately $624.0 million, a 14.8% increase over the previous year. The company recorded a net loss of nearly $121.7 million during this period. However, the net margin improved to negative 19.5% from the negative 29.1% reported in the prior fiscal year, showing progress toward narrowing losses as the platform scales.

According to its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio was roughly 2.6x, indicating a strong ability to meet short-term financial obligations with its current assets. The debt-to-equity ratio was approximately 0.5x, showing that total debt is equivalent to half of the company's shareholder equity. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 124.2% of operating cash flow, meaning reported cash generation is heavily inflated by this non-cash add-back.

CrowdStrike faces ongoing business and reputational damage following the technical incident on July 19, 2024. The company must navigate an intense competitive landscape from rivals like Microsoft (MSFT -0.24%) while managing reliance on Amazon (AMZN +1.61%) for infrastructure. Furthermore, it is managing ongoing securities litigation related to the incident and a complex global regulatory environment.

Fastly deals with significant revenue concentration among a small group of customers, which makes it vulnerable to shifts in usage or restructuring. The company faces stiff competition in the edge computing market from legacy providers and specialized rivals like Cloudflare (NET -1.22%). Operational risks include a dependency on a limited number of suppliers and ongoing securities class action litigation.

Fastly appears to be the more affordable option based on both sales and earnings multiples, while CrowdStrike commands a significant premium for its market leadership.

I'd go with CrowdStrike. Fastly has turned a corner and deserves more credit than it gets, but when I think about which business I want to own for the next decade, the answer is clear.

Every enterprise deploying AI creates new security vulnerabilities, and CrowdStrike keeps winning that business at scale. It delivered what its CEO called the best quarter in company history in Q2 fiscal 2027. Free cash flow hit a record, platform adoption accelerated sharply, and the company raised its full-year outlook substantially. The company has already signaled a record Q3 pipeline, which sets up the Dec. 1 report as one to watch.

Fastly posted record revenue and gross margins in Q2 2026 and raised its full-year guidance, with security revenue leading the way. Profitability is still a work in progress, but the trajectory is looking good.

But CrowdStrike is already the dominant platform in its category, profitable, and accelerating. I'd much rather own the established leader in such an important market than wait for a turnaround to fully play out.
