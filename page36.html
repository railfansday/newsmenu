#362. Parents inflicted fatal physical and sexual abuse on their baby, Warwick court hears

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Michal Stasiak and Kinga Wydrzynska pleaded not guilty to 11 counts relating to death of 13-month-old Isabella Stasiak

A 13-month-old girl was subjected to physical and sexual abuse at the hands of her parents before she was fatally injured by her mother, a court has heard.

Michal Stasiak, 28, and Kinga Wydrzynska, 26, appeared at Warwick crown court on Tuesday, where they pleaded not guilty to 11 counts related to the death of Isabella Stasiak.

Wydrzynska is charged with Isabella’s murder, while both parents have been charged with one count of causing or allowing the death of a child, four counts of causing or allowing serious physical harm to a child, two counts related to the sexual assault of a child and three counts of child cruelty.

The court heard both parents were known to social services and the police.

Isabella died in hospital after sustaining a series of injuries including a fractured skull and ribs, a broken arm and a burn to her entire face, the court was told.

In her opening statement to the court on Tuesday, Annabel Darlow KC said emergency services were called to the Wydrzynska’s home in the early hours of 7 January 2025 where Isabella was found “grey and lifeless”.

Medical experts later concluded Isabella died “because she suffered injuries consistent with a forceful direct impact to her head against a hard surface”, Darlow told the court.

Darlow said Wydrzynska was the only adult present at the home when Isabella was found by police and the injuries were the “devastating culmination of systematic physical abuse”.

“The prosecution say, by the time Isabella died, there was an entrenched pattern of abuse, neglect and mistreatment of the little girl of which both parents were entirely aware,” Darlow told the court.

“Before she died, Isabella was subjected to physical and sexual abuse at the hands of both her mother and father,” Darlow added. “Neither defendant has ever provided any explanation for those injuries to their daughter.”

Jurors were told Isabella was born in December 2023 and immediately taken into the care of social services. After being placed in emergency accommodation, Isabella was looked after by foster parents for four months before she was returned to her parents in April 2024 – nine months before her death, the court was told.

Members of the jury were also told Stasiak was arrested by police for common assault after a report was made by Wydrzynska on 14 December 2024, less than a month before Isabella’s death.

Wydrzynska made repeated attempts to withdraw the report, jurors were told. However, Stasiak was placed on police bail and prohibited from living at the family home or contacting Wydrzynska.

In addition, the court was told police officers had attended the family home after a complaint was made by a neighbour in October 2024.

Officers attended the property and were only responded to when the police threatened to force entry, Darlow said. Officers spoke to the parents and saw Isabella and “did not have any immediate safeguarding concerns” after the visit, the court was told.

#3532. Is SpaceX Stock a Buy After Its Latest Earnings Report?

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

SpaceX (SPCX +1.14%) posted its third-quarter earnings report on Oct. 20. The aerospace and AI company's revenue surged 161% year over year to $14.6 billion, beating analysts' estimates by $1.6 billion. Its EPS of $0.35 also cleared the consensus forecast by $0.19, marking a major improvement from its net loss of $0.36 per share a year earlier. Those headline numbers were impressive, but is SpaceX's stock worth buying in this choppy market?

In the first nine months of 2026, SpaceX generated 47% of its revenue from its connectivity (Starlink) segment, 40% from its SI ("super intelligence") segment, and 12% from its space segment. On a generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) basis, Starlink is SpaceX's only profitable business -- and it supports the expansion of its unprofitable space and SI businesses. But if we only look at its adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) -- which generously excludes interest payments on its debt, depreciation on its hardware, and stock-based compensation -- its margins look much better.

Data source: SpaceX. YOY = Year-over-year.

The SI segment -- which houses xAI, Grok, X, Cursor, and its other AI assets -- continues to grow as it secures big infrastructure deals with Anthropic, Alphabet's (GOOG +0.84%) (GOOGL +1.19%) Google, and other big companies. The space segment is growing more slowly, but Starship -- the largest rocket ever built -- should ignite its growth again.

For 2026, analysts expect SpaceX's revenue to surge 161% to $48.8 billion with a GAAP net profit of $3.6 billion -- or $0.21 per share. For 2027, they expect its revenue to jump 264% to $177.6 billion as its net income soars 1,356% to $51.8 billion, or $3.87 per share. Elon Musk claims SpaceX's annual revenue could surpass $1 trillion by 2030.

SpaceX's stock doesn't seem expensive if you expect it to hit those targets. At $150, it trades 11% above its IPO price of $135 and at 38 times next year's earnings. #5458. EIA: Renewables could overtake natural gas output in 2027

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Renewable energy is still outpacing fossil fuels as the leading new sources of electricity on the U.S. grid. Electrical generation from renewable energy sources has increased by 10%, or more than 43 GW, in the first eight months of 2026, according to an analysis of Energy Information Administration (EIA) data by the SUN DAY Campaign.

Renewables are expected to account for more than 40% of the United States’ total electrical output over the next year. At the start of September, renewable energy accounted for 34.5% of utility-scale generation in the country.

Utility-scale and small-scale solar are the two fastest growing renewable energy sources being tied to the grid. Compared to the first eight months of 2025, utility-scale solar projects increased by 20%, and smaller systems increased by 12% in 2026. Between September 2025 and August 2026 utility-scale solar increased by 27.1 GW and small-scale solar 6.5-GW. Battery energy storage systems’ capacity increased by 17.6 GW.

New natural gas and nuclear power only grew by 2.6% an 1.3%. Coal’s electrical output decreased by more than 8%. If new renewable energy proliferation continues at its expected pace, EIA is predicting that it could overtake natural gas output by summer 2027.

“Each new issue of EIA’s ‘Electric Power Monthly’ reinforces the theme that the growth of solar, wind and battery storage has become unstoppable,” said Ken Bossong, executive director of the SUN DAY Campaign. “Meanwhile, the share of the nation’s electrical generation and generating capacity provided by fossil fuels and nuclear continues to shrink.”

An inquiry into the deaths of 33 detainees in Nigeria's Minna state has found they died as a result of severe overcrowding, poor ventilation and failure by officers to respond to distress calls.

Forensic and medical evidence showed the "entirely preventable" deaths had resulted from acute respiratory failure linked to prolonged confinement, aggravated by heat stress and dehydration.

The evidence did not support initial claims of a disease outbreak.

The detainees were found dead on 17 September while being held by the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps on suspicion of illegal mining. The investigation found that the mining sites had been licensed.

'I have never felt heat like that' - a survivor on watching 33 cellmates die in Nigeria

Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo said those found responsible for the deaths would face the full force of the law.

He added that the detention hall had been cleaned before investigators arrived, raising concerns about possible attempts to compromise evidence.

Implicated officers will face suspension and the report would be forwarded to the attorney general of the federation for further legal action, Tunji-Ojo said.

Family members reclaimed the bodies of their relatives from Minna's General Hospital last month

The investigating committee found that the raid on the miners had been conducted without an approved operational order, proper planning or consultation with the Mining Marshals Unit.

It said that 50 detainees had been held in a cell measuring about 20 sq m, with no running water or functioning toilet, and inadequate food, drinking water and airflow.

Adults and minors were detained together, and some detainees were held beyond lawful detention periods.

Officers reportedly ignored repeated calls for help, despite a medical worker having raised concerns about the conditions.

"Detainees repeatedly banged on cell doors and walls, shouted for assistance and pleaded for water," said the head of the investigating team, Jonathan Kure.

"These distress calls were heard by officers assigned to nearby duties... but were not acted upon."

Kure said the detention conditions amounted to cruel, inhuman and degrading treatment.

"The tragedy demonstrates the consequences of systemic disregard for detention standards, supervisory responsibilities and the welfare of persons in custody," he added.
