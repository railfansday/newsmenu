#1655. Michael Carrick insists he was NOT waving an emotional farewell to fans after Man United's humiliating 3-0 thrashing by Bournemouth - but admits he is unsure about his future

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By CHRIS WHEELER, NORTHERN SPORTS WRITER and LUKE POWER, SPORTS REPORTER

Published: 16:43 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 19:46 GMT, 25 October 2026

Michael Carrick's future as Manchester United head coach is hanging by a thread after defeat to Bournemouth at Old Trafford pushed him closer to the brink.

It was the sixth time Carrick’s team have lost in 12 games this season as United sank to 16th place in the Premier League table, piling more pressure on the club to appoint a third manager this year.

Asked if he expects to be sacked, Carrick said: ‘I totally understand the situation. I'm not expecting anything either way.

‘I’m carrying on as normal. I love this place. I'll always do what I feel is going to be the best for the football club and I won’t change that now, whatever decision is made.

‘It's a tough situation, there's no hiding from it. What it looks like and what's next I really don't know. I love what I do and I feel the responsibility. We'll have to wait and see.

‘Of course, I take responsibility. I respect the position that I'm in, and the responsibility is mine. What else can I say really right now?

‘I've tried to do everything I can to not be in this position, of course I have. For a number of reasons, we've not been able to stop that happening to the position we're in right now.

Michael Carrick insists he was not saying farewell to Manchester United fans as he did a lap of Old Trafford after their 3-0 defeat by Bournemouth

‘I've been at the club a long time. It hurts an awful to see us going through this at this moment in time. The situation is where we are and we haven't had results or performances that have been good enough.’

Some bookmakers last night suspended betting on Carrick being sacked with Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann - both currently out of work – among the frontrunners to replace him.

Carrick said he had not spoken to chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox over his future. ‘No, just briefly they came down like they always do after a game, but nothing out of the ordinary,’ he added.

The United fans showed their support for Carrick, while turning on the club’s ownership, Wilcox and the players as United sank to a 3-0 defeat.

The 45-year-old looked visibly emotional as he walked around the pitch at the end thanking the fans and placing a hand over his heart.

‘It wasn't a farewell from me,’ Carrick insisted. ‘I probably didn't really expect that off the supporters, if I'm honest, but I've always felt the support and that connection with them.

‘It will take a bit of time to digest, but I certainly appreciate their support. It's difficult for me to have a day like this at this club and feel the emotion.

‘I'm feeling it for my players, they're going through a tough spell. There's a lot of emotion in the changing room right now, there's a lot of the boys quite hurt, to be fair.

‘It's not like they don't care or they want to be in this position. The ones I've spoken to, it’s affecting them a lot.’

Former United defender Gary Neville had a different view, however, telling Sky Sports: ‘They’ve downed tools and they’ve done this a number of times,’ said Neville. ‘They decide when to switch it on and off like a tap.’

Defender Harry Maguire - standing in as captain with Bruno Fernandes dropped following his criticism of the manager's tactics - spoke out in defence of Carrick at full time, saying: 'At the moment we're not doing enough as players. Michael turned our season on its head last year and we played brilliant football and got brilliant results. This season we haven't performed well enough as individuals.

'We have got to find something, question yourself, look in the mirror, that's the most important thing - think what you can do more and contribute to the team and if we do that we'll get somewhere.'

Former United defender Ashley Young, who won the 2012-13 Premier League title with the club, accused the players of 'giving up'.

'There have been different managers [in recent seasons] but it's the same players,' he said on Sky Sports. 'There have been a few changes in players but they're just not showing anything. You could tell today that it was like they'd given up - they're not running for this manager and they're not wanting to fight.

'The fans deserve so much more, you can tell they want more and they know he's been let down by those players.'

Jamie Carragher said that Carrick's position was 'untenable' at full time but he has also received widespread sympathy, with many considering the owners and top brass at the club to be culpable.

They recruited no defenders over the summer and are now shipping goals for fun - the only clean sheet they have kept all season was against Azerbaijani minnows Sabah.

Bournemouth's Evanilson curled in from long range to complete an afternoon of humiliation

The long-term decision-making of owners Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the Glazer family - as well as  Berrada and Wilcox - has been called into question.

That was the subject of an explosive rant by Lisandro Martinez after the recent 2-0 loss to Atletico Madrid. The defender said earlier this month: 'I expect more (investment) 100 per cent. I expect more from our technical director. I have to be honest.

'As players we have to do more but it is also important when you make a strong squad to be ready for this kind of night.

'I have to control myself a lot but sometimes it's a reflection of the mirror of how our club is at the moment.

'We did a fantastic season last season and now we expect more from the top; more players to compete because many teams bring many players as well.'

But Carrick's authority over the players has been called into question after that swipe and the invective by Fernandes this week.

Club captain Fernandes was dropped after saying in the aftermatch of the Como drubbing: 'It's a decision from the staff to not press man to man and sometimes leave space behind and try to get the ball, wait for them, but sometimes when you give space to these teams they get confidence.'

Asked if he meant that Carrick got his tactics wrong, Fernandes added: ‘That's on the manager and staff to say, not me.

‘Sometimes you have to be more brave under pressure, take the risk of pressing high and making them uncomfortable. As you saw with Como, the centre back was coming to mark me in my own half. We need to do that as a team but we were unable to.'

Wayne Rooney lambasted Manchester United's players in the wake of such comments, describing the group as 'selfish' and 'like babies' amid a dismal run of form.

He told the United Stand podcast: 'These players, they're selfish. They don't care about their team-mates, don't care about the manager, the staff or the fans, they care about themselves. If they're not getting what's right for them, they're like babies and just throw their toys out of the pram and go and deflect the blame on to anyone else but themselves.'

Carrick admitted ahead of the Bournemouth match that the club has 'lost our way' in a five-minute speech.

He took over from Ruben Amorim on an interim basis last season, steering the club to third and Champions League qualification, but has won just two of 12 games this campaign.

#1728. This was the end for Michael Carrick after his gang of the damned were relentlessly dismantled 3-0 by Bournemouth... he will be the next to walk into Man United's endless winter, writes OLIVER HOLT

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 16:39 GMT, 25 October 2026 | Updated: 18:10 GMT, 25 October 2026

The yellowed leaves fell from the trees and danced and skittered across the forecourt at Old Trafford. Squalls battered the old stadium and Manchester United supporters bowed their heads into the winds of Storm Boelo. These are the rites of autumn and, in this part of the city, they double as chronicles of deaths foretold.

In the great churn of unfortunates who have occupied the manager’s position at United since Sir Alex Ferguson’s retirement in May 2013, October, November and December is the favoured sacking season. Jose Mourinho, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer and Erik ten Hag have all met their fates as the branches have been stripped bare.

Now, it was Michael Carrick’s turn. This was the end for him. There can be little doubt about that. The United boss, a favourite son of the fans here when he was a player, a decent, modest, self-effacing man, stood on the touchline in a coat of funereal black, his arms folded in front of him, his face set in a desperate grimace as he watched his gang of the damned being relentlessly dismantled by Bournemouth.

It was hard to watch, just as it had been hard to watch United humiliated by Como in the Champions League last week. The truth was obvious: this United is a rabble, a loose collection of over-entitled, overpaid, over-promoted and overdone losers who have done their bit to throw their boss under the bus.

It is easy to say but the way some of them played against Marco Rose’s intelligent, well-drilled, energetic team, they do not deserve to wear the United shirt. They are not fit for it. They are an embarrassment to it. This comprehensive 3-0 defeat left them in 16th place in the Premier League, one point above the relegation zone. It is where they deserve to be.

Carrick cannot survive this and, particularly, he cannot survive the manner of recent defeats. This was the end for him and it ought to be the end for some of the hapless club apparatchiks who let him down in the summer with their hopeless recruitment in the transfer window.

Manchester United were hammered 3-0 at home by Bournemouth on another dismal day

The damaging defeat leaves Red Devils boss Michael Carrick on the brink of losing his job

The club is in chaos, on and off the field. Bruno Fernandes, the club captain, was dropped after criticising Carrick in midweek. Lisandro Martinez, who was disciplined for criticising the club’s owners, did return against Bournemouth but only in the second half. He is a liability anyway.

This is not all on Carrick, though. In fact, in the list of those responsible for this shambles, he is low on the list. The Glazers, who have pillaged the club, are top of the list. Sir Jim Ratcliffe, the minority owner, who has overseen the descent into this mayhem, is next.

This is a club riven by dissent from top to bottom, a club that has declared war on its most loyal supporters by trying to accelerate the move away from season ticket holders towards tourists and day-trippers because money is king for this regime and season-ticket holders do not spend enough on match days.

It was little wonder that, as Bournemouth scored goal after goal, the supporters turned their ire on the owners and their much-trumpeted plans for a move away from Old Trafford. How empty and futile those plans seem when the club is in this state. ‘You can stick your new stadium up your arse,’ the Stretford End sang.

And so Carrick stood there in that black coat, arms folded, taking the brickbats. ‘You’re getting sacked in the morning,’ the Bournemouth fans yelled at him. Carrick and United are easy meat. ‘You’re going down with the City,’ they chanted delightedly as United melted away in front of them.

The first drama of the day had come before kick-off with the publication of the United teamsheet. It showed that Fernandes, the club captain and the man who has carried the team through many of the lean years at Old Trafford, had been dropped. Harry Maguire was named as skipper in his stead.

After his criticism of Carrick’s tactics following the humiliating Champions League defeat to Como last week, it was hardly a surprise that Fernandes paid the penalty. Carrick would have looked weak if he had started him. Carrick may be standing on a burning deck but he was still trying to reassert his authority amid the flames.

The chants against the Glazers and Ratcliffe started soon after kick-off. So did the Bournemouth chances. Rayan headed just wide from Bournemouth’s first corner and burst through on a breakaway only to drive his shot straight at a defender as United retreated in disarray.

Bournemouth were superb, yet United were woeful and another manager looks set to leave

The defeat leaves United just above the relegation zone with seven points from eight games

United's fans turned on the club's hierarchy as Bournemouth extended their lead (pictured - CEO Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox),  but continued to support Carrick

Bournemouth failed to capitalise as the opportunities kept coming. When Marcus Tavernier escaped yet again down the left wing, he drove his cross straight into the arms of Senne Lammens but as United’s defenders remonstrated with each other, the Stretford End lost its patience and roared out its dismay at what was happening.

Ayden Heaven was lucky to escape with a yellow card for launching himself into the air and into a double-footed tackle that Evanilson skipped out of rather than risk serious injury. Then, from the resulting free kick eight minutes before half-time, Bournemouth took the lead that their play had deserved.

Justin Kluivert swung the ball to the back post and sailed over the massed ranks of the United defence to where Rayan was ghosting in. Rayan was unmarked. He met the ball on the volley with his left foot and rammed it past Lammens. United tried to complain about a foul but they had no grounds.

Five minutes later, Bournemouth doubled their lead. Leny Yoro slipped under pressure on the right side of the United box and Adrien Truffert pounced on the mistake and squared the ball to Tavernier. Tavernier completed the formalities, turning the ball past Lammens from close range.

Old Trafford erupted in a fusillade of boos. A black flag was lowered from the back of the first tier of the Stretford End that said, in red letters, ‘Standards’. The outline of the faces of Roy Keane, Eric Cantona and Bryan Robson were below it, symbols of better times and better players. ‘You’re getting sacked in the morning,’ the Bournemouth fans sang at Carrick.

United were dire. Djordje Petrovic did save well from Bryan Mbeumo just before the interval but it was a rare moment of threat. There were more groans from the crowd when Mason Mount’s corner failed miserably to clear the first defender. It was another symbol of a miserable half for the home team.

Things were still not bad enough to prompt Carrick to call on Fernandes. Maybe nothing would have been. He did bring Matheus Cunha on for Youri Tielemans but Evanilson had a goal disallowed for offside seconds after the restart. Bournemouth should have been three up a couple of minutes later. Rayan, one-on-one with Lammens, hit the ball too close to the goalkeeper.

But United barely posed a threat. They hardly ever do. In seven of their 11 games this season United have failed to score more than once. United have only 10 goals in eight league outings now. They are toothless as well as gutless.

Carrick dropped Bruno Fernandes for the match and then left him on the bench throughout

Bournemouth were superb as they capped off one of the best weeks in the club's history

Bournemouth should have won by more but their third was a beauty. Twenty minutes from time, Lewis Cook played a ball down the line and Lammens hurtled out of his goal in a doomed attempt to try to get to it first.

Evanilson got to it first, took it round the goalkeeper and clipped the ball brilliantly into the empty goal from a tight angle. United’s humiliation was almost complete. In the dying minutes, the United fans chanted abuse at Ratcliffe’s henchmen Jason Wilcox, the director of football, and Omar Berrada, the chief executive.

But everyone knows that it will not be them who pay the price. In autumn’s parade of the damned, Carrick will be the next to walk into Manchester United’s endless winter.
