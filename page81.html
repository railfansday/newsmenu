Pokémon’s attempt at a MOBA is shutting down after just five years of service, the company announced today.

Pokémon UNITE launched in July 2021, available on both mobile and Nintendo Switch, and I genuinely enjoyed playing it at first. But a miserable solo ranked experience quickly turned me away, and it appears the game has stopped being profitable several years later.

The concept of the game was simple enough to attract casual players while iterating on the MOBA genre. You chose to play as a number of different Pokémon and could level up and evolve throughout a match, scoring points at various goals throughout the arena to defeat the opposing team.

“We offer our sincerest gratitude to the many Trainers who have played since service started for the Nintendo Switch version on July 21, 2021,” the announcement read. “We would like to express our thanks to everyone by holding a special event until service ends. The Pokémon UNITE Aeos Crown 2027 Tokyo competitive tournament (an event for Pokémon UNITE only) will also be held as previously announced. We hope you continue to enjoy Pokémon UNITE until the final day of service.”

That tournament takes place just two days before the game will shut down, leaving the winner with an actual physical crown and title for a game that will soon no longer exist. A wonderful collector’s item, I suppose?

The main issues that many players had with the game over the years were its pay-to-win monetization, like consistent releases of new Pokémon that felt powerful and must-buy to keep up with the meta. The matchmaking in ranked felt like an active punishment every time I opened up the game, so I quickly moved on to other things.

Galaxy's crypto trading volumes and lending business grew in the third quarter, while its expanding AI data center operations also added to earnings.

Galaxy Digital Galaxy Digital Holdings (GLXY) reported $323 million in net income for the third quarter of 2026, swinging back into profit after an $85 million loss in the previous quarter, as its crypto trading, lending, and AI data center businesses grew.

The Mike Novogratz-led firm posted $569 million in adjusted gross profit and $455 million in adjusted EBITDA for the three months ended Sept. 30, according to its earnings release on Tuesday. Both were up sharply from the second quarter, when adjusted gross profit stood at $43 million, and adjusted EBITDA was negative $77 million. Net income was lower than a year earlier, however, when Galaxy made $505 million.

Galaxy reported a 12% increase in digital asset trading volumes from the previous quarter, despite lower volumes across the broader spot market. Lending also grew, with the firm's average loan book reaching $1.8 billion, up 27%. Some of that demand came through its Galaxy Onchain Financing Rate product, which launched in July and draws on DeFi lending markets including Aave and Morpho, the firm said.

Adjusted gross profit from digital assets rose to $71 million from $66 million, although the division still lost $9 million on an adjusted EBITDA basis. On Tuesday's earnings call, management said the division would have been profitable without a one-time accounting adjustment during the quarter, but did not specify the size of the adjustment.

Galaxy's Treasury & Corporate business made a much larger contribution, recording $419 million in adjusted gross profit compared with a $42 million loss in the second quarter. Management said the gains came largely from higher valuations of its digital asset holdings and private investments, including positions in AI chip startup Etched and Solana Solana (SOL) treasury firm Forward Industries.

Galaxy said it had $10.4 billion in combined assets under management and assets under stake at the end of September, up 46% from the end of June.

Galaxy also announced Tuesday that its board had authorized another $200 million in Class A common stock repurchases on Oct. 26. The company completed its previous buyback program on Oct. 15, purchasing approximately 9.8 million shares under the $200 million authorization announced in February. The new program has no set end date, and Galaxy is under no obligation to buy back the full $200 million, the firm said.

Galaxy made $79 million in adjusted gross profit from data centers in the third quarter, nearly four times the $20 million recorded in the previous quarter. Adjusted EBITDA for the business came to $69 million.

The figures include the first full quarter of revenue from its Helios I Phase I lease in Texas, after Galaxy delivered all 133 MW of critical IT load to CoreWeave by the end of June.

Galaxy has also increased its total gross data center power pipeline to more than 5.7 GW. At Helios I Phase II, construction is continuing ahead of the first scheduled data hall deliveries in the second quarter of 2027, the firm said in the release.

During Tuesday's earnings call, Novogratz said Galaxy expects to exit 2027 with an annualized leasing revenue run rate of $800 million to $850 million as Helios I Phase II comes online. He also revealed that Galaxy had passed on a potential Helios II leasing deal over the summer after tightening power supplies and growing demand from major AI companies prompted it to reconsider the terms on offer.

"Galaxy delivered a strong third quarter, reflecting the strength of our established Digital Assets franchise, significant appreciation across our investments portfolio, and the growing contribution from our Data Centers business," Novogratz said in the release.

Launched in July 2021 for Switch before migrating to iOS and Android later that year, Pokémon Unite was billed as a friendlier take on the MOBA genre popularized by Dota 2 and League of Legends, thanks to its short matches (10 minutes) and simplified mechanics. Despite its initial popularity, the game is winding down and will go offline for good on March 23, 2027.

An End of Service notice on the game’s website reads,

“We offer our sincerest gratitude to the many Trainers who have played since service started for the Nintendo Switch version on July 21, 2021. We would like to express our thanks to everyone by holding a special event until service ends. The Pokémon Unite Aeos Crown 2027 Tokyo competitive tournament (an event for Pokémon Unite only) will also be held as previously announced. We hope you continue to enjoy Pokémon Unite until the final day of service. After service ends, the 'Pokémon Unite Game Content Guidelines for Online Platforms' will no longer be published or maintained, and we will no longer provide support regarding use of Pokémon Unite videos and images.”

As of today, in-game sales of Aeos gems and Unite Club memberships/renewals have been suspended. Existing Unite Club members will continue to use their benefits until the end of the membership period.

On November 12, an update will kick off the aforementioned final special event, where players can obtain items such as all Unite licenses and Holoware that have appeared in the game up to that point. A library feature will also be added, letting players view certain records and in-game content even after the game shuts down on March 23. Those who don’t download the library feature before the shutdown date will not be able to get it afterward.

Qualifiers for the Aeos Crown 2027 Tokyo Competitive tournament will take place as planned on December 5. After the game shuts down on March 23, players won't be able to log in to Pokémon Unite or play any battles. Only the library feature will remain accessible.

Battles play out with the spectacle that the Pokémon series is known for, and with so many abilities to choose from, no match feels the same as the last.
