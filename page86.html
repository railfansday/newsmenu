#5176. National Home Value Growth Accelerates Yet Continues To Trail Inflation

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

National home values posted modest gains in August, picking up speed from July, though price growth still failed to keep pace with inflation.

The value of single-family homes as measured by repeat transactions rose 2.1% year over year in August, up from a 1.9% annual increase the month before, according to data from the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Index released Tuesday.

The more robust growth came even as the median existing-home sales price slowed to a 1.6% annual gain in August.

However, while nominal home values accelerated nationally, they fell in real terms for the 15th consecutive month, as August's 3.4% inflation rate ran roughly 1.3 percentage points above the home value gain.

Across the 20 cities tracked by the index, Chicago once again took pole position, leading the U.S. for the sixth consecutive month with a 6.7% annual gain, down slightly from July. New York followed with a year-over-year increase of 6.2%. Cleveland took bronze, registering a gain of 4.3%.

"Seasonality continued to weigh on headline monthly results," says Rebecca Kaufman, associate director of commodities at S&P Dow Jones Indices. "On a non-seasonally adjusted basis, theU.S. National and 10-City Composite Indices fell 0.2% and 0.4%, respectively, while their seasonallyadjusted counterparts rose 0.3% and 0.4%. The divergence suggests that the typical post-summerslowdown is obscuring some underlying strength in home prices."

However, Kaufman concedes that affordability remains a challenge.

Referring to August data before mortgage rates crossed the 7% threshold, she notes: "With inflation at 3.4%, Brent crude near $100 per barrel and mortgage rates in the mid-to-high 6% range, households continue to face elevated costs alongside persistently high home prices."

The latest Case-Shiller report highlights a persistent regional divergence in home prices. Seattle posted the nation's steepest annual decline for the third month in a row, slipping 1.6%, followed by Las Vegas (-1.3%), and Denver (-0.8%).

"The years-long East-West divide persists, with six out of the eight Eastern metropolitan marketsrecording greater year-over-year changes in July versus June, compared with just two of the eightWestern metropolitan markets," says Kaufman.

As in June and July, a gap of nearly 9 percentage points separated the strongest housing market, Chicago, which was bolstered by tight resale supply and limited new construction, from the weakest, Seattle.

Looking ahead, Realtor.com® senior economist Anthony Smith reminds that the rate backdrop has become considerably more challenging since the end of August.

The 30-year fixed rate climbed to 7.40% in mid-October, the highest level of the year, before easing to 7.32% as of Oct. 22, after the Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate on Sept. 16 and September Consumer Price Index (CPI) rose to 3.7%.

Pending home sales plunged 7.3% in September to the lowest reading on record and were down 11.3% from a year ago, with declines in all four regions.

"With inventory at its highest level since 2019 and rates above 7%, buyers have more homes to choose from but less power to pay for them," says Smith. The Case-Shiller Index reports on a two-month delay and reflects a three-month moving average of home sales prices.

Homes usually go under contract a month or two before they close, so the August report primarily reflects purchase decisions made in late spring and early summer.

Although the Index's price data is delayed by several months, it is considered one of the best available measures of changing home values, because it is based on repeat transactions on the same properties.

Brighton & Hove Albion head coach Fabian Hurzeler has provided a fresh update on the fitness of his squad ahead of Wednesday’s EFL Cup fourth-round tie away against Manchester City.

The Seagulls are seeking to avoid a third successive game without a win or goal, after following up a goalless draw with Jablonec in the Conference League with a 2-0 Premier League defeat at Liverpool on Sunday.

Full-back Ferdi Kadioglu and midfield summer signing Chema Andres both picked up hip injuries in the loss at Anfield and Hurzeler has confirmed that both players will need to be assessed ahead of the trip to the Etihad Stadium.

“It's just a hit on the hips,” Hurzeler said at a press conference on Tuesday. “Of course, it's very painful, so it's something where both players have to deal with the pain.

“Pain for every player feels differently, so we have to see how they train today, if it's possible, if they can train.

“If yes, then of course there will be an option for tomorrow, but they definitely should be both available for the weekend.”

Versatile midfielder/full-back Wieffer has been sidelined since August with a knee injury, while winger Minteh is yet to play for the Seagulls this term since sustaining a serious leg injury in pre-season.

Brighton are preparing for back-to-back away games against Man City in the space of four days, with their EFL Cup tie followed by a Premier League battle with the table-toppers on Saturday.

“We experienced it a few years ago when we played twice against Chelsea,” said Hurzeler.  “It's about competing tomorrow then recovering, adjusting, adapting things, and competing again - it's really going from game to game.

“We don't focus on Saturday. We want to be the best-prepared team for Wednesday, and that's the main focus. We don't try to be distracted by the weekend game. We play a do-or-die game, so we are fully focused on this.”

Brighton have already prevailed on Manchester soil this season, coming from two goals down at half time to best Man United 3-2 in the EFL Cup third round last month.

However, the Seagulls have failed to win any of their previous 17 visits to Man City across all competitions (D3 L14), albeit drawing both of their last two in the Premier League.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

BYD’s big story in the last year or tear and a half has been its rapid growth outside of China. One of its big hubs and markets is now the gigantic market of Brazil, and things seem to be going well.

According to reporting out of Brazil, BYD is now aiming to double its annual vehicle production capacity at its factory in Brazil. Stamping, welding, and paint facilities are nearing completion of the initial plans, and now the company is aiming for a production capacity of 600,000 vehicles a year. (That’s a lot.)

According to Reuters, “BYD’s Brazil unit is on track to locally source more than 50% of its components, looking to exceed the ​required minimum as it enters 2027, which will satisfy regulatory demands and help it begin regional ​exports, Senior Vice President Alexandre Baldy said in an interview.” The company is apparently keen on being seen as a local automobile producer in Brazil. “Local ​industry groups have complained that BYD, which opened its newest plant in South America’s largest car market ​a year ago, relied on imports and temporarily low tariffs.” BYD is intent on changing the narrative (and the reality) in the 7th most populated country in the world.

BYD aims to become the top selling automaker in Brazil by 2030. That’s quite a noteworthy goal! Looking a little more broadly, one has to think that BYD has its eyes set on becoming the #1 automaker in the world. It dominates the EV market in China, it only sells plugin vehicles, and it is rapidly expanding production, sales, and service across the globe. Is it the company most likely to lead the auto market in the 2030s?

By the end of 2026, BYD’s goal in Brazil is to be delivering 25,000 vehicles a month. However, that’s still less than what it’s been seeing in consumer demand there! “Our ‌demand ⁠has averaged around 30,000 cars per month, meaning we are missing out on roughly 5,000 to 6,000 additional invoiced sales,” BYD Senior Vice President Alexandre Baldy said. BYD also aims to support the significant markets of Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Uruguay, and others from its Brazil factory down the line.

#295. I tried the best smart rings of 2026 — the Oura Ring 5 is the best you can buy

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Gone are the days of clip-on pedometers. Now, it's all about tech wearables that maximize your physical potential with round-the-clock vitals monitoring — but I'm not talking about an Apple Watch or Fitbit. The next evolution of fitness trackers is here, and it's the smart ring: a smaller, more discreet way to track sleep, stress, recovery, and heart health.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

Smart rings monitor your health in a tiny, inconspicuous package. At first glance, they look like any other piece of jewelry, a simple gold or silver ring. But with tiny sensors, these rings track biometrics through your finger. Yes, they'll track your step count and calories burned, but more advanced technology allows them to track sleep and stress levels, all detected through heart rate and variability.

After extensive testing — including some epic marathon training — we selected the best smart rings of the year so far. This year, Oura debuted the Oura Ring 5, which is an exceptional ring for comfort, design, and overall health tracking. While it takes the top spot in our guide, we tried plenty more options.

This guide compares the top options for fitness-minded tech users who want a low-profile health tracker. To compare the rings, we dug into what actually separates these rings: design, battery life, health features, price, subscription costs, and device compatibility.

If you want a holistic view of your health that includes daily metrics alongside long-term analysis of stress resilience, chronotype, and cardiovascular health, the Oura Ring 5 delivers it all. As the latest smart ring from Oura, it now comes in an exceptionally thin package that not only makes it more comfortable for round-the-clock wear but also really looks like jewelry rather than a piece of tech.

Oura continues to have the best app, which is well-designed and organized for easy navigation, so you can glance at your data quickly. Its AI algorithms synthesize data into quick but thorough summaries so you know your readiness for the day. It is the most expensive ring we tested and requires a subscription fee of $5.99 monthly or $69.99 annually, so you must be prepared to make a long-term investment with the Oura Ring.

Oura outdid itself with the Oura Ring 5. Before its release, we rated the Oura Ring 4, the best smart ring available, but the Oura Ring 5 blows it out of the water. It's the most comfortable, accurate, and well-designed smart ring we've tested.

The new Oura Ring 5 still offers the same biometric data and in-app experience, but now, it's 40 percent smaller. It's thinner and lighter, meaning even the battery on the Oura Ring 5 had to shrink to reach its new size. But luckily, it didn't sacrifice battery life as it still lasts six to seven days on a single charge.

The ring contains a red and infrared LED, a green and infrared LED, a movement-tracking accelerometer, and digital temperature monitors that all work together to calculate your biometrics, which are presented in the app. With a slim fit and rounded edges, it looks like a classic band that blends right into your wardrobe but remains comfortable without digging in or pinching your finger. Unfortunately, Oura charges extra for certain finishes (you'll pay $100 more if you want gold instead of silver).

All of the sensors produce accurate data. I wore it simultaneously with an Apple Watch; its step count and heart rate were always on par. It also seamlessly connects with the Apple Watch. Tracking activity directly in the Oura app requires you to pull out your smartphone; however, when paired with the Apple Watch, I could start my workout on the watch, and it would automatically upload to the Oura app. And if I ever forgot to start a workout, Oura would have suggested activity time to add to my log.

While Oura is great at providing a quick snapshot of your data in the 'Today' tab of the app, like a readiness score calculated from sleep, heart rate, and the previous day's activities, it's also invested in long-term health data. Under the 'My Health' tab, it calculates stress resilience, cardiovascular age and capacity, and chronotype for deeper insights into your overall health.

In addition to being compatible with Apple Watch, it works with other apps, including Clue and Natural Cycles, to provide insights into cycle tracking.

The biggest drawback of Oura is its required subscription. You can technically use it without one, but it limits all the data you see. Without a subscription, you can only see your Readiness score, sleep score, and activity, and you won't get all of the extra health insights. If you do want something more affordable, the Oura Ring 4 is still widely available and marked down.

If you want the benefits of a smart ring but don't want to pay a monthly fee for all of your health insights, there are other options than the Oura Ring. The Ultrahuman Ring Air has a shiny exterior and is ready to manage your health data.

It's focused on goal-oriented tracking, improving your workout performance, or increasing your activity. If you would like to share your health data with friends, it allows you to create 'Zones' that you can share with your friends who are also Ultrahuman users. While it certainly leaves some to be desired, the Ultrahuman is a solid alternative to the Oura Ring.

The Ultrahuman Ring Air uses PPG and 6-axis motion sensors to detect all activity, including continuous heart monitoring and activity detection. It tracks sleep, even monitoring how often you toss and turn at night. A no-contact temperature sensor is also available on the inside of the Ultrahuman ring, although I've found its accuracy to not be on point, falling about 10 degrees lower than my Oura ring reported.

To promote an optimized lifestyle, it tracks circadian rhythm, suggesting when to stop your caffeine intake for the day. The app also has a stress monitor that distinguishes when you're relaxed, engaged in activity, or stressed. Plus, it's compatible with Apple HealthKit, so all your information can be shared across apps.

It's incredibly lightweight, the best part of the ring's design. Otherwise, I'm not the biggest fan of the Ultrahuman Ring Air's design. While it's only .2 millimeters taller than the Oura Ring 4, it feels larger because of its hard right angles that often pinched my finger when gripping. Not to mention, its battery life falls about two days short of Oura's.

Like the ring, the app also has a clunkiness problem. It suffers from freezing and needs to be reloaded occasionally, which may result from how cluttered the UX design is. When logging in, you're met with big pop-ups of your stress and internal clock, which, while helpful, are not the usual things I'm looking to get a quick glance at. Getting to my vitals took a few scrolls of the home page. Plus, there's no option to customize the home page.

Read our full review of the Samsung Galaxy Ring.

All of the rings on this list are compatible across platforms, except the Samsung Galaxy Ring. It's for Android users, but especially those with Samsung Galaxy smartphones. The Galaxy Ring offers all of the perks of an Oura Ring but is easily compatible with the Samsung family of devices, like how an Apple Watch is native to iPhones. Although, it should be noted that Oura Rings are also compatible with Samsung phones.

Using Samsung Galaxy AI, the ring gives you an Energy Score, which summarizes your activity and provides recommendations. When you score too low, it gives creepily accurate summaries of factors that might affect your overall readiness.

So, if you're a Samsung smartphone user, you should absolutely choose the Samsung Galaxy Ring over the Oura Ring. It offers all the same insights but is cheaper and doesn't require a monthly fee.

Mashable's former tech editor, Kimberly Gedeon, spent time with the Samsung Galaxy Ring, which had her ready to drop her Apple Watch Series 9. It departs from the classic ring design with a clever take; it has a curved surface to prevent scratches. In her weeks of use, Gedeon noted that it remained in mint condition — the same cannot be said for my Oura Ring.

It tracks heart rate, sleep, physical activity, step count, stress, blood oxygen, skin temperature, and menstrual cycles (like Oura, it's compatible with Natural Cycles). In addition to the Energy Score, it provides a sleep score, too (which gets factored into the Energy Score). It will even track snoring, but you'll have to pay extra through a third-party app, "Sleep Cycle: Sleep Tracker."

If you are transitioning over to the Samsung Ecosystem from Apple's, you'll be thrilled to see that Samsung's steps and exercise trackers look nearly identical to Apple's Fitness app.

The biggest downside to the Samsung Galaxy Ring is its lack of iOS compatibility. While it technically works with all Android systems, you have to use a Samsung smartphone for full access to its features. If that weren't the case, it might take the top spot on this list.

If you want the convenience of a smart ring but aren't looking to make an over-$300 investment, you do have options. The best of which is the Amazfit Helio Ring. This budget health tracker provides all the extensive metrics you get in a more expensive ring. The app is generally well-designed, so the biggest area you see sacrifices in is the actual ring design. It only comes in one finish that looks plasticky rather than a sleek metal finish, and is only available in three sizes.

However, it's not a bad option for under $200 if you don't mind its aesthetics.

In terms of health monitoring, the Amazfit has just about everything you'd want in a smart ring: tracking heart rate, step count, calories burned, and sleep. So, while this is the cheapest model we've tested, it still hits all the marks we'd want to see. Those data points were reasonably accurate when measured against the metrics from the other smart rings on this list. It also produces a readiness score like the Oura or Samsung rings. However, its algorithm isn't as transparent as the other brands.

Where you feel the budget cuts of the Amazfit is in the ring and app design. The ring itself was one of the most uncomfortable I tested. The stiff plastic build was awkward when my fingers naturally swelled throughout the day. It was the only ring I ripped off in the middle of the night because it was getting too uncomfortable. Plus, it only comes with one finish. While it has a metallic sheen, it doesn't distract from the fact that it's still plastic. Plus, it's only available in three ring sizes, so it won't fit everyone.

Plus, the Zepp app, which pairs with the Amazfit Helio Ring, is pretty buggy. Anytime you click into the app, it struggles to load, and it has a tendency to unexpectedly close. When the app does work, it's pretty good, even offering the ability to track meals and calories throughout the day.

While other rings are better designed with more advanced tracking, the Amazfit is good for its price.

#5167. Regal debuts Goal Oriented Agents, putting an actor and a director model on every call

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Enterprise voice artificial intelligence company Regal Voice Inc. today added Goal Oriented Agents to its platform, enabling businesses using them to define what a call has to accomplish, and providing two AI models that split the work of getting there.

The new agents are aimed at a problem Regal says shows up in calls such as auto insurance claims, in which someone reporting an accident tends to explain what happened, where and who was involved in one go. Agents built on conditional logic often circle back for details the caller already gave, just so each field gets filled. Building the whole agent on a single prompt keeps the conversation open, and in Regal’s view it leaves business processes only loosely followed.

Goal Oriented Agents put two models on each call. Regal calls them an actor and a director, and both start work as soon as the caller stops talking. The actor, a smaller and faster model tuned for dialogue, replies right away. Running behind it, the director checks whether the goals for the current stage have been met and can move the call to a new stage with fresh instructions for the actor.

Businesses build the agents out of goal nodes, each listing the goals to be met by the end of the node and any prerequisites that come first. Booking a callback can count as one of those goals. A prerequisite is something the agent has to establish before it can act, such as the caller’s date of birth. Regal said those can be written as plain-language conditions too, for example “the caller mentioned a death in the family.”

The director reviews progress after every turn, so one answer can tick off several items at once. Regal’s sample auto insurance claim has a caller named Dan describing the vehicle damage, photo evidence and injuries in his first message. All three get marked as collected, and the agent goes straight to his name. Once it has that, the director sees location and policy number are still missing, so the actor’s next question is where he is.

Regal said complex intake and qualification calls are among the strongest uses it has seen for the agents. The company named Medicare enrollment, roadside assistance and mortgage qualification as uses the design opens up. Medical intake that complies with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act is another.

Google LLC, AAA and Toyota Motor Corp. are among the more than 200 enterprises Regal says it serves. Customers of Five9 Inc.’s contact center platform gained access to Regal’s voice agents through an integration in August.

Regal is a venture capital-backed startup that has raised $83 million to date. That includes a $40 million round in October 2024.
