#2731. Mamelodi Sundowns Crash Out of CAF Champions League

📅 Oct 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Defending champions Mamelodi Sundowns have crashed out of the CAF Champions League after a 1-1 draw against Angola’s Wiliete on Friday.

The South African champions exited in the second preliminary round on away goals after the teams drew 0-0 in Angola last weekend.

The result ended Sundowns’ hopes of defending the title they won in May. It also marked their first failure to reach the Champions League group stage since 2015.

Wiliete, meanwhile, secured their first-ever appearance in the group stage of Africa’s premier club competition.

Sundowns entered the return leg at Loftus Versfeld as favourites and took the lead after just 11 minutes.

Tashreeq Matthews opened the scoring with a deflected effort from outside the penalty area, putting Miguel Cardoso’s side ahead in the tie.

Wiliete threatened an equaliser midway through the first half, but goalkeeper Ronwen Williams denied Gilberto’s header.

The visitors eventually found their breakthrough four minutes after the restart.

Former Egyptian Premier League striker Mabululu capitalised on a misplaced back-pass from Cassius Mailula. The Angolan forward then beat Williams to level the score at 1-1.

With the away-goals rule now favouring Wiliete, Sundowns needed another goal to progress. However, Wiliete held firm to secure a historic qualification and send the reigning champions out before the group stage.

Mabululu spent several seasons with Al Ittihad Alexandria, finishing as the Egyptian Premier League’s leading scorer in 2022/23 with 16 goals.

The Angola international scored 41 goals in 83 appearances during his earlier spell with the Alexandrian club.

He subsequently moved to Libya’s Al Ahli Tripoli in 2024.

The defeat represents Sundowns’ earliest Champions League exit in more than a decade.

The South African side had reached the group stage in each of the previous 11 editions. They last failed to qualify in 2015, when DR Congo’s TP Mazembe eliminated them.

Mamelodi Sundowns are also the first reigning African champions to fall before the group stage since TP Mazembe in 2016.

Wiliete, a club founded in 2018, advanced to the group stage alongside South Africa’s Orlando Pirates.

The Pirates defeated Namibia’s African Stars 4-0 on Friday to complete a 5-0 aggregate victory, leaving them as South Africa’s sole representatives in this season’s Champions League group stage.

Not since Sir Alex Ferguson’s opening monologues has a Manchester United manager begun his weekly press conference at Carrington without taking a question.

Louis van Gaal once turned up with a dossier of stats ready to refute Sam Allardyce’s claims that United were a long-ball team, and flounced off on another occasion at Christmas – telling us to ‘enjoy the wine and mince pies’ – after using his first answer to demand an apology for stories claiming his job was on the line.

Jose Mourinho had United’s European record over the previous eight years at his fingertips when he gave his ‘football heritage’ speech in the wake of a Champions League defeat to Sevilla in 2018, but at least he waited for the first question.

When Michael Carrick walked into the room at 1.30pm on Friday, it was clear he had something on his mind.

‘I’ll start today,’ said Carrick, taking his seat. ‘I just want to say a few things, really.’

Over the course of the next five minutes and 40 seconds, spanning nearly 1,000 words, Carrick faced the growing crisis at United head on.

Man United boss Michael Carrick began Friday's press conference with a lengthy monologue

Carrick is fighting for his job after United's poor start to the season and their 4-1 loss to Como

He spoke about the responsibility of being United’s head coach and the principles that have made this club great.

He conjured up the spirit of Ferguson, Sir Matt Busby and Sir Bobby Charlton. He mentioned United’s strength in the face of adversity, forged in the dark days after Munich.

He spoke of the humility and hard work epitomised by great United players like Ryan Giggs and Wayne Rooney.

No-one is bigger than the club and no-one is entitled to be there, said Carrick in what felt like a message to his captain Bruno Fernandes and Lisandro Martinez over their recent public criticism.

At times, he looked just as distracted as when he left us in Lombardy on Wednesday night, reeling from a 4-1 Champions League defeat to Como. He spoke hesitantly at times, emotionally at others.

This was a defiant and heartfelt speech from a man who is fighting to save his job, but more importantly to drag his club from the depths of despair and self-destruction as United stumble through their worst start to a season since 1986 with cracks beginning to appear from the boardroom to the dressing-room.

‘My responsibility is to get a team on the pitch that represents this football club and the supporters, and they can be proud of the identity that we show.

‘We have gone away from that just recently in certain games, which I'm totally fully aware of and definitely doing something about.’

David Schwartz, Ripple's CTO emeritus and one of the original architects of the XRP Ledger (XRPL), has argued that the network's growing adoption could eventually translate into stronger demand and higher prices for its native cryptocurrency, XRP.

In a recent discussion on X, the prominent blockchain developer expressed skepticism about the possibility of the XRP Ledger becoming a major financial infrastructure platform without its native token benefiting from that success.

"I think a world where XRPL is highly successful in financial applications but XRP does not have high demand and value is very unlikely," Schwartz wrote.

According to Schwartz, XRP possesses several unique advantages within the network that could help drive its long-term demand.

For starters, XRP is the only native, counterparty-free asset on the XRP Ledger, meaning that it does not depend on any third-party issuer.

Moreover, XRP is required to pay transaction fees and maintain account reserves on the network.

"All of those things can help drive value and demand," Schwartz explained.

His comments address a persistent debate within the XRP community about whether the growing adoption of the XRP Ledger necessarily translates into increased demand for XRP itself.

Schwartz also outlined a hypothetical scenario in which Bitcoin becomes the dominant settlement asset, arguing that XRP could still benefit from the XRP Ledger's success.

"About the only way that could happen would be if we wound up in a world with one totally dominant settlement asset. That seems unlikely to me," he wrote, referring to a scenario in which XRPL succeeds without generating substantial demand for XRP.

He went on to explain that even widespread Bitcoin adoption would not necessarily eliminate XRP's role in facilitating transactions.

"For example, even if you imagine a world where nearly all settlement is done using bitcoin, if XRPL is successful, you will still probably have several versions of wrapped/bridged bitcoin on XRPL and XRP will capture value bridging them."

The recall documents state that 105,794 hybrid Land Cruisers have a plastic fuel tube on the gas tank that connects to a metal fuel tube that sends the combustible liquid to the turbocharged four-cylinder engine. However, if the retaining clip that holds these two parts together is lifted, it can cause the fuel lines to disconnect and cause a leak. Not only will such a leak lead to wasted fuel and the gas engine eventually shutting off, but it also increases the risk of a fire.
