#2677. Man for Man: How Brighton’s Press Suffocated Arsenal

📅 Sep 23, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

von Next Generation am 23.09.2026 in den Kategorien Premier League,Uncategorized mit 0 Kommentaren

BrBrighton arrived at the match fresh off a 3–2 win at Old Trafford, while Arsenal boasted a clean defensive record and a reputation for controlling games and seeing them out like few other teams in Europe. The match ended 3–0, sparking immense hype around Brighton’s pressing. This analysis explores the underlying mechanisms: their man-oriented pressing across the entire pitch, their asymmetrical build-up play, their transition behavior after winning the ball, and the three goals themselves.

Arteta’s Arsenal are among the best sides in Europe at controlling games out of possession. Any opponent who sets out to establish order is playing on the ground Arsenal own. Hürzeler therefore decided against control – not control surrendered in the sense of a low block, but a chaotic game state that Brighton generated themselves and were therefore prepared for.

System & movement with the ball – FC Arsenal

System & movement with the ball – Brighton & Hove Albion

The thread running through ninety minutes: Arsenal were not to settle into an organised phase either with or without the ball. Three decisions delivered that. The build-up was set up asymmetrically – on the right Brighton prepared the long ball and occupied the landing zone before it was played, on the left counter-movements opened the short route. It was never long instead of short, but two prepared options that would have demanded opposite answers from Arsenal. After regains the ball was not secured but risked immediately, because a secured ball gives the opponent exactly the seconds they need to reorganise. And the press lived off intensity and constantly new starting points: when a line was played through, Brighton did not stand still but re-established their shape and triggered again.

All three decisions give up ball control in the classical sense. Control came instead from preparation – if you know where the ball will land and you are standing there beforehand, you control the situation without the ball.

What followed can be pinned to a single number: 41 Arsenal turnovers in their own danger zone. That is not a figure a side of this quality produces because they play badly, but because they never reached the game state their build-up is designed for.

Brightons manorientated press against a 4-3-3

The press was simple in principle and demanding in execution. Brighton set up with man-reference to their direct opponent and switched into pure man-marking on the trigger. Against a side that tracks from the first second, marking assignments can be pulled apart by movement before any pressure exists; Brighton refused to offer that, because their shape stayed compact until the trigger moment.

In the “mid-block,” positioned +1 on the last line, but ready at any moment to transition into a high press upon a pressing trigger.

The steering began with Ayari. He curved his pressing run so that Konsa stayed in his cover shadow and Raya was forced to the other side. Kostoulas marked Gabriel, while Chema Andrés and Groß pushed aggressively onto Arsenal’s No. 8s. Groß in particular tracked the dropping Rice and at times advanced into the first pressing line, in some sequences all the way onto Konsa. That requires Rice to be picked up by a team-mate in the same moment – the man-marking worked through handovers as much as through tracking. Centrally, the striker and the No. 10 rotated accordingly: whoever had the shorter route to the next man on the ball pushed out, while the other covered behind him and took over again on the next trigger.

It only held up because the centre-backs came with it. Vušković and Struijk stepped out onto Ødegaard and the dropping Havertz and prevented an underload in central areas. When Arsenal built with three in the first line, Brighton kept one centre-back zonal in the back line – but with permanent reference to the unmarked player in the No. 6 space, stepping onto him the moment the press was triggered. Arsenal’s numerical advantage in build-up therefore never translated into a free man anywhere on the pitch.

Raya was part of the marking scheme too. Ayari’s cover shadow left only the route through a No. 6, and under man-marking that is not an escape but a duel that begins with the first touch. Raya’s body shape reinforced it: he tended to open only half-way, which effectively removed the switch to the far side from his repertoire. Steered onto his right foot, only the right-hand build-up side remained; the left lay open for long stretches and was unreachable for the one player who could have found it.

Every back pass served as a trigger. When the ball went back to Raya or the centre-backs, Brighton pushed up as a unit and raised the defensive line. The density around the ball came less from the base shape than from the recovery runs: players who had been beaten sprinted back, and they did not run at the ball but with a cover shadow onto the lay-off option behind it. An Arsenal player with a marker in his back therefore had neither the lay-off nor the chance to turn.

This is precisely where Brighton differed from most sides who get played through against Arsenal. The usual response is to stay deep once a line has been broken and wait for the shape to settle. Brighton instead pushed up into the next line and used the mid-block to prepare the next jump back into the high press – even when that press had just been beaten. The press therefore had not one starting point per possession, but a steady supply of new ones, and Arsenal had to solve the same pressure several times within a single attack.

Arsenal’s core problem was the absence of a threat in behind. Their attempts to play through the press came without pace beyond the back line, so Brighton were never forced to turn towards their own goal. That made the press cheap: no threat in behind, therefore no need to drop the line. There are three answers to committed man-marking – movement that drags your own marker away and opens space for a third player, a dribble that beats the assignment one-on-one, or a target man who wins the long balls. Arsenal produced none of them.

When the high press was beaten, Brighton dropped into a 5-1-4 and inverted the priority. The five in the last line formed a deliberate overload against Arsenal’s front line – in exactly the moment a man-oriented side is most vulnerable, because its players are facing forward while the opponents are already moving. Brighton took the depth out of the situation first and only then thought about the ball again.

A “mid-block” in a 5-1-4 formation. The foremost players protected the “red zone” by using their cover shadows and shifting extremely compactly. Arsenal was mostly forced to play around the block, repeatedly giving Brighton time to reorganize.

A single No. 6 held the connection in front, while the four most advanced players stayed narrow and closed the centre with cover shadows rather than pressing the ball carrier. Arsenal found no central switch on the ball-near side and had to play around the block. Every trip around it cost seconds that Brighton used to shift across, so the end of the route offered the same congestion as the start. In some phases six players stood in the last line.

The block was not a terminus. At every Arsenal back pass Brighton jumped out again and restarted the high press – the same trigger as in the high phase, only from a deeper starting position. The spare man in the last line made that possible, usually Vušković: because the overload left him without a fixed opponent, he could step out, cover the depth and pick up a man himself a moment later. That freed a midfielder to push forward without leaving an assignment open behind him – precisely the point at which sides with a similar approach usually come apart.

In the “mid-block” phase, the “+1” players organized themselves to execute early handovers and jump into pressing situations. Here, Vuskovic—acting as the “+1” player from the defensive line—steps up to pick up the Arsenal player operating in the number 10 position.

On top of that came the trap on the touchline. Once Brighton steered the ball wide, players jumped in from every direction: the ball-near winger closed the route back, the No. 6 covered inside, the back line stepped out. The back pass, the switch and the ball in behind did not disappear one after another but at the same time. Take away only the back pass and a side switches play; take away only the switch and they play backwards and start again. When all that remains is an individual action against several opponents with the touchline at your back, it almost always ends with the opposition.

After a regain there are two basic options: risk the ball immediately to launch the attack, or secure it to establish order. Hürzeler consistently chose the first, and it follows directly from the overall idea – a secured ball would have given Arsenal exactly the seconds in which their structure reorganises.

When a high press was bypassed, Brighton succeeded in forcing Arsenal into congested situations in the area in front of the back four. In doing so, the players who had been bypassed shifted to block the passing lanes of the players positioned behind the ball. This created additional pressure on the ball carrier, who was simultaneously facing pressure from the front via a “negative” pressing movement (i.e., pressing towards their own goal).

The counter was prepared before the regain. When Arsenal found a player in the No. 6 space after beating the press, Brighton immediately closed his back pass, so the turnover inevitably occurred in a moment when Arsenal were all ahead of the ball. The density around the ball, a defensive necessity, meant in attack that Brighton were central and therefore on the shortest route to goal. The first ball went to the deepest player, followed by a dribble at speed into the box, with no intermediate step and no square pass for safety.

In the build-up phase, Brighton utilized a diamond shape. De Cuyper and Boscagli engaged in a 2v2 duel on the left flank, while Kostoulas, Gomez, and Kadioglu created a high 3v3 situation on the right. Briefly, both center-backs and a defensive midfielder would spread out to widen the formation. Additional support occasionally came from Groß, dropping back from the number 10 position. Ayari occupied Konsa and Gabriel through constant runs into the open spaces ahead of him.

In deep build-up Brighton baited Arsenal in deliberately. Verbruggen put his foot on the ball while the centre-backs split and lengthened the pressing runs of their opponents. On paper that produced a 3v2 against Arsenal’s first line – except Hürzeler had no intention of playing through that overload on the ground. 25 of 40 goalkeeper passes were long, 62.5 per cent, and they went overwhelmingly to the right.

The structure allowed for a long ball to the right flank, feeding into the 3-3 formation. This created opportunities to contest the second ball or flick on the aerial pass. Additionally, Vuskovic offered a short, low passing option as the free player.

There, Brighton consistently created a 3v3 – an intact structure for the second ball regardless of whether the first contact was won. That is exactly what separates it from an ordinary long ball: the action was not judged by the aerial duel but by the situation that followed it. Kostoulas dropping added to the disorganisation of Arsenal’s high line, because a centre-back had to decide whether to follow him between the lines or let him turn. After winning second balls Brighton played on at high speed and looked for an early shot or an early cross, even against a defensive overload – again the decision for tempo over the clean attack.

Once again, the 3-3 structure on the right flank. Here, from open play.

On the left Brighton worked through pinning rather than through length. Boscagli stepping inside dragged Saka into central areas and took him out of the wide zone Arsenal needed him in defensively, while De Cuyper dropped and offered the mid-range diagonal once the assignments had been pulled apart. The asymmetry was a personnel decision: the players for second balls were on the right, the players who could drag markers out of position on the left. Arsenal would have had to condense against one side and hand over assignments cleanly against the other.

The team’s build-up play was asymmetrical, featuring a high, wide approach on the right contrasted with the left flank: Boscagli often played an inverted role—supplementing the existing diamond formation—which opened up space for de Cuyper. Meanwhile, Groß and Kostoulas occupied the players on Arsenal’s right defensive flank, enabling Brighton to find low-ball solutions against the press.

With the ball in midfield there were two patterns. The first was the deliberate back pass to Verbruggen to pull Arsenal’s block out, followed by the same long-short structure as in deep build-up. The back pass was not a safety option here but the tool that created the space behind the pressing line in the first place.

The second pattern worked through central and wide tension at high speed, supplemented by rotations within their own structure. Arsenal repeatedly failed to hand over their pressing assignments in a coordinated way against it – the same weakness Brighton had solved cleanly in their own press. In possession Brighton’s shape approached a 4-3-3: Chema Andrés as the deepest No. 6, Groß with freedom from the left half-space, Kadıoğlu pushing up early to support Gómez, Boscagli starting slightly deeper on the left.

The 1-0 by Groß in the 31st minute is the whole plan in one sequence. Brighton play back to the goalkeeper from midfield, go long, lose the first contact and win the second ball. After a phase of circulation the next attack meets a back line that has already been destabilised: Kostoulas drops, Arsenal’s midfield and Konsa step out, and Kostoulas lays the ball back against the direction of the pressure to Groß. Because Ayari runs in behind at the same time, Arsenal’s defenders drop – and Groß has the time for the shot from around 20 metres. In the build-up Brighton had used rotations to drag Guimarães over to the left.

The opening goal stems from a positional attack. Kostoulas spots open space and makes a run into it. With a pass played across his standing leg, he feeds the ball to Groß in the space between the lines. As Groß takes a few quick touches and dribbles toward the defensive line, Gabriel and Calafiori drop back together, thereby opening up a shooting lane.

The 2-0 by Kostoulas shortly before half-time follows the same logic from the opposite direction. After a set piece of their own, Brighton clear an Arsenal counter and transition again immediately, 4v5 in Arsenal’s half, and once more the shot comes from distance while the centre-backs drop off.

Much like the goal that made it 1–0, both defenders went to ground near the ball carrier, thereby opening up space for the goalscorer—this time arising from a transition situation.

Both goals share the same cause: Arsenal’s centre-backs stepped out too late to press the man between the lines. React only when the shooting motion starts and you cannot get into the block from 20 to 25 metres. What is striking is that this passivity is the same one Arsenal showed in their own build-up – a side that does not leave its position to offer itself does not leave it to close down a shooter either.

The 3-0 by Chema Andrés came from a corner: Groß delivered from the left, Brighton had created an overload at the far post and protected it with two blocks, and Chema rose completely unmarked to head in off the inside of the post.

Two goals up, many sides would have defended deeper. Brighton came out with unchanged and at times higher pressing intensity, and the coordination was visibly drilled: marking assignments were swapped cleanly even in dynamic situations, and Arsenal’s structural adjustments were absorbed.

Arsenal had managed a few organised possession phases in Brighton’s half in the first period; in the second it became harder. In those phases they built with a back three, Rice as the third centre-back, Ødegaard dropping alongside Guimarães and the full-backs occupying the half-spaces. Arteta reacted around the hour: Zubimendi and Merino for Timber and Guimarães, with Rice moving to right-back, followed shortly by Gyökeres and Eze for Ødegaard and Tzolis. There was no noticeable effect, because the problem did not lie in the shape but in the assignments in front of it.

There was also a layer that usually goes missing in analysis: Brighton used tactical fouls deliberately to stop Arsenal finding a rhythm. Arteta admitted afterwards that the game had been “very difficult to control”, pointing to lost first and second balls and to Brighton’s directness.

Arteta’s Arsenal being outplayed this clearly is rare. That it happened proactively and aggressively is rarer still – most sides who take something from Arsenal do it with a low block and efficiency. Hürzeler deliberately stepped back afterwards and praised his players’ courage, which gets to the heart of it: the plan only works if eleven players are willing to carry it through in every single sequence.

The interesting question is where the idea came from. Chelsea were a common opponent: Brighton had 74 per cent possession there and lost 3-4 against a counter-attacking side – control right down to the numbers, without return. Alonso’s Chelsea tried the opposite route against Arsenal, a deliberate surrender of control, also without success. The likely derivation lies between the two: neither give up control nor chase it, but generate the unsettled game state yourself and be prepared for it.

There is no reason for panic at Arsenal, but Arteta will need answers against a high press. The build-up was predictable for long stretches, and above all the direct option with pace in behind was missing as a threat. As long as that does not exist, a man-oriented press costs very little against this side.

For Brighton the open question is repeatability. Man-marking of this commitment, collective recovery runs with cover shadows and a touchline trap that requires everyone to trigger at once are hard to produce twice in an English week. Whether the plan works was answered on this afternoon. How often it can be produced will decide the season.

#4887. FDA Approves Lilly’s New Oral Combo Pill for Common Breast Cancer Mutation

People with advanced breast cancer that has spread despite hormone therapy, and with tumors that carry a gene change called an ESR1 mutation, have a new treatment option.

The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved the use of an all-oral combo pill on September 18: Inluriyo (imlunestrant) and Verzenio (abemaciclib), both manufactured by Eli Lilly and Company.

Inluriyo blocks estrogen’s signal in certain breast cancer cells and helps break down the receptor that receives that signal, according to the drug’s FDA-approved prescribing information. Verzenio blocks two proteins, CDK4 and CDK6, that can help cancer cells grow and divide, according to its prescribing information.

Verzenio first won FDA approval in 2017, and Inluriyo was approved as a stand-alone treatment in September 2025.

The FDA approved Inluriyo and Verzenio for use together. Inluriyo shuts down the estrogen receptors that fuel the cancer, while Verzenio slows how fast cancer cells multiply.

Neil Iyengar, MD, a breast medical oncologist at Winship Cancer Institute of Emory University, told Healthline that, “the individual treatments are not necessarily new, but the combination is a new concept.”

Some cancer specialists already prescribed the combination, according to Komal Jhaveri, MD, a breast medical oncologist at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York and lead researcher on the clinical trial that led to its approval.

“Now FDA has backed that with an official approval, so more patients can receive it without having any issues,” Jhaveri told Healthline.

The combination is approved for adults whose breast cancer meets all of these conditions:

In ER-positive breast cancer, estrogen attaches to proteins called receptors, which can lead to cancer growth, according to the National Cancer Institute.

Hormone therapy either prevents estrogen from binding to those receptors or lowers estrogen levels, according to the American Cancer Society (ACS).

An ESR1 mutation changes the estrogen receptor in a way that “makes it stay turned on,” Hope Rugo, MD, chief of breast medical oncology for City of Hope, told Healthline.

“And that mutation can develop while a person is receiving hormone therapy, and ultimately it makes the cancer able to resist the hormone therapy,” Iyengar said.

ESR1 mutations appear in about 1% of breast tumors at diagnosis but in 10% to 50% of metastatic cancers that have stopped responding to hormone therapy, according to the College of American Pathologists.

People with metastatic ER-positive breast cancer usually start treatment with a hormone therapy paired with a CDK4/6 inhibitor, such as Verzenio, according to Iyengar.

The CDK4/6 inhibitor doesn’t work on its own, he said. When an ESR1 mutation makes the hormone therapy stop working, doctors can’t continue the CDK4/6 inhibitor by itself.

Until now, those patients typically switched to a newer hormone therapy pill, such as Inluriyo, alone, according to Iyengar.

The new approval allows patients to keep a targeted therapy as part of their treatment.

Pairing a hormone therapy with a targeted therapy helps overcome resistance and controls the cancer longer, according to Rugo.

“We don’t like giving single endocrine therapy all by itself anymore because we like to give patients combination therapy with targeted agents,” she said.

Before the newer pills, the standard treatment after an ESR1 mutation was fulvestrant, a hormone therapy given as two shots per visit, according to Rugo.

Patients receive the shots every two weeks for the first three doses and then monthly, she said.

“But people hate the injection,” Rugo said. “Most patients will do better with the pill because they don’t have to have the injection.”

Researchers added the drug combo to the clinical trial 6 months after it began, Jhaveri said.

To extend how long patients went before their cancer grew, “we understood that maybe we need a little bit more than one drug,” she said.

The team set out to answer the question, “Can we do a better blockade by giving them more than one drug that might target pathways better?” Jhaveri said.

The clinical trial that led to the approval, called EMBER-3, included 874 adults, according to the FDA.

Eli Lilly funded the clinical trial. The approval is based on results from a cohort of 159 participants with tumors that had ESR1 mutations.

Jhaveri said the study showed “that despite getting a CDK4/6 inhibitor in the first line, the combination of imlunestrant plus abemaciclib was effective.”

In that group, those taking the combination went a median of 11.1 months before their cancer grew or they died, compared with 5.5 months for those taking Inluriyo alone, according to the FDA.

Tumors shrank in 35% of people taking the combination and in 15% of those taking Inluriyo alone, the FDA reported. The FDA noted that these results come from a closer look at part of the trial, not from its main research question.

When the FDA reviewed the results, it was too early to tell whether the combination helps people live longer, according to the agency.

“There isn’t any survival data from any of these trials. The trials weren’t powered to look at survival,” Rugo said.

In the EMBER-3 clinical trial, 86% of people taking both Verzenio and Inluriyo had diarrhea, and 9% had severe cases, according to Verzenio’s prescribing information.

Diarrhea is a known side effect of Verzenio, according to Jhaveri. Adding Inluriyo didn’t increase the incidence of diarrhea compared with pairing Verzenio with fulvestrant, she said.

About 6% of people taking the combination stopped treatment due to side effects, according to a 2026 analysis of EMBER-3 published in npj Breast Cancer.

People taking Verzenio should start anti-diarrhea medication, drink more fluids, and contact their care team at the first sign of loose stools, according to the prescribing information for the drug.

Doctors check blood counts and liver function before a patient starts Verzenio, then every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, monthly for the next 2 months, and as needed after that, according to the prescribing information.

A blood test that looks for tumor DNA can show whether breast cancer has an ESR1 mutation, according to the FDA. The FDA approved one such test, Guardant360 CDx, to identify people who may benefit from the combination.

“We would typically test for it when somebody was diagnosed with metastatic breast cancer, and then we would test for it again if their scans showed that the cancer was growing again,” Iyengar said.

Verzenio and Inluriyo, plus an aromatase inhibitor, are currently listed as a first-line treatment option for hormone receptor-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN).

Breast cancer specialists are likely to be aware of the new two-drug combo, but those treated by general oncologists may need to bring it up with their healthcare professional, Rugo said.

Rugo suggested asking when it makes sense to check your blood for an ESR1 mutation, and whether the combination is a good option if your cancer is growing on your current treatment.

The FDA granted accelerated approval to Etcamah (camizestrant), developed by AstraZeneca, on September 4.

Etcamah is for people whose ESR1 mutation shows up during their first treatment with an aromatase inhibitor, a type of hormone therapy, and a CDK4/6 inhibitor.

Doctors start Etcamah and keep the CDK4/6 inhibitor at the same dose the patient was taking when the ESR1 mutation showed up, according to the FDA’s approval notice.

The agency granted Etcamah accelerated approval based on how long patients went without their cancer worsening, measured from the point when the mutation first appeared in the blood, according to the FDA.

The FDA has required follow-up studies because it isn’t yet confirmed that switching treatment at that point, rather than when scans show the cancer is spreading, gives patients a meaningful benefit.

Patients in the clinical trial that led to the approval had blood tests every 2 to 3 months, according to Iyengar.

“So technically speaking, what the FDA has approved is two different strategies,” Iyengar said.

Early results show a short-term benefit from switching treatment based on blood tests rather than waiting for scans, but researchers are waiting for long-term results, he said.

Researchers are also studying whether Inluriyo helps people with early stage breast cancer. That study, EMBER-4, has enrolled more than 8,000 patients, and initial results are expected in 2027, according to Eli Lilly.

Rugo said patients should watch for more drug approvals over the next six months.

“I would just emphasize here that it’s a really exciting time because people are living longer after a diagnosis of breast cancer,” Iyengar said.

#4895. Testosterone Prescriptions for Women Surge Amid Pharmacy Access Barriers

📅 Sep 22, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Over the past few years, there has been a surge in prescriptions of testosterone for females experiencing menopause, according to an analysis by the research analytics company Truveta for Reuters that was published on September 15. However, these results have not been published in a scientific journal.

The rise in prescriptions has also come amid reports of prescription delays and denials at pharmacies.

This may be due in part to the fact that there are currently no testosterone products approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) specifically for females, despite years of off-label use and evidence supporting testosterone therapy for hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD) in postmenopausal females.

However, on September 17, the FDA called for more data on testosterone therapy for females and encouraged manufacturers to conduct studies that could support future approval of female-specific products.

Organizations such as the Menopause Society support the use of testosterone for HSDD in postmenopausal females, but not for other menopause symptoms.

However, Stephanie Faubion, MD, MBA, Director of the Mayo Clinic Center for Women’s Health and Medical Director for The Menopause Society, told Healthline that evidence does not support the use of testosterone for most menopause symptoms.

“There is evidence for its use for hypoactive sexual desire disorder in postmenopausal women. Testosterone does not decline specifically with menopause, but declines as a function of aging,” she said. “It has not been shown to help with things like fatigue, well-being, bone density, muscle mass, or mood.”

However, despite a lack of supporting evidence or guideline recommendations, some clinicians have been prescribing testosterone off-label to help with fatigue and loss of muscle strength.

Sherry Ross, MD, board certified OB/GYN and Women’s Health Expert at Providence Saint John’s Health Center in Santa Monica, CA, said that testosterone is much more important in the menopause conversation than people realize.

“This powerful hormone helps in promoting muscle strength, bone growth, libido, balancing mood and energy, and reducing anxiety. Since menopause negatively affects all these systems, giving it some consideration can be life changing in perimenopause and menopause,” she added.

Testosterone has long been thought of as a “male” hormone. However, females also naturally produce testosterone. Testosterone levels decline with age, but this decline is not specifically caused by natural menopause.

Faubion also stated, “When postmenopausal or late perimenopausal women use testosterone, it’s very important that their levels are maintained in the normal range for a premenopausal woman.”

HSDD is a common sexual health condition characterized by persistently low sexual desire that causes significant personal distress.

One study found that 52.4% of naturally menopausal females experienced low sexual desire, while 6.6% had HSDD.

HSDD also affects around 1 in 8 females ages 45–65.

“Testosterone has been shown to increase sexual desire and improve other aspects of sexual functioning, including arousal and sexual satisfaction,” said Faubion.

However, this is still considered an off-label use of testosterone.

Ross noted that testosterone can be prescribed in various forms, such as lozenges, ointments, creams, and gels. She also noted that the dose you are given should be carefully discussed with your healthcare professional.

This is especially important because there are currently no FDA-approved testosterone products specifically for females. As a result, clinicians may prescribe products formulated for males at much lower doses.

Because products formulated for males may need to be divided to provide a lower dose, some females may have difficulty getting their prescriptions filled.

Nurse practitioners told Reuters that they’d had prescriptions rejected by pharmacies, and many of their patients had trouble getting them filled.

Amazon Pharmacy also told Reuters that it doesn’t dispense some testosterone prescriptions that require females to customize doses in order to ensure safe and compliant dispensing. It added that the policy is not specific to testosterone or based on a person’s ‌sex or ⁠gender identity.

Along with the FDA’s encouragement for more data and studies, there are more than 30 trials studying the use of testosterone for various health conditions in females underway or planned, according to ClinicalTrials.gov.

For example, Avivo Bio is developing a testosterone therapy designed specifically for females and is studying its potential effects on breast tissue.

“Testosterone should no longer be an afterthought when managing the symptoms of perimenopause and menopause,” said Ross.

“More research is needed to continue supporting its benefits beyond improving libido, but the train has left the station, and people are enjoying the ride. Its whole-body benefits should not be minimized or downplayed.”

Current clinical guidance supports testosterone therapy for HSDD in postmenopausal females, while more research is needed to determine whether it provides benefits for other menopause-related concerns.

#4905. Think You’re Too Young to Worry About Alzheimer’s? Maria Shriver Says Think Again

📅 Sep 20, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Maria Shriver wants women to know that they’re nearly twice as likely as men to develop Alzheimer’s disease.

As an author, journalist, and founder of the Women’s Alzheimer’s Movement at the Cleveland Clinic, Shriver has been spreading awareness about Alzheimer’s since her father was diagnosed with the disease in 2003.

Now, she’s teamed up with Eli Lilly’s Brain Health Matters campaign to raise awareness about the signs and symptoms of Alzheimer’s disease, and how early detection and intervention can make a big difference.

For Shriver, spreading awareness is a gift. “[Particularly] to women who say that once they find out that they’re vulnerable, they’ll change how they’re walking through their life, but they need the information,” she told Healthline.

She wants everyone to know there are proactive strategies to help support brain health.

“I’m really hopeful that people will actually take away from this, that their brain health matters, that there are things that they can do, and go from… feeling like there’s nothing I can do to feeling there are things I can do,” Shriver said.

Since she first started advocating for Alzheimer’s, the research on brain health has come a long way.

“[There] was no hope, and there was nothing you could do, and you just felt powerless, and you felt it was something that just happened to you as you aged,” said Shriver. “There’s been a sea change in that.”

The science, Shriver said, will only continue to advance. “I do believe that in my lifetime, there will be a cure, but I’m excited about what we know today. I’m excited that the conversation includes brain health,” she said.

Genetics plays a complicated role in Alzheimer’s disease, said Heather Snyder, PhD, senior vice president of Medical & Scientific Operations at the Alzheimer’s Association.

“[There’s] still a lot that we don’t know and a lot that we’re understanding about risk and what may make somebody’s risk higher related to their genes, or lower,” Snyder told Healthline.

For example, having a first-degree relative with Alzheimer’s, such as a parent or sibling, can increase your risk of developing the disease, but does not guarantee it.

“Most people with a family history will not necessarily develop Alzheimer’s,” said Snyder.

Researchers have identified over 100 genetic variants linked to a greater chance of late-onset Alzheimer’s (age 65 and older), the most common form of the disease. The known genetic factors for Alzheimer’s have started influencing treatment and research.

While genetic factors are outside of your control, up to 45% of dementia risk could be prevented or delayed with modifiable risk factors.

#4914. Aerobic Exercise May Improve Brain Health in Postmenopausal Women

📅 Sep 19, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Regular aerobic exercise may help postmenopausal women strengthen executive function, according to new research.

The study, published in the Journal of Alzheimer’s Disease on August 1, suggests that cognitive benefits may appear within 3 to 6 months. This highlights that exercise can be a practical strategy for preserving brain health after menopause.

Brain health strategies are crucial during and after menopause, with two-thirds of cases of Alzheimer’s disease occurring in women. Menopause can influence cognitive decline, as well as resilience.

Dung Trinh, MD, a physician at MemorialCare Medical Group and Chief Medical Officer of Healthy Brain Clinic in Irvine, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study, said the findings are encouraging, but noted some limitations.

“This was a secondary analysis of a relatively small study, so I would view it as important evidence that deserves confirmation rather than as definitive proof,” he told Healthline.

The study included 93 cognitively healthy females ages 20 to 67, 43% of whom were postmenopausal. Each participant had below-average cardiorespiratory fitness and didn’t exercise regularly.

The participants were randomly assigned to groups for either a 6-month aerobic exercise program or a stretching-and-toning program.

The greatest cognitive benefits were seen in individuals who engaged in 45 minutes of aerobic activity four times a week, who saw improvements in executive function. These cognitive gains were first observed at 3 months and strengthened through the remainder of the program.

Executive function involves working memory, cognitive flexibility, and inhibitory control. In daily life, this includes managing tasks, making decisions, staying organized, and maintaining independence.

“Stretching and mobility work have important benefits — namely, flexibility, comfort, range of motion, balance, and stress reduction, to name a few. However, aerobic activity produces a more sustained cardiorespiratory and metabolic stimulus,” said Kecia Gaither, MD, a double board certified OB-GYN and maternal fetal medicine specialist, and associate professor of Clinical Obstetrics and Gynecology at Weill Cornell Medicine. Gaither wasn’t involved in the study.

Aerobic activity is rhythmic and continuous, increasing your breathing and heart rate. Moderate intensity means you can talk but not sing while participating in the activity.

Aerobic exercise can affect the brain in various ways.

“Exercise may help by improving cardiovascular fitness, reducing vascular risk factors, and supporting brain plasticity, all of which are relevant because vascular and metabolic health are closely tied to later cognitive decline,” Trinh said.

While aerobic exercise doesn’t prevent Alzheimer’s disease or slow the progression of dementia, it can help improve overall cognitive function as you age.

One of the ways aerobic exercise can affect the brain is in its structure. It can help protect brain regions such as the hippocampus, which is important for memory.

Aerobic exercise can also benefit the brain’s white matter. Regular aerobic activity may support connections in the brain that help with focus and processing information.

Short-term workouts can also have immediate cognitive benefits, like boosting thinking skills right after you work out.

Cognitive decline appears to occur more frequently in women than in men. This suggests that hormonal factors likely contribute to this decline.

Cognitive complaints are more common among postmenopausal women than premenopausal women. This may be due in part to reduced ovarian function and decreased serum estrogen levels.

This leads to menopausal symptoms that have an emphasis on vasomotor symptoms. Vasomotor symptoms include hot flashes, sweating, and dizziness.

It’s possible that estrogen has a neuroprotective role in the body, which may also help explain why cognitive decline is more common in women, especially those who are postmenopausal.

“The researchers tried to account for age statistically and still found a menopause-related effect, suggesting the result may not be explained by age alone,” Trinh said. “Menopause and aging are tightly linked, so the study cannot completely separate those influences.”

Trinh noted that the study highlights a gap in research: Menopause status is often overlooked in exercise and brain-health trials, even though women have a higher lifetime burden of dementia.

“Future studies with larger samples, better characterization of perimenopause and postmenopause, and Alzheimer’s biomarkers such as amyloid and tau would help determine whether these cognitive improvements translate into lower long-term dementia risk,” he said.

#4921. Microdosing GLP-1s Like Wegovy, Zepbound May Still Lead to Weight Loss

📅 Sep 18, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

People may still be able to experience weight loss by staying on lower starting doses of GLP-1 medications, such as Wegovy or Zepbound, for longer, according to research published in Biology Methods & Protocols on August 31.

Staying on lower doses of GLP-1 medications for longer may overlap with a practice commonly referred to as “microdosing,” which has become a trend among celebrities and on social media.

However, the study did not establish that participants were intentionally microdosing, and using these medications outside their approved dosing schedules is not FDA- or manufacturer-approved.

The study included electronic health records from 814 individuals who met the criteria for sustained low dose semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy), defined as at least three 0.25 mg doses spanning at least six months. It also included 1,016 individuals who met the criteria for sustained low dose tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), defined as 2.5 mg doses spanning at least six months.

They found that those taking sustained low dose tirzepatide for one year saw around a 5.5% weight loss, while those taking sustained low dose semaglutide for the same period saw around a 2.2% weight loss.

The researchers also compared weight loss trajectories between those taking low dose and high dose semaglutide. After matching participants based on age, sex, BMI, and type 2 diabetes status, they found that those on low dose semaglutide experienced 1.7% weight loss while those on high dose semaglutide had about 12% weight loss.

“These findings introduce an important treatment paradigm toward increasing the awareness around ‘microdosing’ for patients whose goal is cardiometabolic health with lesser side effects than typical dose escalation of these medicines and an associated modest sustained weight loss in the 2-7% range from baseline,” Venky Soundararajan, PhD, co-founder, chief scientific officer for nference, and co-author of the study, told Healthline.

Mir Ali, MD, medical director of MemorialCare Surgical Weight Loss Center at Orange Coast Medical Center in Fountain Valley, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study, said it isn’t surprising to see that people taking GLP-1 medications can lose weight even at the lowest dose.

“I have seen this in my own practice. Although these starter doses are generally intended to help patients adjust to the medication, some people may experience meaningful weight loss even at these lower doses,” he added.

Soundararajan noted that weight reductions remained evident through the 15-month follow-up. This suggests that the initiation dose may potentially serve as a sustained maintenance strategy over many months, rather than simply a brief stepping stone to a higher dose, particularly for a patient who has already achieved the weight loss and cardiometabolic benefit they are seeking.

However, Ali cautioned, “In my experience, many patients eventually reach a point where they stop losing weight on the lowest dose, although individual responses can vary considerably. Some patients may achieve satisfactory results on a low dose, but most will need some degree of dose escalation to continue losing weight and reach their goals.”

Wegovy (semaglutide) and Zepbound (tirzepatide) are GLP-1 medications approved for weight management as well as other conditions. Other GLP-1s are only approved for the management of certain conditions like type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and obstructive sleep apnea.

Novo Nordisk, the manufacturer of Wegovy, recommends starting at a weekly dose of 0.25 mg and increasing the dose every 4 weeks. The recommended maintenance dose is 2.4 mg once weekly, although some adults with obesity may increase to 7.2 mg once weekly if additional weight loss is needed.

Eli Lilly, the manufacturer of Zepbound, recommends starting at a weekly dose of 2.5 mg. It then recommends increasing to the next higher dose every 4 weeks until you reach the recommended maintenance dose or the maximum dose of 15 mg.

Your healthcare professional may adjust how quickly your dose is increased based on your response to the medication and any side effects you experience. Talk with them about the appropriate dosing schedule for you.

It is important to keep in mind that while some people experience fewer side effects, microdosing may not reduce the side effects of GLP-1 medications for everyone.

“Compared with patients escalating to conventional higher doses, sustained lowest-dose semaglutide was associated with lower 24-month rates of constipation (23.3% vs 36.3%), nausea (39.7% vs 50.1%), and low blood pressure (1.7% vs 5.4%),” he said.

“This suggests that precision obesity care may increasingly involve asking not only, ‘How much weight can this drug help someone lose?’ but also, ‘What is the lowest dose that achieves this patient’s cardiometabolic goals with the best tolerability?’”

Ali also noted that these findings reinforce that GLP-1 medications can be effective for weight loss even at their lowest doses.

“However, the amount of weight loss at these doses tends to be more modest than what is seen with higher maintenance doses.
