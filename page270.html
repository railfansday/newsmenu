#1681. Darryl Bakola Could Reach €30m as Juventus Face New Rivals

📅 Oct 13, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Milan have entered the pursuit of Sassuolo midfielder Darryl Bakola, joining Juventus and Roma in a reported race that could grow further, according to Get Italian Football News. The report describes Juventus as holding a preferential position, but there is no indication of a bid or agreement.

Kaustubh Pandey’s report says Milan’s interest follows Bakola’s impressive campaign and his performance against the Rossoneri. It also relays Daniele Longo’s report that Ruben Amorim views the Frenchman as a suitable fit for his system.

Bakola’s capacity to receive the ball in tight spaces and play vertically is identified as a strength. That profile has attracted several clubs, while the Juventus interest in Bakola keeps the Bianconeri firmly in the picture.

The reported advantage stems from Giovanni Carnevali, who signed Bakola during his time at Sassuolo and is said to retain a good relationship with the club. That connection is presented as a route to a preferential move, not as evidence that Juventus have agreed terms or begun formal negotiations.

Milan’s arrival adds competition, while Bakola has also been recommended to Roma as a possible replacement for Manu Kone. The report says English clubs are beginning to monitor him as well, leaving Juventus’ reported position vulnerable to interest beyond Serie A.

Get Italian Football News puts a possible valuation at €30 million by the January window if Bakola continues to impress. That is a conditional estimate, not a confirmed asking price or agreed fee; Sassuolo’s decision on whether to sell remains unknown.

Sassuolo’s record of selling emerging players offers a reason to take the prospect of a major move seriously. The report points to Tarik Muharemovic’s transfer to Leeds United last summer as an example, but gives no confirmed timetable for Bakola’s future.

For Juventus, the next marker is whether reported interest turns into concrete movement as the January window approaches. Milan, Roma and the English clubs tracking Bakola could all complicate that route if his performances continue to raise his profile.

#4382. Castlevania: Belmont&#039;s Curse Review - Worthy Of The Whip

📅 Oct 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Castlevania has been a dormant franchise for years, but Belmont's Curse revives the series with fiery passion. There are hundreds of indies inspired by Konami's gothic vampire-hunting games, putting a lot of pressure on the publisher to live up to its far-reaching legacy. Konami's surprising, yet effective solution is to bring in a new developer: Evil Empire, best known for its work on the Castlevania-inspired Dead Cells. The resulting game has a fresh perspective, a brand-new protagonist, and whip-smart execution.

Rose Belmont and her father Trevor have been summoned to Paris, a city under supernatural siege. Rose is eager to prove her worth, partially to impress her father, who killed Dracula 23 years ago, and partially because she wants revenge for her bewitched arm, a curse from Dracula, passed down from her late mother. It's a solid premise, and while the narrative is not particularly complex, Rose's character arc has standout moments that will stick with me.

For newcomers, Belmont's Curse stands alone perfectly fine, though it is technically a sequel to Castlevania III, which the first two seasons of Netflix's Castlevania are based on. Arguably, it's a better sequel to the show than the game; the art style and character designs have clear anime inspirations, and some voice actors reprise their roles. I enjoyed seeing them in a new light, especially as they passed the torch to Rose.

The best example of this torch-passing is a deck of tarot cards, gifted to Rose by her late mother, Sypha. It's my favorite mechanic in Belmont's Curse. Each is a specially crafted vessel to hold the power of Paris' most powerful figures, friend or foe. As you approach each target, Rose will gather clues from her surroundings to aid in hunting them down or besting them in battle. This investigation ranges from just-okay, linear moments in the early game to cryptic, map-spanning missions in the late game. Creatively designed quests to chase down the last handful of tarot cards were my favorite moments in the whole game.

Once you find your targets, you absorb their souls into a tarot card, allowing Rose to speak with them. What starts as a lonely journey becomes almost like a road trip, as Rose has a packed backseat of antagonists and allies who can chime in whenever they see fit.

Each card also unlocks a spell, ranging from fireballs and a magic cross to a blast turning foes to stone. They also come with a list of Works of Mercy: achievements that upgrade your spells upon completion. These range from "Use This Spell 20 Times" to "Kill 44 Skeletons" or "Find All The Rooms In The Clock Tower." It provides a fun reason to use abilities you might not otherwise, which is especially nice when you become overwhelmed by options in the game's closing hours. I was swayed on more than one spell after achievement-hunting for upgrades.

Speaking of combat, the action in Belmont's Curse is magnificent. Rose is light on her feet and only becomes lighter as she gains new abilities, becoming a whirlwind of death by the game's conclusion. Between six weapon categories, a variety of perk-granting relics, and your deck of cards, players are flooded with choice in how to approach encounters. I constantly switched up my loadout, often mid-fight, to adapt to the enemies that appeared. Fights were tough enough to keep me on my toes but never insurmountable, and a forgiving checkpoint system keeps repeated deaths from becoming frustrating. Inventive and intense boss battles against Dracula's coterie of minions are also a major highlight.

After 16 hours, I ended my playthrough with 92 percent completion. I just couldn't resist doubling back to earlier zones to see the new areas I could access with my newfound traversal abilities, and Belmont's Curse never failed to reward me. Between two dozen weapons to find, upgrades to health and mana, and even some huge, optional areas, I'm excited for the satisfaction that awaits me as I dive back in to reach 100 percent.

Belmont's Curse is a strong reminder of why the Castlevania series is so influential, with a rich world, tense combat, and a map chock-full of secrets. While this latest entry certainly pays homage to the Castlevanias of yore, it also breathes fresh life into the franchise, making me excited for its future. Castlevania is back, and between Rose Belmont and Evil Empire, it's in great hands.

Neither report confirms a specific target or a completed transfer plan.

The case for reinforcement is clear from the numbers and the available options. A run of fitness concerns affecting Juventus has further narrowed the choices available to him.

The league position is an early warning, not a verdict on the season. Still, the comparison with Igor Tudor’s opening run is uncomfortable: across their first six matches, Tudor took 12 points and Spalletti has taken 11, with both teams scoring nine and conceding five. Get Italian Football News notes that Tudor was later dismissed, although the shared figures do not mean the two situations are identical.

For Juventus, the more immediate concern is the conversion of attacking opportunities. The team has managed only one win in its last four Serie A fixtures, according to the report, and the current returns from its central forwards give the club a concrete reason to assess its options. A January addition would be an editorial conclusion from those figures, not confirmation that a signing has been authorised.

Tuttosport also reports that Juventus have started outlining ideas for the January window, with a forward identified as a priority in its coverage. The paper says Spalletti is increasingly persuaded that Kolo Muani needs an attacking partner. That points to a question of fit as well as headcount: whether the club can add a dependable scorer who complements the current forward line.

Randal Kolo Muani returned to Juventus in a summer transfer reported at €41 million, but he has scored once in the first six matches covered by Get Italian Football News. His earlier spell in Italy offered a far more productive sample: eight goals and one assist in 16 appearances during a six-month loan from Paris Saint-Germain in 2024-25.

Nick Woltemade, who joined on loan from Newcastle on deadline day, had not scored in four Serie A appearances at the point described in the report. Spalletti has given Kolo Muani freedom to move across the frontline, with Nico Gonzalez making runs as a second striker, but the approach has not yet produced enough finishing. The numbers do not settle what either player can offer over a full season; they do explain why another reliable option belongs in the January discussion.

The Cagliari match also fed into Spalletti’s thinking about a partnership. Tuttosport reports that he sees scope for Kolo Muani to operate alongside a second forward, with the pair able to exchange roles. That is a reported tactical preference, not evidence that Juventus have selected a player to fill the role.

The shortage is not limited to the strikers. Get Italian Football News reports that Kenan Yildiz, Manuel Locatelli, Khephren Thuram, Jeff Ekhator and Jeremie Boga have been unavailable or restricted by injuries. Those absences reduce Spalletti’s options across the squad, making a forward signing helpful but insufficient on its own.

The report also identifies difficulty managing key moments and a lack of personality as separate concerns. Juventus had gone 14 consecutive matches under Spalletti without winning after conceding first, while Nico Gonzalez and Gleison Bremer were emerging as leaders in a squad the source describes as still needing to mature. A new striker cannot by himself change that record or restore every absent player.

Get Italian Football News argues that Juventus are not yet keeping pace with Inter and Roma or showing the consistency required to challenge for the Scudetto. Its account places those concerns alongside the team’s thin squad; it does not name a January target. The immediate test comes against Lazio at the Allianz Stadium, a match the report frames as important to Juventus’ Champions League qualification race.

On the evidence available, a dependable forward should be at the top of Juventus’ January priorities: Kolo Muani has one goal in six matches and Woltemade none in four league appearances. That is a recommendation drawn from the scoring record, not a confirmed club decision. The next meaningful indicators will be whether the forwards improve before the winter window and whether Juventus can recover players currently missing through injury.

#3323. UnitedHealth shares are doing something unusual ahead of earnings

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The only thing more expensive than health insurance is perhaps the costs of insuring shares of health insurers. Case in point: UnitedHealth Group, which reports earnings Tuesday.

Implied volatility is running at twice realized volatility ahead of the October 13 quarterly earnings release. In other words, options prices - the cost of insuring the underlying stock - are expensive, which sets up well for short premium strategies.

In the case of UnitedHealth shares, traders can sell November premium to cut the cost of a longer-dated January call by more than half. UnitedHealth heads into Tuesday's report with much of the damage from its prior missteps already priced in. While rising healthcare costs always remain a headwind, management's 13% to 16% EPS growth target looks achievable. The stock has basically been flat over the last five years.

By the way, according to the company, 90% of United's 52 million customers worldwide are in the U.S. By its own statistics, the company covers about 12% to 13.5% of the U.S. population.

Growing enrollment is critical to EPS growth, but so too is underwriting margin. In the managed care and health insurance business, underwriting margin represents the core operational profitability generated directly from managing healthcare risk. It measures the financial return an insurer achieves strictly from collecting premiums and managing medical care. On the enrollment side, ACA marketplace-driven enrollment appears poised to decline, a headwind for revenue in a segment that had been a growth driver. Fortunately, it's a smaller piece of the enterprise than Medicare and Commercial. Commercial fee-based enrollment grew ~2.9% YoY between 2024 and 2025, while total Medicare enrollment grew 4.5%. The commercial segment growth may not look impressive as a headline number, but given the U.S. workforce grew ~1.4% over the same period, United appears to be gaining market share.

At 17 times forward earnings, and with free cash flow at 5.7% of enterprise value and 6.5% of market cap, the stock isn't priced for any recovery. On the options side, the market is implying 40% volatility for a stock that's been realizing 20%. Granted. The 40% number captures this week's earnings, and it's likely elevated because the stock has had a couple of eye-watering earnings moves in the past 8 reported quarters. In January, the stock fell almost 20% following earnings, and April 2025 saw a similar sharp decline.

As it stands, UNH is about 12-13% below the midpoint of the two-year high and low. The stock has traded lower in five of the last 8 earnings. Current options prices imply a 6.5% earnings move, according to Cboe LiveVol data. That's the second highest implied move in almost two years.

Sell the November 340/420 strangle to help finance the purchase of the January 2027 400 call. Buying the Jan 400 call costs $18.20. Selling the Nov 420 call at $5.80 and the Nov 340 put at $4.80 brings in $10.60, for a net debit of about $7.60, cutting the call's cost by roughly 58%. If UNH finishes November between 340 and 420, both short legs expire worthless and the January call is held for $7.60, with a breakeven of $407.60 at January expiration. A rally through 420 is covered by the long 400 call. The real risk is below: a close under 340 means buying shares there, for an effective basis near $347.60 including the debit.

#4688. Viola Davis Says Shingles Pain Was a ‘9 Out of 10’: How It Changed Her Life

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Emmy, Grammy, Oscar, and Tony Award-winning actress and producer Viola Davis was 50 years old and at the height of her career when she developed shingles.

“All of a sudden, I got a rash in the middle of traveling and juggling [back-to-back] movies that started in the middle of my stomach [and] wrapped around my back,” she told Healthline. “And the pain was definitely at a 9 out of 10.”

Focused on work, Davis didn’t see a doctor right away. Her symptoms eventually subsided but later returned on her upper back.

“That is when I was like, ‘Oh, my goodness, I just got bitten by a poisonous spider,” said Davis. “It felt like really bad nerve pain…then someone mentioned the word shingles.”

After seeing a doctor, Davis learned she had shingles, which is caused by the same virus responsible for chickenpox. She also learned that the risk increases with age as the immune system naturally weakens.

“It can also lead to herpetic neuralgia, lifelong pain that can result from a shingles outbreak,” Robyn Liu, MD, member of the board of directors at the American Academy of Family Physicians, told Healthline.

Shingles can affect daily life, including work and sleep.

“Many people don’t realize that shingles can be associated with nerve pain, and for some, that pain can last for months or even years after the rash has resolved due to potential long-term complications,” Jennifer Caudle, DO, family medicine physician and associate professor at Rowan University School of Osteopathic Medicine, told Healthline.

Nearly one in three people in the U.S. develops shingles in their lifetime.

“Anyone who has had chickenpox can get it, but the risk goes up as we age,” said Liu.

After learning about shingles, Davis said she realized that she could no longer ignore her body.

“You just cannot. I mean, I know that people say it at nauseam, but, for me, it meant slowing down,” she said. “But then the shingles slowed me down…even though I was getting up, I was working out, [the] pain and the sluggishness was awful.”

Now 61, Davis has teamed up with GSK for its “Shingles, actually” campaign to raise awareness about shingles.

“[I’m] sending the message out there for women to really honor themselves and honor their bodies,” she said. “Their bodies literally speak. They’re the other character in their lives.”

After her experience with shingles, Davis began scheduling regular annual checkups.

“Before that time, I did absolutely go to my doctor, but only when it was absolutely necessary, you know, to get my colonoscopy, get my mammogram, but I never went if something felt odd,” said Davis.

Now, whenever she feels off, she doesn’t hesitate to see her doctor. She makes it a point to be honest with them about what’s going on with her body, and she is not shy about asking questions.

“I feel that it’s my job. It’s what I owe myself, and not only [do] I owe myself, it’s what I owe my daughter, it’s what I owe my husband, it’s what I owe my elderly mom, who is in advanced dementia [and] I take care of her,” Davis said.

Caudle stressed the importance of bringing questions to your doctor.

“That’s what we’re here for. Sometimes patients ​tell me they don’t realize they can ask questions or introduce a topic that their healthcare provider didn’t bring up first,” she said. “But a simple question like, ‘Am I at risk for shingles?’ can be a great place to begin.”

Davis has made stress reduction a priority. When she developed shingles, she was juggling an intense workload and adjusting to life as a new mom after adopting her daughter.

“I was someone who just ignored stress,” said Davis. “[When] we meet each other, it becomes a contest: Who can work through their stress the best? I felt like I had to strike while the iron was hot.”

Today, Davis makes stress reduction a priority through activities such as spraying her pillows with lavender, taking Epsom salt baths, meditating, going to therapy, exercising, spending time outdoors, using vagus nerve stimulation, and connecting with family and friends.

#4398. Valor Mortis Review – Napoleonic Novelty

Valor Mortis is a brave new game from developer One More Level. It competes not only against the studio’s excellent Ghostrunner series, but also the ever-expanding Soulslike subgenre of action games. William is just another soldier in Napoleon’s continent-stretching army, but when he dies, he somehow reawakens on a destroyed battlefield with the Emperor speaking directly to him, seemingly telepathically. He moves forward at Napoleon's command and, over 25 hours, gains BioShock-like control over fire, time, and more that you control with your left hand. These powers are few, but they complement your combat kit nicely, which relies primarily on swords and guns to get the job done. By the end, you’ll have multiple firearms, like a pistol and long-range musket, to use alongside different blades and psychic powers to kill the zombie remnants of Napoleon’s army and the ravaged and cursed citizens of the land his war has left behind.

Boss fights should be skill checks, exams to ensure you’re paying attention to everything in your toolkit, but the first half of Valor Mortis’ difficulty is poorly balanced, giving you no space to learn and react to enemies. Getting whacked once and dying provides no room to understand what I did wrong, and though this issue showed its face most in boss fights, even standard mob enemies felt like they had access to random insta-kill moves, too.

Parrying is your best friend, though you can block at the cost of some health or dash out of the way, and learning how to counter hulking soldiers, zombie dog-things, and lab experiments gone very, very wrong is satisfying. So, too, is using my kit of psychic powers like fire to light enemies aflame or stasis, which allows me to freeze enemies with explosive effects. Mixing and matching my saber, rapier, or claymore with my shotgun-like blunderbuss or pyro hand encouraged me to use everything I had to win. It’s impossible to get through Valor Mortis without using every tool in your kit, and it’s clear One More Level is confident in the combat it’s crafted here.

There are sequences where William must wall-run, grapple, swing off poles, and jump from a shrinking platform to the next, and it’s a blast of fast adrenaline in the otherwise intentionally slow, focused gameplay. It’s no surprise One More Level displays masterful command over this type of action – it’s what makes Ghostrunner so good – but it also reminded me of what I like about that series over Valor Mortis; I imagine this is an unwanted side effect. The same can be said for Valor Mortis’ best feat of storytelling: a B-plot centered on the Mice, orphaned children who seek a different kind of leader after what Napoleon has done to their homes, which is clearly inspired by BioShock’s Little Sisters storyline. It even includes a purposefully obfuscated mortality system to help the Mice, which affects a few small sequences later in the game. It’s done well, but not as well as its source material.

This B-plot with the Mice, which largely plays out in side quests, is easy to discover because Valor Mortis keeps veering off the golden path in enticing and rewarding ways. There are countless items to find, like grenades or extra-powerful bullets, as well as myriad minibosses and side quests to take on, and reaching these pick-ups, enemies, and more will often require you to explore this beaten landscape in ways your main path forward won’t. It’s a testament to One More Level’s chapter design, as each takes you to a new location that builds on the last while leaving its own unique mark on William’s adventure.

I definitely died more than 100 times from start to finish. I wish a good portion of those deaths were fairer – I would have liked to learn from them rather than feel my annoyance mount higher. When Valor Mortis’ various systems coalesce into a French army-uniformed package in the second half, however, it becomes a game that demands a lot from you and returns your efforts in kind with satisfying rewards, or at the very least, the lantern to rest at you’ve been begging to see. One More Level’s first go at the Soulslike falls short of its premise, but it’s a confident step into the genre that leans on the studio’s prior expertise to deliver something tense and novel.

Heart failure is a chronic condition in which the heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body’s needs. Despite its name, heart failure doesn’t mean the heart has stopped beating.

A separate 2026 report from the Heart Failure Society of America (HFSA) found that heart failure contributed to more than 450,000 deaths in the United States in 2023, with particularly sharp increases in mortality among adults under 65.

Approximately 7.7 million U.S. adults (3%) are living with heart failure, according to the HFSA report. That number is projected to rise to 8.7 million by 2030, 10.3 million by 2040, and 11.4 million by 2050.

The GBD researchers said the findings highlight the growing burden of heart failure associated with population growth and aging, as well as the need for prevention and treatment strategies targeting common causes such as ischemic heart disease and hypertension (high blood pressure).

Between 2010 and 2022, heart failure hospitalization rates increased among adults under 65 while declining among those 65 and older. Between 2001 and 2020, estimated heart failure mortality rates more than doubled among adults ages 25 to 44 and nearly doubled among those ages 45 to 64.

“I think we’re seeing the impact of cardiovascular risk factors developing earlier in life. Conditions such as obesity, diabetes, hypertension, and sleep apnea are affecting younger adults at higher rates than in previous generations,” Kevin Shah, MD, Program Director of Heart Failure Outreach at MemorialCare Heart & Vascular Institute at Long Beach Medical Center in Long Beach, CA, who wasn’t involved in the analysis, told Healthline.

“When those risk factors are present for decades, they can eventually lead to heart failure at a younger age,” he said.

Amit Blumfield, MD, a cardiologist at Manhattan Cardiology in New York City who was also not involved in the analysis, noted that improvements in early detection may also play a role.

“Our tools to detect early heart failure are also improving with time, and as a result, patients may receive an earlier diagnosis and can hopefully start medical therapy to prevent progression or possibly even reverse the process entirely.”

Heart failure contributed to 452,573 deaths in the United States in 2023, accounting for approximately 14.6% of all deaths that year, according to the HFSA report.

Age-adjusted heart failure mortality rates increased by approximately 37% between 2010 and 2023, with preliminary 2024 data suggesting the upward trend is continuing.

This increase appears to have reversed more than a decade of previous progress in reducing heart failure mortality.

Ischemic heart disease and hypertension were among the most common causes of heart failure in older adults, according to the GBD report. However, the underlying causes varied considerably by age group and region.

Among children and young adults, congenital heart abnormalities and rheumatic heart disease (RHD) were common causes of heart failure. Heart failure associated with alcohol use was most prevalent during middle adulthood.

#4715. Michael Strahan Shares the Words That Helped His Daughter Through Brain Cancer Treatment

📅 Oct 10, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

For years, Michael Strahan appeared in commercials for St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital, delivering a message he never imagined would become so personal: “No parent should have to hear that their child has cancer.”

But in 2023, the beloved TV host and former NFL player faced that reality when his daughter, Isabella, was diagnosed with brain cancer at 19 years old.

“[That] line that I used to deliver, usually at the end of the commercial…here I am actually filling that line in my life in real time,” Strahan told Healthline.

Strahan believes his longtime connection with St. Jude was more than a coincidence. After years of advocating for families affected by childhood cancer, he found himself on the other side of that experience.

“When I needed that family…they were there for me,” he said. “I’m just appreciative, and I think things come back to you; give out good, good comes back.”

In October 2023, Isabella began experiencing vomiting, headaches, and balance issues during her freshman year at the University of Southern California (USC). Tests revealed that she had medulloblastoma, a fast-growing cancerous brain tumor located in the cerebellum. She underwent emergency brain surgery, radiation therapy, and four rounds of chemotherapy.

While medulloblastoma typically occurs in children between the ages of 3 and 9, it can also affect young adults like Isabella.

“[The] biology in adolescents and young adults can differ meaningfully from pediatric cases, requiring specialized expertise at centers of excellence for this disease,” Arnab Chakravarti, MD, director of the Brain Tumor Program at The Ohio State University, told Healthline.

Medulloblastoma is sometimes confused with glioblastoma, the most common type of malignant primary brain tumor in adults. Glioblastoma is particularly aggressive, with a median overall survival of just 15 months and a 5-year survival rate of less than 10%.

However, Chakravarti explained that medulloblastoma has a significantly higher cure rate than glioblastoma.

“[This is] a point of genuine hope that deserves to be communicated clearly,” he said.

Radiation therapy is a cornerstone of medulloblastoma treatment. Isabella received proton therapy, which uses precisely targeted radiation to treat tumors while limiting exposure to surrounding healthy tissue.

“For medulloblastoma, this physical precision is transformative,” Chakravarti said. “Proton craniospinal irradiation dramatically reduces [collateral] exposure — preserving cochlear function and hearing, reducing cardiovascular risk, protecting the thyroid, and most critically, sparing developing brain tissue in ways that translate into measurably better neurocognitive outcomes years after treatment.”

He added that multiple prospective studies have shown that children and young adults treated with proton craniospinal irradiation experience less cognitive decline and better quality of life compared to those treated with conventional radiation.

Today, Isabella is back at USC, embracing the everyday routines she once had to put on hold.

“I am back at USC, so…it’s been great, in the midst of exams, so it’s a little stressful, but I actually [am] inviting the stress in because at one point I didn’t have exams, so I’m happy to be back,” Isabella told Healthline.

Along with attending classes, Isabella is enjoying activities such as working out and cooking. She continues to undergo MRI scans of her head and spine every three months, but said that after another year, the scans will become less frequent, occurring every six months.

“But really…I don’t have a lot of doctor’s appointments,” she said.

“It feels like a brand-new life, but it’s a good one. And that’s wonderful,” Isabella said.

Isabella said her family’s support played an important role in helping her through cancer treatment. Spending so much time together in the hospital brought them closer as they watched movies and played cards and board games to pass the time.

“[It] really, really bonds you to people, and just having my dad and my whole family there was something that [you] can’t beat,” she told Healthline.

Michael said that despite the difficult circumstances, he cherishes the time he spent with his daughter. Before her diagnosis, Isabella was a typical teenager who spent much of her time at school or with friends.

“They say you spend the most time with your kid for the first few years of their life, and then once school starts, you don’t get a chance to spend as much time as you would like with them,” he said.

While Michael said he and Isabella already had a close relationship, her diagnosis brought the family even closer and helped them reflect on what mattered most.

“[What] was really important was family, and relationships, and the love that you have for each other, and the support you have to give each other,” he said. “I think that our relationship now is better than ever.”

Today, Michael makes an effort to visit Isabella at USC whenever he’s in Los Angeles during the NFL season.

“Some weekends she [has time for me]. Some she doesn’t,” he said. “But just to have that relationship with your child, where they want to hang out with you, is a good feeling, and that’s the relationship that we have.”

Michael and Isabella are sharing their story to raise awareness about brain cancer and offer hope to other families facing similar challenges.

They recently participated in a fireside chat in New York City hosted by Real Chemistry and SMAC, where they discussed their family’s experience navigating Isabella’s diagnosis, mental health, caregiving, and the impact of sharing their story.

Throughout her treatment, Isabella also documented her experience through a series of YouTube vlogs and the ABC primetime documentary, “Life Interrupted: Isabella Strahan’s Fight to Beat Cancer.”

For Michael, helping others is a value he learned from his parents. He recalled how his father used his transportation business in Germany to support people in need after retiring from the military.

“[He] would take donations, and use his money and his trucks, and drive stuff to Yugoslavia and Czechoslovakia, and drop it off there at local churches and the shelters for people, who he had no connection with,” said Michael. “So for me, I think I do what I feel is on my heart and what’s important to me.”

For parents caring for children with cancer, Michael emphasized that it’s OK to feel overwhelmed or emotional. He encouraged them to show their children love and support while helping them maintain hope for the future.

“I think positivity is the main thing because it was so tough on her at the time when she was [saying], ‘I can’t do it. I can’t do this. I can’t do that,’” said Michael.

Michael said he made a conscious effort to replace words like “can’t” and “if” with “when” while encouraging Isabella during treatment.

“Anytime she got down on herself, I’d say, ‘when you go back to college, when you do this, when you do that,’” said Michael. “And I just think the positive attitude and the love that you can give and the confidence that you can display towards your loved one [or] your child who’s going through something like this is the only thing you can do because you’re at the mercy of medicine.”

Now in her senior year at USC, Isabella is looking ahead to life after graduation.

“[There’s] a lot of interesting opportunities and things that I want to pursue,” she said.

Michael has no doubt she will find her way.

“She’s so talented, and this experience, in my opinion, has opened up my eyes to more talent that she has that I didn’t realize…the talent to connect people, and be in front of a crowd. I always thought she’d be nervous because I get nervous, but she’s better than me,” he said.
