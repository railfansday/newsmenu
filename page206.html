#4646. What Makes This 2001 VW New Beetle on BaT So Special?

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

This ultra-limited-production RSi is not like other New Beetles.

If you were looking for a sporty two-door in 2001, you could have bought yourself a Porsche Boxster. Or, perhaps, you could have bought yourself two Porsche Boxsters, one in silver for formal occasions and another in bright yellow for the weekend. What's crazier than buying two nearly identical Porsches? Spending the same amount of money on a single Volkswagen.

The New Beetle RSi was the most expensive Beetle ever sold, and one can now be found on Bring a Trailer (which, like Car and Driver, is part of Hearst Autos). One of only 250 examples sold to the public, all of them silver, this Pac-Man-shaped wunderbug hails from the era when VW boss Ferdinand Piëch was forever doing nutty things like stuffing twelve-cylinder engines into Volkswagen-badged luxury sedans and ordering engineers to build the Bugatti Veyron.

See all results for used cars for sale near 10330

Piëch personally owned a New Beetle RSi (his was blue), as the car was much more than just a special edition of a semi-circular Golf. Pairing six-cylinder power with Haldex all-wheel-drive, it was more powerful than ordinary versions of the New Beetle, and it also featured carbon-fiber bodywork and an extremely wide 18-inch wheel and tire package.

For the time, this slightly cartoonish-looking VW was an absolute rocket, especially in the corners. With all that grip and the ability to get power down early on exit thanks to all-wheel drive, it was a little Pokémon-looking pocket rocket. If only it hadn’t been so eye-wateringly expensive—the equivalent of $80,000 when new, not adjusted for inflation—it would have been a credible rival to the likes of the Golf R.

Power comes from a 3.2-liter VR6 engine, which also appeared in the fourth-generation Golf R32. It’s rated for 221 horsepower and 236 pound-feet of torque, but the real appeal is the fantastic soundtrack. Stir things up with the six-speed manual gearbox, and this Beetle flies.

Inside, it’s awash with carbon fiber panels and aluminum accents. The fixed-back racing buckets are particularly incredible, and VW’s designers thoughtfully included a grab bar on the dashboard for your terrified passengers to cling to.

This example has the equivalent of just 11K miles on the odometer, and while the bodywork is unobtanium, the mechanicals are mostly shared with VW products that did come to the U.S. The only question is whether to leave the engine stock, or call up the mad scientists of Canada's HPA, and have them fit one of their ferocious turbocharging kits good for well over 500 horsepower.

Impersonation Risk Detection tries to spot scams traditional security methods might miss.

What makes social engineering scams so effective is that they’re able to get around traditional security checkpoints and convince you to make a risky choice without realizing it. A feature in iOS 27 called Impersonation Risk Detection tries to better defend against that reality on iPhones and iPads — but it’s not on by default.

Also: Chrome warns you about suspicious websites now

The security feature, which debuted in iOS 27 and iPadOS 27, warns you when it flags suspicious signals in a supported app. Its goal is to help you stop and double-check before making sensitive changes that could expose you to a scam, which something like two-factor authentication can’t protect you from to the same degree.

Let’s say you’re using an app on your iPhone or iPad that supports Impersonation Risk Detection. You’re about to perform a sensitive action, such as making a payment, changing your password, or sharing your account details. Impersonation Risk Detection steps in to analyze your Apple account and the information on your device to look for signs of a scam.

Based on the results, Apple will send one of the following three risk levels to the app:

The app then decides what to do next. If a high level of suspicious activity is found, the app could ask you to verify your identify, show a warning, or delay your action before it gets you into trouble. The app makes this determination, not Apple.

Also: I thought I had the new Siri AI in iOS 27 – until I found it hidden in my settings

Apple will analyze certain information related to an interaction to generate the risk level, but it never receives the data itself. The company doesn’t analyze your content in such apps as Mail, Messages, and Photos. But Apple does learn the type of action you attempted when an app requests a risk assessment. Supported apps receive only the risk level and not the data used to create it.

Impersonation Risk Detection is turned off by default because a supported app requires a certain level of access to your actions and activities.

To enable the setting in iOS 27 or iPadOS 27, go to Settings and select Privacy & Security. Swipe toward the bottom of the screen and tap the setting for Impersonation Risk Detection. At the next screen, turn on the switch for “Share with App Developers.” After turning this setting on, you may have to wait 24 hours for it to take effect.

Also: Got iOS 27? Do these 5 things first after upgrading

Any apps that participate in Impersonation Risk Detection will appear on the same screen, so you can always check them periodically for recent activity. You’ll see which apps asked for a risk assessment and which actions triggered the request. Here, you can also turn off access for individual apps.

Both built-in and third-party apps can support the feature, though Apple didn’t list any specific programs on its support page. However, with social engineering scams so pervasive, hopefully enough apps will jump on this bandwagon to better prevent people from becoming victims.

#2033. Man City's perfect record is over and now we'll see what Enzo Maresca's side are really made of, writes TOM COLLOMOSSE - it's easy to ignore the storm when you're winning, but dark clouds loom after Aston Villa defeat

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Published: 14:27 GMT, 24 October 2026 | Updated: 16:33 GMT, 24 October 2026

Come on then, Enzo. Let’s see what you’ve got now.

Until lunchtime on Saturday, Manchester City had shown no signs of being affected by the chaos engulfing the club as Enzo Maresca’s men reeled off seven straight Premier League wins. We will know soon enough whether Aston Villa’s fourth straight home victory over City has broken the spell.

It easy enough to block out the noise when you win every league game. Easy to forget your club’s ongoing battle with the Premier League after they were found guilty of breaking financial rules between 2009 and 2018 – a verdict City have appealed.

These players and coaches would not be human, though, if they had been able to avoid worrying about the future. What if our appeal fails? Will we be relegated? If so, how far? What happens to our contracts? Our careers? City’s next fixture, at home to the impressive Brighton, may tell us a great deal about what is to come.

Troubling times for City, and no less unsettling for the Premier League. Because if City escape on a technicality, what then? The damage to the competition’s image would be colossal. Please, just let the verdict be swift.

Nicolas Jackson scored twice to power Aston Villa to victory and inflict Manchester City's first Premier League defeat of the season

City goalkeeper Gianluigi Donnarumma was at fault for Jackson's winner after letting the ball slip up his body

City did not play badly here. They hit the frame of the goal twice through Iliman Ndiaye and Josko Gvardiol and were denied by some outstanding work by Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki, who looks one of the buys of the season. So too does Nicolas Jackson, who scored in each half, either side of Erling Haaland’s equaliser. This must have tasted especially sweet for Jackson, who was shoved towards the exit by Maresca at Chelsea a little more than a year ago. Against the world’s best No9, Jackson won the battle of the centre-forward hands down.

Unai Emery claimed that Nicolas Jackson’s shooting had resembled a rugby kicker earlier in the season before roaring into form as Aston Villa ended Manchester City’s perfect start.

Jackson’s brace downed the Premier League leaders and the striker now has six goals in his last six games in all competitions following a £65million move from Chelsea in the summer.

The 25-year-old has impressed Emery with his grasp of the tactical instructions at Villa and is now repaying the Spaniard’s faith in front of goal.

‘How he was working, how he was defending - wow,’ Emery said. ‘And then he has the power to attack the spaces and the duels.

‘His challenge has been when he can have chances to score [to] always [find] the corner because he was shooting so much in the beginning of the season like up in rugby.

‘He was shooting a lot of balls over the crossbar. He is progressing as well. We need him more running in behind because we need the runners on the field, and one of the runners normally is the striker, and he has quality and power. He’s coachable.’

Gianluigi Donnarumma should have done better with Jackson’s second and Enzo Maresca insisted that City should have left with something from the game.

‘I think we deserved more,’ Maresca said. ‘We haven't been clinical enough in the box. Sometimes when you concede the first goal was mistake, the second goal came from a transition.

‘We spent most of the game in their side, in their box. Sometimes you have to be more clinical but also more precise with the last pass, more accurate. We're going to learn from that.’

‘We had many chances,’ said Maresca. ‘We played most of the game in their box but you have to be clinical and avoid mistakes like the two goals we conceded.’

All fair enough, though Maresca must have noticed a softness to City that simply was not there in their greatest days. Ndiaye and Jeremy Doku tormented Villa full-backs Matty Cash and Ian Maatsen throughout and on another occasion, their creativity would have produced more. When it comes to the other end of the pitch, the less said, the better.

Marc Guehi was dragged off at half-time after he was at fault for Jackson’s opener and then picked up a booking for a foul on the Villa forward. Ruben Dias seemed more interested in scrapping with John McGinn than stopping him playing. Enzo Fernandez looked slow and sluggish, a shadow of himself.

Enzo Maresca insists that he is unfazed by the form of Marc Guehi after another mistake led to Aston Villa's early opener.

Guehi – taken off at the break - had also made two errors in victory over Ipswich Town last week.

‘I'm not worried about Marc at all,’ he added. ‘I know he is playing every minute since the season started with us and the national team.

‘It's not easy but the change was also because he was on a yellow card and the way we want to be aggressive sometimes to have one yellow card can be dangerous. But I'm not worried at all.’

None of this should detract from Villa or their outstanding coach. Unai Emery has somehow managed to travel under the radar for much of his second spell in English football and has been overlooked by Manchester United twice since he arrived at Villa four years ago.

‘He’s just a tactical genius,’ said Cash. ‘Especially against City at our place, he’s always got a plan for something and he’s always coming up with new ideas.’

#3050. What Rob Manfred and the MLBPA said about salary cap fight as lockout looms over 2026 World Series

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

If not listed, please contact your TV provider.

The small market Milwaukee Brewers vs. Tampa Bay Rays World Series will not be enough to deter MLB's owners from pushing for a salary cap. Prior to Game 1 on Friday, commissioner Rob Manfred said this year's World Series between two teams that finished the regular season in the bottom half of the league in payroll only strengthens the case for a salary cap.

"I think it shows that we leave a lot on the table in some of our smaller markets by not having them have an opportunity to compete on a regular basis," Manfred said Friday (via ESPN). "The Brewers have been a well-run franchise forever. It's been 40 years since we're here. Once every 40 years is not enough."

The Brewers are in the World Series for the second time in franchise history and the first time since 1982, and, respectfully, Manfred is wrong about them being a "well-run franchise forever." Milwaukee posted 12 consecutive losing seasons from 1993-2004 and also was a bit of a laughingstock for a few years in the mid-1980s. The Brewers are very well run right now, but not for the last 40 years.

Anyway, both the Brewers and Rays are seeking their first World Series championship. MLB is guaranteed its third first-time champion in eight years, joining the 2019 Washington Nationals and 2023 Texas Rangers. Furthermore, 17 of the 30 teams have reached their league's Championship Series in the last 10 years, and 24 of 30 teams have made the postseason in the three wild-card era.

The teams that haven't made the postseason since the third wild card format became permanent in 2022 have failed to do so largely because of incompetence, not payroll. MLB has healthy competitive balance. It's a 30-team league. There will always be teams with long title droughts because that's the math. Get into the postseason and you can win, and it has never been easier to get in.

"The salary cap is the opposite of competition," MLB Players Association executive director Bruce Meyer said Friday (via ESPN). "The salary cap tells teams you can't compete too much. The results of these playoffs obviously further undermine their argument for why they think they want it. But again, that's not the reason why they want it."

MLB proposed a $243.5 million salary cap and $171.2 million salary floor in June, with limits on contract length and size. The MLBPA has insisted it will not agree to a salary cap. The collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1 and, barring a surprise agreement, it is expected the owners will lock out the players at that time.

MLB has framed the need for a salary cap around competitive balance. Even if that is the league's top priority, a salary cap would also serve to limit player payroll (each team's largest expense) and boost franchise values. CBA talks and the lockout are all about money. Everything else is secondary until the money gets figured out.
