#527. Here’s Every Company Fighting Subpoenas in Suno’s Legal Fight With Universal and Sony Music

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Venture investors and a top streaming platform are among those challenging evidence demands in the landmark AI training copyright lawsuit.

As Universal Music Group (UMG) and Sony Music continue battling Suno in court over seismic questions about AI training, both sides have sought to gather evidence from other key market players. But not everyone wants to give it over.

The landmark music industry lawsuit is nearly two years into an arduous evidence discovery process that’s set to continue through mid-2027. After that, a judge will decide whether or not it was “fair use” for Suno to train its popular AI music service on millions of unlicensed tracks owned by UMG and Sony (Warner Music Group was initially a plaintiff in the lawsuit as well, but it reached a licensing settlement with Suno last year).

For much of the lengthy and complex discovery process, the labels and Suno have been exchanging documentary evidence and deposing each other’s witnesses. Then there is what’s known as “third-party discovery” — that is, subpoenas served on anyone that’s not UMG, Sony or Suno. Oct. 14 was a key deadline for these third-party subpoenas, prompting a slew of filings that shed light on the companies that don’t want to provide the evidence sought.

One such filing came from Apple Music. According to a legal petition filed by the streamer and obtained by Billboard, Apple did willingly comply with six different document subpoenas that came from both the labels and Suno throughout the discovery process. The company says it provided “granular” data about the prevalence of Suno-generated music on its platform, as well as complex details about how it detects and tags AI music.

This apparently has been sufficient for the labels. But, according to Apple, Suno still wants more. The AI platform is allegedly demanding that an Apple representative testify at a deposition covering such wide-ranging topics as label licensing and streaming fraud. Apple says this is “overbroad” and is asking a judge to block Suno’s deposition subpoena.

Also on the Suno side, the AI company filed a discovery petition against the Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC). Suno says the nonprofit group, which collects and distributes certain digital royalties to publishers and songwriters, hasn’t complied with its subpoena seeking data on how the music streaming market changed after its platform launched in 2023.

According to Suno, the MLC’s data is “highly relevant” to the labels’ theory that AI music has eaten into their market share and royalty distributions. This could be key to the court’s fair use calculation; among other factors, the judge must weigh whether Suno-generated tracks are now competing with the same music that was used to train the AI model.

This element of the fair use inquiry is also central to another subpoena petition brought by UMG and Sony against DIY distributor DistroKid. As previously reported by Billboard, the labels demanded that DistroKid supply a monthly tally of AI-generated tracks going through its pipes, as well as the streams and royalties generated for each song.

UMG and Sony say these metrics from DistroKid, the largest DIY distributor in the industry, will help them prove that machine-generated music is competing with human-made works. DistroKid has so far refused to provide the data, claiming it “has no bearing” on the lawsuit’s central issue of AI training. UMG is separately suing DistroKid for flooding streaming platforms with “AI-generated slop.”

The labels have additionally gotten into subpoena skirmishes with two of Suno’s biggest venture investors: Lightspeed Venture Partners and Bond Capital Management. Lightspeed filed a petition to ax a document subpoena that sought, among other things, the pitch decks provided by Suno.

As for Bond, the labels are probing the firm’s decision to participate in Suno’s $400 million Series D funding round this past June. “A sophisticated venture firm like Bond does not lead a nine-figure round in a defendant that is facing mass copyright infringement liability without evaluating those claims, the training practices that gave rise to them, and the market the company intends to capture,” wrote UMG and Sony in a petition to compel Bond’s compliance with the subpoena. “That evaluation, and what Suno told Bond to secure its capital, are what plaintiffs seek.”

In another investment-related discovery matter, UMG and Sony just got permission on Monday (Oct. 26) to depose C.C. Gong, a former principal at Suno investor Menlo Ventures. Earlier this year, Gong posted on X, “I’ve personally shifted most of my listening to Suno. I was so tired of Spotify giving me the same overplayed recommendations.” The labels say this statement from a key player in Suno’s fundraising is key evidence that AI music replaces human songs.

Reps for Sony, Suno and the MLC declined to comment on the subpoenas, and reps for UMG, Apple Music, Lightspeed, Bond and Menlo did not return requests for comment.

A spokesperson for DistroKid told Billboard, “DistroKid is a third party in the ongoing litigation between Universal Music Group, Sony Music and Suno. We are committed to transparency and will continue to cooperate with the legal process as this matter proceeds.”

#1569. Lucas Bergvall: Tottenham put on alert as as top European club 'renew' transfer interest in long-term target

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By Ben Sully | 27 Oct 2026 19:51, Last updated: 27 Oct 2026 19:54

Barcelona have reportedly renewed their interest in Tottenham Hotspur midfielder Lucas Bergvall.

The Sweden international was the subject of a bid from Newcastle United in the summer transfer window.

Spurs rejected the offer despite Bergvall appearing to be keen to pursue a move away amid concerns over his game time.

In a surprise turn of events, Bergvall not only remained in North London, but he also signed a new long-term contract with the club.

Despite penning fresh terms at Spurs, the 20-year-old has continued to find regular playing time hard to come by, having made just two Premier League starts this term.

According to TEAMtalk, Barcelona are among some major European clubs that are keeping a close eye on Bergvall's situation.

The report claims that Hansi Flick's side have 'renewed' their interest in a player they have been monitoring for some time.

Barcelona were among those linked with Bergvall when he completed his move to Spurs from Djurgarden in 2024.

In addition to the Blaugrana, Napoli and Juventus are also among Bergvall's admirers, with the latter keen to strike a deal over a loan move in the January transfer window.

Bergvall is currently behind the likes of Sandro Tonali, Mateus Fernandes, Conor Gallagher and Rodrigo Bentancur at Tottenham.

The midfielder will face similar issues if he were to join Barcelona, with Flick able to call upon Rodri, Pedri, Gavi, Marc Bernal and Frenkie de Jong.

As a result, there is no obvious reason as to why the Catalan giants would pursue a deal unless one or two of their current options depart in the upcoming transfer windows.

While a move to Barcelona seems unlikely, Bergvall would still benefit from a move away from Spurs, even if it were a loan switch to a club that could offer him regular minutes to aid his development.

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Digital taper system for benefit expected in budget, but those already hit with overpayments will still have to pay

“On the surface it sounds really positive – and I really hope that it is,” says unpaid carer Guy Shahar of the government’s decision to abolish the notorious carer’s allowance cliff edge.

But Shahar is angry that most carers hit with unjust overpayments in the past will still have to repay them, for all the promise of a different future. And he notes that the cliff edge remains in place for four more years, potentially catching out more carers.

“They are effectively saying: ‘We know that the system was unfair but we will still punish you for having been the victim of an unfair system,’” he says.

The chancellor is expected to announce the introduction of a new digital taper system into the carer’s allowance benefit in the budget, in effect ending a rule that has landed hundreds of thousands of carers with life-shattering financial penalties.

Unpaid carers have broadly welcomed the move – and there is a general acknowledgment that ministers are moving positively, if slowly, to reform a system with inbuilt injustices and terrible management that birthed what became known as the carer’s allowance scandal.

But there is also disbelief that the new system – which must be built from scratch – will be live before 2030, and a feeling that the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has a lot more to do to regain the trust of unpaid carers.

Shahar and his wife, Oksana, who care for their autistic son, Daniel, were victims of the cliff edge. This rule meant that a carer who earns more than the weekly earnings limit allowed – currently £204 – will forfeit their entire week’s carer’s allowance payment, now £86.45.

A carer who earned a penny over the limit for a year would breach it by 52p but would be asked to repay £4,495.40. Last year, the Shahars were landed with a £10,000 repayment bill for relatively trivial earnings breaches dating back five years.

Oksana had earned more than the allowed limit by just £1.92 a week on average while juggling caring with part-time minimum wage jobs. In some weeks, she was paid just 38p more than the carer’s allowance earnings threshold.

Over the period, she earned a total of £505 more than the rules allowed – but was duly issued with a bill for £10,130.45 – plus a £50 civil penalty. The Shahars are challenging the charge at tribunal but the stress is real and ongoing.

Unpaid carer Elizabeth Tait, who looks after her disabled son, Oliver, is staggered that the taper system is years away: “We live in the age of tech; it is ludicrous it is going to take so long. They need to make it a priority.”

Tait, a former teacher and now a private tutor and educational consultant who ended up with a bill for £1,623 after unwittingly breaching the earnings threshold, says the carer’s allowance cliff edge symbolises a “whole crazy system”.

Reforming carer’s allowance is essential but it needs to be part of a wider examination of the crucial role of unpaid care in propping up England’s social care arrangements, she says: “We care willingly and lovingly, and yet we end up a casualty of the system.”

Andrea Tucker, who for years acted as an unpaid carer for her mother, is one of a handful of carers to challenge a cliff edge penalty and win, successfully overturning a £4,600 overpayment at a tribunal in February 2025.

As a result, she did not have to repay the money, although she suffered months of stress along the way. She welcomes the decision to introduce a taper but believes carers punished unfairly by draconian rules should be compensated.

“People repaying overpayments should no longer have to, and they should be given back what they have already paid,” she says.

Tucker recalls how for years she carefully monitored her monthly earnings from her part-time charity shop role to stay under the limit. Some weeks she would work six hours for nothing so as not to risk losing carer’s allowance.

Even so, she was hit with an overpayment when DWP officials decided – unlawfully – that she could not average her earnings over a one-year period.

There is raw bitterness among carers about the cliff edge, she says. “My friend is paying back their overpayment at £10 a month. She went £12.50 over as a result of receiving holiday pay. She always tells me: ‘I hope I die before I have paid it all back.’”

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Anchorage Positions Itself as Custody Backbone of US-Mexico Stablecoin Corridor

Anchorage Digital is planting itself at the center of one of the world’s largest money-transfer routes, expanding a custody-and-listing arrangement with Latin American crypto platform Bitso to hold the stablecoins moving between the United States and Mexico.

Announced Tuesday, the deal puts Anchorage Digital Bank’s custody beneath both sides of the corridor. MXNB, Bitso’s Mexican peso stablecoin, is now custodied by the federally chartered crypto bank across six blockchains: Arbitrum, Base, Ethereum, Polygon, Avalanche, and Arc.

In turn, Bitso has listed USDPT and USAT, the U.S. dollar stablecoins from Western Union and Tether, both of which Anchorage issues and custodies. The companies did not disclose financial terms.

The target is the U.S.-Mexico remittance corridor, which moves tens of billions of dollars a year and ranks among the biggest cross-border payment flows on earth. Much of that money still travels over correspondent banking, wire transfers and cash-pickup networks that are slow and costly, while about 15% already runs through Bitso’s infrastructure.

Anchorage is betting that stablecoins, which settle onchain in seconds around the clock, will increasingly replace those legacy rails, and that doing so requires custody that is regulated and sits wherever the liquidity is.

“The US-Mexico corridor is where stablecoins stop being a thesis and start moving real money at scale,” said Nathan McCauley, Anchorage’s CEO and co-founder, arguing the firm is now “the institutional custody layer on both sides of that flow.” Ben Reid, Bitso’s head of stablecoin, said clients can now access the full dollar-peso corridor fully onchain, backed by a federally chartered bank.

The move extends a broader race to wire stablecoins into Latin America’s payments plumbing. Circle recently pushed deeper into the region with a USDC settlement pilot in Colombia, part of a wider expansion into dozens of emerging markets as stablecoins go mainstream.

For Anchorage, the deal builds on its deepening stablecoin footprint. The bank issues Tether’s U.S.-regulated USAT stablecoin, while Bitso has been pushing its MXNB peso coin across networks like Arbitrum. By anchoring both ends of the corridor, Anchorage is aiming to become the default custodian for any issuer or bank entering the route.

#3445. PayPal Just Raised Its Profit Forecast, and the Stock Barely Moved. Here’s Why

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

At first glance, PayPal's (PYPL +2.00%) third-quarter results looked rather strong. Adjusted EPS grew by 8% year-over-year and handily beat analyst estimates, and management raised its full-year profit guidance.

However, the stock initially fell modestly after the earnings report, and as of 2 p.m. ET on earnings day, it was higher by just 1%, despite the impressive earnings beat and guidance raise. Here's what is worrying investors, and why a beat and raise isn't enough to move the stock higher.

In the third quarter, PayPal's revenue grew by just 2% to $8.58 billion, which fell short of expectations. Transaction margin dollars, a gross profit indicator, rose by only 2%.

The bigger issue is branded checkout. This is the PayPal button you can click on when shopping online. It's PayPal's most profitable business, but payment volume grew by just 1% on a currency-neutral basis. That is the fourth consecutive quarter that we've seen branded checkout growth in the 1% to 2% range. The company's transaction take rate, which is the share of each dollar it keeps as revenue, declined to 1.58% from 1.64% a year earlier, and management is only forecasting slight growth in transaction margin dollars for the fourth quarter.

To be fair, several parts of PayPal's business are performing quite well. Venmo is a standout, with payment volume growing 14% year over year. Buy now, pay later (BNPL) volume grew 25%. And payment volume on PayPal and Venmo debit cards in the United States grew by more than 60%.

These are all becoming significant growth engines for the business, and management expects financial services products like these to make up more than 20% of transaction margin dollars this year. But that still leaves 80% of transaction margin dollars coming from low-growth revenue streams.

PayPal grew its earnings per share by 8%, despite growing gross profit by just 2%. How did this happen? The answer is that cost reductions and buybacks are the primary drivers of earnings growth. The company has eliminated over 2,000 roles and is targeting over $1.5 billion in annual savings over the next few years, and PayPal has spent $6 billion buying back its own stock over the past 12 months, reducing its weighted share count by 11%.

Don't get me wrong. Cost-cutting and buybacks certainly can create value. But it's not the quality earnings growth we'd get from the core business growing again, which is what investors really want to see.

The bottom line is that PayPal trades for less than 10 times its expected 2026 earnings, so there isn't exactly a ton of growth priced in. The market wants branded checkout to grow again, but it isn't confident that management can deliver. And until it does, earnings beats that are primarily fueled by expense reductions and buybacks aren't likely to move the stock too much. I'm holding onto my shares, but I'll be watching branded checkout volume very closely over the next few quarters.

Fabregas also explained why Maxence Caqueret has started the last three matches when he was originally left off Como’s UEFA squad list at the beginning of the season.

“Max represents what today’s football is all about: Be quiet, work hard, bring your expertise and show the coach you’re ready to play. We have a meritocracy: You play if you deserve it. I’ve always shown that in my three years as a coach.”

Peter Young is a Senior Reporter with Football Italia.
