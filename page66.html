Kawhi Leonard will miss at least one week with swelling in his left knee. The Toronto Raptors said on Tuesday that the star will go through a treatment and rehabilitation plan for his knee and be reevaluated in a week.

The positive note in the release is that an MRI "revealed no acute injury" but given Leonard's injury history, him missing time this early in the season with knee discomfort and swelling is a concern for a Raptors team that had hopes of contending in a stout Eastern Conference.

Leonard played in the first two wins of the season for Toronto against the Bulls and the Wizards, averaging 19.5 points and 7.5 rebounds in those two starts but was off with his shooting to start the year. Then on Sunday he sat out with knee discomfort in a loss to the Timberwolves, and now he will miss at least a week of action and potentially more.

With a timetable of one week from Tuesday for his reevaluation, Leonard will miss at least the next four games for the Raptors. That includes Toronto's lone trip to Los Angeles this season, where they'll play a back-to-back against the Lakers on Sunday and the Clippers on Monday. So this injury means Leonard will not be playing at the Intuit Dome this season.

Leonard was traded to the Raptors from the Clippers this summer. The deal did not officially go through until the league finished its investigation into the Clippers and salary cap circumvention involving Leonard. The Clippers were severely punished by the NBA (losing five first-round picks, getting hit with a $30 million fine and seeing owner Steve Ballmer and other executives suspended), while Leonard got traded and only had to pay a $700,000 fine.

Leonard's return to the Intuit Dome figured to be greeted with plenty of animosity from the Clippers faithful for how his tenure there ended. But fans will have to keep waiting to greet Leonard back.

#1450. Spalletti: ‘Juventus players wouldn’t dare to dictate the system we play with’

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Luciano Spalletti has urged his Juventus squad to put in the same performance as they did in the first half of their 2-0 victory over Lecce when they take on Genoa in Serie A on Wednesday evening, and has made it clear that he is the only one responsible for picking the team’s tactics and formation, not the players: ‘They wouldn’t dare to dictate the system we play with’.

The seventh-placed Bianconeri are hoping to make it back-to-back victories when they travel to Genoa in Serie A on Wednesday evening (19.45 GMT, 20.45 CET), after picking up a much-needed 2-0 win away against Lecce on Sunday.

Juventus had been beaten 3-0 by Lazio the weekend prior and were held to a 1-1 draw by Cagliari the week before that, and with eight points separating Juventus and the top of the Serie A table, there is already some catching up to do.

Spalletti sat down to give his thoughts on Juve’s form, their upcoming test against Cagliari and his decision on team tactics during his pre-match press conference on Tuesday.

First up, Spalletti gave his pre-match assessment of the opposition, Genoa, who he feels will give Juventus several different things to think about.

“[It will be] a very tough team to face with their atmosphere and from a tactical point of view. They have multiple options in games: They have the physicality of Osmajic, Ostigaard, they’re good at set-pieces and at attacking spaces when they go man-for-man during the game. They also have skill with Messias, Baldanzi and El Shaarawy.

“Then there’s De Rossi, who makes a difference as well. He was my man on the pitch, he was the one who dictated my thoughts through a megaphone, my leader on the pitch.”

Spalletti faced multiple questions about his recent switch of formations. It had been suggested in the press that the players ‘asked’ to change things around, which supposedly led to Spalletti’s recent switch to a 3-5-2.

Spalletti insists that he is the only one responsible for picking the team and their tactics, though.

“This is a good set-up and it starts from the evaluation of our players, but it doesn’t make us forget the composition of our squad. We can’t penalise those who have a different set of characteristics: A small rotation is all that’s needed for it to become a 3-4-2-1, and anyone can fit into the two. Kolo Muani can play in that space and almost be like an attacking midfielder or an outside forwards. He’s one of those players.

“It’s a solution that is designed to take us into the future, not to just play these games or to try and motivate one or two players. When some people said that the group requested this approach, that is something that is not up to the players.

“I am dealing with a group of serious players, more serious than the questions that are asked of them. They wouldn’t dare to dictate the system we play with, otherwise that would be disrespectful to their teammates. It feels like there’s a made-up Whatsapp group where these sorts of questions are passed around.”

Spalletti was asked for a comment on Harry Kane’s victory at the Ballon d’Or ceremony on Monday night and whether he has spoken to any of his players about their potential of reaching the same heights.

“My players and I have other goals to talk about,” Spalletti replied. On Kane, he added: “I talked about it with my staff and we thought it was right that it was given to him. It was a logical choice given the quality of the player. He has frightening numbers, even if he’s not always highlighted like some other players. That’s how it should be.”

The next question Spalletti faced was about the schedule: Juventus have already played three games over the last nine days and have another four to go before the next international break on November 8, a fortnight away.

“We’re on the ball, the team is on the ball, the club is on the ball,” Spalletti insisted. “We have to be ready for the game because it will be a massively difficult match. We have to play like we did in the first half [against Lecce], and obviously it’s risky if we start suffering after that.

“The closeness of the matches is risky in terms of fatigue but also your state of mind. Eventually. It comes down to whether you want to do it, if you shy away from it or try to find new solutions to get through this period we’re currently in. We’ve seen that the team has the right mentality. They’ve pushed, fought, and battled to win games. They misplaced a few too many passes in the second half [against Lecce], but they showed they’re in the right place.”

Spalletti was asked if there is a chance he will revert back to a 4-3-3 at some stage.

He replied: “When I talked about making a small rotation, that was about the 3-4-2-1. If we go back to a four-man defence, we’re mixing it up even more again. You lose the tactical system on the pitch, but in certain circumstances, it helps to have an extra body in midfield.

“In the second half against Lecce, they went to a back three and we had to take a midfielder out and go to a 3-4-2-1. That gives us three or four options to do something different in attack without using up all five of our substitutions.

“It’s fairly easy to identify which 16 players will play in the match. It doesn’t matter who player from the start, but what is important is what they show if they only have 20 minutes available. We expect some serious responses, like the ones we had in Lecce.”

Juventus sit in seventh place ahead the midweek round of Serie A fixtures, with four wins, two draws and two defeats on their record after eight league games. What does Spalletti make of the team’s ranking?

“We need to go and win games and put in convincing performances like we did against Lecce,” Spalletti made it clear. “We have to identify the solution we want on a tactical level and remove a few of the mental doubts. We have to bring home results and have a clear mind.”

Spalletti was asked if the upcoming match against Napoli this weekend might affect his team selection against Genoa on Wednesday.

He replied: “We have to take everything into account. The team will stay the same, but today I’ve seen a lot of focus on the choices that we are going to make. I feel clear about it.”

And lastly, what does Spalletti make of Randal Kolo Muani’s recent form?

“He always causes fear and his teammates take advantage of that,” said Spalletti. “He always strikes fear into his opponents.

“I loved it when Douglas Luiz made it 2-0 [against Lecce], look at his reaction and how much he cares, that tells you where his mind is. He had the reaction of a very happy player, one that was happy for his teammates being able to go and score. He’s happy if the team wins, even if he doesn’t score. That state of mind is everything for me and I have total trust in him.”

Peter Young is a Senior Reporter with Football Italia.

#3294. One week out, prediction markets see Democrats as favored to sweep Congress

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The 2026 U.S. midterm election will be held a week from Tuesday, with the Republican Party looking to defend majorities in both the House and Senate, though Democrats are seen as two-to-one favorites to flip both chambers blue on prediction markets.

Speculators on platform Kalshi place a 63% chance that Democrats win control of both the House and Senate. Those odds have stayed relatively stable since the beginning of October. Meanwhile, there's just under a 10% chance that Republicans sweep both chambers, and a 29% chance Democrats win the House while the GOP maintains its majority in the Senate.

Contracts on the market are resolved using official data from the U.S. Congress.

Odds and gambling platforms do not use methodologies used by traditional political polling, and therefore are not substitutes for political polls.

On Sunday, prediction markets gave high odds to right-wing candidate Flavio Bolsonaro to win in the Brazil presidential election before polls closed, though the incumbent left-wing president Lula da Silva was re-elected. Some markets in Democratic primaries this election cycle also drew criticism for giving sky-high odds to some candidates winning who ultimately only narrowly won or lost.

Platforms, however, say that odds traders place often end up happening that exact percentage of the time. That means, essentially, that a candidate with a 20% chance to win on an event contract exchange pulls out an "upset" win 20% of the time.

Before early September, Democrats had less than a 50% chance to sweep both chambers of Congress, though their odds have risen from around 30% earlier in the year before the start of the U.S.-Iran war and higher gas prices that have hit consumers.

On Polymarket, Democrats have a 65% chance to sweep both houses.

Polls broadly show Democrats favored in the national popular vote for the House, and competitive in a series of red-state Senate races that will decide control of the upper chamber. Though House Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La., has cast doubt on those findings.

"We've been from coast to coast," he said in an appearance on Fox News Monday, "and I'm telling you, the polling is inaccurate."

#3425. Apple iPhone Duo vs Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold: The differences beyond foldable style

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

Everything you need to know before buying a foldable phone from Apple or Google.

The iPhone Duo and the Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold are two of the most premium foldable smartphones on the market, but they prioritize different things. Apple opts for a "passport-style" design, while Google sticks with a more traditional squarish foldable screen. The specs differ significantly, so you'll want to consider a lot before buying either. From screens to cameras to performance, here's how the iPhone Duo differs from the Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold.

The iPhone Duo's wider, passport-sized design is not exactly a new form factor — Samsung also embraced it with its Fold 8 this year — but it still stands out. When closed, its outer screen is 5.4 inches across. Unfold it, and you get a large 7.6-inch screen, approaching the size of the iPad mini's display. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is different, with an 8-inch inner display and a 6.5-inch outer display.

There's no right or wrong answer, so consider what's best for you. The Duo's wider aspect ratio makes it better equipped for consuming content when it's open, but the external screen may seem small. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is the opposite. The square aspect ratio makes some content — like vertical or widescreen videos — look a bit odd, but the external screen is much closer to what you'd find on a conventional smartphone. Speaking of screens, each one has its own unique features. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold has brighter screens, peaking at 3,600 nits. The Duo only reaches 3,000 nits, but its foldable screen has a nano-texture layer to reduce glare and minimize the crease.

The iPhone Duo is also heavier and thicker, weighing almost 9 ounces (254 grams) and measuring 11.3 mm when closed. The Pixel weighs 8.4 ounces (239 grams) — it may not sound like much, but you can feel the difference after holding it for a long time.

It's also thinner than the iPhone, at just 10.1 mm. Even so, Engadget's review of the Pixel 11 Pro Fold notes that it also feels significantly bulkier than Samsung's foldables. Neither is really thin by today's standards. The materials differ, too: Apple uses a titanium frame and Ceramic Shield glass on the back, while Google opted for a fiberglass composite back and its own ceramic glass on the outer screen. Both are IP68-certified for water and dust protection, which is still relatively rare among foldable phones.

Apple has attempted to optimize iOS 27 for the iPhone Duo's form factor, adding unique Duo-only features like split-screen mode. The interface also adapts to any orientation you choose — from closed to open and even in "tent" mode when it's half-folded and propped open on a table.

Despite Google's optimizations and several years' head start, Android still doesn't always take full advantage of foldable phones. Many apps simply stretch their interface to fit the larger screen, rather than displaying additional controls or different actions when you open or close the screen. It'll be interesting to see what proportion of iOS developers adapt their top apps for the iPhone Duo once buyers have it.

The Pixel 11 Pro Fold has a big advantage in the camera department. In addition to a 48-megapixel main camera, it also features an ultra-wide and a telephoto sensor for optical zoom, each with 10 megapixels. Engadget's review called it the best camera system on any foldable, despite its ultra-wide and telephoto sensors having lower resolution than those on the standard Pixel 11 Pro. The iPhone Duo has two 48-megapixel cameras—wide and ultra-wide— but no optical zoom or variable aperture like the iPhone 18 Pro.

It's still hard to compare battery life, especially since the iPhone Duo hasn't hit stores yet. Apple claims it lasts up to 31 hours of video playback on the inner screen and up to 44 hours on the outer screen. The thing is, real-world usage figures can be quite different. Google's foldable lasted about 27 hours during review testing, playing video on the external screen, more than the 24 hours the company promises. However, on charging, the Duo has the edge, reaching 50 percent in about 20 minutes. That is, of course, with a 60W power adapter. The Pixel 11 Pro Fold is limited to 30W max charging and takes 30 minutes to reach 50 percent.

Then there's the price: the iPhone Duo starts at $1,999 with 256GB of storage, but it can go as high as $3,199 for the 2TB version. The iPhone Duo hits stores on October 23 — though you can already set up your pre-order it on Apple's website. You can already buy a Pixel 11 Pro Fold, which starts at a slightly lower price of $1,899 with 256GB of storage. The prices are pretty close, so the decision will come down to your priorities for foldables. Do you want a killer camera, or do you already prefer iOS? That will make your decision easier.

While the Champions League is often used as the final decider of who the best team in Europe and the world is, these rankings can change much more quickly than just once a year.

Teams come into form and go out of it all the time, so who are the current 10 best teams in the world?

We’ve looked at league finishes, Champions League performances and other metrics to come up with our verdict.

The project at Brighton has culminated in the perfect manager working with the perfect group of players, who can now start to look towards the very top of the Premier League table.

That may be a little ambitious, with five points already separating them from the top, and Fabian Hurzeler also has to balance a Conference League run. But you cannot deny Brighton are one of the best teams in Europe right now, given the way they play football.

Defensively, there are concerns, with Marc Guehi in particular having made a poor start to the year, but they are still very strong.

Madrid do not have a great recent record in the league, but they will not care too much.

The Spanish giants’ focus is always the Champions League, and they have probably their most balanced squad in years, even if it is still stuffed with Galacticos.

The 2-1 victory at the Camp Nou was proof of their credentials as one of Europe’s best, and even if there are questions over whether Jose Mourinho is still among the elite, you would still fancy them to go far.

The Bundesliga isn’t exactly competitive when it comes to the title, but the quality is still strong, so Bayern’s feats there are to be applauded.

That competition, though, is proving to be a bit of a sticking point for the club. They have failed to reach the final since 2020 and have been knocked out at the quarter-final stage four times since then.

Barcelona have enjoyed a period of dominance in La Liga for over a decade and have won the trophy in three of the past four seasons.

Their lightning start to this campaign, albeit with a Clasico defeat, suggests they are the favourites to retain it once again, and they are a club with a sublime level of attacking talent.

To back up their credentials, they have possibly the best player in the world in their team, and it is hard to think of a better midfield than Rodri, Pedri and co.

The largest stain on their copybook, though, is the 11 years they have gone without winning the Champions League.

In that time, six different clubs have won it, not to mention Madrid winning it five times. And aside from a run of four trophies in nine years, Barcelona have not been overly successful in the competition throughout their history.

A Premier League title and a place in the Champions League final were the final hurdles for Mikel Arteta to overcome before being accepted as an elite manager.

Arsenal are just an exceptionally hard team to beat. They have some of the best defenders in the world, one of the world’s best keepers and one of the best box-to-box midfielders too.

Their attack is lacking compared to the others on the list, but when you can keep your opponents out, scoring one is all you need.

The relative lack of competitiveness in Ligue 1 is a stick often used to beat PSG, but you cannot ignore back-to-back Champions League wins.

The gap in quality between PSG and the rest of their league opponents means the club can effectively rest their stars for half a season before unleashing them at the business end against players with far more minutes in their legs.

Is that fair? Is that the natural result of such financial disparity? PSG don’t care about either answer if it means more European titles.

It means PSG are the best team in the world, with the freshest players and a manager who has three Champions League medals at home. An unbeatable combination.

#3680. Matthew Perry Made His Movie Debut In A Forgotten River Phoenix Drama

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Mary Robertson's documentary "The One About Matthew Perry" –- a play on the episode titles of "Friends," the sitcom that launched Perry into the television stratosphere -– is one of the most brutally valuable portraits of how fame and fortune solve nothing. It's the old "Richard Cory" story: a person who seems to have it all inexplicably ends their life due to hidden sadness and/or distress.

The narrative surrounding Perry, before he died, has long been one of an incurable addict. He was acutely aware of his sickness, but he couldn't maintain his sobriety. As he says, in his own words, in the documentary, he found booze and pills and whatever else to be a halcyon escape from the joylessness of his life.

As such, it's doubly tragic to realize that Perry's first big-screen role came opposite River Phoenix in William Richert's bawdy coming-of-age movie "A Night in the Life of Jimmy Reardon." Per Perry's autobiography, we know he had been drinking since the age of 14, and, according to Gus Van Sant's "Pink," Phoenix's major issue was not heroin but alcohol. They were two bruised peas in a pod, and while they were wildly different people (in the documentary, Perry is described as a cheeseburger dude whereas Phoenix was a practicing vegan), they were perfectly matched on screen.

If you or anyone you know needs help with addiction issues, help is available. Visit the Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration website or contact SAMHSA's National Helpline at 1-800-662-HELP (4357).

"A Night in the Life of Jimmy Reardon" should've been a horned-up coming-of-age classic in the ribald mode of "Fast Times at Ridgemont High." You've got a never-sexier Phoenix romancing Meredith Salenger, Ione Skye, and Ann Magnuson. But it wasn't a low-aiming sex comedy like the "American Pie" films. William Richert was adapting his novel, "Aren't You Even Gonna Kiss Me Goodbye?," and he envisioned Phoenix's title character as a half-assed beatnik who wanted out of Chicago.

Perry's Fred Roberts was his moneyed, Harvard-bound, easily manipulated best friend who got nothing out of the relationship. It's one of the few instances in his career where Perry was cast as a dupe. Early in the film, Jimmy hits up Fred to cover half of his college tuition, which he coughed up in an abortion scam. Fred resists until Jimmy promises to get him a girl who'll relieve him of his virginity. They find the girl, but, predictably, she ends up with Jimmy.

Perry and Phoenix were good together, but as a charmless rich kid, the former was playing way against his strengths. I've never seen Perry more directed, which leaves me wishing he could've collaborated with a filmmaker who might've unlocked the actor we never got to see. He was so talented, and so willing to please, but sometimes tragedy is inescapable.

After finishing in fifth position, the Argentine became the third-oldest player ever to finish inside the top ten of the Ballon d’Or. Only Gianluigi Buffon and Stanley Matthews, the winner of the first edition, were older when they reached that mark.

Buffon was 39 years and 10 months old when he reached the top ten in 2017, just a few months older than Messi now, while Matthews was 41 years old when he won the inaugural award in 1956.
