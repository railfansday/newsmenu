#3199. Quenton Nelson trade rumors: Eagles among teams that showed interest in Colts guard

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

The Philadelphia Eagles were among teams that showed interest in Quenton Nelson before the Indianapolis Colts traded their All-Pro guard to the Seattle Seahawks on Tuesday morning, according to a report from NFL insider Jeremy Fowler. The Green Bay Packers and Chicago Bears were also mentioned to be in the mix.

Fowler notes that these teams "showed varying levels of interest," so, it's unclear just how serious the Eagles were about acquiring Nelson. Or exactly when their interest was expressed.

It's not hard to imagine Howie Roseman calling the Colts about Nelson's availability because 1) he's always ready to make a big trade and 2) the Eagles' offensive line situation hasn't been very good this season. It was previously reported that Roseman was shopping for a starting caliber blocker.

But with the Eagles dropping their fifth straight game to drop to 2-5, it's much easier to picture the Eagles as sellers instead of buyers ahead of the 2026 NFL trade deadline. This doesn't mean Roseman can't acquire a player who could conceivably help the team for years to come. It does seem like the Eagles won't be interested in high-cost one-year rentals, however, which is what Nelson qualifies as since the 30-year-old is a free agent after this season.

A few more Eagles thoughts related to this trade:

The Eagles host the Seahawks in Week 15. This move makes Seattle better, which is good if you're rooting for Philly to lose and maximize their draft positioning.

Nelson was acquired in exchange for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick. The Eagles got more for trading Cam Jurgens (plus a 2027 seventh-round pick) for a 2027 fifth-round pick and a 2028 second-round pick.

The Nelson trade might point to more of a buyer's market than a seller's market, which isn't what the Eagles want to see if they're planning to deal players ahead of the deadline.

#3772. 3 Questions to Ask Yourself Before Doing a Roth Conversion in 2026

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

If your traditional IRA or 401(k) balance keeps growing, you may be headed toward a good but frustrating problem. Eventually, you'll be forced to take required minimum distributions (RMDs), which could drive up your taxes in retirement and potentially cost you more in Medicare premiums. But a Roth conversion could come to the rescue.

With a Roth conversion, you move funds from a traditional IRA or 401(k) into a Roth IRA. From there, your money gets to grow tax-free, withdrawals are tax-free, and you don't have to concern yourself with RMDs.

But a Roth conversion isn't the right choice for everyone. If you're thinking of initiating a Roth conversion this year, make sure to run through these key questions first.

Your tax bracket should play a big role in your Roth conversion decision. If your current one is high, a Roth conversion may not make sense.

Let's say you're a high earner in the 32% bracket. That means for every $1 you move to a Roth IRA, you'll owe $0.32. A $100,000 Roth conversion could cost you $32,000 in taxes, which makes it a less appealing prospect.

There's more to consider than just your current tax bracket before doing a Roth conversion. Also consider your future one.

If you expect to be in a lower tax bracket in retirement than you're in today, a Roth conversion may not pay off at all. Going back to our example, if you convert at 32% today but only end up in the 24% tax bracket in retirement, you'll have paid the IRS extra on your savings.

Of course, the total math isn't that simple because there are other factors to consider, like the benefits of having tax-free retirement income. Tax-free withdrawals could help keep your Medicare premiums down and potentially get you out of paying taxes on your Social Security benefits.

Still, the tax bracket question of now versus the future is an important one to answer. Some people may find themselves in a higher tax bracket in retirement due to accumulated savings. It's not a given that you'll always end up in a lower one, so think about your specific financial circumstances.

If charitable giving is a big part of your retirement plan, a Roth conversion may not make sense. Qualified charitable distributions allow you to transfer funds to charities directly, thereby bypassing taxes while satisfying RMDs.

It doesn't pay to do a Roth conversion if you intend to donate most of the money to charity anyway. In that situation, you're paying taxes instead of waiting to unload that money without an increase in your tax bill.

Roth conversions can be a strategic move. Just make sure a Roth conversion is right for you before taking the plunge.

U.S. House Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La., committed to a peaceful postelection transfer of power, calling concerns that President Donald Trump and congressional Republicans could contest the results if Democrats win control of Congress "absurd."

"We're going to follow the Constitution. The Constitution says when a state certifies the election of members of Congress, then they're administered the oath. That's what I'll be doing on Jan. 3 to everybody who's there," Johnson said Tuesday during a campaign stop with New York Republican Rep. Mike Lawler, one week before the Nov. 3 midterm election, in response to a question from MS NOW. "I may not like some of those outcomes, but that's our system. And for anybody to suggest otherwise is absurd."

Democrats and voting rights advocates are bracing for potential challenges if control of the House flips, as is widely projected to happen, or control changes in the Senate, which is more closely contested. Republicans have a majority in both chambers of Congress.

Trump, who has continued to falsely claim that widespread fraud cost him the 2020 election, has fueled those fears by calling to "nationalize" elections earlier this year and repeatedly questioning the security of mail voting, while also suggesting noncitizens could vote in large enough numbers to skew results. His administration sued California on Oct. 15 in a fight over noncitizens voting.

The Department of Justice also sued Alaska on Oct. 22 to block the certification of its results in the event of voter confusion. GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan will appear on the ballot beside Daniel J. Sullivan, a retired teacher whom Republicans have said is on the ballot only as a spoiler candidate.

Johnson, who in the wake of the 2020 election was a key congressional ally of Trump's in his quest to overturn results, on Tuesday said questions about a peaceful transfer were "a little silly."

"Of course we'll have a peaceful transfer of power. But it will not be transferring, because we are going to keep the majority and I'm going to be the speaker of the House and I will keep steady hands at the wheel," Johnson told reporters.

#4569. In Brooklyn Decker and Andy Roddick’s North Carolina Home, Southern Hospitality Meets Modern Charm

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

The home was, decidedly, not love at first sight for Brooklyn Decker and Andy Roddick. “One of the rooms had caved in; there was water pouring in through the ceiling,” Decker says of the first time the couple saw the Charlotte, North Carolina, Georgian-style house.

But even in its dilapidated state, the 1960s-era home was still intriguing: It was located in the favored neighborhood for the young family, and its traditional, elegant proportions were still evident through its disrepair. At first it was Roddick, the former number-one men’s singles tennis player, who urged Decker to keep the house on their short list. Soon model and actor Decker, who grew up in nearby Matthews, warmed at the chance to rehabilitate a proper Carolina home with details like dental crown molding and classic brick. “We’d moved so many times [before this house],” she says. “And we were excited to try something new. For us, that meant a historic house with some old Southern bones.”

In the entry, Roddick is seated on a vintage sofa covered in a Zak and Fox fabric. Decker stands next to the walnut staircase built by Masterpiece Staircase and finished by Tony Montognese. The custom chandelier is by Kalin Asenov, the rug is vintage, and the artwork is by Kieran Brennan Hinton.

The family’s living room, clad in mahogany paneling, is Decker and Roddick’s favorite space in the whole home. The fireplace was designed by Decker, fabricated by Tyler Ernsberger, and stained by Tony Montognese, with animal carvings done by Stan Dunham. The 1930s coffee table is by Paul László, and the light fixture is a vintage Longobard for Poliarte. The chairs, clockwise from top right, are by BDDW, vintage Swedish sourced from PRB Collection, and a vintage Guglielmo Ulrich.

The home’s foyer received the biggest glow-up, with the staircase being entirely redesigned from a curving one. Chandelier and flush-mount light designed by Decker and Kalin Asenov, sideboard by BDDW, the mirror and sofa are vintage, millwork by Bill Truitt Woodworks (with finishes by Tony Montognese), and the walnut flooring is by Richard Marshall Fine Flooring.

Decker and Roddick were no strangers to home renovation, but as evidenced just from their first walkthrough, this latest undertaking would be on a scale they had not yet explored. The home had been little touched since its construction in 1964, and the couple would be only the second owners. Aside from obvious fixes like leaking ceilings and sagging floors, the layout, which was chopped up into a warren of tight quarters, needed a full gut to allow the space to breathe and gain more light. “We were parents of two young kids,” Decker says. “So the question was, How do we respect the grandeur of the house but also make it feel hospitable to a young family and one that can live up to the demands of everyday life?”

The dining room shows off many of the home’s thematic colors, including red, yellow, and green. Drapery fabric by Kravet, the pink chairs are vintage Kerstin Hörlin Holmquist, dining chairs by Studio Valle de Valle, antique farm table sourced from Obsolete, painting by Tyler Buck Guinn, and the pendants are by Of Enso, made with a vintage silk gingham from Decker’s personal collection.

The first-floor front room continues the nature theme thanks to its hand-painted custom mural. The sofa is a vintage Carl Malmsten upholstered in a Rose Uniacke fabric; the blue club chairs are vintage and sourced from Eneby Home; the leather chair is a vintage Hans Wegner; coffee table designed by Decker and fabricated by Dunham and Ernsberger Custom Cabinetry; and the armoire is a vintage walnut wet bar sourced from 1stDibs.

The kitchen hall’s allover paneling was fabricated by Bill Woodworks and installed by Blockhaus. Flush mounts by The Urban Electric Co.; the painting is a vintage William Skilling; sideboard by Bicyclette; and the rug is vintage found on Etsy.

The kitchen is painted in a muted shade of Decker’s favorite color: green. The double islands were designed by Decker and Dunham and fabricated by Ernsberger Custom Cabinetry; the pendants are by Roman and Williams Guild; the fittings by Waterworks; and the painting is an antique signed by Adelaide Briggs.

The breakfast nook brings the garden inside with its collection of plants and green accents. The table was custom-designed by Decker and Dunham and fabricated by Ernsberger Custom Cabinetry. The chairs are by Studio Ashby and upholstered with a Lee Jofa fabric, and the light fixture is a vintage Hans-Agne Jackobsson.

To help with the transformation, the couple engaged both Ruard Veltman Architecture for the architectural design and Stanwick Dunham for contracting. The group transformed the nearly 7,000-square-foot structure into a home that celebrates its lineage without being mired in the past. Nowhere is this effort more evident than in the gracious entryway, whose original carpeted staircase curved up from stark black and white tiles. And while that design wasn’t in poor taste, its severity read as both formal and cold. The solution was twofold: both to square-off the staircase and to clad the entire area in rich walnut paneling. The result not only allows sunlight to flow down from the second story, but showcases warm millwork. “We wanted it to look like it predated our arrival, and also be a bit more humble in its materials,” Decker says.

The primary bedroom’s wood floors and cream walls are softened up with the addition of a full wall of drapery, with fabric from William Morris. The chandelier is a vintage Carlo Scarpa sourced from 1stDibs, the bed from The Expert, linens from Nordic Knots, chair by Commune for George Smith, and the painting is by Alexandra Valenti.

Another view of the bedroom spotlights the family’s dog finding his light. Art by Tyler Hays Studio, nightstands by BDDW, vintage daybed from 1stDibs, and the vintage chair is sourced from Evan Reid.

The couple’s closet displays shoes almost in a museum-like setting, inside an antique French shoe cabinet sourced from 1stDibs. Ottoman from John Derian, the paint color is Farrow & Ball White Tie, and the chandelier is from RH.

The home’s primary bath was completely gut-renovated, but Decker wanted the design to appear original to the house. Tile from Waterworks, tub from Water Monopoly, side table by BDDW, painting by George Buckner of The Florida Highwaymen, and the pendant is an antique French copper lantern sourced from 1stDibs.

For the home’s interior design, Decker had no need of hiring a professional: She’s become a decorator herself. Over the last six or so years and mostly by word of mouth, the actor has spearheaded a variety of design projects (her latest, along with her design partners at By Idiots, being the interiors of Intown Golf Club’s new properties in Houston and Raleigh). For her family home, the brief was to keep the Georgian influence while updating the aesthetic. And it remains an evolving one: “Whenever I see that furniture dolly out, I know I’ll be involved at some point,” Roddick says.

Decker achieved the cozy yet elevated mix through color, using muted shades of green (her color), red, blue, and yellow; wallpaper with natural images like leaves or rivers; and plenty of vintage patterned rugs to soften up the wood floors. “It was originally old English meets early Georgian meets Shaker,” she says. “But when our kids became older, we really wanted to incorporate and take seriously what they wanted. Because, how cool to be eight and actually have some say in the space around you? So now the house is a hodgepodge of explosive color, a lot of which is totally inspired and dictated by them.”

The children’s playroom has a vintage feel while still being cozy. Sofa by RH, mohair chair and ottoman by Sam Klemick, wallpaper by Charles Voysey, and the shades are Fabricut fabric.

The couple’s daughter wanted her new room to match her softball team’s colors of red, white, and blue. Bed designed by Decker and fabricated by Liven UP, vintage chandelier sourced from PRB Collection, drapes from Anthropologie, and the rug is sourced from Hall Pass Vintage.

Decker’s office adds a bit of whimsy with a ceiling papered in a Pierre Frey design. Rug by Studio MRS for Nordic Knots, sofa by Coley Home, the desk is vintage, and the chandelier is by Apparatus.

An upstairs landing is one of Decker’s favorite places to snuggle with the family pets. Pendant by Roman and Williams Guild, dresser by Orior, the chairs are vintage Paolo Buffa upholstered in a Rose Uniacke fabric, table is vintage sourced from PRB Collection, and the pillows were made by Chancery Custom with vintage tapestry fabric sourced by Decker.
