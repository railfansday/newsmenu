#893. Wigan pine for FA Cup glory as not even victory can lift them off foot of League One

📅 Oct 18, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Gary Caldwell, club captain in 2013 and now manager, needs much more than the weekend win against Reading to disperse the clouds

If the 2013 FA Cup final represented a blip for the Sheikh Mansour-era Manchester City in their quest for total domination of English football, it was a historic moment for their conquerors, Wigan Athletic.

Memories of that famous day at Wembley still endure for Latics supporters despite the relegation from the Premier League that followed. Yet watching the modern‑day Wigan, who remain bottom of League One despite a 1-0 win against Reading on Saturday, makes the toppling of City in a Cup final seem an epoch ago.

The club, perhaps understandably, continue to hark back to their halcyon days. Last season they brought back Gary Caldwell – club captain in 2013 – for his second stint as manager, having led the club to promotion from this division in 2015-16. Choosing to swap Exeter for a return to the Latics was seen as a risky move by Caldwell, considering the shoestring budget imposed upon him by Mike Danson, the owner who is keen to sell.

Finances are ultra-tight at the Greater Manchester club amid dwindling crowds and losses on the pitch and on the balance sheet. The local businessman Danson and his family saved Wigan from liquidation in June 2023 after mismanagement from previous owners brought a points deduction and relegation from the Championship. Three successive mid-table finishes in League One under their stewardship have brought a semblance of stability, but this season they have plummeted.

The problem is the Dansons have been unable to find an ambitious buyer to take the club to the next level. Now the prospect of a first season in the fourth tier since 1996‑97 is a distinct possibility, although victory against Reading – the Latics’ second league win of the campaign, sealed by Scott Hogan’s 71st-minute strike – made the picture a little rosier.

“Some of our play was tremendous,” Caldwell said. “Our spirit, our character, our determination not to let the ball go in the net was fantastic. It was pretty stressful towards the end. We need to try and build on this. You have to keep the players believing and committing. There’s still so many aspects of our game that need to be better, but a result makes you feel better about yourself psychologically.”

The presence of Leam Richardson in the opposition dugout at the Brick Community Stadium gave fans more reason to reminisce about better days. Richardson, who claimed the League One title as Wigan manager in April 2022, was sacked in November of the same year just 16 days after signing a three-year contract.

Caldwell has also found himself under pressure. The former centre‑back has been criticised by fans for his team’s uninspiring style of play, which has produced just six goals in nine games, the fewest in League One. They went 447 minutes without a league goal until Hogan, the veteran forward, converted after Christian Saydee’s cross was parried out by the Reading goalkeeper, Joel Pereira.

And while Wigan’s keeper, Joe Whitworth, was regularly called into action to see out the win, the home team were still good value for three points, allowing Caldwell and his squad to breathe. The manager’s backers will rightly point to the lack of spending by the club in the summer, with the £1.5m generated by the sale of Sam Tickle to Bristol City not yet reinvested on players and an overreliance on young loanees to prop up the squad.

Danson and the club’s hierarchy have issued rallying cries to supporters to return to the stadium in an effort to generate more revenue, thus making the club more appealing to new owners, but why should fans part with hard‑earned cash to watch such insipid football?

#5948. Electric Mercedes-AMG GT SUV unveiled with 1,153 hp and 710 kW charging

📅 Oct 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mercedes-AMG has unveiled the GT SUV, an all-electric performance SUV with up to 1,153 hp from three axial-flux motors and DC fast charging at up to 710 kW.

It’s AMG’s answer to the Porsche Cayenne Electric, and on paper, it may be more impressive. The first units hit US dealerships in summer 2027.

AMG has been tuning Mercedes SUVs for decades, but the company says this is the first full-size SUV it has developed entirely on its own. It’s the second vehicle on the dedicated AMG.EA electric architecture, after the electric GT 4-Door Coupé revealed earlier this year, and it borrows that car’s motors and battery. Mercedes teased the 1,000+ hp electric AMG SUV back in August.

Mercedes is launching three versions in the US:

Those are peak numbers, and Mercedes’ acceleration times are still estimates. Continuous output is 711 hp on both the GT 63 and GT 55, and 362 hp on the GT 53.

The axial-flux motors are the interesting hardware here. They’re flat, pancake-shaped motors that deliver a lot more power for their weight than the radial-flux motors in most EVs. Mercedes bought UK axial-flux specialist YASA in 2021, and the tech has already made its way down to the AMG CLA 45 with three axial-flux motors.

The GT 63 and GT 55 get a 128 kWh (usable) 800-volt battery pack, while the GT 53 makes do with 106 kWh. Mercedes uses tall, narrow cylindrical cells with an NCMA cathode and silicon in the anode, and it claims more than 298 Wh/kg at the cell level. Each cell is bathed in non-conductive oil for cooling.

The charging specs are the most impressive we have seen coming from a legacy automaker. Mercedes rates the big-battery versions for up to 710 kW DC, with a 10-80% charge in 12 minutes. That’s enough to add up to 454 km (282 miles) of WLTP range in 10 minutes. The GT 53 peaks at 600 kW and does 10-80% in 11 minutes.

But there’s a catch for US buyers. Most of the fastest public chargers here, like the ones IONNA is rolling out across the country, are rated for 400 kW. Mercedes lists NACS among the five charging standards the GT SUV supports, so Supercharger access shouldn’t be a problem. Charging anywhere near 710 kW will be.

The fastest charger in the US pipeline is a 750 kW unit that has already slipped to next year, while China already has 2,250 kW chargers in service.

WLTP range is 620 to 700 km (385 to 435 miles) for the GT 63 and GT 55, and 597 to 668 km (371 to 415 miles) for the GT 53. Expect lower EPA numbers. This thing is heavy: the GT 63 SUV weighs 2,695 kg (5,942 lb), even with a slippery 0.27 drag coefficient.

Like the GT 4-Door, the SUV gets AMG’s simulated engine sound. In its AMGFORCE Sport+ setting, it plays back recorded V8 sounds on the GT 63 and GT 55, and a six-cylinder on the GT 53, with virtual gearshifts you can trigger with the paddles. Mercedes CEO Ola Källenius is a big believer in the fake V8. Thankfully, there’s a “Silent” setting – so the fake sounds are optional.

Rear-axle steering of up to 6 degrees comes standard. The GT 63 also gets AMG Active Ride Control+ as standard, which swaps mechanical anti-roll bars for active hydraulics and air springs.

Inside, there’s a 14-inch central screen merged with a 10.2-inch driver display, plus an optional 14-inch passenger screen. It’s a five-seater with 21.4 cubic feet of cargo space behind the rear seats and a small 2.8-cubic-foot frunk.

Acceleration is a dead heat. The Mercedes has a bigger pack and charges 310 kW faster on paper. Mercedes hasn’t released pricing yet.

The 0-60 times don’t matter much anymore. Every flagship EV now does it in the 2-second range, and the GT 63 SUV and the Cayenne Turbo Electric literally tie at 2.4 seconds. Nobody is cross-shopping a 6,000-lb SUV on launch times.

Charging at 400kW flat would do 10-80% in 14 minutes on a 128kWh pack. The real-world improvement if you had access to a 700kW charger is a couple of minutes. And we're starting to see 500kW pop up in Europe.

Very much in the category of first-world-problems.

What matters is the battery and the charging. A 128 kWh usable pack with silicon anodes, oil-immersed cells and a 710 kW peak is serious engineering, and it puts Mercedes clearly ahead of Porsche on the spec that actually affects how you live with an EV. That’s not something we could say about Mercedes’ first-gen EQ cars, and AMG deserves some credit for it.

But this is a halo product, and the market for $150,000-plus electric SUVs is tiny. Mercedes knows it: it just cut the EQE and the Maybach EQS SUV from its 2027 lineup. And in the US, there’s almost nowhere to charge at 710 kW today, so buyers will mostly be paying for headroom on future stations.

Now, I’m curious to see the regen on those axial flux motors. I’m hearing they can recover so much power that they can fully replace brakes, even in performance driving.

#4531. Steam's Achievement Sets beta aims to stop new content breaking your completion status

📅 Oct 17, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Valve is beta testing Steam Achievement Sets, a feature that lets developers group a game's achievements into collections.

In an announcement, the company said that games "live longer than ever, continuing to receive updates through new DLC, expansions, and seasons", but that achievements added with new content "can be hard to discover and can accidentally break the completion status of players who have already finished the game".

That's why developers can now specify the number of sets and the purpose of each "to match their game's specific needs", so one game might group its achievements into single-player and multiplayer groups, while another could use eight class-based sets. Players still unlock achievements as usual, and Steam then sorts what they've unlocked into the developer-created sets, which Valve says makes it "easier to see what you have completed and to find other related achievements you can unlock next".

Each set has its own progress bar, and completing one adds a "set complete" trophy to the player's profile. If new achievements are later added to a set, completion is "preserved".

Developers can also create new sets as a game grows, which Valve says makes it "easy for returning players to see what new things they can unlock, while also preserving their prior completion status until they play again".

There are two types of set. Base Game sets "contain achievements that can be earned by all players who play a game", with progress and trophies shown in blue, while DLC sets group achievements that can only be earned once additional content has been bought and are shown in purple.

Interestingly, DLC achievements "are not included in your overall game progress until you have access to the content", so players can track what they have unlocked in new content separately.

Developers can also archive individual achievements or entire sets, which Valve says could suit "season-based games or enabling time-limited holiday achievements". Players who had already unlocked an archived achievement or set "will still see it on their profile", but archived achievements "won't show up on the profile pages of users who missed the unlock nor block their progress toward completing a set or game".

Alongside the sets comes an updated achievements page, reachable from the Games List on the web and from games in the Steam Library, and Search now has filters for specific sets and for locked or unlocked achievements. Players can also compare their progress with "up to three friends, including rare achievements, completed sets, and progress in DLC sets".

Elsewhere, the Perfect Games tab is now called Completed and "expands to show both completed games and games with completed achievement sets", games played via Family Sharing now appear in the Games List, and friends who played a game that way are eligible for friend comparison. Store and Library pages will also show if a game has achievement sets.

The beta is available to players and developers, and Valve says it will "continue making improvements during the beta", adding that "as developers begin using the new tools, you'll start seeing these changes appear for supported games".

Games that have already added achievement sets are Against the Storm, Age of Wonders 4, American Truck Simulator, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, Crusader Kings 3, Dredge, Euro Truck Simulator 2, Forza Horizon 5, Flock Around, He is Coming, LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight, Left 4 Dead 2, Portal 2, PowerWash Simulator, PowerWash Simulator 2, Team Fortress 2, and Victoria 3.
