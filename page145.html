#278. JetBlue stock surges 20% as company outlook calms investors

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

JetBlue Airways reported a bigger loss in the third quarter compared with last year, but shares surged as the carrier's upbeat forecast that it will return to profitability appeared to calm investors after a Monday rout.

JetBlue stock was up nearly 19% in early trading Tuesday after hitting a new low a day earlier as investors fretted about the company's financial future.

The Monday drop came after the Wall Street Journal reported Friday that a group of the airline's senior creditors is reportedly working with restructuring adviser PJT Partners.

"I think there is a significant disconnect between the narrative in the bond market and what our financial results are demonstrating, CEO Joanna Geraghty said on the company's earnings call Tuesday.

The airline said its revenue per available seat mile, a key metric measuring how much operating revenue a carrier generates for every seat flown one mile, would be up as much as 22% in the fourth quarter, bringing the annual increase to an expected 13.5% to 14.5%

JetBlue on Monday posted a net loss of $257 million for the three months ended Sept. 30, compared with a loss of $143 million a year earlier. The carrier's fuel bill rose 61% to $868 million.

The carrier said it ended the quarter with $2.2 billion in liquidity and that its unencumbered asset base is now $7 billion, including $2.8 billion in aircraft and engines.

JetBlue had total debt of $8.4 billion as of Sept. 30, the carrier said in its earnings filing Monday.

"Importantly, we believe we have the liquidity and financing options to create a strong bridge to at least $1 EPS and positive free cash flow in 2028," CFO Ursula Hurley said in a statement. "We remain focused on disciplined cost execution and capital management as we work toward sustained profitability and positive free cash flow."

JetBlue last posted an annual profit in 2019. The airline is in the middle of a multiyear turnaround plan that includes cutting cuts and boosting revenue by investing in premium seats, like a new domestic first class section that recently went on sale.

Fuel costs have eaten into airline profits across the board. JetBlue said travel demand has remained strong despite higher fares.

JetBlue's average fares rose more than 19% to $245.11 in the third quarter from $205.67 in the year-earlier period.

The airline has struggled not just with higher costs but also with competition from larger rivals.

It sought to acquire now-defunct carrier Spirit Airlines, but that deal was upended by a federal judge who ruled it was anticompetitive. A judge also blocked a regional partnership between JetBlue and American Airlines, which the airlines argued would help them compete better against carriers in the congested Northeast U.S. JetBlue now has a partnership with United Airlines.

#4955. NBA Embraces the CFTC on Prediction Markets as the NFL Fights It in Court

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

NBA Embraces the CFTC on Prediction Markets as the NFL Fights It in Court

The NBA has struck a deal with the top U.S. derivatives regulator to help police prediction markets, becoming the latest major sports league to formalize an integrity partnership as betting on games moves onto federally regulated platforms.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission said Monday it signed a memorandum of understanding with the basketball league to protect the integrity of professional basketball in prediction markets.

Under the agreement, the two will share information and communicate regularly on integrity matters while working to keep the markets fair and transparent.

“The NBA joins the MLB and NHL in taking a responsible step toward protecting sports integrity and market participants from abuses in prediction markets like insider trading and fraud,” CFTC Chairman Michael Selig said in a statement, thanking Commissioner Adam Silver for prioritizing “the integrity of professional basketball and America’s financial markets.”

The pact reflects how quickly sports-outcome betting has migrated to CFTC-regulated venues like Kalshi and Polymarket, which let users trade “event contracts” on real-world outcomes, including who wins a given game.

That shift has raised integrity concerns familiar to the gambling world, chiefly the risk that insiders could trade on nonpublic information. The league-regulator MOUs are meant to build a channel for flagging suspicious activity, following the NHL’s move to become the first major league to license its trademarks to a prediction market and MLB’s integrity agreement tied to a Polymarket deal.

The NBA is a pointed addition, given the league is still reeling from one of the biggest betting scandals in recent memory. An October 2025 FBI operation led to the arrest of Miami Heat guard Terry Rozier and a coach over alleged involvement in gambling schemes, including one centered on manipulating game outcomes to influence bets. The case put a spotlight on exactly the kind of insider misconduct these partnerships are meant to catch, making the NBA’s cooperation with the CFTC especially resonant.

The partnership also lands amid a fierce jurisdictional fight over whether these markets fall under federal or state authority.

The CFTC recently moved to define event contracts as “swaps” squarely within its remit while excluding casino-style gambling, and a court battle pitting the NFL against Kalshi has drawn the Supreme Court’s attention. Notably, the NFL has so far balked at signing on, making the NBA’s cooperation a contrast among the major leagues.

The stakes are only growing. Analysts at Bernstein have projected prediction markets could generate billions in revenue by the end of the decade, and new entrants keep arriving, with Blockchain.com among the latest to seek CFTC approval to offer event contracts.

#4367. The EV Tax Credit Could Come Back. Here's What Stands In The Way

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Browse this and many other websites without banner ads, personalized tracking and video ads.

Some Democratic leaders are seeking to revive the $7,500 federal electric vehicle tax credit if they regain control of Congress after the upcoming midterm elections. Bringing back the incentive would likely boost EV sales and help the U.S. catch up with other countries in the global EV race, but it would have to overcome a mountain of political hurdles before that happens.

More than 160 House Democrats are backing proposed legislation from California Rep. Mike Levin and Illinois Rep. Sean Casten to restore Biden-era clean energy tax credits that were axed last year, Politico reported Monday. Called the Cheap Energy Act, the bill seeks to revive tax credits under the Inflation Reduction Act, including EV incentives. It also aims to modernize the electricity grid without making ordinary customers foot the bill for upgrades needed for AI data centers.

But the political argument for EV incentives has changed. Politico reported that the tax credit may now be a liability for Democrats, following years of Republican attacks on EVs. President Trump has repeatedly disparaged EVs, once saying, “They don’t go far. They cost a fortune.” He softened his stance after Tesla CEO Elon Musk poured hundreds of millions of dollars into his 2024 presidential campaign, but Trump has continued to attack broader clean energy policies.

The policy shift has caused a resurgent gas car industry in the U.S., even as Europe, China, and much of the developing world moves rapidly toward EVs.

This incessant thrashing of EVs may be taking their toll. Voters might associate the tax credit with benefitting wealthy coastal elites who could already afford EVs without it, Politico reported. And EVs continue to suffer from deep polarization across America. According to a Pew Research Center survey from April, Americans were more likely to consider hybrids than EVs. Interest in EVs was far down from what it was in 2022, with popularity waning among Democrats as well.

The bigger obstacle to restoring the credits, however, may be divisions within the Democratic Party itself. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called for reviving clean energy tax credits at a gala in Washington but notably left EVs out of his remarks, per the outlet. Democratic Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan has also struck a cautious tone on EVs. “I want all of the energy,” she told Politico. “I don’t want just one. I don’t want to pick and choose.”

Even if Democrats regain control of both chambers in November, restoring the tax credit would require legislation to pass the House and Senate and be signed by the president, who could veto it. Overriding that veto would require a two-thirds majority in both chambers, which would be a much higher bar than a simple majority. It's not impossible, but many Republicans would need to support it too, which given the current climate seems unlikely.

The perception problem with EVs, however, doesn’t square with reality. The tax credit had income limits and was designed to benefit low and middle income earners, not the ultra wealthy. It helped drive a record 1.3 million new EV sales in 2024 in the U.S., and helped erect a domestic battery manufacturing industry to reduce America’s reliance on China. Much of this investment has gone in Republican governed states like Georgia and South Carolina, boosting local economies and generating thousands of jobs.

EVs today are also an order or magnitude superior to combustion cars in terms of software, refinement, efficiency, and operating costs. Automakers continue pushing the boundaries with range, with many new EVs able to cover more than 400 miles on a single charge. America in Q3 surpassed 80,000 public fast-charging ports, a new milestone.

The technology is clearly advancing rapidly, but it will also need favorable policies for sustained growth. If not, it could remain hostage to political cycles while the rest of the world races away.

At the 2020 Battery Day, Elon Musk took the stage and announced that the EV maker would move away from 2170 cells to 4680 cells. These new and “improved” batteries eventually made their way into the packs of 2023 Tesla Model Ys and later the Cybertruck.

It looked promising, with Musk touting that the 4680 form factor (46mm in diameter and 80mm tall) would deliver up to five times the capacity of a 2170 cell and up to six times the power. In addition to the larger size, Tesla also considered improving its chemistry, with a high-nickel cathode and a silicon anode.

However, once the 4680s left the production line and reached consumers, as with the 2023 Model Y AWD, Tesla owners were disappointed: the battery delivered poor charging performance, and Tesla canceled the 4680 version of the Model Y entirely.

After the Model Y, the Tesla Cybertruck was slated to receive the new 4680s in its battery pack. When Tesla announced the Cybertruck, the crowd was disappointed to learn the electric pickup would use revised versions of the 4680 cells, dubbed Cybercells. Tesla said this second outing had 10% higher energy density, but was about on par with the 2170 cells that it replaced.

On top of the only modest bump in energy density, customers also complained that charging performance was still subpar, with charging times stretching beyond 30 minutes from 10% to 80% state of charge. Tesla did try to rectify the issue with a software update and roll out its 800-volt charging standard, but that did little to improve the Cybertruck’s reputation at the plug.

After the October 15 launch of the Tesla Roadster this year, sources learned it uses 4680 cells. Of course, most people’s eyes were glued to the stunt demo with the thrusters, but hardcore Tesla fans were wincing at the fact that Tesla’s still trying to make its 4680 work after all this time.

In an interview with Hagerty, Tesla’s chief of engineering, Lars Moraby, said the Roadster uses “special cells” with special chemistry. That, in turn, will improve charging performance over the Cybertruck. The Roadster’s 4680 cells use aluminum instead of steel to house the cells, improving heat dissipation and theoretically improving charging performance.

The main reason charging performance on the Cybertruck and Model Y was so poor was subpar thermal management. The Roadster will use double-sided cooling jackets and cool each 4680 cell individually. Improving the cooling system improves not only charging performance, but also real-world performance. Moraby said that Tesla Roadster owners will be able to push the car on track for about 20 minutes at a time, with a 10% to 80% charge happening in just 10 minutes.

For now, that is all claims and yet-to-be-delivered promises. Tesla has also yet to deliver the Roadster to consumers, even asking for a hefty sum from interested clients who want to reserve one right now. Early adopters might think the brand has finally fixed its 4680 format once and for all, but I think the rest of us should wait until Tesla actually delivers the Roadster after all these years.
