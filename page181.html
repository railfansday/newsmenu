#5352. <a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/sponsored/technology-personalizes-healthcare-hospitality" hreflang="en">Technology That Personalizes Healthcare Hospitality</a>

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Technology That Personalizes Healthcare Hospitality" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Beyond Automation: How Technology Can Help Deliver More Personalized Healthcare Hospitality

Healthcare foodservice balances hospitality, nutrition, diet compliance and patient preference in every order. That complexity makes healthcare an important proving ground for food technology.

Compass Digital and food robotics company Blendid recently introduced Blendid Pro in Rochester, Minnesota. Developed with Morrison Healthcare, the robotic beverage system operates within a hospital production kitchen, preparing customized smoothies, shakes and protein drinks for patients, employees and visitors.

L.J. Gearhart, director of innovation at Compass Digital, Compass Healthcare’s technology arm; Vipin Jain, CEO and co-founder of Blendid; and Chef Michael Raub, Morrison Healthcare executive chef, discuss how the collaboration is shaping the future of healthcare foodservice.

Why is healthcare foodservice such an important area for technology innovation?

RAUB: Healthcare foodservice has to balance hospitality, nutrition, diet compliance and patient preference in every order. Technology can help manage that complexity, but only when it improves the food experience and gives patients more consistent, personalized choices. Giving chefs more time to do what they do best is crucial to maintaining our highest standards.

GEARHART: Healthcare foodservice is a high-frequency, high-touch part of the patient experience. Teams balance dietary needs, food safety, clinical requirements, personal preference and operational efficiency, often within one order. That makes foodservice a meaningful place to prove whether technology can create value in real healthcare workflows.

The test is not whether a technology is new; it is whether it addresses a meaningful need for patients, associates and the operation. Blendid Pro applies robotics to a specific production challenge while keeping culinary expertise and patient needs central.

JAIN: Healthcare makes the potential of food robotics particularly tangible. Customization can have greater significance for patients who experience poor appetite, fatigue, swallowing challenges or treatment-related discomfort. Others may require careful control over ingredients, nutrients or texture.

Technology that can reliably execute an approved recipe with precision can help culinary and nutrition teams deliver more individualized options. Automation therefore becomes more than a matter of speed; it becomes a platform for personalization and consistency.

How did the healthcare environment influence the development of Blendid Pro?

RAUB: It had to work inside a real hospital kitchen, not around it. That meant using approved ingredients, reliable recipes and sanitation standards while producing beverages that tasted good and felt appropriate for patients. It also had to perform consistently to ensure proper viscosity for patients with special needs.

JAIN: We could not simply place a robotic smoothie kiosk designed for another setting inside a hospital. Healthcare has distinct operational, sanitation and dietary requirements, and the system needed to fit naturally into an established production kitchen.

We spent approximately 18 months developing and refining the solution with Blendid, Compass Digital and Morrison Healthcare. The process included prototype testing, recipe development, tasting sessions and input from culinary, clinical, operational and engineering experts.

The kitchen should not have to reorganize itself around the technology. The technology needed to adapt to ingredients already in use, approved recipes, existing supply-chain processes and the team’s preferred workflow.

GEARHART: That approach is essential to successful healthcare innovation. A solution may perform well in a controlled demonstration, but its value depends on how it works during a busy day in a working hospital kitchen and whether it strengthens connected services.

At Compass Digital, our role is to connect technology to the realities of the operation: what associates need, how the solution fits existing processes, what data should be visible and what must be true for it to create sustainable value.

What does the future of automation in healthcare foodservice look like?

RAUB: The future is more personalized, but still human. Automation should help chefs deliver more choice, consistency and hospitality so patients receive what fits their needs and feels thoughtfully prepared. Automating lower-level tasks can give chefs more time to focus on hospitality.

JAIN: We believe the future will be increasingly personalized. Food technology will enable culinary and nutrition teams to provide more options tailored to individual needs without sacrificing operational consistency.

Smoothies and nutritional beverages are one application. Blendid foodOS platform is capable of executing a broad range of customized recipes and offer flexible foodservice options in a kitchen.

GEARHART: The future will be defined by partnerships among technology, culinary talent, clinical expertise, operations and hospitality. Blendid Pro is one example of how robotics can become a practical layer within that larger model.

Over time, the opportunity is to connect patient needs and meal ordering, personalized production, delivery, tray visibility, operational analytics and the workflows that support healthcare teams. That same thinking is behind Elevra, Compass Digital’s foundation for connecting Environmental Services, Food and Nutrition through shared data, workflow visibility and intelligent assistance.

Patients may never see the technology behind it. They should simply receive an option that meets their needs, tastes good and helps them feel cared for. The opportunity is to use robotics and digital capabilities to make healthcare more consistent, responsive, coordinated and human.

#5348. <a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/walmart-taps-startup-general-medicine-expand-better-care-services" hreflang="en">Walmart taps startup General Medicine to expand Better Care Services</a>

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Walmart taps startup General Medicine to expand Better Care Services" style="max-width:100%; height:auto; border-radius:8px;" loading="lazy">

Walmart is teaming up with startup General Medicine to expand its Better Care Services, turning the digital healthcare platform into a more comprehensive healthcare marketplace.

With the updated experience, which launches today, consumers can compare providers, see their potential costs with and without insurance and connect to care. Walmart and General Medicine provided Fierce Healthcare with a first look at the partnership.

Walmart rolled out Better Care Services in January as a digital healthcare network offering virtual urgent care and behavioral health consultations. Walmart executives declined to provide utilization figures for Better Care Services but said adoption has "exceeded its expectations."

Now powered by General Medicine's "healthcare store" infrastructure, the updated Better Care Services has evolved from a directory of provider organizations to a site where customers can search and browse more than 900 different products and services, including urgent care—virtual via multiple providers and in-person via Solv—as well as lab tests and a wide range of telemedicine services for primary care, chronic conditions, mental health and care coordination.

The expanded marketplace brings together services offered by General Medicine, alongside more than 10 existing third-party providers, with plans to add more providers over time, according to Walmart executives.

With the expanded experience through Better Care Services, customers can see estimated out-of-pocket costs based on individual insurance information, alongside cash prices where offered, and evaluate available services based on factors such as location, cost, availability and personal preferences. Customers can also access the booking or other fulfillment options available for each service.

Consumers can access Better Care Services on Walmart.com, through the Walmart app or Sparky, Walmart's conversational AI shopping assistant. General Medicine powers the underlying platform and manages Better Care Services customer accounts and information separately from Walmart's retail business.

Consumers can shop by provider brand or by care need. Some of the healthcare categories available on Better Care Services now include urgent care, cold and flu, skin care, lab tests, mind and mood, weight loss, sexual health, women's health, men's health, digestive health, pain and injury, heart and chronic conditions.

"That updated platform is making it easier and effortless for customers to find care, to book care and to understand their insurance costs," Leslie Fletcher, vice president of growth and partnerships at Walmart, told Fierce Healthcare in an interview.

"We see a real need to make that effortless from end to end. The strategy for Walmart is that customers are already turning to us for everyday health and wellness needs, from their Rx to their food to their OTC [over the counter]. It's just fragmented, so we want to be able to take a customer from symptom to care to care delivered, and that could be anything in your combined basket, same day, within minutes, even delivered to your door or picked up at a store. And this inner layer helps us deliver that faster experience and make it super actionable for the customer," Fletcher said.

General Medicine, started by the founding team that built PillPack and Amazon Pharmacy, is an online marketplace where customers can search, compare and buy care, see insurance and cash prices before deciding, and get guidance from a licensed clinician. General Medicine launched nationwide in May 2025. Earlier this month, the company picked up $120 million in series B funding to build out its "healthcare store" and expand into more categories of care. It's raised $152 million to date.

Walmart brings the customer-facing experience, while General Medicine provides the technology that connects care options, coverage information and potential costs behind the scenes, executives said.

General Medicine makes Better Care Services more "shoppable," making it easier for consumers to understand their insurance coverage and compare their out-of-pocket costs across multiple providers, noted Amanda Deegan, head of Better Care Services at Walmart.

"We'll be able to show that co-insurance, show the cash pay price across providers before that care is delivered or even booked," she noted.

TJ Parker, General Medicine CEO, says the partnership with Walmart will bring its healthcare shopping experience to a much larger audience. About 150 million customers shop at Walmart each week through its U.S. physical and online channels.

"We’re building the care catalog that makes options easier to understand, compare, and buy. Walmart is an important partner in bringing that vision to life, showing how a more shoppable healthcare experience can meet customers where they already turn for everyday needs," he said.

Walmart sees the marketplace as part of a broader strategy to connect more pieces of a consumer's health journey across the retailer's entire portfolio of services, including pharmacy, grocery and over-the-counter health and wellness products.

"Yes, this is a digital experience, but it's also leveraging the pharmacists we have across 4,600 stores," Fletcher said, describing a model where a Walmart customer at the pharmacy counter who needs a medication refill can quickly connect with a clinician through Better Care Services and ultimately fill the resulting prescription at Walmart.

"It empowers the pharmacists, and we believe that's part of our secret sauce as well, that human touch you have with all the stores," she said.

Seven years ago, Walmart had bold ambitions to become a big player in healthcare, aiming to disrupt primary care by building doctor-staffed clinics inside or adjacent to its Supercenters. The retailer built out more than 50 health centers and acquired telehealth company MeMD in 2021. Five years later, in 2024, Walmart shuttered all 51 of its Walmart Health clinics, citing a lack of profitability. It also shut down its virtual care business.

Walmart has shifted its healthcare strategy to focus on operating a digital healthcare marketplace, which plays to the company's existing physical and retail assets without having to deliver the clinical care itself.

Walmart aims to bring a retail-like experience to healthcare.

"Just like we give our customers walking into our store the choice of bananas and strawberries and apples, we are giving them that same choice when it comes to healthcare. We're really good at retail. We understand what customers want. We're really good at understanding how to merchandise that, and we think these same principles can absolutely apply to healthcare, which traditionally has not done that," Deegan said.

Walmart’s bigger advantage may be at the fulfillment end of the healthcare journey, allowing customers to move from receiving care to filling prescriptions and potentially combining prescriptions, groceries and other health products for same-day delivery, Fletcher noted.

General Medicine plays two roles in Walmart’s Better Care Services: it provides the underlying technology that powers the marketplace and is also one of the care providers consumers can choose. Walmart determines which providers participate, and General Medicine appears only in the categories where it offers care.

The company spent six months making its technology "multi-tenant," allowing its healthcare store infrastructure to power experiences beyond General Medicine's own platform. "Given the breadth of selection that Walmart has, we're excited about, moving forward, all the opportunities to integrate this experience with the broader Walmart ecosystem," Parker said.

The Walmart partnership could serve as a model for General Medicine to power healthcare marketplaces for other organizations. Parker said the company is exploring similar partnerships.

"The infrastructure has been built in a way that it could be extensible to other types of entities," he said, citing opportunities to work with health systems, manufacturers and payers.

#2503. Arsenal transfer news: Gunners 'offer' moves them ahead of Liverpool in race for £20m wonderkid

By Lewis Blain | 26 Oct 2026 07:58, Last updated: 26 Oct 2026 07:59

Arsenal are reportedly stepping up their pursuit of Mexican wonderkid Gilberto Mora as they look to beat several European rivals to his signature.

The Gunners have already proposed a transfer plan that would involve signing the teenager before sending him out on loan.

With Premier League rivals Liverpool also heavily linked in recent months, Arsenal could be positioning themselves to secure one of Mexico's most exciting young talents.

Arsenal are said to have made an 'offer' for Mora, with former Mexico defender Claudio Suarez confirming the Gunners have shown serious interest in the Club Tijuana youngster.

"I know for a fact that he's had offers," Suarez said speaking to Unanimo (via Sport Witness). "Arsenal showed interest, and so did Bayern Munich. Arsenal's plan was to sign him and then loan him out, since he might not have been a starter for them right away."

The 18-year-old has emerged as one of Mexican football's brightest prospects, scoring 15 goals in 62 appearances for Tijuana while also establishing himself on the international stage.

Mora scored for Mexico in their Gold Cup semi-final victory over Honduras last year and featured four times at the 2026 World Cup this summer, further raising his profile among Europe's leading clubs.

Arsenal have tracked his development for some time, with previous reports indicating they were keen to get ahead of the competition before the January transfer window.

Mora's release clause is reportedly worth $26.8m (£20m), potentially giving interested clubs a clear route to securing his services. However, any move to England must comply with FIFA regulations governing international transfers of minors, meaning the timing and structure of a deal are important.

Arsenal appear to be moving ahead of Liverpool in the race for Mora, with their reported offer and proposed loan plan suggesting they are now further along in their pursuit of the teenager.

The Gunners' strategy is particularly appealing because it offers a clear pathway for his development - rather than expecting an 18-year-old to immediately break into Mikel Arteta's first team, the Premier League champions could secure his long-term future while allowing him to gain regular senior experience elsewhere.

Mora's goalscoring record and international experience suggest he already has considerable potential, but his next move must be about development as much as reputation. A carefully chosen loan could help him adapt to European football without the immediate pressure of performing at the Emirates.

Liverpool and Bayern remain stiff competition, but Arsenal's proposal gives them a slight advantage at this stage, and while there is no confirmation that a deal is close, the Gunners appear to be doing more to turn their interest into a concrete transfer plan.

At around £20m, Mora would represent a significant investment, so Arsenal fans will need patience, but their approach could put them in pole position to secure one of Mexico's most exciting young players.

Howe would be 'interested in holding talks' over becoming the next United manager if the club sack Carrick, according to Sky Sports News.

However, United's deteriorating situation could offer Howe an opportunity to return sooner than expected. He was previously recommended for the job by former sporting director Dan Ashworth, while current director of football Jason Wilcox is understood to favour candidates with Premier League experience.

Julian Nagelsmann is the other leading contender, with United's head of recruitment Christopher Vivell familiar with the German from their time together at RB Leipzig. United's interest in Howe is understandable given his achievements at Newcastle. He transformed the Magpies into regular European challengers, secured Champions League qualification twice and ended the club's long wait for a domestic trophy by winning the EFL Cup in 2025.

His experience of rebuilding a struggling Premier League side could be particularly attractive to a United hierarchy searching for a way out of the club's current crisis.

Howe might not be the appointment every United supporter wants, but he would offer a very credible option if the club decide Carrick's time is up.

There is an argument that Carrick deserves some sympathy. United's recruitment problems have contributed to their struggles, and changing managers will not automatically resolve the deeper issues at Old Trafford. The club must take responsibility for the squad and structure they have assembled rather than treating another dismissal as a complete solution.

Nonetheless, Howe has demonstrated that he can build a competitive team, improve players and deliver results in the Premier League. His two top-five finishes during his managerial career, alongside the Magpies' return to the Champions League, provides some evidence of what he could achieve with the right backing.

Nagelsmann may offer greater tactical intrigue, but Howe's domestic experience could make him a sensible appointment for a club that urgently needs some stability.

Ultimately, Howe could be one of the most realistic options available to United, provided the hierarchy gives him the support and time needed to rebuild.

If Carrick cannot turn things around, the former Newcastle boss has a compelling case to be considered.

McInnes had been smarting at being left out of the Rangers squad for the League Cup Final the day before, a classic encounter at Celtic Park which saw a Paul Gascoigne-inspired Ibrox side defeat Hearts 4-3.

Having featured throughout their run to the final, including scoring in the semi-final win over Dunfermline, McInnes felt he had a strong case for a place on the bench at the very least.

Former Rangers manager Walter Smith passed away five years ago

Current Rangers boss Derek McInnes admits he soaked up the wisdom of the dugout legend

‘There were lots of players in the Rangers dressing room closer to the gaffer than me but, as a manager, he respected me and I certainly respected him.

‘Once I became a manager, our relationship became a lot stronger and his eagerness to help and involve himself in my career was pleasing. I enjoyed all the moments I had with him, either face to face, sitting next to him at games or just on the phone.

Smith told McInnes of the importance of making your presence felt when you walk in a room

Nelson said federal officials are interested in bringing the two parties together to advance critical initiatives. For example, last year, insurers pledged to ease prior authorization and support electronic avenues for determinations. But that work can only go so far without providers at the table, too.

Efforts to address healthcare costs will be more effective, he said, if they come from a "coalition of the willing" rather than a solution imposed on the industry. That's why, in addition to the surveys, the company is hosting forums that bring in leaders from hospitals and health systems to discuss where to go next.

"These are all people who are trying to fix these issues," he said. "We all wake up in the morning trying to do the same thing. We are way more aligned than people like to believe."

The next evolution will get other key stakeholders—like electronic medical records companies—to unlock true interoperability that drives further change, Nelson said.

The survey found signs of progress in easing those administrative burdens, too. Sixty-seven percent of providers named administrative hurdles as their biggest challenge, a decrease of six percentage points from the second quarter's survey results.

Among those who said administrative challenges were the largest burden, 27% said prior auth was the largest contributor, down from 31% in Q2.

The survey also found that 25% of providers believe interoperability can improve outcomes, increasing by seven percentage points from the second quarter.

Beyond administrative challenges, providers (94%) believe fraud is a critical challenge that the healthcare system needs to address, according to the survey results. Nearly 60% of those surveyed believe addressing fraud could significantly reduce healthcare costs.

Close to a third (30%) of those surveyed said they recommend payers lean on AI to identify potentially fraudulent claims, and nearly 90% said they believe payers can substantially reduce fraud, waste and abuse annually.

Mitigating fraud has been a major topic of interest in healthcare and a particular focus of the Trump administration in recent months.

"There is a shared mindset," Nelson said. "Providers want to get that fixed as well. They don’t want to be colored in a negative light, and health plans don’t either."
