The Federal Reserve, the central bank of the United States, provides the nation with a safe, flexible, and stable monetary and financial system.

The Federal Reserve Board on Wednesday finalized changes to enhance the transparency and public accountability of its stress test and reduce volatility in its stress test-related capital requirements. The two final rules are largely similar to the proposed rules from 2025. The Board also requested comment on a proposal to better capture differences in banks' business models to generate fee income.

The Board conducts stress tests to ensure that large banks are sufficiently capitalized and able to lend to households and businesses even in a severe recession. In December 2024, the Board announced it would modify the test to improve its resilience.

"The stress test is an essential component of our regulatory capital framework," said Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle W. Bowman. "Today's changes preserve its resilience by ensuring that it is transparent, granular, and risk-sensitive. The public will now have greater assurance that the risks banks take will be reflected appropriately in their stress test losses and their capital requirements."

The first final rule requires the Board to invite public input annually on the stress test scenarios and any material model changes. It also updates the framework that guides the design of the hypothetical scenarios and adopts the models that will be used for the 2027 stress test. In addition, the final rule makes several adjustments to the stress test calendar. The final rule also updates the global market shock component, which is a set of shocks to specific market components applied to banks with large trading books. These banks will now be tested against two global market shock components each year, and the Board will use the shock that produces the largest losses for each firm to calculate the firm's stress test results.

The second final rule requires the Board, when calculating stress capital buffer requirements, to average the results from the two most recent annual supervisory stress tests for firms subject to the stress test in both years. The Board will begin averaging stress capital buffer requirements in 2028 to ensure that only models incorporating public input are used in the calculation.

The proposal would request public comment on a revision to the Board's noninterest income model that would improve the stress test's ability to capture business model diversity across firms. If adopted, these adjustments would replace the current noninterest income model that projects each bank's fee income under stress. Comments are due 60 days after publication in the Federal Register.

Together, the changes are likely to reduce year-over-year volatility in capital requirements by approximately 50 percent and are not expected to materially affect aggregate capital requirements.

#4834. Insomnia Isn’t Just Exhausting — It May Raise Your Risk of Stroke, Dementia

📅 Sep 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Insomnia may do more than leave you tired. A review published in Sleep Medicine Reviews on September 7 has linked it to a higher risk of stroke, depression, and hospitalization.

Insomnia is characterized by difficulty falling or staying asleep despite having an adequate opportunity to sleep and typically involves impaired daytime functioning.

“Sleep is such an important health factor when we think about overall well-being,” Joseph You, MD, cardiologist with Manhattan Cardiology in NYC, who wasn’t involved in the study, told Healthline.

He noted that sleep deprivation and poor-quality sleep have long been linked to adverse cardiovascular events and other health problems.

Insomnia is the most common sleep disorder in adults. Chronic insomnia affects approximately 22% of the general population.

“Chronic insomnia is generally characterized by ongoing sleep difficulty despite having an adequate opportunity to sleep, rather than simply having an occasional bad night,” noted Christopher Yi, MD, board certified vascular surgeon at MemorialCare Orange Coast Medical Center in Fountain Valley, CA, who wasn’t involved in the study.

To examine the association between insomnia and stroke, the researchers identified nine studies involving more than 1.3 million people. Six were included in the meta-analysis, which found that insomnia was associated with a 26% higher risk of stroke.

It is important to keep in mind that association does not equal causation. However, several factors may help explain the link between insomnia and stroke.

Persistent sleep disruptions can trigger overactivation of the sympathetic nervous system.

The sympathetic nervous system is part of the body’s involuntary nervous system that triggers your “fight or flight” response in stressful or dangerous situations.

“Being too wired can impair sleep, and impaired sleep, in turn, can make us too wired. A state of higher adrenaline and cortisol raises blood pressure, blood sugar, and increases inflammation, all of which have serious health effects,” said Alex Dimitriu, MD, double board certified in Psychiatry and Sleep Medicine, and founder of Menlo Park Psychiatry & Sleep Medicine, who wasn’t involved in the study.

The review authors noted that these changes can not only raise blood pressure and heart rate but can also increase vascular tone while reducing heart rate variability. Continuous cortisol release can also promote hypertension, systemic inflammation, and disrupt glucose and lipid metabolism.

Together, these changes may promote plaque formation and compromise vascular health, potentially contributing to a higher risk of stroke.

“I would emphasize that this study demonstrates an association and does not prove that insomnia directly causes stroke or other cardiovascular events,” cautioned Yi.

“There was significant variability among the studies, and factors such as underlying medical conditions and the use of sleep medications could influence the results. The authors themselves caution that an independent causal role for insomnia has not been established.”

The researchers also looked at five studies examining the association between insomnia and hospitalization. The studies included mostly older adults and ranged in size from 1,101 to 484,707 participants.

Three of the studies, involving 19,745 participants, were included in a meta-analysis. Researchers found that people with insomnia had a 28% higher likelihood of hospitalization for any reason.

The researchers noted that insomnia may be a key marker of poor health and may exacerbate the severity of underlying health conditions.

Dimitriu said a good indication that it may be time to seek help is when sleep problems begin to affect your life, health, or happiness. If you are unable to sleep at least 6–7 hours when given the opportunity, or regularly feel tired or sleepy during the day, he recommends speaking with a healthcare professional.

You recommended seeking help as soon as you begin to suspect you may have insomnia. Doing so may also help identify other risk factors or lifestyle habits that could be affecting your sleep and overall health.

The researchers reviewed six meta-analyses examining the association between insomnia and depression and other mental health disorders.

Although most were rated as having low or critically low methodological quality, the findings consistently linked insomnia with a higher risk of depression.

According to the researchers, chronic insomnia can lead to daytime fatigue, attention issues, and reduced executive functioning. These factors can increase rumination, negative thinking, and maladaptive coping strategies, which are all risk factors for depression and anxiety.

Separately, two meta-analyses found that insomnia was associated with a higher risk of suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, and suicide death.

Sleep disturbances are among several risk factors for suicide, including independently of depression.

This isn’t to say that insomnia causes depression or suicidal behaviors, only that it may be one among various risk factors.

The researchers reviewed five meta-analyses examining the association between insomnia and dementia. The analyses included between 5 and 39 studies and ranged from 36,005 to more than 3.1 million participants, with follow-up periods ranging from 2 to 40 years.

Overall, most of the analyses linked insomnia with a higher risk of dementia. Three also found that insomnia was associated with a higher risk of Alzheimer’s disease.

The researchers noted several possible explanations for the association. Sleep disturbances may contribute to inflammation and impaired clearance of substances such as amyloid-beta and tau from the brain, processes that could potentially contribute to neurodegeneration.

However, these findings show an association between insomnia and dementia and do not establish that insomnia causes dementia.

#4844. 8-Hour Time-Restricted Eating Window May Lead to Greater Weight Loss

📅 Sep 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Follow Healthline on our socials for all things health.

Limiting your eating to an 8-hour window may result in more weight loss than a 12-hour window, according to a new study published in the journal Nutrition & Diabetes on September 12.

Time-restricted eating (TRE) is a form of intermittent fasting that limits eating to a set daily window, such as 8, 10, or 12 hours, without necessarily changing what you eat.

The eating window is followed by fasting for the remaining hours. Common approaches include 16:8, which involves 16 hours of fasting and 8 hours of eating, and early TRE, which involves eating earlier in the day.

The researchers used data from 124 adults across five TRE trials lasting between 3 and 6 months. At baseline, participants had an average eating window of about 15 hours and at least one component of metabolic syndrome.

Their food and beverage intake was tracked using an app.

Participants were assigned either to a TRE group or to the control group. The control group participants maintained their habitual intake, received nutritional counseling, or were given guidance on healthy eating.

Those in the TRE group limited their eating windows to 8, 10, or 12 hours, with no restrictions on dietary quality or energy intake.

After 3–6 months, the researchers found that participants assigned to an 8-hour eating window lost about 3.7 pounds (1.7 kg) more than those assigned to a 12-hour window and about 5 pounds (2.3 kg) more than those in the control group.

However, there was no statistically significant difference in weight loss between the 8-hour and the 10-hour windows. The 8-hour window also showed no significant impact on blood pressure, glucose, or cholesterol.

“The findings are interesting and add to the evidence that shortening the daily eating window may help with weight loss, but I would be cautious about concluding that eight hours is the optimal window,” Dalia Perelman, MS, RDN, CDCES, research dietitian at Stanford Medicine, who wasn’t involved in the study, told Healthline.

“One finding I find particularly interesting is that people who shortened their eating window more relative to their usual pattern tended to lose more weight. This suggests that the benefit may not be about everyone following exactly the same eight-hour window, but rather about meaningfully shortening one’s habitual eating period,” she added.

The researchers also found that the 10-hour eating window significantly reduced systolic blood pressure compared with the 12-hour window. There were no significant differences between the eating windows for glucose or lipids.

However, a 2020 study found that a 10-hour eating window reduced weight, blood pressure, and cholesterol, among adults with metabolic syndrome who were already taking medication.

“[These] effects are generally modest, and TRE should be viewed as one potential component of an overall lifestyle approach rather than a treatment on its own,” said Perelman.

“I think TRE is best viewed as one possible tool rather than a magic formula. When we eat appears to matter, but what and how much we eat still matter as well,” she added.

“A nutritious diet, limiting highly processed foods, avoiding eating late at night, and finding an eating pattern that is enjoyable and sustainable are probably more important than rigidly adhering to a specific number of hours.”

Perelman also told Healthline that “Older adults at risk for muscle loss should also make sure they are consuming adequate calories and protein.”

She added that people with diabetes who use insulin or medications that can cause hypoglycemia should discuss TRE with their healthcare professional because changing when they eat may require medication adjustments.

As with any eating plan, time-restricted eating should be realistic and sustainable for your lifestyle.

Perelman noted that health is about more than what and when you eat. She said that sharing meals with family and friends, social connection, and enjoyment of food are important too.

A rigid eating schedule that interferes with those things may not be beneficial overall.

“Rather than focusing on achieving a precise eight-hour window, I would prioritize a balanced, nutrient-rich diet low in highly processed foods and, when possible, finish eating earlier in the evening,” Perelman said.

#3429. IPO postponements are accelerating in third quarter, even beyond Oura

📅 Sep 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Biometric ring maker Oura raised eyebrows on Wall Street Tuesday, postponing its initial public offering due to "uncertainty" in market conditions while also claiming there was "strong demand" for stock.

IPO delays have accelerated in 2026, even as Oura faced some company-specific pushback from the Street. Market conditions for new offerings are suddenly becoming a headwind for companies thinking about going public now, largely because of surging bonds yields, analysts say.

"The fact that we've had three or four in a row – a string of postponements – I think that does tell you something about the market," Matthew Kennedy, senior strategist at IPO specialist Renaissance Capital, told CNBC. "You can't really point to all four of them and say it's company-specific issues."

Four companies in different sectors, all aiming to raise at least $50 million, announced postponements or pulled their IPOs altogether in the past week, according to Renaissance Capital data. That makes seven in the third quarter, up from four in the second quarter and three in the first.

Before Oura's postponement on Tuesday, nuclear power component maker Holtec Nuclear withdrew its IPO last Friday; materials company Amaero postponed its IPO last Wednesday; and Bamboo Insurance postponed its IPO on Sept. 22.

"I've got a little bit of sympathy for market conditions as a rationale," said Jay Ritter, director of the IPO Initiative at the University of Florida's Warrington College of Business. "The fact that three prominent companies are doing this does indicate that it's not company-specific."

It's been a solid year for IPOs, with about $146.9 billion in proceeds coming in 110 deals, excluding special purpose acquisition companies, Renaissance Capital data shows. Those figures include mega offerings from SpaceX and South Korean memory maker SK Hynix in the second quarter.

Still, that's down 30% from this time last year. In all of 2025 there were 202 IPOs, the most since 2021 when there were close to 400. Total proceeds are up 394% this year, largely due to the SpaceX, SK Hynix and Cerebras offerings.

Health care and industrials are tied for the top sector to launch IPOs year-to-date — both accounting for 24% of the total. Technology is third, at 18% of the total.

So far this year, 59% of all of the 2026 offerings are selling at or above the IPO price although, notably, SpaceX, SK Hynix and Cerebras are all lagging.

Lately, macroeconomic factors and concern about the artificial intelligence buildout are weighing on the market for new offerings.

"IPO activity came in below expectations in the third quarter of 2026, as more concerns about AI spending, a 19-year high in bond yields and resumed rate hikes weighed on the fall pickup," Renaissance analysts wrote in a report to clients last week.

The Renaissance IPO ETF peaked in June this year when SpaceX launched.

"AI-related stuff, including the build-out of the big infrastructure, still has a lot of enthusiasm for it. Things like data centers, there's big demand there, but it's largely a commodity business," Ritter said.

Analysts said that Oura, whose business is concentrated in its biometric ring product, likely faced its own set of issues.

"I would draw a line [around] Peloton, GoPro, FitBit and Oura. Investors have been burned pretty badly by narrow consumer products, and that is the reaction Oura is getting," said Gil Luria, head of technology research at DA Davidson. "I don't think it has to do with tech or tech allocation, I think it has to do with a narrow consumer product."

Companies themselves also have more alternatives than in the past to raise capital in private markets rather than public markets, lawyers who specialize in public markets said.

"The depth of private capital and alternatives is enormous now, and much more complex and diverse," Ian Schuman, chair of capital markets and public company representation practices at Latham & Watkins, said. "You don't necessarily, absolutely need to tap the public markets, if you're not getting the value you want."

#4864. Ultra-Processed Foods Linked to Higher Risk of Multiple Diseases, Death

Eating more ultra-processed foods (UPFs) is associated with an increased risk of several serious health problems and even a greater chance of early death.

A large study published on September 22 in Family Medicine and Community Health found that those who ate the most UPFs were more likely to develop chronic illnesses, including:

These individuals also had a higher overall risk of death.

While there is no commonly accepted definition of UPFs, a classification system known as NOVA defines ultra-processed foods to be “industrial formulations with five or more ingredients” that contain substances that aren’t typically used in culinary preparations.

These additives are typically intended to make UPFs feel, taste, or look more appealing. According to the researchers in the new study, UPFs also tend to be high in sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats but low in essential nutrients such as fiber and vitamins.

UPFs are heavily altered from their original form and often contain additives intended to make them feel, taste, or look more appealing. They tend to be high in sugar, salt, and unhealthy fats but low in essential nutrients like fiber and vitamins, the researchers noted.

The researchers added that because many chronic diseases often occur together, reducing UPF intake could be an important step in preventing multiple health problems at once.

The researchers conducted their study by combining the results of several different studies to get a clearer overall picture.

Specifically, they looked at studies where groups of people were followed over time to see how their habits affected their health.

To be included, studies had to involve adults aged 18 or older who were initially free of specific diseases. The studies also needed to compare people who ate the most ultra-processed foods to those who ate the least.

The NOVA classification system was used to identify ultra-processed foods, such as candies, packaged snacks, sugary drinks, and ready-to-eat meals.

The researchers used statistical methods to compare the highest consumers of UPFs with the lowest consumers and examined dose-response relationships — in other words, how the risk changed with increases in UPF consumption.

Johannah Katz, MA, a registered dietitian at Nourish, who was not involved in the study, told Healthline that there isn’t currently a cutoff point at which ultra-processed foods become harmful.

“What this new research suggests is more of a dose-response relationship; the more ultra-processed food in the diet, the higher the observed risk for several chronic diseases,” she said.

Katz said she wouldn’t advise people that they necessarily need to eliminate all UPFs.

“A more realistic goal is to keep them from becoming the foundation of the diet and prioritize whole or minimally processed foods most of the time,” she said.

Katz further noted that just because something is ultra-processed doesn’t necessarily mean it is unhealthy.

“[A] sugary soda and a fiber-rich packaged whole grain bread may both meet criteria for ultra-processing, but they’re nutritionally very different,” she said.

Vahista Ussery, MS, MBA, a registered dietitian nutritionist and chef as well as the Co-Founder of To Taste, advocates for a step-wise approach to reducing UPF intake. She was not part of the study.

“The first step would be to try to swap your favorite ultra-processed foods with healthier versions,” she said, noting that while many products marketed as healthy are, in fact, still ultra-processed, they can be a stepping stone to making larger changes.

Ussery said the next step is to start cooking more at home using whole ingredients.

“When you cook from home, you are fully in control of what you put into your body,” she said. “Look for simple recipes that aren’t intimidating and that you can prepare with the equipment you have on hand.”

Next, work on reducing your fast food intake, which tends to be heavily processed and less nourishing.

“If you have to go to a fast food restaurant, try to order the fresher options or consider adding fresher options and shrinking portion sizes of ultra-processed foods,” Ussery advised. “For example, order a small milkshake versus [a] large and add a side salad to fill you up instead.”

Finally, she pointed out that ultra-processed foods are “designed to be addictive,” which can make it difficult to stop eating them.

“The more you do, though, the easier it gets. Try to resist the temptation!” Ussery urged.

Netherlands dominated from the start but they were forced into an early change when Cody Gakpo suffered a knock and Ruben van Bommel came on.

After half an hour, Crysencio Summerville was clipped in the box and Meerdink made it 1-0 from the penalty spot. It was almost 2-0 before the break but Denzel Dumfries could not convert when through on goal.

Serbia made a double change at the break with Luka Jovic and Dusan Tadic coming on. A minute into the second half, the change worked as Jovic was given space in the box to net after Netherlands failed to clear the ball.

Netherlands went in search of the lead again but Tijjani Reijnders blazed a huge chance over while Ruben van Bommel hit the post. Meerdink did eventually make it 2-1 with a lovely finish after a quick 1-2 with substitute Guus Til.

There was to be no hattrick for Meerdink as he was brought off and Netherlands played the final ten minutes with Til through the middle.

Serbia pressed for a late equaliser but the Netherlands had a golden chance to make it 3-1 when Til found himself in on goal. He had Summerville and Van Bommel to his left but overhit the pass and the later’s finish was too weak to find the net.

The Netherlands held on for the victory which takes them to the top of the group with a clash against Greece next on Thursday.

The Federal Reserve Board on Thursday requested public comment on two proposals related to establishing a regulatory framework for Board-supervised payment stablecoin issuers under the GENIUS Act.

The first proposal would require that Board-supervised payment stablecoin issuers fully back their stablecoins with certain permissible reserve assets, such as short-term Treasury bills and certain other high-quality, liquid assets. The proposal would also establish standardized capital requirements to address certain credit and operational risks of payment stablecoin activities, as well as risk management standards, in accordance with the law. Separately, the proposal would introduce rules for Board-supervised firms that safekeep the assets backing payment stablecoins. It would also clarify the permissibility of stablecoin and related activities for Board-supervised banks.

The second proposal would establish a tailored application process for Board-supervised banks applying to issue payment stablecoins. These applicants would be required to submit a business plan and financial information, among other documents. The proposal would also create a process governing appeals, hearings, and final determinations for applications.

The comment period will close 60 days after publication in the Federal Register.
