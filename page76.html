Living in New York City, I see a lot of vehicles that are truly incorrect for the area in which they're driven. That phenomenon got me thinking, though: Surely we've all done something similar, driven the worst possible vehicle for a circumstance and had to make do. Today, I want to talk about your most ill-suited automotive experiences.

This is a very broad category, and I'm intentionally keeping it that way. Did you take a ski trip in an open-differential Miata, with no room to put your skis and only one powered wheel to get you through the snow, or have you autocrossed a Wrangler? Have you taken a four-hour road trip in a torrential downpour, on a motorcycle, with waterproof gear that you quickly found the limits of? Well, I have too, and today we're all going to share our dumb ideas.

My worst-picked vehicle was probably a press loaner Chevy Colorado Trail Boss I got some time back, specifically a Stars and Steel-edition truck. I jumped at the chance to get it on loan, because I love a deeply stupid vehicle, but only once I got my hands on it did I realize it wouldn't fit in my building's parking. Then, once I started circling my block looking for street spots, I realized that the giant black America-themed pickup stood out uncomfortably in my largely Hispanic neighborhood. I ended up blasting transsexual hyperpop with the windows down to try and look less like ICE.

My most ill-suited vehicle was a Colorado in Queens, but what was yours? The ongoing war of words between Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum took an unexpected turn Monday courtesy of Celtics television analyst and SiriusXM radio host Brian Scalabrine.

To briefly recap: Last Thursday, Brown posted a cap emoji on an Instagram video where Tatum talked about his reaction to the Celtics trading Brown to the Sixers. The next night, Tatum told reporters, "This ain't no f–king Shaq and Kobe, there's no beef." Brown concurred Saturday, telling reporters, "There's no beef, but there might be a little lamb chops and some gravy." (He also added that Tatum had "hell of a PR team," which is a phenomenal dig. 10/10, no notes.)

That brings us to Monday. On his SiriusXM radio show, Scalabrine said Brown "demanded to be traded" from the Celtics, "not through the media, but to the organization."

"If you're actually doing that, and you're going to say Tatum is the one that's lying, your whole basis of this thing is a lie," he concluded.

As it turns out, Scalabrine might need to look in a mirror before calling someone else a liar. On the NBC Sports Boston broadcast prior to Monday's game against the Celtics and Chicago Bulls, Scalabrine cleared up his report by essentially rebutting everything he said earlier in the day.

"He technically didn't demand to be traded," Scalabrine said. "I just summed it all up, I just summed up the entire word salad and kept it simple, but I think that's what got me in trouble there."

When asked what Brown's demand was, Scalabrine replied, "I have no idea. I don't know what the ultimatum was or the demand or whatever it was, but he technically did not demand to be traded. It was 'Do these things right here or else.'"

A representative from Brown's team told ESPN's Kris Rhim that Scalabrine's report was "10000% a lie." The representative added that once the Celtics informed Brown that they planned to trade him, his "only request was to have input on his destination," which Chris Hine of the Philadelphia Inquirer also reported.

Journalism is currently in a state of disrepair for countless reasons. The rise of AI will only make things exponentially worse. (It already has.) But one of the fundamental tenets of journalism—at least back in its heyday—was that you double-check what you're reporting before you actually report it. Once upon a time, they had these things called "editors" and "fact-checkers" who were an extra layer of review to ensure that journalists weren't reporting inaccurate information. (Most of them have now been laid off because they didn't make their company's Line Go Up quickly enough.)

Of course, the live, off-the-cuff nature of radio and TV make it far more difficult to vet information in real time. That's why you do that work beforehand before spouting it to a national audience. We've all been on the internet long enough at this point to know that aggregators will pounce on any juicy material and disseminate it widely. Lo and behold, that's exactly what happened to Scalabrine on Monday.

As the father of two young boys, teaching accountability has been one of the biggest parenting challenges to date. It's hard to own up to your mistakes and admit that you screwed up; it's far easier to blame someone else or deflect responsibility. But is that the world we want to live in?

Scalabrine shouldn't necessarily lose his job over a mistake like this, but he should feel compelled to apologize and own it. No hedging, no wishy-washy, "Well, there's nuance, but…" The only words out of his mouth should be, "I screwed up, I deeply regret the error, and I will not report anything unless I am 100 percent sure it is accurate moving forward."

But given the state of everything these days, this faux pas will probably put him in line to be the next White House press secretary by next week.

#3498. Apple launches 14-inch MacBook Pro with M6 and 24-hour battery

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The entry-level pro laptop gets more cores, faster AI and Wi-Fi 7, but keeps its June price of $1,999.

Apple’s 14-inch MacBook Pro with the M6 chip

Apple has updated its 14-inch MacBook Pro with the M6 chip, promising faster graphics, better on-device AI performance, and up to 24 hours of battery life, the longest runtime the company has offered in a Mac.

The new MacBook Pro was announced today alongside a refreshed iMac. Pre-orders open on Wednesday in 35 countries, with deliveries and in-store availability starting on November 6.

The laptop looks much the same as its predecessor, and it comes in space black and silver and retains three Thunderbolt 4 ports, HDMI, an SD card slot, a headphone jack, and MagSafe charging.

The main change is happening inside. The M6 has 12 CPU cores and 12 GPU cores, two more of each than the M5 in last year’s model.

Every GPU core also includes a Neural Accelerator designed to speed up AI workloads. Apple claims the M6 has the fastest CPU core in the world, based on its own tests.

Compared with the M5 MacBook Pro, Apple says the new model delivers up to 30% faster graphics performance and runs AI tasks, such as processing a large language model locally, up to 25% faster.

The Neural Engine, which handles tasks such as image generation and photo enhancement, is up to 1.9 times faster.

Apple also points to improvements in everyday creative and development work. Rendering a 3D scene in Blender and building an app in Xcode are both up to 1.3 times faster, according to the company. All these figures come from Apple’s own testing.

The gains are more pronounced for people upgrading from older machines. Compared with the 13-inch MacBook Pro with an M1 chip from 2020, Apple claims up to 11 times faster graphics and 13 times faster AI performance.

Battery life reaches up to 24 hours, which Apple says is a record for the Mac. Performance remains the same whether the laptop is plugged in or running on battery. With a higher-wattage power adapter, it can charge to 50% in around 30 minutes.

The Liquid Retina XDR display remains unchanged, offering up to 1,600 nits of brightness for HDR content. Buyers can opt for a nano-texture finish to reduce glare. The new model also supports Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6 and can drive two external displays. Memory starts at 16GB and can be configured up to 32GB.

“With the powerful M6 chip, we’re taking performance to the next level,” said Shelly Goldberg, Apple’s vice president of hardware engineering.

The MacBook Pro ships with macOS 27, which introduces Apple’s new AI-powered Siri to the Mac. The assistant can search messages, emails, and photos, as well as perform actions across apps.

It remains in beta, supports six languages, and has daily usage limits. Apple first introduced the assistant on the iPhone with iOS 27.2.

The new MacBook Pro starts at $1,999 in the US and £1,999 in the UK. That matches the M5 model’s price immediately before launch, but not its original price.

Apple raised the starting price by $400 in June, from $1,599, as memory and storage costs increased, EveryMac reported.

Apple also highlighted the materials used in the laptop: the enclosure is made from 100% recycled aluminium, while the battery uses 100% recycled cobalt.

#182. We spent 12 months testing red light masks: Here are 6 that gave us real results (and 2 to skip)

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

No one is seriously questioning whether the best red light masks work anymore. The professional skincare community and dermatologists largely agree on their effectiveness. The more nuanced question is which red light therapy devices provide the best visible results. When choosing a red light mask or LED skincare device, factors like light wavelength, irradiance, and bulb placement all determine whether a mask is actually doing anything for your fine lines or inflammation.

Since consistency is so key for visible results, the best red light mask is the one that you'll actually feel like wearing on a daily basis. So, over months and months of at-home testing, Mashable reporters and editors used popular red light therapy devices from Shark, Solawave, CurrentBody, and more. After trying over 10 different products, we ultimately found six that delivered consistent results for our testers — and two we can't recommend.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

There are some ways to tell that a red light face mask won't be effective before you buy it and use it for a few months.

Wavelength is the first factor anyone will tell you to consider. You want to find products that emit light within the therapeutic window, or the range of wavelengths that can be effective when treating common skin conditions. There is now an abundance of evidence that specific wavelengths of red light can be effective for:

Reduction of fine lines, dark circles, and crow's feet

To be effective, the light coming from the mask must penetrate different layers of your skin. There are optimal wavelengths for each type of light a face mask emits, and any reputable brand will put their mask's wavelengths front and center in the listing. These carefully chosen ranges are strong enough to penetrate your skin, but gentle enough to avoid over-activating delicate face skin.

Red light does its work in the middle layer (dermis) of your skin, requiring wavelengths between 630 and 633 nm to reach past the top layer. There, red light stimulates cells to produce rejuvenating proteins like collagen and elastin, evening out surface redness, and preventing scarring or post-pimple red spots.

Most red light masks also use near-infrared red light therapy, which requires longer wavelengths to travel further than regular red light. Near-infrared light between 830 and 850 nm reaches into tissue under the dermis to further stimulate cell turnover, firm skin, and heal deep-seated spots or stubborn dark circles.

Blue light is often used to target acne, and some of the best red light masks use a combination of blue and red light therapy.

Blue light operates on the top layer (epidermis) of your skin, so its ideal wavelength is shorter than either red light standard. Blue light at 415nm has an antimicrobial effect, ideally killing the bacteria that cause acne before a breakout starts. If hellbent bacterial acne makes it past the blue light phase — or if you're also dealing with hormonal or cystic acne not caused by bacteria — red light swoops in to neutralize inflammation and speed up the healing process.

The thing is, most mainstream LED face masks hit those wavelength marks. So, how do you choose between masks if their wavelength numbers are all the same?

Irradiance is another major element that determines whether or not a red light mask actually works. While wavelength refers to the length the light can travel, irradiance refers to the amount of light energy that's actually reaching your skin per session (not just the intensity of light being generated at the bulb). If wavelength numbers are correct but irradiance is low, the mask will still be kind of a dud.

Irradiance is officially expressed as milliwatts per square centimeter. The optimal irradiance range for a red light face mask is pretty wide (anywhere between 30 and 100 mW/cm²), though many brands don't directly provide that number.

Most brands do note the length of each session, which is one way to quickly gauge irradiance without getting into calculations. Dr. Mamina Turegano sums it up best on her YouTube account: "Higher irradiance means more effective energy delivery per minute of use." Longer sessions don't automatically mean that results will be more powerful — a mask with sessions that last for less than 10 minutes has higher irradiance than a mask with sessions that last for 20 to 30 minutes. Since a mask with lower irradiance needs to sit on your face for longer, you might be less likely to keep up with wearing it.

The number of bulbs and the placement of bulbs inside the mask can also impact efficacy, especially if you're expecting results in a specific area of your face. Masks with more bulbs packed evenly across the face will provide more even coverage across every inch of your face than masks with more sporadically placed bulbs.

Some masks also provide more light sources overall by emitting both red and blue light simultaneously from every single bulb, like the Shark CryoGlow. This would be a better option for full-face acne prevention than masks with blue and red LEDs only concentrated in certain spots, like the Dr. Dennis Gross DRx SpectraLite.

Big Brother does a lot of things that can't be done on some of the best reality shows, but there are rules. For example, lying about powers and advantages, which is common on Survivor, is a big no-no. I always assumed this was a rule because it was unfair to the Houseguests. However, series EP Rich Meehan recently said it's more about how it would impact the episodes.

Those who watch the BB live feeds with a Paramount+ subscription likely know that producers can struggle to edit a week's worth of content down into three hours of television. On that note, Meehan spoke to EW and presented the idea of how much more challenging it would be if these Houseguests were then able to make up lies about powers they didn't have:

Imagine if five or six people are making up powers, and then you’ve gotta tell all the story that isn’t real. So then you have all the actual story, all the alliance-building, everything else, and then everyone will [make up powers] if everyone knows they’re allowed to do it. And then it becomes a game of who can make up the best power.

At first, I was annoyed to hear it was mainly to make storytelling easier for episodes, but I get what Meehan is saying. If there aren't hard-and-fast rules about the details Houseguests can or can't lie about, then it would just devolve into everyone lying in a game where deception already plays such a huge role.

Rick Devens' Wife Clapped Back At A Big Brother Fan Trolling After Her Cancer Diagnosis

Lying about powers was a hot-button issue in Season 28 of Big Brother, as there was a big fight involving Yash Patel and the Icons when it came to Dee Valladares' BB Bribe. Houseguests were confused because Dee and Yash had conflicting stories about what her power actually did, and both claimed they couldn't lie about it.

Big Brother's producers didn't step in and clarify who was right in that case, and that had me wondering what the difference was in that instance. Perhaps it was because Dee technically broke her power by turning one action into six, which many fans argued shouldn't have been allowed.

Which circles back to my assumption that the rule was in place because it was unfair, but honestly, it just seems as though it is more about convenience. A cynical person would say producers allowed Yash and Dee to lie about the power because it made for good TV, regardless of the rules. I just think it makes it difficult to win Big Brother when it feels as though there's a shaky consistency in how certain regulations are enforced.

Fortunately, no one was so broken up about Big Brother waffling on rule enforcement that it impacted the ratings. CBS gave the series a two-season renewal, and there are whispers that an all-star season is on the way. I know the vets won't settle for any shakiness when it comes to the rules, so hopefully, they stash the BB Bribe away.

Big Brother is done for the season, but another summer of fun will surely be here before fans know it. For those who can't wait, check out previous seasons on Paramount+ and relive some of the series most dramatic moments.

#3822. Roblox users have made a playable version of Counter-Strike and full, working PlayStation games that you can play right now

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Multiple remakes of beloved games including Counter Strike, The Simpsons: Hit and Run, and Super Hot, are available to play on Roblox right now. These have not been uploaded by the original game's creators.

One game - which is listed as an 'experience' in Roblox parlance, has been released by Roblox user Takeoff Interactive and listed as 'just a passion project', is a game "inspired by Counter-Strike" and has no monetisation according to its description. Eurogamer played this game today, and it looks and plays exactly like Counter-Strike.

The game includes the standard bomb diffusion mode, where two teams fight over two bomb sites. There are missing sound effects, the smoke doesn't look as good, and a friendly bot stabbed me in the back while diffusing a bomb, but otherwise it looks to be a largely identical recreation of Valve's game, albeit without Valve's involvement.

As for the PlayStation Experience, it's a full working PlayStation 4 dashboard with a media gallery, web browser, and trophies. There's even a server-based chat feature, in which Roblox players can talk to each other. The dashboard includes seven playable games - most of which are indie games. These include: The Simpsons Hit and Run, Cluster Truck, Superhot, Gang Beasts, Five Nights at Freddy's, Undertale, and PaRappaTheRapper.

Eurogamer played The Simpsons Hit and Run, and likewise found it to be a largely identical recreation of Activision's game. Players can drive around Springfield and cause mayhem much like the original game, albeit with a slightly dodgy objective guidance arrow.

Eurogamer also played Superhot, which once again looks and plays much like the original game, though it's not quite as visually impressive. This version of Superhot, Simpsons Hit and Run, and the PlayStation Experience as a whole was uploaded to Roblox by user Redwing Works, not Activision, not the Superhot team, and not Sony.

It is worth noting these are not Roblox games that are merely inspired by popular releases outside its ecosystem. Previously, a few popular Roblox games inspired by the game but not openly copying it exist. Counter Blox, for example, is a tactical FPS which uses Roblox assets and models, while Arsenal is another FPS inspired by Counter-Strike's Gun Game mode in which each kill progresses you to different weapons.

This is not the first time a popular game has been recreated on Roblox without the permission of its creators. The Peak developers urged people to pirate their game rather than play a "microtransaction-riddled ripoff slop" on Roblox.

Eurogamer has contacted Sony and developers for comment.
