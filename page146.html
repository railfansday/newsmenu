#751. Apple officially rolls out iOS 27.2. Heres whats new

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Apple has a fresh new iOS update for everyone to check out.

Monday marked the official launch of iOS 27.2, which is now available as a software update on all compatible iPhones. To install it, simply open the Settings app, tap General, and then tap Software Update. No need to fiddle with beta software this time — this is the real deal.

As for what's new, iOS 27.2 isn't the most monumental update in the world, but there's plenty to mess around with. For starters, Siri AI is now available in several new languages: French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, and Spanish. It's also been updated for English-language users so it will now be able to recall past conversations you've had with Apple's chatbot. Additionally, the Messages app will offer context-sensitive suggestions to take quick actions in Calendar or Reminders based on what's being said in the text conversation.

Dual Capture now works on FaceTime group calls, meaning you can have both your front and rear cameras active at the same time while talking to more than one other person. For new iPhone Duo owners, iOS 27.2 also adds Apple Pencil support. Meanwhile, the Edge Light feature uses the iPhone Duo's display to light up your face during video calls in low light.

There are plenty of other small changes to check out, too, such as individual profiles in the Apple TV app and a reimagined Up Next view in the Podcasts app. Multiview will also let sports fans watch up to four events simultaneously, though the feature appears to be limited to sporting events on Apple TV.

In total, iOS 27.2 might not be the most seismic update in the world, but there's still a good amount to play with.

#5476. Maryland state renewables program hopes to fill gap left by OBBBA

Gov. Wes Moore announced that the Maryland Energy Administration (MEA) is launching “Megawatts for Maryland,” a $200 million auction program to build new clean energy generation over the next two years and help lower power costs for ratepayers. MEA will administer the program, which was established through the Utility RELIEF Act, Moore signed into law earlier this year.

“We’re using every tool at our disposal to bring Marylanders relief as energy costs skyrocket in our region and across the country,” Moore said. “This program will tackle rising electricity demand with cleaner, cheaper, in-state energy generation that will drive down costs for the long-term.”

The program reportedly plans to bring “hundreds of megawatts” of clean energy to the Maryland grid, with the goal of powering more than 150,000 homes in the state. The program can draw up to $100 million from the state’s Strategic Energy Investment Fund annually, and will reportedly award grants to renewable energy generation and storage developers. The funding is for curbing development and construction costs of renewable energy generation and energy storage projects. The press release states that it was developed to fill in for the investment tax credits the Trump administration through the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

“Maryland families and businesses need reliable electricity at prices they can afford. That’s why we made increasing our state’s energy supply a central part of the Utility RELIEF Act,” said Joseline A. Peña-Melnyk Maryland House of Delegates speaker. “This $100 million investment puts that commitment into action, using a competitive process to bring new solar generation online while making responsible use of public dollars. Building more energy capacity here at home is an important part of our long-term strategy to ease pressure on utility bills and create a more affordable energy future for Marylanders.”

Megawatts for Maryland is the latest step in the state’s goal to lower energy costs. Moore issued an executive order that would manage energy costs affected by data center development in the state in September, and the state issued $300 million in utility cost relief through its Utility RELIEF Act.

#4964. South Korea Arrests Seven in First Big AI-Assisted Hacking Case

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

South Korea Arrests Seven in First Big AI-Assisted Hacking Case

Police in Seoul have arrested seven people in a hacking campaign that used generative AI to break into about 90 organizations and steal 8.2 million records.

The Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency announced the case Monday and called it the first time in South Korea that suspects in a large-scale AI-assisted hacking operation have been apprehended.

The targets included hospitals, universities, private cram schools, golf courses and subsidiaries of large conglomerates, according to police—places that hold the names, contact details and records of ordinary people. One funeral services provider lost 1.22 million records, and one report said data from the academies included information on minors.

The alleged ringleader is a 32-year-old who studied arts and physical education and has no IT background, per local reports. He told investigators he taught himself hacking through YouTube and online blogs, though police say he also did simulated attack work at a security company from 2019 to 2022.

He started with website vulnerability scanners, software that probes a site for weak spots. In late August, reports say, he added an AI coding agent—a program that writes and runs code on its own—and an autonomous penetration-testing system, a tool that automates the mock break-ins companies normally hire experts to run. Police say he then breached 60 companies in 44 days, from August 31 to October 13.

Police said an attack of this size once required a hacking group, and that AI now lets one person pull it off in a short time. Local reports name the autonomous tool as Artex. The campaign ran about 13 months, from September 2025 through this October, and the case opened in June after one victim filed a complaint.

Criminal use of AI has been building for years, mostly in reports from AI companies rather than police. WormGPT, a ChatGPT clone built without safety guardrails, was used in 2023 to write convincing phishing emails—fake messages designed to trick people into handing over money or passwords.

In August 2025, Anthropic said a single criminal used its Claude Code tool to run a vibe hacking extortion campaign against at least 17 organizations, with ransom demands from $75,000 to $500,000 in Bitcoin. In November, the company said a Chinese state-sponsored group used Claude to carry out 80% to 90% of an espionage campaign against about 30 targets, though only a small number of the intrusions succeeded.

Researchers at Cambridge, Edinburgh, and Strathclyde universities pushed back on the hype in a recent study of 97,895 cybercrime forum threads. They found AI’s main impact so far in already-automated areas such as SEO fraud, social media bots and romance scams, with little sign that coding tools helped low-skill actors beyond pre-made scripts.

The ringleader was referred to prosecutors in custody on Friday and faces charges under South Korea’s information and communications network law, plus extortion and attempted extortion. Police said they will keep investigating the six accomplices and try to identify additional victims, and 15 organizations have reported damage so far.

That’s an important distinction because wind turbines, both offshore and onshore, have earned the wrath of Trump ever since the early 2000’s, when he lost a legal battle against an offshore wind farm that allegedly spoiled the view from one of his golf courses in Scotland. Unlike wind turbines, marine energy devices are barely visible above the surface of the water, so the view-spoiling argument loses quite a bit of steam against them.

That helps explain why the US Department of Energy continues to pour taxpayer dollars into marine energy R&D, even while Trump systematically tries to destroy any and every wind turbine in sight (see more Trump v. wind turbines here).

As for potential competition against President Trump’s allies in the fossil fuel industry, no worries. Marine energy is a newly hatched global industry, and it lacks the supply chain and workforce needed for rapid scale-up before it can make any significant contribution to the renewable energy transition. Much of the activity still consists of testing, demonstration, and pilot projects.

Still, if all goes according to plan the payoff will be considerable. The US Department of Energy calculates that wave energy alone could introduce 1,170 terawatt-hours per year onto the nation’s grid.

Siting opportunities for tides and currents are less expansive, but these two parts of the marine energy industry could still make a significant contribution to local and regional decarbonization efforts. The Energy Department estimates that tidal energy could introduce 220 terawatt-hours per year into the grid.

The US has been pushing itself forth as a global influence on the marine energy industry since 2010, when the US Navy and Marine Corps partnered with the Energy Department on a wave energy test facility in Hawaii. The Hawaii facility has been upgraded several times since then, and the Energy Department has also established the TEAMER program, which makes a network of US marine energy laboratories available to innovators from around the world. The jewel in the crown is PacWave South, an open ocean test facility off the coast of Oregon which just celebrated its official launch over the summer.

The hum of activity is somewhat more muted around tidal energy. To date, the work has focused on locations where other on-site renewable energy resources are inadequate or impractical. That includes the “RITE” demonstration turbines in the East River in New York City, with the East River being a tidal waterway and not an actual river as commonly understood. Cobscook Bay and Western Passage in Maine are additional targets for tidal energy deployment, along with Cook Inlet in Alaska.

Other nations already have a running start on tidal energy. Among them is Sweden, where the startup Minesto has been developing its “Dragon Class” series of tidal energy devices that resemble high tech underwater kites. The Dragon devices are designed to optimize energy harvesting from low-flow tidal streams and ocean currents.

Minesto has been focusing its business on tourist destinations and other remote locations where the demand for renewable energy runs headlong into aesthetic objections. To date, the company lists active projects in the Faroe Islands, Wales and Taiwan.

In the latest news, earlier today Minesto described a new Memoradum of Understanding with the UK investment firm Morteza Capital, aimed at providing clean power solutions for the Republic of Maldives. The “Nopef” economic development fund has also chipped in to assist both the Maldives and Minesto. Nopef is supported by Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, the Faroe Islands, Greenland, and the Åland islands of Finland.

The Maldives is a ripe spot for tidal energy development. It consists of hundreds of low-lying islands that are particularly exposed to sea level rise and extreme weather. As a poster child for the climate crisis, the Maldives government aims to extricate itself from the grip of imported diesel fuel. However, the Maldives economy is heavily reliant on tourism, limiting opportunities for wind and solar energy.

That’s where tidal technology comes in. “The Maldives faces significant challenges in transitioning towards renewable energy. Its geographically dispersed islands rely heavily on imported diesel for electricity generation, resulting in high energy costs and carbon emissions. At the same time, tourism is a cornerstone of the country’s economy, placing particular importance on developing renewable energy solutions that preserve its pristine natural environment and scenic landscapes,” Minesto explains.

Perhaps some day in the sparkling green future, Minesto’s Dragon undersea kites will make their way to the US, much in the same way that other renewable energy hardware has been imported or manufactured here. In the meantime, it’s fair to ask why marine energy made the list of favored resources under Trump’s “American Energy Dominance” plan, while wind and solar did not. For that matter, hydropower, biomass, and geothermal are also enjoying the warm embrace of Trump’s “American Energy Dominance” policy, along with nuclear energy and energy storage.

Only wind and solar got the short of the stick, and why? Who knows! Regardless, solar has become the most economical, accessible, and quickest way to get more kilowatts into the hands of an energy-thirsty public.

And, despite Trump’s extraordinary efforts to banish wind turbines from view forever, the nation’s wind industry is already poised for a fresh round of growth largely fueled by new tax deadlines. According to the latest Wood Mackenzie report, the uptake of wind turbines hit a three-year high under Trump’s watch in Q2 of this year, and US onshore wind energy capacity is set to increase 39 gigawatts by 2030, which is just around the corner.

#5247. Ben Napier Says It ‘Felt Good’ To Hand ‘Home Town Takeover’ Spinoff to New Hosts—and Explains Why He and Erin Couldn’t Host It

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

HGTV’s smash show “Home Town” is officially international with the debut of “Home Town Takeover Canada,” and the accomplishment has left original host Ben Napier feeling very sentimental.

The new series premiered on Oct. 25 and is hosted by Bryan and Sarah Baeumler, whom reality TV fans will recognize as the hosts of “Renovation Island.”

Ben told People magazine the Bauemlers are “the perfect hosts” for the show.

“Bryan and Sarah are rock stars,” he said. “They’re crazy popular, and it felt good handing it off to them.”

Set in Port Alberni on Vancouver Island, the newest installation is part of the “Home Town Takeover” spinoff, which previously saw Ben and his wife and co-host, Erin Napier, traveling to transform small towns across the United States.

“This was never something that we even dreamed of, being on TV,” Ben said. “And so, the fact that this show about us working to bring back our little town has now gone to multiple states and now across the border to Canada is just crazy.”

While the Napiers hosted the spinoff of their original show for the first three seasons—focusing on Wetumpka, AL, Fort Morgan, CO, and Sebring, FL—they could be involved in “Home Town Takeover Canada” only as special guests, due to legal restrictions.

“Our production company is Canadian. The people who make our show, they’re passionate about this Canadian takeover,” Erin told Cinemablend. “But because we are Americans, we are not legally allowed to host the show. We can only be in it as, like, guest stars.”

“Yeah, a Canadian broadcast show has to be hosted by Canadian talent,” Ben confirmed.

Ben likened the experience of traveling to Canada to launch the series to being “on vacation,” as he and Erin were required to take a backseat for this particular set of projects.

“There were several times I jokingly told Bryan or Michael, who’s the project manager—they would ask me about something—and I would throw up my hands and say, ‘I’m on vacation. This is your little red wagon,’” he said.

Bryan and Sarah also opened up about the new series and the experience of putting their own mark on the legacy the Napiers had begun—which they have “so much respect for.”

“Erin and Ben have built something people love. As Canadians, getting to shine a light on a Canadian community makes it especially meaningful,” they said. “We wanted to bring that same heart to the show, along with our own approach to construction and design.”

Sarah also gave the Laurel, MS–based couple a special shoutout in an Oct. 26 Instagram Story.

“Thank you Ben and Erin for welcoming us into the Home Town Takeover family,” she wrote, sharing some photos of the couples on-set in Port Alberni.

In a preview for the series, Bryan and Sarah gush over their excitement “to tackle the biggest project—by far—of our lives,” noting that they had just three months to help “transform” the town, breathe new life into small businesses and residential homes, and give “back to people who are working hard to move it forward.”

“Home Town Takeover Canada” is not yet available to watch in the United States, but airs Sundays on HGTV in Canada and can be streamed on a Canadian service called Citytv+.

According to an Aug. 1 social media update from Erin, fans can expect more stateside “Home Town” on the way in the near future, as production is underway for Season 11 of the show—which debuted its first season in January 2016.

“When is more ‘Home Town’ coming? Soon!” she teased in a caption alongside an Instagram Reel breaking down the family’s daily routine during a “typical day” of filming, which she added “runs year round,” although they had previously been on a brief hiatus for the “summer break.”

While an official release date has yet to be confirmed, “Home Town” has historically set season premieres for December or January.
