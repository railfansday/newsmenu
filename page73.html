#3876. EA workers complain of ‘terrible’ morale amid layoffs and ‘heavily pushed’ generative AI

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"A cold dead place that's slowly decaying as leadership pilfers every last cent," one person said.

After last week’s shuttering of Mass Effect and Dragon Age studio BioWare, workers at EA are beginning to speak out, and they’re not happy.

In a new report by Chris Kerr at Vertical Slice, multiple sources from within the company sounded off about how everything feels while working at any of the company’s various studios, and to put it bluntly, the vibes are trash. And to no one’s surprise, some of it has to do with higher-ups pushing generative AI “to see how few people they can keep employed and still make a video game.”

“Morale has been terrible,” one source told Vertical Slice. “The line we kept hearing is ‘do less, better’ but it just felt like we were doing the same amount with less people. Upper management was very excited about Claude and the possibilities of [generative] AI, much to the chagrin of my team.”

“The message from every single manager I’ve heard is ‘this is the future, you have to get onboard,” said one person.

Amid EA’s announcements last week that it would “continue investing in its sports games, the online shooter Apex Legends and action-adventure projects, including the next Star Wars Jedi title and a new game starring Marvel’s Iron Man,” along with DICE and Respawn working together on a new title, likely a new Star Wars Battlefront.

“They’ve been fortunate that [EA Sports FC] sells and makes so much money that it can finance their bad bets,” another source said.

Vertical Slice said that numerous sources they spoke to all “expressed a striking sense of detachment and resentment towards their employer,” and went so far as to describe the company as “a cold dead place that’s slowly decaying as leadership pilfers every last cent, leaving the rest of us to worry about how we feed our families.”

And with more layoffs possible (if not extremely likely), one can only wonder how any of these games will get made if morale truly is this low.

The crypto figures appeared in SoFi's earnings for the first time, as record quarterly revenue of $1.39 billion sent shares up nearly 15%.

SoFi reported 503,000 crypto products at the end of the third quarter, according to its latest earnings release, which included the metric for the first time in its financial services product breakdown.

SoFi reported $161.6 million in gross crypto transaction revenue in the quarter, up 20% from the prior quarter. Net crypto transaction revenue totaled just $1.6 million after accounting for associated costs.

The fintech company's crypto products sit alongside its SoFi Invest brokerage offering, which grew 37% year over year to 4.18 million products as of Sept. 30. SoFi reported record third-quarter net revenue of $1.39 billion, up 44% year over year, and net income of $241 million.

"We had exceptional Q3 results against the backdrop of any environment, but they are even more remarkable given how 2026 has unfolded," said SoFi CEO Anthony Noto in the release. SoFi's stock traded higher by nearly 15% on Tuesday morning, although shares are still down 40% in the year-to-date period.

After two years pausing crypto services, SoFi eyed a return in May 2025 amid a more favorable regulatory environment. SoFi Crypto officially launched later that November, allowing members to buy and hold dozens of tokens directly from FDIC-insured checking or savings accounts.

The company has recently expanded into stablecoins, tokenized deposits, and crypto infrastructure.

#3100. Kawhi Leonard will be out at least 1 week because of swelling in left knee, Raptors say

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Kawhi Leonard popped up on the injury report Sunday, an hour before tip-off in Minnesota. The Toronto Raptors then held him out for what ended up being a 117-94 loss to the Timberwolves.

The 35-year-old Leonard, in his first season back with a Raptors franchise he led to its first NBA championship in 2018-19, was a late scratch due to swelling in his left knee.

That issue will keep him away from game action for at least one week, the team announced on Tuesday.

Also in their announcement, however, the Raptors said medical imaging "revealed no acute injury." That's a positive sign for Toronto at least, as Leonard begins a treatment and rehabilitation program to address his knee problem.

Granted the Raptors state that Leonard will be re-evaluated in one week, but ESPN's Shams Charania reported Tuesday that the forward is expected to miss "a period of weeks."

The Raptors and Leonard will use this time to ramp the two-time NBA Finals MVP back up following his offseason stoppages, per Charania.

After the NBA investigation surrounding the Los Angeles Clippers' salary cap circumvention delayed a trade that ultimately returned Leonard to Toronto, he was left with a limited runway to the regular season.

"Yeah it's always frustrating missing time," Leonard said in a message he posted to social media on Tuesday in the wake of his announced absence.

"I came back to compete with this team and I'm focused on getting back to 100. Toronto, I've appreciated the love since I've been back. Returning soon."

Because of Leonard's reduced preseason work, he averaged only 26 minutes in his first two regular-season outings. In the aftermath of the Raptors' opener, a 135-92 home victory over the Chicago Bulls, Leonard expressed that he was itching to play more in that contest. In fact, he actually asked head coach Darko Rajaković to let him back into the game in the fourth quarter of the blowout.

"We had a plan for him," Rajaković said postgame that night, "but he was like, 'Coach, I would like to play a little bit more there.' So, I mean, the game was kind of at that point a little bit out of the reach, but I wanted just to save him, and he was like, 'No, no, I want to go back.' So I put him back."

Leonard wrapped his season-opening performance with 23 points on 5-of-17 shooting, plus 6 rebounds, 4 steals, 2 assists and 1 block.

Even though he negotiated for more minutes against the Bulls, afterward, Leonard didn't offer a prognosis on when he'd return to midseason form.

"You can't rush greatness," Leonard said postgame back then, when a reporter inquired if the seven-time All-Star had a target for when he'd like to be at that level again.

"Whenever the medical [staff] thinks I could push forward and I'm feeling great, whatever numbers we're looking at or they're looking at, then that's when that time will come. So yeah, I'm just happy to be out there and try to do the best I can with my minutes."

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Our gifting experts are obsessively searching for holiday gifts that are unique, thoughtful and suitable for any budget.

The Fathom’s built-in inverter can keep the lights on in your home during an outage — not bad for under $30,000.

Ford’s upcoming Fathom electric truck — expected to arrive early next year — is reasonably priced at $28,350, especially given that the average transaction price for a new EV is closer to $55,000. But the added bonus is a feature that could save you money, and literally save your bacon.

The automaker’s “clean sheet” reboot of its EV ambitions will be offered with bidirectional charging, which allows the truck to send power from its battery back to the house it’s plugged into. That could keep the lights on and the fridge running during an extended power outage.

Historically, true bidirectional power has been a premium feature on more expensive electric cars and has required additional, costly home hardware. Ford says it’s bringing costs down by building the tech into what will be its cheapest EV yet and making it more accessible to a wider range of homeowners.

So far, Ford hasn’t revealed much of the electric truck beyond the name and camouflaged prototypes.

The upcoming Fathom will be based on what Ford calls its Universal EV Platform, designed with the aid of its 2023 acquisition, startup Auto Motive Power, with bidirectional power on the plate from the start.

The hardware required to invert the battery’s DC energy to an estimated 10 kW of AC home power will be built into the truck, so it won’t require a separate wall-mounted inverter box. Ford says the Fathom’s system will meet all applicable UL 1741 requirements and IEEE 1547 standards to ensure the vehicle is safely connected to the grid.

As for the actual hardware you plug into your car, all signs point to the Fathom using the NACS plug type pioneered by Tesla and now widely adopted by new EVs in North America.

In terms of the communication standard — the one that governs how AC power is routed from the slim wall box to the car’s onboard charger — that’ll be via SAE J3068, a software protocol developed for high-power, heavy-duty AC charging.

Ford says that bringing the power-routing hardware directly into the vehicle reduces the cost of Fathom’s wall charger, making it easier for homeowners who need multiple chargers, whether for a multi-EV household, a second home or a detached garage. In other words, you pay for the advanced bidirectional technology once (built into the vehicle’s price) rather than buying it multiple times for each wall outlet you want to install around your property.

However, while Fathom’s Universal EV Platform eliminates the need for a wall inverter box, it doesn’t completely bypass the need for additional hardware to make your home ready to accept bidirectional power.

Say you wanted to take advantage of the Home Power Management feature to draw energy into the Fathom’s battery when power is cheapest, and then use that energy to offset peak-hour usage when power is most expensive. You’ll have to install at least the Ford Home Power Station in your home, at an additional cost.

The automaker hasn’t specified pricing, but says that it’s working to reduce costs and expects this component alone to cost half as much as the first-generation Ford Home Energy system, which debuted at around $1,300. That could put the new system at roughly $650.

For full-featured Ford Home Backup Power, which lets the EV supply power to the house during a power outage, you’ll need both Ford’s Home Power Station and Home Backup Power Panel, each a separate purchase. Here again, Ford expects to price its second-generation hardware “at least 60% lower” than the equivalent first-gen package, which cost roughly $5,000. So expect a ballpark of $2,000 for that extra peace of mind that your fridge won’t spoil when the lights go out.

That’s just the cost of the equipment itself. Your installation costs will depend on where you live and your specific house. Still, making the hardware less expensive for your home and vehicle is a step in the right direction. And considering the rising cost of energy (both electricity and fuel), this setup may pay for itself relatively quickly.
