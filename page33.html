#3463. ASM lifts its 2027 sales forecast by a quarter as AI chipmakers spend

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Dutch chip-equipment maker now expects to hit its 2030 sales goal three years early and plans a €300M buyback.

ASM International raised its 2027 revenue forecast to more than €5.7B, from above €4.6B, as memory and AI chipmakers expand. That reaches its 2030 target three years early. Third-quarter revenue rose 44% to a record €1.16B, and ASM plans a buyback of up to €300M.

ASM International has raised its 2027 sales forecast by about 24% as companies building AI data centres drive demand for chips, Bloomberg reported. The Dutch chip-equipment maker now expects revenue of more than €5.7B next year, up from above €4.6B forecast in July.

That puts ASM on track to hit its 2030 sales goal three years early, the company said on Tuesday. It also plans to buy back up to €300M of its shares.

ASM makes machines that lay down very thin layers of material on silicon wafers, a key step in making chips. Demand is coming from memory makers and from firms producing advanced chips for AI. It is one of several European firms profiting from the AI boom by selling tools rather than building AI models.

“Capacity remains tight across our customer base, and customers are moving quickly to increase output,” said chief executive Hichem M’Saad.

Third-quarter revenue rose 44% at constant currencies to a record €1.16B, while net income fell to €308.1M. Both beat analysts’ estimates. ASM’s US-listed shares rose 6.7% on Tuesday.

Its customers include TSMC, which plans to spend up to $64B this year after a sixth straight record profit, and Intel. ASM warned that its own supply chain is “stretched,” but said it is working with suppliers and customers to ship faster.

Fellow Dutch toolmaker ASML said this month that it cannot meet demand in 2027 or 2028 at current output levels.

#4777. CFTC sues Utah to block action against prediction markets

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The US state filed a lawsuit against Kalshi last week, seeking to recover up to $60 million in profits related to event contracts on prediction markets.

The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) announced legal action against Utah officials after the state’s attorney general said he would sue to compel Kalshi and its affiliates to return up to $60 million in profits allegedly obtained illegally.

In a Monday filing in the US District Court for the District of Utah, lawyers for the commodities regulator sued the State of Utah and its Attorney General Derek Brown a week after officials announced legal action against prediction market platform Kalshi and its affiliates over allegedly illegal betting.

According to the CFTC, Utah was attempting to bypass congressional intent to “federally preempt state law” by “restrict[ing] the functioning of CFTC-registered exchanges.” CFTC Chair Michael Selig has been claiming for months that the regulator has “exclusive jurisdiction” over prediction markets, leading to several lawsuits between state and federal authorities.

“The event contracts targeted by Utah are ‘swaps’ under the federal [Commodity Exchange Act], and the exchanges that offer these event contracts for trading are CFTC-regulated Designated Contract Markets [DCMs],” said the lawsuit. “Utah’s efforts to prevent a CFTC-regulated DCM from offering CFTC-approved financial products intrude on the exclusive federal scheme Congress designed to oversee United States commodity derivatives markets.”

Toyota has initiated a new recall, and it’s a serious one. According to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, 228,367 Toyota Sienna Hybrid 7-seat minivans are being recalled due to a dangerous defect in the second row, where seat backs “may fail to remain secured in the event of a crash.” As a family vehicle that transports kids, this is particularly worrisome, and the recall (26V690) covers vehicles produced from September 16, 2021, to July 20, 2026. That means it affects model years 2022-2026, but only a very specific few that were built with seatbelt pretensioners from a certain supplier.

The NHTSA recall report explains the issue in detail and says that the vehicles in question have an integrated seatbelt with a pretensioner that quickly retracts during high-speed frontal crashes, securing the passenger. The seatbelt systems feature an outboard recliner that uses interlocking teeth to secure the belt, and when the pretensioner deploys, it creates oscillation between the cam and pawls within this recliner mechanism. In certain scenarios – with variables including the type of crash, the seat back’s position, and pretensioner deployment times – this oscillation can allow the cam to rotate. If this happens, the recliner mechanism disengages from the locked position, and that can have obvious negative consequences. But despite this issue appearing across multiple model years, it was only discovered last year. Fortunately, it was discovered by Toyota, not an extremely unlucky buyer.

The Best Minivans for 2027: Four Contenders, One Clear Winner

These large, comfortable, and feature-packed vans should be even more popular than they are.

Over the course of January to November 2025, Toyota says it was conducting sled testing using a modified seatbelt and second-row seat assembly with an eye to finding improvements for future seats when certain tests revealed the disengagement of the rear seatback’s outboard recliner mechanism. This led to an investigation that ran from December 2025 to August of this year. More sled testing was conducted, during which Toyota found that internal friction affected the recliner. Yet more testing was undertaken, and the automaker began field recoveries to assess how Sienna second-row seat assemblies being used every day were performing. Over September and October, Toyota conducted crash tests to validate the results of sled testing, and that’s when it discovered the issue and began the process of initiating a voluntary recall.

Dealers have already been informed of the impending Toyota Sienna recall, but owners will only be notified of a remedy in December. When the remedy is ready, Toyota Siennas must be brought to dealers for assessment, and most vehicles will need nothing more than an updated SRS ECU software file to better control the pretensioner. However, roughly 200 2022-2025 Sienna Hybrid minivans will need their two second-row seatbacks replaced with new components featuring a different recliner mechanism. Whichever group owners fall into, a dealer visit is necessary, and the fix will be applied at no cost.
