Morning Minute: Tokenized Stock Battle Looms Over Robinhood’s Earnings Call

Somebody bought a full-page ad in the Wall Street Journal this weekend to tell public company CEOs that crypto is coming for their shares.

“YOUR STOCK. THEIR TOKENS. YOUR PROBLEM,” it read, paid for by the Common Sense Leadership Fund, a conservative nonprofit. They listed eight risks - pricing disparities, obscured ownership, impaired capital raising, cybertheft, market manipulation, AML and KYC loopholes, absent investor protections, legal liability. And the call to action was blunt: the SEC has given you the right to opt out, you have 30 days, this is your only chance.

The war against tokenized stocks is brewing.

Nine companies objected to having their shares tokenized on the OKX-ICE venue on October 15. By October 19 it was 16. By October 23, the list hit 22, including Wells Fargo, Cisco, Marvell, Arm, Moderna and Intuit joining Microsoft, Alphabet, Goldman Sachs, Verizon, Broadcom and UnitedHealth. That’s a two-and-a-half-fold increase in eight days, and 35% of the 63 companies the venue planned to list.

OKXICE even amended its rules on October 19 to let issuers tokenize their own shares, which would hand them control. Not one objector has taken it. They don’t want a better version of the product. They don’t want it.

Robinhood reports third-quarter results after the bell, and no company has more riding on how this fight resolves.

Robinhood Chain launched its public mainnet on July 1. In its debut quarter it generated roughly $51 million in revenue. That’s more than Ethereum’s $41 million, while Ethereum’s own network revenue fell 35%. It processed about $65 billion in DEX volume, fourth among all networks.

Of that, $13.7 billion was tokenized equities. One fifth of everything trading on the chain, in a single quarter, from a zero start.

Robinhood’s stock tokens have already drawn fire (AMC’s CEO called them “contemptible”) and the company is building an entire ecosystem on top of them. Long.xyz pairs memecoins against tokenized NVDA and TSLA, and meme-versus-stock pairs now drive most stock-token volume on the chain. Orbio funds AI inference credits with creator fees paid in tokenized NVDA. Pull the stock tokens out, and a lot of that architecture has nothing to stand on.

So will Vlad Tenev address the tokenized stock war? It’s the billion-dollar question. A tweet from Monday makes it seem like perhaps he will. We will find out soon enough...

#3415. Windows finally gets a built-in internet speed test tool – how to try it

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Our gifting experts are obsessively searching for holiday gifts that are unique, thoughtful and suitable for any budget.

Tech > Services & Software > Operating Systems > Windows > Windows 11

Available to Windows Insiders, the built-in tool measures your upload and download speeds over Ethernet or Wi-Fi.

Does the internet performance on your Windows PC ever feel sluggish? Testing the actual speed can tell you if it matches what you expect or what’s promised from your internet provider. To run a speed test in the past, you typically had to go to a dedicated website or install a special app. Now Microsoft is rolling out an integrated speed test tool designed to save your time.

Also: The right way to run an internet speed test

Currently available to Windows Insiders using the Experimental channel, the new built-in internet speed tester measures your download and upload speeds over Ethernet or Wi-Fi. The tool first tests your download performance and then segues to uploads. The final results show you the average speeds for both.

To try the new tool, you need to be part of Microsoft’s Windows Insider Program if you haven’t already joined. When setting this up, make sure you choose the Experimental channel. Since these Insider builds can be unstable, don’t do this on your primary PC. Instead, use a spare computer, a dual-boot setup, or a virtual machine. I run the Insider builds in a VM through VMware Workstation Pro.

Even with the Experimental channel, I had to enable a special flag for the test. To do this, go to Settings, select Windows Update, and then click Windows Insider Program. Select the option for Feature Flags and scroll down the screen to the entry for Quick Settings Speed Test. Click the drop-down button next to it and change the setting to Enabled if it’s not already set that way. Then restart your PC.

Also: Slow Wi-Fi? I found 10 proven ways to fix your internet

After you log back in, you can access the new speed test one of two ways. Left-click on the System tray and choose Speed test from the Quick Settings menu. Otherwise, right-click on the Network icon and select “Test internet speed” from the pop-up menu. With the tool open, just click the Test button to kick it off. The graph will show the download and upload speeds as they’re being tested, with the results giving you the numbers for both.

If this sounds familiar, that’s because Windows 11 already includes an internet speed test tool. In your current version of Windows 11, right-click on the Network icon in the System tray and choose “Perform speed test” from the menu. But as you’ll see, this test isn’t integrated into Windows. Instead, it takes you to a Bing search page where you then have to run the test and see the results on the page. In contrast, the new tool is built into Windows, making it a vast improvement over the existing one.

Also: Ookla’s Wi-Fi diagnostic tool offers one-tap troubleshooting

“Because the test runs against your active internet connection, you can quickly understand how your current connection is performing without leaving your workflow,” Microsoft said in its blog post. “This updated experience builds on the internet speed test we previously introduced for Windows Insiders, bringing the test and its results directly into Windows.”

Microsoft didn’t reveal the source behind the speed test. But the Info icon for the tool says “Powered by SpeedTest,” an indication that this is from internet speed tester Ookla. To gauge the results, I ran both the built-in speed test and the one at Ookla’s website several times, and the performance for both downloads and uploads were similar between the two.

Since the new speed test is only now hitting the Insider Experimental channel, we might have to wait a while before it officially rolls out to all Windows 11 users. But the tool is convenient to use, runs smoothly, and appears to be accurate. So, hopefully we won’t have to wait too long.

#395. We tested the most popular Ninja appliances, and these are our favorites

📅 Oct 27, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The popularity of Ninja and Shark appliances is undeniable. The brand touches nearly every room in your home. It makes vacuums to keep your floors clean, air purifiers to refresh the air, hair stylers for at-home blowouts, and an appliance for every step of the cooking process. But with such an expansive catalog, it can be hard to sort through the best of the brand.

These are the tech, tools, and products — from laptops to e-readers, from earbuds to robovacs, and more — that Mashable ranks best in class.

We wanted to test our way through the best Ninja had to offer in kitchen appliances. We brought the most popular products from the brand into our kitchens to see how the Creami, Slushi, Foodi, and Ninja Kitchen System stacked up.

Read our full review of the Ninja Creami Scoop and Swirl.

If you are an ice cream enthusiast in need of a sweet, frozen treat every night, you need the Ninja Creami Scoop and Swirl. It builds off Ninja's existing Creami technology to spin and churn your homemade pints of ice cream, froyo, and fruit whip. However, the Swirl also has a soft serve dispenser for serving up creamy, fluffy swirls of ice cream. It's the most impressive Creami for the hosts with the most, but it's also just straight-up fun.

Having tested both the basic Creami and the Scoop and Swirl, the latter is the best ice cream maker from Ninja. Its ease of use is exceptional. To use, mix up your concoctions and let the pints freeze for 24 hours. Once frozen, put them in the Creami, where a blade spins through the ice cream to give it a fluffy, smooth texture.

Any model in the Creami lineup is great, but the Swirl is the best because it nails texture. The Swirl manages to get more into the pints when spinning them for a light and spreadable treat. When making my favorite chocolate hazelnut frozen yogurt, it only needed one spin in the Swirl to get the perfect texture. Previously, when I used the Ninja Creami Deluxe, I needed to spin the ice cream several times to get the perfect whipped consistency.

My caution to those interested in a Ninja Swirl is that it's expensive and quite large, occupying a lot of counter space. However, for an ice cream aficionado, it'll be a worthwhile investment. And word of warning: it's so loud, but that will be the case for any Ninja Creami model you go for.

If you're trying to make the decision between investing in a blender or a food processor, the answer is to get both and get them with the Ninja Blender Kitchen System. This blender is for the kitchen multitasker who wants to do it all without shelling out money for multiple appliances. It's for beginners and kitchen professionals alike, as you can use manual settings or use BlendSense technology to take the thought out of it.

Maybe it's because I've been an apartment dweller for so long, but my biggest pet peeve is a one-use appliance. If you're going to invest in a new kitchen tool, you want it to work overtime. That's why I adore the Ninja Detect Kitchen System, it's a full-sized blender, food processor, and personal blender.

Having owned and used multi-use kitchen appliances, I find that you sometimes have to make some sacrifices and lose features. That's not the case with the Ninja Detect Kitchen System. It has an incredibly powerful motor and no matter what attachment you're using, you can choose from a variety of settings with each attachment.

The device uses one base to run everything, which saves space on multiple bases. Just swap out the attachments, whether that be the personal-sized blade and cup or the big 72-ounce pitcher. It's exceptionally powerful and able to quickly mince vegetables or crush ice.

While it's nice to have a single base for the different attachments, it saves space in one sense. However, beware that the attachments are still very large and take up quite a bit of room.

Air fryers are great for anyone, but the Ninja Crispi is made for meal preppers. With its portable design, it stores nicely and can be used on the go. Plus, it actually works. The controls are simple, and the glass baskets come with storage lids for easy food storage.

Since we originally tested this device, it's become a favorite of the Mashable shopping team. Not to mention, the Crispi has gotten quite a bit of attention online as folks seek a plastic-free air fryer, and the Crispi is really the best option available.

There are certain devices I look at and think, there's no way that's going to work, always hoping to be proven wrong. The Ninja Crispi is one of those devices, with its glass basket and air-frying lid, I was skeptical of its efficacy. I'm pleased to say I was wrong. It delivered food that was crispy on the outside and fluffy on the inside.

When it comes to testing air fryer crispiness, my go-to trials are always tofu and frozen French fries. The Ninja Crispi handled both with flying colors. In less than 20 minutes, it air-fried cubes of tofu tossed in cornstarch to crunchy perfection. Frozen French fries also turned out great. I tossed a handful into the container, not bothering to lay them without any overlap, and still, the Ninja Crispi made every fry perfectly crispy on the outside while remaining soft on the inside.

Air fryers are an appliance of convenience, able to get food hot and crispy quickly, and the Ninja Crispi maximizes that with its design that allows you to meal prep and store food directly into glass baskets, where you will then reheat it. The storage containers are all dishwasher safe, a rare trait in air fryer baskets.

Compared to other air fryers, the settings on this device may be a little limited, as you can't adjust the temperature. However, I have tested the Crispi on a variety of foods, and I didn't miss being able to adjust the temperature.

If you've ever felt intimidated by using an espresso machine, the Ninja Luxe Café Premier strikes the perfect balance of user-friendly design with high-end settings.

Plus, if you're a divided household, with latté drinkers or drip coffee loyalists, this machine bridges the gap with the ability to grind and brew both espresso and coffee. This machine may not be for professional baristas, but it will be for someone who has expert taste but prioritizes convenience.

While my partner and I are espresso enthusiasts, any time our parents come into town, we run into the problem of not having a good drip coffee setup. The Ninja Luxe Café Premier is the ideal solution for offering both without any pods. Instead, it grinds beans to serve brew espresso, coffee, and cold brew. It has adjustable grind settings while providing suggested grind settings based on your chosen coffee setting — much easier than troubleshooting on a standard espresso machine.

Using the machine is pretty foolproof. While I do have a barista background and know my way around an espresso machine, I enjoyed just how easy it was to use. The machine tells you exactly how fine to grind your beans, grinds them, and then pulls an espresso shot with a rich layer of crema, all with the press of a button.

Milk frothing is automated on the Luxe Café Premier. The milk pitcher includes a steaming wand as well as a circular frother at the bottom. While I enjoyed the seamlessness of getting steamed milk, this is where the machine won't be for everyone. If you prefer to steam your own silky microfoam for lattés, you won't be able to with this machine, as you can't angle the wand without placing the pitcher on its pedestal.

I've had this machine for over a year and still love it, using it daily to make both espresso, cold brew, and drip coffee.

Put away the blender! If you're going to make frozen drinks this summer, let it be with the Ninja Slushi. It's a little extra but if you're looking to host all summer long and want something to impress your guests, do it with the Ninja Slushi, which freezes and spins drinks until they're icy.

You can make frozen drinks with a lot of different appliances, like a blender or a Ninja Creami, but the most foolproof way is with the Ninja Slushi. It's certainly not a necessary appliance, but if you're a frozen drink fanatic, it's essential.

Unlike the Creami, it freezes drinks in the machine and churns them until it has the perfect slushy texture. It does require a bit of math work, as you need the right sugar or alcohol levels to perfect the texture.

That being said, at $349.99, it's quite expensive. As my colleague Leah Stodart pointed out, people pay twice that for an espresso machine, so depending on your enthusiasm for frozen drinks, it may be a worthy price to pay.

So you only have space for one appliance in your kitchen, and you can't decide between a rice cooker and a slow cooker? Choose the Ninja Foodi Everyday Possible Cooker. This device doesn't look like a lot, essentially a hot plate and non-stick pot, but it's incredible how much it can do. It makes rice in the blink of an eye, can boil a pound of pasta, and slow-cooks food to tender perfection.

Ninja makes some big claims with the Ninja Foodi Everyday PossibleCooker as it supposedly makes rice 50 percent faster than a traditional rice cooker. As the No. 1 fan of my rice cooker, I obviously had to test this claim first. The Ninja Foodi does indeed make rice 50 percent faster — almost.

While my rice cooker cooked one cup of white rice in 25 minutes, the Ninja took just 15 minutes, and even better, it was perfectly fluffy without any overcooked crisp bits on the bottom. And it does much more than that. I made slow-cooked BBQ chicken, and it produced tender, moist chicken that was a crowd-pleaser.

Ninja has plenty of other multicookers, but this one is my favorite because it's the most affordable option and replaces multiple appliances at once.

There's a big camp of people who need their water to be flavored. If you happen to be in that group, then the Ninja Thirsti might prove itself to be an essential kitchen appliance. It delivers both sparkling and still options and can infuse water with flavor. There are subtle, unsweetened options like a hint of lime or caffeinated, sweet fruity options for at-home energy drinks.

Having previously used Sodastream carbonated drink makers, I was pleased by Ninja's higher-tech spin on CO2 machines. Rather than pumping CO2 into bottles of water, the Ninja Thirsti delivers personalized glasses of flavor-infused beverages. It allows you to adjust flavor and carbonation levels for a customized drink.

The flavor add-in is what sets the Ninja Thirsti apart from other drink makers. With others, you need to drop in flavor after carbonation, but the Ninja Thirsti infuses it right into your cup, taking out the guesswork of flavoring your drink. However, to use this feature, you have to use Ninja's own flavor pods.

There's one part of the Ninja Thirsti that I really don't like, and it's that the brand recommends using cold water for the best product. For room temperature water enthusiasts, this is not ideal. Plus, it requires refrigerating the water ahead of time, which requires some forethought. Not to mention, the water tank's lid isn't super secure, leaving it susceptible to spillage.

Otherwise, this is a fantastic carbonated drink maker, best for the flavored water devotee.

There are plenty of kitchen appliance brands out there, so why Ninja? Generally, we find its products to be some of the best value products. They tend to be the most affordable options on the market that still deliver excellent performance.

Plus, Ninja products are really beginner-friendly devices that are easy to use with minimal setup required. The appliances bring ease with delicious end products. The Creami is a perfect example of this. Traditional ice cream making requires a bit of skill, knowing how to cook custards to the right temperature with hours of churning. But the Creami simplifies ice cream making with a user-friendly product that still delivers a fantastic end result.

Just because they're beginner-friendly devices doesn't mean they're only for beginners. They're convenient devices with professional-level performance that help develop a more efficient kitchen.

Having rounded up the best Ninja kitchen appliances means we tested a lot of products and not all of them made it to the list, two notable exceptions being the Ninja Creami Deluxe and Foodi Grill and Air Fryer.

The Ninja Deluxe was my introduction to the Creami lineup, and it is great. It spun up creamy frozen confections and even featured a frozen drink setting. However, compared to the Swirl, it required several re-spins to get the right texture.

Meanwhile, the Foodi Grill was another great multicooker from Ninja. The indoor grill made delicious, juicy chicken breasts with actual char marks. Plus, its air-frying capabilities were a little too good. It got things crispy fast, but often felt like it dried out food in the process. Plus, it was just so large that it didn't feel like a practical device for most people.

That being said, these devices are good, but not in our all-star line-up.

The United States Forest Service team at Tongass National Forest have awarded the official 2026 Worst at Hide-and-Seek Award to a baby bear who needs a bit more practice at this game. The baby bear belongs to Osso, who won the 2026 Mama Bear Award for her prowess.

“It [the cub] stole our Rangers’ hearts,” the team writes. “It looked like it wanted to play hide and seek but couldn’t fully grasp the concept. Our Rangers were glad the cub was bad at the game, because it meant they saw it more.”

Osso had three cubs this season. However, Rangers only spotted her with two cubs in August and believe that one died. Black bear cub survival rate can range from 51 to 88 percent depending on the location.

Tongass is the nation’s largest national forest and covers most of Southeast Alaska, including the capital city of Juneau. The area includes Mendanhall Glacier (a popular destination for Alaskan cruises), numerous hiking trails, eagles, and plenty of bears. One of the hotspots is Anan Creek, which is home to one of the largest runs of pink salmon in Southeast Alaska.

According to Friends of Anan, 39 known bears feast on the salmon at Anan Creek. The population includes 31 black bears (Ursus americanus) and eight brown bears (Ursus arctos). Like their counterparts further to the north in Katmai National Park, the bears return here every summer to eat salmon and teach their young how to fish and survive. And also like the bears of Katmai, every bear here has their own habits.

The U.S. Forest Service tracks and documents every bear and explore.org’s cameras broadcast live streams of their antics.

This new tool proves it's still best to limit the info you share with chatbots.

Researchers at Georgia Tech have created a tool that can recover deleted conversations with AI assistants from Android phones. That has potentially promising implications for getting other types of erased information off phones during investigations, for example. But it also means your on-device AI chats, which are supposed to be private, may have a longer shelf life than intended.

Many privacy-conscious AI users prefer to run assistants locally on their devices, because their data isn’t sent out to the cloud, where it could be vulnerable to prying eyes or data breaches. Instead, the chat session uses your device memory.

But, as it turns out, some information from those sessions can stick around in your phone’s working memory even if you’ve deleted the chats themselves. Georgia Tech researchers created ORISA, a tool that searches that memory for clues to deleted conversations. They designed a fake note-keeping AI assistant for their experiment using the ChatterUI Android app, gave it a set of 100 fictional pieces of information about a project, and then deleted that information.

Also: You can run local AI on your Android or iOS device

Using ORISA, the researchers then searched the device memory for remnants of that information that could be used as context to restart the session. ORISA found enough usable information to create a new working session with 26 fictional facts from the original set of 100. That’s more than the 14 the original assistant session had access to, “because the rest had fallen outside its active memory,” the researchers explained.

“It can help establish what information someone shared with an AI assistant and what assistance they received,” Ph.D. student Ken Xu, and lead author of the study, said of the experiment.

ORISA was developed to fill a cybersecurity gap. The researchers noted that a downside of local AI is its lack of “centralized logging and enforcement.” That means bad actors can fine-tune models for crimes, for example, and then make the evidence untraceable by deleting them after they’ve used them.

Until now, that reality has made it difficult for investigators using forensic memory techniques to recover useful evidence of that activity.

“ORISA leverages the batch tokens and key-value (KV) caches preserved in process memory to reconstruct token sequences and restore session-level attention states,” the researchers wrote.

That means a tool like this is most relevant to those trying to stop cybercrime rather than to those digging into your personal data. That said, if hackers recreate a technology like this or get their hands on a tool like ORISA itself, they could theoretically use it to gather deleted information from your chats and recreate your queries using that information as context. But ORISA’s efficacy also depends on time — the researchers noted that it’s harder to recover deleted information as time goes on, because your phone’s memory gets overwritten by newer activity.

Either way, our long-standing advice remains: Don’t share sensitive information with chatbots, even in a local session.
