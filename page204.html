#4014. FBI investigating theft of more than 6000 PSA-graded trading cards, including almost 3000 Pokémon cards

📅 Oct 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

"Recovering every stolen item and returning it to its owner is our top priority."

The FBI and Santa Ana police are investigating the theft of 6451 PSA-graded cards, including nearly 3000 Pokémon cards, from a FedEx truck in California.

PSA confirmed the investigation, saying the orders were stolen on 13th October from a FedEx truck after pickup at its Santa Ana facility.

The company says that "recovering every stolen item and returning it to its owner is our top priority", and that it has given investigators video footage, shipment manifests, and item-level records.

In total, 6451 items were taken. PSA has published a list of every stolen card, including its certification number and, for serialised cards, the serial number. It deactivated the certification numbers and shared the list with marketplace sellers, auction houses, and digital commerce platforms, so the cards can't be easily resold.

According to TheGamer, which broke down the list by category, Pokémon cards account for 2978 of the stolen items, followed by 898 One Piece cards, 880 basketball cards, 640 baseball cards, and 433 football cards. The New York Times reports that the haul is collectively worth more than $963,000.

PSA has refunded the grading fees on all affected orders, and says these refunds are separate from compensation for the cards themselves. Orders sent with a PSA return label are covered by its insurance, with payments already going out. Customers who used their own label will work with a dedicated PSA team, and those who bought through a dealer should contact them directly. Compensation is based on fair market value, up to each item's declared maximum insured value, and customers can choose to return the item if it is recovered.

Anyone with information is asked to contact the FBI's Los Angeles office or Santa Ana police.

In July, Cheshire Police confirmed that brothers Keith and Shane Johnson had been caught after breaking into Celestial Collectables and stealing Pokémon cards to the value of £62,000.

NEAR Protocol's open interest has dropped 9.5% over the past 24 hours, according to data from Coinalyze. This coincides with a 7.9% decline in the token's price.

NEAR is currently trading near $4.50, with a market capitalization of approximately $5.86 billion and daily trading volume of around $618 million.

Open interest measures the total number or value of outstanding futures and perpetual contracts that have not been settled or closed. It helps gauge the amount of exposure remaining in the derivatives market, although it does not independently indicate whether traders are bullish or bearish.

When prices and open interest fall simultaneously, the combination generally suggests deleveraging. Traders may be voluntarily closing positions, or positions may be liquidated as prices move against them.

The decline in open interest therefore indicates that derivatives exposure is shrinking rather than being aggressively rebuilt.

NEAR has lost approximately 13.2% over the past seven days, reversing part of a powerful September rally that lifted the token from below $2 to above $5.

The price has also retreated from its recent high near $5.86, suggesting that selling pressure has strengthened in the short term.

Despite the correction, NEAR remains substantially above its summer lows. The key question is whether buyers can defend current support levels or whether continued deleveraging will lead to another leg down.

The $4.50-$4.55 area is the first support zone to monitor. A sustained move below this range could expose NEAR to the Oct. 8 low near $4.29. A further decline would weaken the recovery structure established during the summer.

On the upside, NEAR needs to reclaim the $4.90-$5.00 range to signal that buyers are regaining control. A breakout above that zone could open the way toward $5.33-$5.45.

Near Protocol continues to develop its cross-chain and AI-related infrastructure, but those longer-term themes have not prevented the token from correcting alongside the broader market.

For now, the combination of falling prices and declining open interest suggests that traders are reducing risk.

Whether NEAR stabilizes around $4.50 or extends its losses will depend on broader market conditions, derivatives activity and the token's ability to recover key technical levels.
